Forgive and forget

Real forgiveness means wiping the offence from your memory entirely — if you still remember what someone did, you have not truly forgiven them.

What The Bible Says

Forgiveness is commanded without question: Matthew 6:14 promises the Father's forgiveness to those who forgive men their trespasses, Ephesians 4:32 tells us to forgive one another as God in Christ forgave us, and Colossians 3:13 says the same. But forgetting is never commanded of us — the 'remembering no more' in Hebrews 8:12 is God's promise about His own forgiveness, not an order for ours, and 1 Corinthians 13:5 says love thinketh no evil, which is about not keeping a score, not about losing your memory. I hold that the saying confuses God's forgetting with ours: we are told to release the debt and refuse revenge, not to pretend the past never happened.

Details

Saying: Forgive and forget
Closest Verse: Hebrews 8:12
Origin: The phrase dates from the 1300s and was a proverb by the mid-1500s; a well-known early form is Cervantes' 'Let us forget and forgive injuries' in Don Quixote, and Shakespeare has 'Pray you now, forget and forgive' in King Lear. It comes from literature, not from Scripture.
Related Books: Matthew; Ephesians; Hebrews; Colossians
Related Topics: Forgiveness; Mercy; Reconciliation
