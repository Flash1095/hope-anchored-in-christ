The seven deadly sins

People take it as a given that the Bible lists seven deadly sins — pride, greed, lust, envy, gluttony, wrath and sloth — a fixed set, named and counted in Scripture.

What The Bible Says

Each of those vices is warned against in Scripture, but the set of seven is not from the Bible at all — it is a church list, shaped by monks and popes centuries after the apostles. The verse people usually mean is Proverbs 6:16-19: "These six things doth the LORD hate: yea, seven are an abomination unto him" — but that list is a proud look, a lying tongue, hands that shed innocent blood, a heart that devises wicked imaginations, feet swift in running to mischief, a false witness, and one who sows discord. Only pride appears on both lists. My position is that the seven deadly sins are a useful catalogue of human failure, but they are the church's list, not the Bible's, and the Bible's own seven are mostly outward acts — lying, murder, perjury, sowing division — while the church's seven are mostly inward appetites.

Details

Saying: The seven deadly sins
Closest Verse: Proverbs 6:16
Origin: The list began as eight "evil thoughts" of the desert monk Evagrius Ponticus (c. 346-399), was carried west by John Cassian (c. 360-435) in The Institutes, and was reshaped into seven by Pope Gregory the Great in his Moralia in Job (c. 590): vainglory folded into pride, sadness into acedia (sloth), envy added, pride set as the root. Thomas Aquinas later expounded Gregory's list as the "capital sins" in the Summa Theologica (1265-1274), and the Catechism of the Catholic Church names them in paragraph 1866.
Related Books: Proverbs; Galatians
