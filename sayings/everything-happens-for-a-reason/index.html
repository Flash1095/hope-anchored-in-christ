Everything happens for a reason

People take it to mean that every event, even tragedy and evil, was planned by God for a purpose, so nothing is ever truly senseless.

What The Bible Says

Romans 8:28 says God works all things together for good for those who love Him: He redeems what happens, which is not the same as saying He caused it or that it was good. Joseph told his brothers, "ye thought evil against me; but God meant it for good" (Genesis 50:20): their evil was real evil, and God bent it toward good. Jesus refused the same shortcut when a tower fell and killed eighteen people, saying they were no worse than anyone else (Luke 13:4). I believe the saying goes too far: it turns every wound into God's plan, when Scripture says God is sovereign over suffering without being the author of evil, and that He brings good out of what was never good.

Details

Saying: Everything happens for a reason
Closest Verse: Romans 8:28
Origin: A modern saying with no single known author. Early forms of the sentiment appear in 19th-century writing, George Borrow's Lavengro (1851) and Jules Verne (1874), and the exact modern wording appears in American magazines around 1916-1917. It is usually hung on Romans 8:28, which says something different.
Related Books: Romans; Genesis; Luke
