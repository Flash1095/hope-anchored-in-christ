The road to hell is paved with good intentions

Good intentions on their own count for nothing — a person can mean well all the way to ruin, because meaning well is not the same as doing well.

What The Bible Says

Proverbs 14:12 warns that "there is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death," and Jesus said the broad way leads to destruction (Matthew 7:13). James adds the other half: knowing good and not doing it is sin (James 4:17), and faith without works is dead (James 2:17). I believe the proverb's warning is thoroughly biblical — the Bible is full of people who meant well and still went wrong — but the sentence itself is an English proverb, not a verse.

Details

Saying: The road to hell is paved with good intentions
Closest Verse: Proverbs 14:12
Origin: A proverb with no single author and a disputed history. It is often attributed to Bernard of Clairvaux, but that attribution comes only from François de Sales writing in 1604, some 500 years later, and the line is not found in Bernard's works. Early English forms appear from the 1500s; John Wesley quoted "Hell is paved, saith one, with good intentions" in a 1741 sermon, and the full modern wording first appears in print in Henry G. Bohn's A Hand-book of Proverbs (1855).
Related Books: Proverbs; Matthew; James
Related Topics: Hell; Judgment; Good works; Wisdom
