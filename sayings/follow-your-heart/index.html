Follow your heart

Your heart knows the way — trust your feelings, follow what feels right, and you will end up where you belong.

What The Bible Says

The Bible says the opposite, and bluntly: "The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it?" (Jeremiah 17:9). Numbers 15:39 warns the people not to seek after their own heart and their own eyes, and Proverbs 3:5 says to trust in the LORD with all thine heart and lean not unto thine own understanding. The verse people sometimes quote for it, Psalm 37:4 — "He shall give thee the desires of thine heart" — only works if you delight yourself in the LORD first. I hold that feelings are real and worth listening to, but the Bible never makes them the guide; a deceitful heart followed faithfully will lead you faithfully astray.

Details

Saying: Follow your heart
Closest Verse: Jeremiah 17:9
Origin: No single author or first use is known. The idea runs through Romanticism in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, and Emerson's Self-Reliance (1841) says "Trust thyself"; the exact phrase appears in literary works and proverbs by the nineteenth century and rises in print from the early twentieth. Its modern ubiquity owes much to Disney — Cinderella (1950), "A Dream Is a Wish Your Heart Makes" — and to Steve Jobs's Stanford commencement address in 2005.
Related Books: Jeremiah; Numbers; Proverbs; Psalms
Related Topics: Wisdom; Trust; Discernment; Humility
