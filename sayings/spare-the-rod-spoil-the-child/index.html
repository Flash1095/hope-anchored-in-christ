Spare the rod, spoil the child

Most people who quote it mean that children who are not disciplined firmly will turn out spoiled, and that physical discipline is what the Bible prescribes.

What The Bible Says

Proverbs 13:24 does say, "He that spareth his rod hateth his son: but he that loveth him chasteneth him betimes," and Proverbs 22:15 and Proverbs 23:13-14 speak of correction in the same spirit, while Hebrews 12:6 says the Lord chastens those He loves. But a proverb is wisdom to weigh, not a command to strike, and the Bible's picture of discipline is loving, timely correction, never cruelty. My position is that the rhyme itself is not a verse, while the heart of it — loving discipline that does not leave a child to himself — is genuinely biblical.

Details

Saying: Spare the rod, spoil the child
Closest Verse: Proverbs 13:24
Origin: Samuel Butler's satirical poem Hudibras, Part II (1664), where the line appears in a passage about lovers, not children: "Love is a boy by poets styl'd; Then spare the rod, and spoil the child." Butler was riffing on Proverbs 13:24, not quoting it.
