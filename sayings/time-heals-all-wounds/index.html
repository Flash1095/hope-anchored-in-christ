Time heals all wounds

Given enough time, every hurt fades — grief, betrayal and loss all mend on their own, because time itself does the healing.

What The Bible Says

The Bible names a healer, and it is not time: "He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds" (Psalm 147:3), and "The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart" (Psalm 34:18). Time passes — Ecclesiastes 3:1 says to every thing there is a season — but time on its own has never bound up a wound; people carry old hurts for decades, and some wounds only deepen with the years. I believe the saying is a kind word meant well, but it puts the healing in the calendar instead of in God, and it leaves the grieving person waiting on the clock rather than turning to the Healer.

Details

Saying: Time heals all wounds
Closest Verse: Psalm 147:3
Origin: The sentiment is ancient — it is traditionally credited to the Greek comic poet Menander (c. 342-290 BC), "time is the healer of all necessary evils," though that attribution is repeated mainly by quotation collections rather than surviving fragments, and some sources credit the poet Diphilus instead. Chaucer has the same thought in Troilus and Criseyde (1380s): "As tyme hem hurt, a tyme doth hem cure." The exact modern wording first appears in print in H. Rider Haggard's novel Morning Star (1910): "Time heals all wounds."
Related Books: Psalms; Ecclesiastes
Related Topics: Healing; Suffering; Hope; Comfort
