Idle hands are the devil's workshop

A person with nothing to do will drift into trouble — empty time is where temptation and mischief get their foothold.

What The Bible Says

The Bible agrees that idleness breeds trouble: 2 Thessalonians 3:11 warns of some who walk disorderly, working not at all, but are busybodies, Proverbs 6:6 sends the sluggard to the ant to learn wisdom, and Proverbs 18:9 says the slothful in his work is brother to him that is a great waster. Proverbs 16:27 in the KJV does not say this at all — it speaks of an ungodly man digging up evil — though the 1971 Living Bible paraphrase rendered it as 'Idle hands are the devil's workshop.' I believe the warning itself is sound Scripture, but the saying is a proverb about it, not a verse from it.

Details

Saying: Idle hands are the devil's workshop
Closest Verse: 2 Thessalonians 3:11
Origin: The idea traces to St. Jerome, who wrote in the late fourth century, 'engage in some occupation, so that the devil may always find you busy,' and Geoffrey Chaucer repeated Jerome's advice in The Tale of Melibee, part of The Canterbury Tales, around 1386. The exact English wording is a later proverb, not a Bible quotation.
Related Books: Proverbs; 2 Thessalonians
Related Topics: Diligence; Diligent work; Temptation; Satan
