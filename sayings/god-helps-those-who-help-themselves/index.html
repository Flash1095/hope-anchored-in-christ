God helps those who help themselves

People take it to mean God rewards self-reliance: His help goes to those who first get moving on their own, and waiting on God without acting is just laziness.

What The Bible Says

Scripture says almost the opposite: "when we were yet without strength, in due time Christ died for the ungodly" (Romans 5:6), and Paul learned that God's strength is made perfect in weakness (2 Corinthians 12:9). The Bible does call for diligence, and trusting the Lord with all your heart (Proverbs 3:5) goes with working faithfully rather than sitting idle, but help comes because God is gracious, not because we earned it by helping ourselves first. I hold that the saying gets the order backwards: God helps the helpless, and then His help moves us to act.

Details

Saying: God helps those who help themselves
Closest Verse: Romans 5:6
Origin: The idea is ancient Greek, told in Aesop's fable of Hercules and the wagoner, where the god tells the stuck farmer to put his own shoulder to the wheel. The English wording first appears in Algernon Sidney's Discourses Concerning Government (1698) and was made famous by Benjamin Franklin in Poor Richard's Almanack (1736).
Related Books: Romans; 2 Corinthians; Proverbs
Related Topics: Grace; Diligence; Faith; Good works
