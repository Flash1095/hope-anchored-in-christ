Charity begins at home

Look after your own family and your own household first — your duty to those closest to you comes before giving time or money to strangers or outside causes.

What The Bible Says

There is real Scripture behind the duty part: 1 Timothy 5:8 says that if any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith, and Galatians 6:10 tells us to do good unto all men, especially unto them who are of the household of faith. But the Bible never makes family the limit of generosity — 1 John 3:17-18 warns against shutting up our compassion toward a brother in need, and Matthew 22:39 commands love of neighbour as of self. My position is that the verse and the proverb overlap but are not the same thing: providing for your own is a duty, and using it as an excuse not to give beyond your own is not.

Details

Saying: Charity begins at home
Closest Verse: 1 Timothy 5:8
Origin: Sir Thomas Browne first printed the phrase in its modern form in Religio Medici in 1642 — 'Charity begins at home, is the voice of the world' — and he wrote it to argue against it, not to endorse it. The idea itself is older: Wycliffe expressed it in English in 1382, and the sentiment goes back further still.
Related Books: 1 Timothy; Galatians; 1 John; Matthew
