To err is human, to forgive divine

Making mistakes is part of being human, the saying goes, and forgiving others is the godlike thing to do — so both halves sound like wisdom straight from Scripture.

What The Bible Says

The two halves are true in their own way, but no verse puts them together. Romans 3:23 says all have sinned and come short of the glory of God, and Ephesians 4:32 tells us to forgive one another even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven us. The Bible never treats forgiveness as something divine in us, though — it treats it as something God does first, and then asks of us. I hold that the line is good poetry and sound counsel, but it is Alexander Pope's, not Paul's.

Details

Saying: To err is human, to forgive divine
Closest Verse: Ephesians 4:32
Origin: Alexander Pope, An Essay on Criticism, Part II, line 525 — written 1709, published in London in May 1711: "To Err is Humane; to Forgive, Divine." The shorter "to err is human" is older, a calque of the Latin errare humanum est, attributed to Seneca.
Related Books: Romans; Ephesians; Colossians
Related Topics: Forgiveness; Sin; Grace; Mercy
