The Lord's Prayer

Guide
The Lord's Prayer
Category
Passage Study
Summary
The Lord's Prayer is the prayer Jesus taught his disciples to pray — short enough to memorize, deep enough to pray for a lifetime. Six petitions move from God's name to our bread to our forgiveness, and end in his kingdom. This passage study prays through the prayer phrase by phrase, so it becomes your own pattern for prayer rather than words you rush through.
Scripture
Matthew 6:9-13; Luke 11:1-4
Steps
  1. Memorize the prayer (Matthew's version), and pray it slowly each morning for a week before studying it
  2. Study it phrase by phrase, asking what each petition assumes about God and about you
  3. Compare Matthew's and Luke's versions, noting the differences and what they emphasize
  4. Pray each petition in your own words, turning "give us" and "forgive us" into specific requests
  5. Notice the plurals — "our," "us," "we" — and ask what it means to pray this prayer as part of a people
  6. End by using the prayer as an outline for your own prayers: worship, kingdom, provision, forgiveness, protection
Questions
  • Why does Jesus begin with God's name and kingdom before our needs — and what does that order teach?
  • What does it mean to pray "your kingdom come" — and what would it look like if it came in your life today?
  • Why does Jesus tie our forgiveness to our forgiving — and what does that mean for a wound you carry?
  • What is the difference between "lead us not into temptation" and asking God to keep you from testing?
  • How does praying this prayer together, with "our" and "us," change the way you think about your own needs?
Application
Pray the Lord's Prayer slowly every morning this week, pausing after each petition to make it your own — and let it become the shape of your prayers.
