Hebrews 11

Guide
Hebrews 11
Category
Passage Study
Summary
Hebrews 11 is the Bible's hall of faith: Abel, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Rahab, and many more, whose trust in God shaped history. It defines faith, then shows it — in people who obeyed without seeing, endured without reward, and died still believing. This passage study walks the hall slowly, so their faith can strengthen yours.
Scripture
Hebrews 11:1-40
Steps
  1. Read the whole chapter in one sitting, noting the repeated pattern: "By faith..." followed by an action
  2. Study the definition in verse 1 and verse 6, asking what faith is and what it requires
  3. Follow Abraham's section (verses 8-19) closely, tracing faith through leaving, waiting, and offering
  4. Compare the victories (verses 32-35a) with the sufferings (verses 35b-38), and ask why both are called faith
  5. End with verses 39-40, asking what it means that they did not receive what was promised — yet
  6. Write your own "by faith" sentence: one action your faith requires of you now
Questions
  • What is faith, according to Hebrews 11 — and how is it different from wishful thinking?
  • Why does the chapter praise people who never saw the promise fulfilled?
  • How can both triumph and torture be expressions of the same faith?
  • Which person's faith in this chapter most resembles your own situation — and which one challenges you most?
  • What does it mean that God has "provided something better for us" (11:40) — and how does that complete their story?
Application
Take one step of faith this week that you have been postponing — an obedience, a risk, a trust — and do it because God is faithful, not because the outcome is certain.
