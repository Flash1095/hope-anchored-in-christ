Anxiety

Guide
Anxiety
Category
Topical Study
Summary
Anxiety is a human experience the Bible takes seriously — and it offers something better than denial: a God who invites us to bring every worry to him. From Philippians 4 to the Psalms to Jesus' own words, Scripture gives anxious hearts practices, promises, and a Person to trust. This topical study is gentle and practical, for anyone whose mind will not quiet.
Scripture
Philippians 4:6-7; Matthew 6:25-34; 1 Peter 5:6-7
Steps
  1. Read Philippians 4:6-7 and Matthew 6:25-34 together, noting what Jesus and Paul actually promise — and what they do not
  2. Name your anxieties specifically, in writing: vague worry grows in the dark, and naming it is a first act of honesty
  3. Practice the Philippians pattern daily: present each worry to God, with thanksgiving, and ask for his peace
  4. Pray a psalm of trust (Psalm 23, 27, or 46) aloud when anxiety rises, letting its words steady you
  5. Identify one practical step within your control for each worry — and one thing you must entrust to God
  6. Talk with a trusted friend, pastor, or counselor about persistent anxiety
  7. Scripture's wisdom includes wise counsel
Questions
  • What is the difference between concern that moves you to act and anxiety that paralyzes you?
  • Why does Jesus say not to worry about tomorrow — is that a command, a comfort, or both?
  • How does thanksgiving change anxiety, in your experience — and why would Paul pair them?
  • What does it mean to cast your cares on God — is it a one-time act, or a daily practice?
  • When does anxiety need more than a Bible study — prayer, community, rest, or professional help — and how do you know?
Application
Each evening this week, write down one worry and one thing you are thankful for, and hand the worry to God in prayer before you sleep.
