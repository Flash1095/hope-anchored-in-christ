Psalm 23

Guide
Psalm 23
Category
Passage Study
Summary
Psalm 23 is the best-loved passage in the Bible — six verses that carry people through green pastures and dark valleys alike. David pictures God as shepherd and host, providing, guiding, protecting, and welcoming. This passage study lingers on every line, so the psalm becomes a companion for your own valleys, not just a funeral reading.
Scripture
Psalm 23
Steps
  1. Read the psalm aloud slowly three times, pausing after each verse to picture it
  2. Study the shepherd imagery verse by verse, asking what each picture promises (provision, rest, guidance, protection, presence)
  3. Trace the shift in verse 4, where "he" becomes "you" — and ask what changes when prayer becomes personal
  4. Compare Psalm 23 with John 10, where Jesus calls himself the good shepherd
  5. Pray the psalm line by line, inserting your own valley, your own enemies, your own table
  6. Memorize the psalm, and carry it into one hard moment this week
Questions
  • Why does David begin with "The Lord is my shepherd" — what does that claim settle before anything else?
  • What does it mean that God prepares a table "in the presence of my enemies" — blessing without removing the threat?
  • How does the psalm hold together green pastures and the valley of the shadow of death — and which one are you in now?
  • What does "your rod and your staff, they comfort me" mean — how does God's discipline comfort?
  • How does this psalm change the way you face death — your own, or someone you love?
Application
When worry or grief rises this week, pray Psalm 23 aloud, slowly, until its promises are louder than your fear.
