Love

Guide
Love
Category
Topical Study
Summary
Love is the Bible's greatest command and its clearest picture of God: "God is love." Yet love in Scripture is not sentiment — it is patient action, costly sacrifice, and truthful commitment. This topical study follows love from 1 Corinthians 13 to 1 John, so you can learn to love the way God loves.
Scripture
1 Corinthians 13; 1 John 4:7-19; John 13:34-35
Steps
  1. Read 1 Corinthians 13 slowly, substituting your own name for "love" — and notice where it convicts you
  2. Study John 13:34-35 and 15:9-17, asking what Jesus' "new command" actually requires
  3. Trace love through 1 John, noting how love for God and love for others prove each other
  4. Examine one relationship where love is hard, and ask what patient, kind, truthful action looks like there
  5. Practice one "1 Corinthians 13" quality each day for a week — patience on Monday, kindness on Tuesday, and so on
  6. End by asking someone close to you how loved they feel by you, and listen without defending yourself
Questions
  • Why does Paul say love matters more than gifts, knowledge, or sacrifice — and do you agree?
  • What is the difference between loving someone and liking them, or approving of them?
  • How do you love an enemy, a difficult family member, or someone who has hurt you — practically?
  • Why does John say you cannot love God while hating your brother — what does that reveal about love's nature?
  • Where is love most costly for you right now, and what would it look like to pay that cost?
Application
Choose one person to love actively this week — not with feelings, but with a specific kind act, a patient conversation, or a forgiven debt.
