Summary
Philippians is Paul's warmest letter, written from prison to a church he loved — and it is full of joy. Joy in suffering, joy in service, joy in Christ: Paul shows that joy is not a mood but a settled confidence in Jesus. This guide walks through the letter's four chapters, so you can learn joy that survives hard circumstances.
Scripture
Philippians 2:1-11; Philippians 4:4-9
Steps
  1. Read the letter in one sitting, marking every mention of joy, rejoicing, or gladness
  2. Study the Christ hymn (Philippians 2:5-11) line by line, asking what Jesus' humility means for your relationships
  3. Trace Paul's own example — his chains, his contentment, his gratitude — and compare it with your response to hardship
  4. Work through Philippians 4:4-9 as a practical guide: rejoice, pray, think, practice
  5. List the people Paul names in the letter, and ask what Christian friendship looks like in each case
  6. End by writing a short letter of encouragement to someone, in Paul's spirit
Questions
  • How can Paul be joyful in prison — and what does that say about where joy actually comes from?
  • What does it mean to have "the mind of Christ" in a real conflict with a real person?
  • Why does Paul connect joy with prayer and with right thinking — and which of those do you neglect?
  • What does contentment look like for you: is it settling, or trusting?
  • Who in your life needs the kind of encouragement Paul gives the Philippians, and what would you say?
Application
Each morning this week, name three things to be thankful for before you start the day, and tell one person why you are grateful for them.
