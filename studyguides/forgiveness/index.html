Forgiveness

Guide
Forgiveness
Category
Topical Study
Summary
Forgiveness stands at the heart of the gospel: God forgives us in Christ, and forgiven people learn to forgive. Yet forgiveness is also one of the hardest commands to obey, especially when the wound is deep. This topical study follows forgiveness through Scripture — received, extended, and lived — so you can take one honest step toward it.
Scripture
Matthew 18:21-35; Ephesians 4:31-32; Colossians 3:12-14
Steps
  1. Begin with the parable of the unforgiving servant (Matthew 18:21-35), and notice the scale of the two debts
  2. Study what God has forgiven you — Colossians 2:13-14, Ephesians 1:7 — before you think about forgiving others
  3. Read Joseph's forgiveness of his brothers (Genesis 45, 50:15-21) as a long, realistic picture of the process
  4. Distinguish forgiveness from excusing, forgetting, and reconciling — and ask which confusion holds you back
  5. Name one person you need to forgive, and pray for them by name for a week, even if feelings have not changed
  6. If reconciliation is possible and safe, take one small, wise step toward it — a conversation, a letter, a gesture
Questions
  • Why does Jesus tie our forgiveness of others to God's forgiveness of us — is that a threat, or a description of how grace works?
  • What is the difference between forgiving someone and trusting them again?
  • How do you forgive when the other person has never apologized, or never will?
  • What does forgiveness look like when the wound is ongoing rather than past?
  • Where do you most need to receive forgiveness yourself — and have you actually asked for it?
Application
This week, pray daily for one person who has hurt you, asking God to bless them — and notice what that prayer does to your own heart.
