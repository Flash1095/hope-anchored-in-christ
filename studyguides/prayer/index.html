Prayer

Guide
Prayer
Category
Topical Study
Summary
Prayer is how God's people talk with God — honestly, regularly, and about everything. The Bible shows prayer in every form: praise, confession, petition, silence, and struggle. This topical study gathers the Bible's teaching and examples of prayer, so you can build a prayer life that is real rather than performed.
Scripture
Matthew 6:5-15; Philippians 4:6-7; James 5:13-18
Steps
  1. Read the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9-13) slowly, phrase by phrase, and pray each phrase in your own words
  2. Study one prayer of the Bible per session — Hannah, David, Daniel, Paul, Jesus — and note its shape
  3. Practice the pattern of Philippians 4:6-7 for a week: every anxiety named, every request made, every thanks given
  4. Keep a simple prayer list with three columns — asking, waiting, answered — and review it weekly
  5. Try praying a psalm aloud each day, letting its words carry what you struggle to say
  6. End by setting a regular time and place for prayer, and protecting it for a month
Questions
  • Why do you think Jesus warns against showy prayer — and what does showy prayer look like today?
  • What do you find hardest about prayer: starting, staying, believing, or waiting?
  • How does the Bible's honesty in prayer — complaint, doubt, anger — change what you allow yourself to say to God?
  • What does it mean to pray "in Jesus' name" — is it a formula, a confidence, or both?
  • How would your week change if prayer were your first response instead of your last resort?
Application
Set aside ten minutes tomorrow morning for prayer before anything else, using the Lord's Prayer as your outline — and repeat it daily for a week.
