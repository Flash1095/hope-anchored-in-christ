Romans

Guide
Romans
Category
Book Study
Summary
Romans is Paul's fullest explanation of the gospel: how sinners are made right with God, how grace changes a life, and how God's promises hold for Jew and Gentile alike. It moves from the bad news of universal sin to the good news of justification by faith, then to the new life of the Spirit. This guide works through Romans section by section, so the letter's logic becomes your own conviction.
Scripture
Romans 1:16-17; Romans 8:1-39
Steps
  1. Read Romans 1-8 in a few sittings first, so you feel the argument's flow before studying details
  2. Trace the key words — sin, law, grace, faith, righteousness — and note how Paul defines each one
  3. Study one section per session (1:18-3:20, 3:21-5:21, 6-8, 9-11, 12-16), summarizing it in a sentence
  4. Pay special attention to Romans 8, reading it aloud and marking every promise the Spirit guarantees
  5. Ask at each section: what does this change about how I see God, myself, and other people?
  6. Finish by reading Romans 12 slowly and listing the concrete practices Paul expects of a transformed life
Questions
  • Why does Paul spend three chapters on sin before he explains grace — and what does that order teach us?
  • What is the difference between being justified by faith and being made perfect by effort?
  • How does Romans 8 change the way you face guilt, suffering, or fear about the future?
  • What does Romans 9-11 teach about God's faithfulness when his promises seem delayed?
  • Which command in Romans 12 is hardest for you right now, and what would obeying it look like this week?
Application
Memorize Romans 8:1 or 8:28, and return to it whenever guilt or worry tries to rewrite what God has said about you.
