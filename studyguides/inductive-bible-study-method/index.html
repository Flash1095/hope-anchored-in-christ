Inductive Bible Study Method

Guide
Inductive Bible Study Method
Category
Study Method
Summary
Inductive Bible study is a simple, powerful way to study Scripture for yourself: observe what the text says, interpret what it means, and apply what it demands. No expertise required — just a passage, a pen, and honest questions. This guide teaches the method step by step, so you can study any passage with confidence. It pairs with the site's article "How to Study the Bible," which introduces the same approach.
Scripture
2 Timothy 2:15; Psalm 119:105; Acts 17:11
Steps
  1. Choose a short passage (10-20 verses) and read it three times, in two translations if possible
  2. Observe: mark repeated words, contrasts, commands, and questions, and list the facts — who, what, when, where, why
  3. Ask the six questions of every passage: What does it say? What did it mean to its first readers? What is the main point?
  4. Interpret: use context (the surrounding chapter and book) before consulting any commentary or study note
  5. Cross-check: compare your conclusion with the rest of Scripture, and distrust any interpretation that contradicts it
  6. Apply: write one specific action, attitude, or prayer the passage requires of you this week — then do it
  7. Share: tell someone what you found, and ask what they see in the same passage
Questions
  • Why does observation come before interpretation — and what goes wrong when you skip it?
  • How do you handle a passage you find confusing or uncomfortable, without forcing an answer?
  • What is the difference between what a passage meant and what it means for you — and how do you connect them honestly?
  • How do commentaries and study Bibles help without replacing your own study?
  • Which step do you tend to skip — observing, interpreting, or applying — and what would change if you did it?
Application
This week, study one passage inductively from start to finish, and write down the one application you will actually carry out.
