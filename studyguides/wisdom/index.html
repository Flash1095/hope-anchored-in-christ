Wisdom

Guide
Wisdom
Category
Topical Study
Summary
Wisdom in the Bible is skill for living well under God — knowing what is right and doing it, in ordinary decisions and hard ones. It begins with the fear of the Lord and grows through Scripture, experience, and wise company. This topical study gathers wisdom's teaching from Proverbs, James, and the life of Jesus, so you can make your next decision wisely.
Scripture
Proverbs 2; James 1:5-8; James 3:13-18
Steps
  1. Read Proverbs 2 as a whole, noticing how wisdom is found: seeking, crying out, and receiving
  2. Study James 3:13-18, contrasting earthly wisdom (envy, ambition) with heavenly wisdom (peace, mercy, sincerity)
  3. Bring one real decision to the study, and test it against three questions: Is it true? Is it loving? Is it humble?
  4. List the voices shaping your decisions — friends, media, fear, habit — and ask which ones are wise
  5. Seek counsel from one wise, godly person about your decision, and actually listen
  6. End by asking God for wisdom directly (James 1:5), and waiting quietly before deciding
Questions
  • What is the difference between knowledge, intelligence, and wisdom?
  • Why does wisdom begin with the fear of the Lord rather than with information?
  • How do you recognize false wisdom — in yourself, in advice, in culture?
  • What decision are you facing where you most need wisdom, and what would a wise person do?
  • How does Jesus himself embody wisdom — and what does following him change about your choices?
Application
Before your next significant decision, pause and pray James 1:5, then seek one piece of godly counsel before you act.
