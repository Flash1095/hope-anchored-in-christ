David

David

Summary

David is the Bible's most human hero: shepherd, psalmist, warrior, king — and adulterer, murderer, and repentant sinner. His life holds triumph and failure side by side, and his psalms give voice to both. This character study follows David from the sheepfolds to the throne, so you can learn what it means to be a person after God's own heart, even after failure.

Steps

  1. Read David's story in sequence (1 Samuel 16 through 2 Samuel 12, with selected psalms), noting his rise and his fall
  2. Study David and Goliath (1 Samuel 17) asking what David trusted and what everyone else feared
  3. Read Psalm 51 alongside 2 Samuel 11-12, tracing repentance from exposure to restoration
  4. Compare David's response to failure with Saul's, and ask what made the difference
  5. Pray through one psalm of David per session, noticing how he speaks to God in every season
  6. End by asking what "a man after God's own heart" means for your own failures and hopes

Questions

  • Why does God call David a man after his own heart, given what David did?
  • What does David's repentance in Psalm 51 teach about facing your own sin honestly?
  • How did David's early faithfulness in small things (shepherding, music) prepare him for great ones?
  • Where do you see yourself in David's story — in his courage, his failure, his worship, or his repentance?
  • What does it mean to trust God the way David did when facing your own "giant"?

Application

If there is a failure you have hidden, bring it to God this week in the spirit of Psalm 51 — honestly, without excuse — and ask for a clean heart.
