Psalms

Summary

The Psalms are the prayer book of God's people — 150 songs that give words to joy, grief, anger, guilt, wonder, and hope. They teach us to pray honestly, not politely, and to bring every emotion before God. This guide moves through the five books of the Psalter, sampling laments, hymns, thanksgivings, and wisdom psalms, so you can learn to pray the Psalms yourself.

Steps

  1. Begin by reading one psalm aloud each day, slowly, as a prayer rather than a text to analyze
  2. Sort the psalms you read into types — lament, hymn, thanksgiving, wisdom, royal — and note what each type teaches about prayer
  3. Study a lament (such as Psalm 13 or 42) closely, tracing its movement from complaint to trust
  4. Pray a psalm back to God in your own words, substituting your own situation for the psalmist's
  5. Memorize one short psalm (such as Psalm 23 or 121) and carry it through an ordinary week
  6. Read the Psalms alongside the life of David where the headings give a setting, and ask how the song fits the story

Questions

  • Which emotion do you find hardest to bring honestly to God — and which psalm gives you words for it?
  • Why do so many psalms end in trust even when nothing in the situation has changed?
  • What does it mean to pray words you do not yet feel — is that honesty or hypocrisy?
  • How do the Psalms reshape the way you think about worship: is it feeling, truth, or both?
  • Which psalm would you give to a friend who is grieving, and why that one?

Application

Choose one psalm to pray every morning this week, and let its words set the tone for the day before you check anything else.
