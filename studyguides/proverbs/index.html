Proverbs

Proverbs

Book Study
Summary
Proverbs is God's wisdom for everyday life — short, sharp sayings about speech, money, work, friendship, and the fear of the Lord. It does not promise that life is simple, but it shows the path of wisdom through a complicated world. This guide samples Proverbs by theme, so you can begin to live wisely rather than just admire wisdom.
Scripture
Proverbs 1:1-7; Proverbs 3:1-12
Steps
  1. Read Proverbs 1-9 first as a connected argument, listening for the voice of Wisdom calling
  2. Then sample the collections by theme: speech, money, work, anger, friendship, marriage, pride
  3. Copy out five proverbs that convict you, and keep them where you will see them daily
  4. Compare a proverb with its opposite — wisdom and folly side by side — and ask which pattern your life follows
  5. Study Proverbs 3:5-6 and 8:1-36 together, asking what it means to trust God rather than your own understanding
  6. End each session by turning one proverb into a specific decision for the next day
Questions
  • What is "the fear of the Lord," and why does Proverbs call it the beginning of wisdom rather than the end?
  • Which area of your life — speech, money, work, relationships — most needs wisdom right now?
  • Proverbs are general truths, not guarantees: how do you read them honestly when life breaks the pattern?
  • How does the wisdom of Proverbs point beyond itself to Christ, whom Paul calls the wisdom of God?
  • What is one foolish pattern you keep repeating, and what would the wise path look like instead?
Application
Choose one proverb to practice for a week — especially one about your speech — and ask someone close to you whether they notice a difference.
