Paul

Guide
Paul
Category
Character Study
Summary
Paul is the Bible's greatest turnaround: a persecutor of Christians became the apostle to the Gentiles, planting churches across the Roman world and writing much of the New Testament. His life shows what grace does to a person — completely. This character study follows Paul from Damascus to Rome, so you can see grace, mission, and suffering through one transformed life.
Scripture
Acts 9; Galatians 1-2; Philippians 3
Steps
  1. Read Paul's story in sequence (Acts 7-9
  2. 13-28, with Galatians 1-2 and Philippians 3), noting the before and after
  3. Study the Damascus road (Acts 9) closely, asking what changed — and what did not — in Paul
  4. Follow one missionary journey on a map, noting the pattern: preach, suffer, encourage, move on
  5. Read Philippians 3 as Paul's own summary of his life, and ask what he counts as loss and gain
  6. Study Paul's suffering (2 Corinthians 11-12) and his contentment (Philippians 4), asking how they fit together
  7. End by asking what Paul's life teaches about your own past — is any past beyond grace?
Questions
  • Why does God choose Paul, of all people, to carry the gospel to the nations?
  • What does Paul's transformation teach about grace — is it gradual improvement, or total reversal?
  • How did Paul keep going through beatings, shipwrecks, and betrayal — and where does that endurance come from?
  • What does Paul's life say about ambition: what did he stop chasing, and what did he start living for?
  • Who in your life seems far from God — and does Paul's story change how you pray for them?
Application
Write a short version of your own grace story — who you were, how Christ met you, who you are becoming — and share it with one person this week.
