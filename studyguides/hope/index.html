Hope

Guide
Hope
Category
Topical Study
Summary
Hope in the Bible is not wishful thinking — it is confident expectation grounded in what God has done and promised. From Abraham to the resurrection, God's people have lived by hope in dark times. This topical study traces hope through Scripture, so your hope can rest on something stronger than your circumstances.
Scripture
Romans 15:13; Hebrews 6:19-20; 1 Peter 1:3-9
Steps
  1. Gather the key hope passages (Romans 5:1-5, 8:18-25, 15:13, Hebrews 6:19-20, 1 Peter 1:3-9) and read them together
  2. Trace hope through one life — Abraham, David, or Jeremiah — noting how hope survived disappointment
  3. Compare biblical hope with optimism: what is the difference, and why does it matter?
  4. Study Romans 8:18-25, asking how hope relates to suffering rather than denying it
  5. Write your own "hope statement" — what you are confident God will do, and why
  6. Share that statement with one person who needs hope, and pray it together
Questions
  • What is the difference between hoping that things improve and hoping in God — and which kind do you usually practice?
  • How has hope carried you through a hard season before, and what did it actually rest on?
  • Why does Hebrews call hope an anchor — what does an anchor do that a feeling cannot?
  • What promise of God do you find hardest to believe right now?
  • How can you be a person who gives hope to others without offering empty comfort?
Application
Anchor your week to one promise — Romans 15:13 or Hebrews 6:19 — and repeat it each morning before you face the day.
