Mark

Guide
Mark
Category
Book Study
Summary
Mark's Gospel is the shortest and fastest-moving — a breathless account of Jesus' ministry that asks one question from start to finish: who is this man? Action follows action, and the disciples keep misunderstanding, until a Roman soldier at the cross finally sees. This guide follows Mark's pace, so you can feel the urgency and answer the question for yourself.
Scripture
Mark 1:1-15; Mark 8:27-30
Steps
  1. Read Mark in a few sittings, noticing the word "immediately" and the speed of the story
  2. Track the question of Jesus' identity: what do the demons, the crowds, the disciples, and Jesus himself say about him?
  3. Study the three passion predictions (Mark 8:31, 9:31, 10:33-34) and the disciples' reactions to each
  4. Follow Peter through the Gospel, from his confession to his denial, and ask what discipleship costs
  5. Read the cross and resurrection account (Mark 14-16) slowly, after the fast pace of the rest
  6. End by answering, in your own words, the question Jesus asks in Mark 8:29: "Who do you say I am?"
Questions
  • Why does Mark move so fast — and what does that speed do to the way you read about Jesus?
  • Why do the disciples misunderstand Jesus so often, and where do you misunderstand him the same way?
  • What does Mark's emphasis on suffering say about what following Jesus actually involves?
  • Why does Mark end the way he does — and what response does that ending demand?
  • If someone asked you who Jesus is, based only on Mark, what would you say?
Application
Tell one person this week, in your own words, who Jesus is according to Mark — not a rehearsed answer, but the one you actually believe.
