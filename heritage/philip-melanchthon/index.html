Philip Melanchthon

Name
Philip Melanchthon
Type
Figure
Era
16th Century
Summary
German scholar (1497-1560) and Luther's closest colleague, who gave Lutheran teaching its first systematic and confessional form.
Contribution
He wrote the Augsburg Confession, the Lutheran churches' founding statement of faith, and his textbooks and systematic works carried Reformation doctrine into universities across Europe. After Luther's death he led the Lutheran movement through its fiercest internal disputes.
Key Works
Loci Communes; Augsburg Confession
Testimony
Known for a gentler temperament than Luther, he sought clarity and agreement in doctrine and spent his life teaching the next generation.
