William Tyndale

English scholar and translator (c. 1494-1536) who produced the first printed New Testament in English translated from the original Greek and Hebrew sources.

Contribution

Working in exile, he translated the New Testament and much of the Old Testament into plain English so ordinary readers could read Scripture for themselves, and his phrasing underlies much of the later King James Version. His work made an English Bible available despite a ban on it.

Testimony

He was hunted across Europe, betrayed, strangled, and burned at the stake near Brussels; his reported last prayer was that the king of England's eyes would be opened.
