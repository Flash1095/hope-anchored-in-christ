Sadducees

Group
Sadducees
Period
Second Temple period, especially the first century AD
Origin
The Sadducees were the party of the priestly aristocracy, named perhaps for Zadok, the priest of David and Solomon. Their power rose and fell with the Temple: they were its guardians and its beneficiaries, and they faded from history when the Temple fell in AD 70.
Beliefs
Sadducees accepted only the written Law of Moses as binding and rejected the oral tradition, the resurrection of the dead, angels and spirits, and the idea that fate or providence overrules human choice. Their religion was Temple-centered: sacrifice and priesthood, not resurrection hope or angelology.
Practices
They ran the Temple and its sacrifices, controlled its treasury, and filled the high priesthood and the Sanhedrin's chief seats. Their worship was correct and orderly, and their politics were cautious: keep the peace with Rome, keep the Temple standing, keep the money flowing.
Role
Sadducees were the wealthy conservative elite of Jerusalem — few in number, powerful in office, and unpopular with the crowds, who leaned Pharisee. Their authority came from birth and Temple office, not from teaching or popular piety.
Relationship To Jesus
Jesus clashed with the Sadducees over the resurrection, answering their riddle about marriage after death with a reply from Moses that left them silenced. They joined the chief priests in arresting him and later in arresting the apostles, above all because the apostles preached the resurrection they denied.
Key Members
Caiaphas; Annas
Scripture
Matthew 22:23-33; Mark 12:18-27; Luke 20:27-40; Acts 4:1-2; Acts 23:6-10
Summary
The Sadducees were the priestly aristocrats of the Temple party — wealthy, conservative, accepting only the written Law, denying resurrection and angels, and vanishing with the Temple they guarded.
