The Twelve Apostles

Group
The Twelve Apostles
Also Called
The Twelve; The Apostles
Period
The ministry of Jesus through the early church
Origin
Jesus chose twelve men from his wider disciples and named them apostles — 'sent ones' — deliberately echoing the twelve tribes of Israel. After Judas's betrayal, the remaining eleven chose Matthias to restore the number, and Paul later claimed apostleship by the risen Christ's direct call.
Beliefs
The Twelve believed Jesus was the Messiah and, after the resurrection, the risen Lord. Their authority rested not on learning or office but on having been with Jesus from his baptism to his ascension and on witnessing the resurrection.
Practices
They lived with Jesus, were sent to preach and heal in pairs, shared his last meal, received his commission, and after Pentecost preached, healed, and governed the Jerusalem church. Their names became the standard of the church's teaching: what the apostles taught was the faith.
Role
The Twelve were the core of the new Israel Jesus was forming — fishermen, a tax collector, a Zealot, and a betrayer at one table. Their authority shaped the church's doctrine, its leadership, and its memory of Jesus himself.
Relationship To Jesus
Jesus chose them, taught them privately, warned them, washed their feet, and sent them to the world. They argued, doubted, fled, and were restored — Peter denied him and preached at Pentecost weeks later. Judas's betrayal and replacement show that even the inner circle was not chosen for perfection but for witness.
Key Members
Simon Peter; Andrew; James, Son of Zebedee; Philip; Matthew; Thomas; Simon the Zealot; Judas Iscariot
Scripture
Matthew 10:1-4; Mark 3:13-19; Luke 6:12-16; Acts 1:12-26; Ephesians 2:19-20
Summary
The Twelve Apostles were Jesus' chosen inner circle — twelve men named to echo Israel's tribes, sent to preach and witness, whose teaching became the foundation of the church.
