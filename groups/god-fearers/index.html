God-Fearers

Group
God-Fearers
Also Called
God-Fearing Gentiles; Worshipers of God
Period
The first century AD, and the wider Greco-Roman world
Origin
God-fearers were Gentiles drawn to Israel's God who attended synagogues, prayed, gave alms, and honored the Law without fully converting to Judaism. They stood between the pagan world and the synagogue: welcome at worship, but not full members of the covenant people.
Beliefs
They believed Israel's God was the one true God, that idols were empty, and that the Jewish Scriptures spoke truth. Many accepted the moral law and the hope of Israel while stopping short of circumcision and full ritual observance.
Practices
They attended synagogue on Sabbaths, listened to the Law and the Prophets, gave to the poor, and prayed to Israel's God. Some kept dietary rules loosely; all lived visibly apart from pagan temples and civic idolatry, which cost them socially.
Role
God-fearers were the bridge community of the ancient world — Gentiles already halfway to the synagogue, and therefore the first and most natural hearers of the Christian message. When Paul preached in a new city, he began in the synagogue, and God-fearers were often the first Gentiles to believe.
Relationship To Jesus
Jesus never names God-fearers, but his ministry prepared their welcome: he praised a Roman centurion's faith and a Canaanite woman's persistence. In Acts, Cornelius the centurion — a God-fearer — becomes the first Gentile household baptized, and Lydia, a worshiper of God, becomes the first convert in Europe.
Key Members
Cornelius; Lydia
Scripture
Acts 10:1-2; Acts 13:16; Acts 13:26; Acts 16:14; Acts 17:4
Summary
God-fearers were Gentiles drawn to Israel's God — synagogue attenders who prayed and gave but had not fully converted — and they became the first Gentile hearers of the gospel.
