Rulers of the Synagogue

Group
Rulers of the Synagogue
Also Called
Synagogue Rulers; Archisynagogoi
Period
The first century AD
Origin
Every synagogue had a ruler — usually one man, sometimes a small board — who managed its affairs. The ruler was a respected local leader, often wealthy, who kept the building, chose readers and preachers, and kept order in worship.
Beliefs
Synagogue rulers held the mainstream faith of their communities: the Law, the Prophets, Sabbath worship, and the hope of Israel. They were not a party; they were local officials, and their views varied town by town.
Practices
They arranged Sabbath services, invited visiting teachers to speak, maintained the scrolls and the building, disciplined disorders, and represented the synagogue to the town. Their seat of honor was near the front, facing the congregation.
Role
Synagogue rulers were the civic-religious backbone of Jewish towns — the men who made weekly worship happen and whose invitation could give a traveling preacher his hearing, or shut the door on him.
Relationship To Jesus
Jesus preached in synagogues by rulers' invitation and clashed with them when his healings broke their Sabbath rules. Jairus, a ruler, begged Jesus to heal his daughter; Crispus, ruler at Corinth, believed with his whole household; another Corinthian ruler, Sosthenes, was beaten outside the tribunal. Their mixed response mirrors the synagogue's wider divided reaction to Jesus.
Key Members
Jairus; Crispus
Scripture
Mark 5:22-43; Luke 8:41-56; Luke 13:14; Acts 13:15; Acts 18:8; Acts 18:17
Summary
Rulers of the synagogue were the respected local officials who ran each synagogue's worship — arranging services, inviting preachers, and keeping order — and whose mixed response to Jesus mirrors their communities' own division.
