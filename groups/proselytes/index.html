Proselytes

Group
Proselytes
Also Called
Converts to Judaism
Period
From the Second Temple period through the first century AD
Origin
Proselytes were Gentiles who went all the way: they converted fully to Judaism, accepting circumcision (for men), baptism, and offering, and taking on the whole Law. They became full members of the Jewish people, distinct from God-fearers who stopped short.
Beliefs
Proselytes believed Israel's God was the only God and that joining his people meant joining his covenant completely — Law, Sabbath, festivals, purity, and Temple. Their conversion was a public break with their old gods, families, and civic religion.
Practices
They were circumcised, baptized, and offered sacrifice; they kept Sabbath and festivals, ate by the purity rules, and worshiped in the synagogue as full members. Their children were born Jews.
Role
Proselytes were the proof that Judaism in the ancient world was a missionary faith: its synagogues drew converts from every nation, and its festivals in Jerusalem included proselytes from Rome to Parthia. They also show why the early church's Gentile question was so sharp — full conversion had always meant becoming Jewish first.
Relationship To Jesus
Jesus criticized Pharisees who crossed sea and land to make a proselyte and then made him worse than themselves — a warning about conversion without transformation. In Acts, proselytes appear at Pentecost, among the Seven, and in Paul's synagogue audiences, and the church's decision not to require circumcision of Gentile believers was a deliberate break with the proselyte pattern.
Key Members
Nicolas
Scripture
Exodus 12:48-49; Matthew 23:15; Acts 2:10-11; Acts 6:5; Acts 13:43
Summary
Proselytes were Gentiles who converted fully to Judaism — circumcised, baptized, and bound to the whole Law — the full members whose pattern the early church deliberately did not require of Gentile believers.
