Zealots

Group
Zealots
Also Called
Zealot Party; Sicarii (radical offshoot)
Period
First century AD, through the revolt of AD 66-70
Origin
The Zealots were the party of armed resistance to Rome, growing from the refusal to pay tribute to a pagan emperor and from the conviction that God alone was Israel's king. Their spirit goes back to Judas of Galilee's tax revolt; their end came with the destruction of Jerusalem and the last stand at Masada.
Beliefs
Zealots believed God would deliver Israel if Israel would fight, that paying tribute to Caesar was treason against God, and that compromise with Rome was apostasy. They shared the Pharisees' zeal for the Law but turned it toward revolution.
Practices
They organized in the hills, raided, assassinated collaborators, and refused the census and the tax. The most radical offshoot, the Sicarii — 'dagger-men' — murdered opponents in crowds with concealed blades, a tactic the book of Acts remembers in passing.
Role
Zealots were the resistance: popular among the poor and the young, feared by the priestly elite and by Rome, and a constant pressure on every Jewish leader to choose between rebellion and accommodation. Their rising dragged the nation into a war it could not win.
Relationship To Jesus
One of Jesus' own twelve was Simon the Zealot — a former revolutionary following a teacher who refused the sword, told his followers to pay Caesar's tax, and rebuked Peter for using a blade at his arrest. The contrast is the point: Jesus gathered a Zealot and a tax collector at the same table and offered a kingdom that came by the cross, not by revolt.
Key Members
Simon the Zealot
Scripture
Matthew 10:4; Mark 3:18; Luke 6:15; Acts 1:13; Acts 21:38
Summary
The Zealots were the party of armed resistance to Rome — convinced God alone was king, refusing tribute to Caesar, and ending in the revolt that destroyed Jerusalem; one of their former members sat among Jesus' twelve.
