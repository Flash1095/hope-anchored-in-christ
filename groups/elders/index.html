Elders

Group
Elders
Also Called
Elders of Israel; Elders of the People
Period
From Moses through the New Testament period
Origin
Elders were the established pattern of Israel's government from the beginning: the respected heads of families and towns who judged disputes and spoke for their people. By the first century, 'elders' also meant the lay aristocracy of Jerusalem — wealthy, non-priestly leaders who sat on the Sanhedrin alongside chief priests and scribes.
Beliefs
Elders held no single party theology. As lay leaders they tended to defend custom, order, and the institutions that gave them standing, and in the Sanhedrin they voted with the chief priests more often than with the Pharisees.
Practices
In towns they judged at the city gate, witnessed marriages and land deals, and settled disputes. In Jerusalem they governed with the council: arresting, trying, and sentencing, and negotiating with Rome.
Role
Elders were the backbone of local authority — the men ordinary people brought their quarrels to, and the council that spoke for the nation in Jerusalem. Their authority came from age, wealth, and standing rather than from priestly birth or scribal learning.
Relationship To Jesus
The elders joined the chief priests and scribes in opposing Jesus, questioning his authority and consenting to his condemnation. In Acts they oppose the apostles as well, though the early church then adopted 'elders' as its own word for local church leaders — a deliberate reuse of a trusted title.
Scripture
Exodus 3:16; Exodus 24:1; Deuteronomy 31:28; Ruth 4:1-11; Matthew 21:23; Acts 4:5-8
Summary
The elders were Israel's traditional lay leaders — heads of families and towns who judged at the gate, and in Jerusalem the wealthy non-priestly members of the Sanhedrin who governed with the chief priests.
