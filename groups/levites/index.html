Levites

Group
Levites
Period
From Moses through the New Testament period
Origin
The Levites were the tribe set apart to serve God instead of receiving land. Aaron's sons became priests; the rest of Levi's descendants became their assistants, carrying the tabernacle in the wilderness and later staffing the Temple's music, gates, treasuries, and teaching.
Beliefs
Levites shared the priests' convictions about holiness, sacrifice, and the Law. Their identity was service: they belonged to God in place of Israel's firstborn and lived from the tithes the other tribes brought.
Practices
They sang and played in Temple worship, guarded the gates, kept the stores and treasuries, assisted at sacrifices, and taught the Law in towns and villages. In the wilderness they carried the tabernacle's parts on the march; in Jerusalem they kept the whole house of worship running.
Role
Levites were the Temple's workforce and Israel's local teachers — respected but not wealthy, scattered in forty-eight towns, and present in ordinary life in a way the Jerusalem priesthood was not. A Levite passing a wounded man on the Jericho road was, for Jesus' hearers, a painfully familiar figure.
Relationship To Jesus
Jesus mentions a Levite in the parable of the Good Samaritan, where priest and Levite both pass by — a pointed picture of religious duty failing at mercy. In Acts, Barnabas the Levite sells a field for the church, and the early church's Levite converts show the gospel reaching the Temple's own servants.
Key Members
Barnabas
Scripture
Numbers 3:5-13; Numbers 18:21-24; Deuteronomy 10:8-9; 1 Chronicles 15:2; Luke 10:31-32; Acts 4:36-37
Summary
The Levites were the tribe set apart to serve the priests and run the house of God — singers, gatekeepers, treasurers, and teachers, scattered through Israel's towns and living from the people's tithes.
