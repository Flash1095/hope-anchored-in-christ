The Seven

Group
The Seven
Also Called
The First Deacons
Period
The early church, Jerusalem
Origin
When the Jerusalem church grew, a dispute arose: Greek-speaking widows were being overlooked in the daily food distribution. The apostles asked the community to choose seven men, full of the Spirit and wisdom, to take charge of the tables — the church's first organized servants, later called deacons.
Beliefs
The Seven believed the gospel was for Greek-speaker and Hebrew-speaker alike, and that practical mercy was not a distraction from the word of God but part of its work. Their appointment settled that serving tables and preaching the word belonged to the same mission.
Practices
They administered the daily distribution, served the widows, and preached. Stephen, the first named, debated in the synagogues and became the church's first martyr; Philip carried the gospel to Samaria and to an Ethiopian official.
Role
The Seven were the church's answer to its first internal crisis: a practical, Spirit-filled team whose service kept a divided community together and whose preaching pushed the gospel beyond Jerusalem.
Relationship To Jesus
Jesus is not their direct founder — the apostles appointed them — but their ministry flows from his teaching that greatness is service. Stephen's martyrdom, witnessed by the young Saul, became a turning point: the persecutor who approved his death would become the apostle who carried the gospel to the Gentiles.
Key Members
Stephen; Philip
Scripture
Acts 6:1-7
Summary
The Seven were the Jerusalem church's first servant-team — seven Spirit-filled men chosen to feed overlooked widows, whose service kept a divided church together and whose preaching pushed the gospel outward.
