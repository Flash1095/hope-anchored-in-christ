Nicolaitans

Group
Nicolaitans
Period
The early church, first century AD
Origin
The Nicolaitans appear only twice, both in the letters to the seven churches in Revelation, where the risen Christ says he hates their deeds and their teaching. No writing of theirs survives, and no other New Testament book names them.
Beliefs
Almost nothing is known with certainty. Early church writers later linked them to Nicolas of Antioch, one of the Seven — a tradition, not a biblical statement, and one the evidence cannot confirm. What Revelation condemns is teaching that made peace with idolatry and immorality, likely the same compromise condemned in the letter to Pergamum: eating food sacrificed to idols and sexual immorality.
Practices
Their practices can only be inferred from what Revelation condemns: a teaching that let believers eat idol food and join pagan sexual practice while claiming faith in Christ.
Role
The Nicolaitans matter less as a movement than as a warning: the risen Christ names false teaching in his own churches and hates it. Their obscurity is itself the lesson — a group can matter enormously to one congregation and vanish from history.
Relationship To Jesus
Jesus — the risen Lord speaking in Revelation — is their only biblical judge: he commends Ephesus for hating their deeds and warns Pergamum to repent of tolerating their teaching. This entry records only what Revelation says and labels the Nicolas connection as later tradition, because Scripture does not make it.
Scripture
Revelation 2:6; Revelation 2:14-15
Summary
The Nicolaitans are named only in Revelation, where the risen Christ condemns their deeds and teaching — a shadowy early group whose link to Nicolas of Antioch is later tradition, not Scripture.
