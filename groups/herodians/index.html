Herodians

Group
Herodians
Period
First century AD
Origin
The Herodians were supporters of Herod's dynasty — the political party that backed the Herodian kings and their arrangement with Rome. They appear only in the Gospels, always in alliance with the Pharisees against Jesus, and no writing of their own survives.
Beliefs
Little is known of their doctrine. What defines them is politics: loyalty to Herod's house, acceptance of Roman rule as the price of Jewish self-government, and hostility to any popular movement that threatened the arrangement.
Practices
They plotted and questioned rather than preached. In the Gospels they join Pharisees in trying to trap Jesus with the tax question and in plotting his death — political operators, not teachers.
Role
Herodians were the court party: men whose standing depended on Herod's throne and Rome's favor. They were a small, pragmatic faction, and their alliance with the Pharisees — their natural opponents — shows how threatening Jesus seemed to both religion and politics.
Relationship To Jesus
The Herodians appear in Jesus' story only as opponents, twice joining Pharisees against him. Jesus never debates their views, because they had no teaching to debate — only a throne to protect.
Scripture
Matthew 22:15-22; Mark 3:6; Mark 12:13-17
Summary
The Herodians were the court party of Herod's dynasty — pragmatic supporters of Roman-backed rule who appear in the Gospels only in alliance with the Pharisees against Jesus.
