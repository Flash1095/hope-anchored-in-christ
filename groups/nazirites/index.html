Nazirites

Group
Nazirites
Period
From Moses through the New Testament period
Origin
A Nazirite was an Israelite — man or woman — who took a special vow of separation to God for a set time, or in rare cases for life. The Law of Moses spells out the vow: no wine or grape products, no cutting the hair, and no contact with a dead body.
Beliefs
Nazirites believed holiness could be chosen, not just inherited: that a life set apart by visible signs could honor God and witness to his people. The vow's visible marks made consecration public.
Practices
For the vow's length they abstained from wine and grapes, let their hair grow uncut, and avoided corpses — even family dead. At the vow's end they brought offerings to the Temple and shaved their head. Lifelong Nazirites, like Samson and Samuel, carried the marks from birth by their parents' vow.
Role
Nazirites were living sermons in Israel: walking reminders of consecration, recognizable at a glance by uncut hair and abstinence. Their vow bridged priest and layperson — a temporary priesthood any Israelite could take up.
Relationship To Jesus
Jesus was not a Nazirite — he drank wine and touched the dead — a contrast the Gospels quietly note. But Nazirite devotion frames two of his forerunners: Samuel, vowed before birth, and John the Baptist, whose abstinence from wine is often read as Nazirite-like, though Luke never uses the word. Paul later paid for Nazirite vows in Jerusalem to show he still honored the Law.
Key Members
Samson; Samuel
Scripture
Numbers 6:1-21; Judges 13:3-5; Judges 16:17; 1 Samuel 1:11; Amos 2:11-12; Acts 21:23-26
Summary
Nazirites were Israelites under a special vow of separation — no wine, uncut hair, no contact with the dead — a visible, voluntary consecration any Israelite could take up for a time, or for life.
