Priests

Group
Priests
Also Called
Aaronic Priests; Sons of Aaron
Period
From Moses through the destruction of the Temple in AD 70
Origin
God set apart Aaron and his sons to serve as priests, and the priesthood passed father to son within the tribe of Levi. Twenty-four divisions rotated through Temple service, so most priests lived in towns across the land and came to Jerusalem only for their appointed weeks.
Beliefs
Priests believed God had appointed sacrifice, purity, and blessing as the way a holy God dwells among a sinful people. They guarded the sanctuary, taught the Law's purity rules, and offered the daily, Sabbath, festival, and sin offerings that kept Israel's worship going.
Practices
Their work was hands-on and bloody: slaughtering and burning offerings, sprinkling blood, burning incense, trimming lamps, baking showbread, blessing the people, and examining skin diseases and houses for ritual impurity.
Role
Priests were Israel's mediators, standing between God and the people at the altar. They were supported by tithes and offerings rather than land, and ordinary Israelites met them mainly at festivals, at the Temple, and in moments of birth, disease, and guilt.
Relationship To Jesus
Jesus honored the priests' God-given role — he sent healed lepers to them as the Law required — while challenging the Temple system they ran, above all when he cleared the courts. The Letter to the Hebrews presents Jesus himself as the great high priest who offered himself once for all, ending the need for repeated sacrifice.
Key Members
Aaron; Ezra
Scripture
Exodus 28:1; Leviticus 8:1-36; Numbers 18:1-7; 1 Chronicles 24:1-19; Luke 1:8-23; Hebrews 7:11-28
Summary
The priests were Aaron's descendants, set apart to offer Israel's sacrifices, guard the sanctuary, and bless the people — mediators at the altar from Moses until the Temple fell.
