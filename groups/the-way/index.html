The Way

Group
The Way
Also Called
Followers of the Way
Period
The early church, from Pentecost through the first century AD
Origin
'The Way' was the earliest name the followers of Jesus gave themselves, before 'Christian' was coined at Antioch. It comes from Jesus' own claim to be the way, and from the Jewish language of walking in God's way — a life, not just a belief.
Beliefs
The Way believed Jesus was the Messiah, risen from the dead, and that his teaching was the true path of God's people. Its members were Jews who saw themselves as Israel fulfilled, not as a separate religion — a distinction that would take decades to sharpen.
Practices
They met in the Temple courts and in homes, shared meals including the breaking of bread, prayed together, held property in common where need required, baptized new believers, and preached Jesus in synagogues and streets.
Role
The Way was a community inside Judaism that quickly became a movement across the empire. Its members were arrested, scattered, and debated by synagogues and councils, and its growth among Gentiles forced the church's hardest early question: must a Gentile become a Jew to follow the Jewish Messiah?
Relationship To Jesus
The Way exists because of Jesus: it is his followers continuing his life after his ascension. Saul persecuted the Way before meeting the risen Lord on the Damascus road, and Luke uses the name for the movement throughout Acts, even after 'Christian' appears.
Key Members
Simon Peter; Stephen; Barnabas; Paul
Scripture
Acts 9:1-2; Acts 19:8-10; Acts 22:4; Acts 24:14; Acts 11:25-26
Summary
'The Way' was what Jesus' earliest followers called themselves — a Jewish movement of people walking in his path, meeting in homes and Temple courts, before the name 'Christian' was ever used.
