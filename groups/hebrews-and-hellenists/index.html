Hebrews and Hellenists

Group
Hebrews and Hellenists
Also Called
Hebrew Christians; Hellenist Christians
Period
The early church, Jerusalem
Origin
In the Jerusalem church, two groups of Jewish believers lived side by side: Hebrews, who spoke Aramaic and kept the customs of Judea, and Hellenists, Greek-speaking Jews from the Diaspora whose Scriptures were the Greek translation and whose habits were shaped by the wider world. The division was language and culture, not doctrine — but it nearly split the church.
Beliefs
Both groups believed Jesus was the Messiah and shared the apostles' teaching. They differed in language, custom, and daily life: which synagogues they attended, how they buried their dead, and whose widows got fed first.
Practices
They worshiped in different synagogues, spoke different languages at home, and brought different expectations to the common table. When the daily food distribution overlooked Hellenist widows, the apostles answered with the Seven — a team chosen, by tradition, from the Hellenist side itself.
Role
Hebrews and Hellenists were the church's first culture clash: proof that the gospel crossed language barriers from the start, and a warning that shared faith does not automatically end favoritism. Their story is the New Testament's first lesson in handling diversity without division.
Relationship To Jesus
Jesus ministered in both worlds — Aramaic-speaking Galilee and Greek-speaking Decapolis — but the Hebrew-Hellenist tension arose after Pentecost, when his followers had to live as one body across a language line. Stephen, a Hellenist, became the first martyr, and Philip, another, carried the gospel to Samaria — the Hellenists pushed the mission outward.
Key Members
Stephen; Philip
Scripture
Acts 6:1-7; Acts 9:29; Acts 11:19-21
Summary
Hebrews and Hellenists were the two cultures of the Jerusalem church — Aramaic-speaking Judeans and Greek-speaking Diaspora Jews — whose overlooked widows forced the church's first lesson in handling diversity without division.
