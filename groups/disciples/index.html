Disciples

Group
Disciples
Also Called
Followers; Learners
Period
The ministry of Jesus through the early church
Origin
In the ancient world a disciple was a learner attached to a teacher, sharing the teacher's life in order to become like him. John's disciples, the Pharisees' disciples, and Jesus' disciples all fit the pattern — but Jesus redefined it around himself: his disciples were not students of a subject but followers of a person.
Beliefs
Disciples believed Jesus was the Messiah and Lord, and that following him meant learning his teaching, obeying his commands, and sharing his mission. Their faith was not private opinion but a whole life reordered around him.
Practices
They traveled with Jesus, listened to his teaching, watched his miracles, asked their questions in private, shared his meals, and were sent out to preach, heal, and cast out demons. After the resurrection their shared life — teaching, fellowship, breaking of bread, and prayer — became the pattern of the church.
Role
Disciples were Jesus' inner circle and his wider following at once: the Twelve, the Seventy-Two, and the crowds of men and women who stayed, left, or followed from a distance. Women disciples — Mary Magdalene and others — supported and followed him, a striking fact in that culture.
Relationship To Jesus
Jesus called his disciples, taught them, corrected them, washed their feet, and sent them. They misunderstood him, argued about greatness, deserted him at his arrest, and then — after meeting the risen Lord — became the witnesses who carried his name to the world. The Gospels do not hide their failures, which is part of why their testimony is credible.
Key Members
Simon Peter; Matthew; Mary Magdalene; Andrew; Thomas; Philip
Scripture
Matthew 4:18-22; Matthew 28:18-20; Luke 9:23; John 6:66-69; Acts 2:42-47
Summary
The disciples were Jesus' learners and followers — the men and women who shared his life, learned his teaching, failed him, and then carried his name to the world.
