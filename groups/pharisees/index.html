Pharisees

Group
Pharisees
Period
Second Temple period, especially the second century BC through the first century AD
Origin
The Pharisees emerged in the troubled century after the Maccabean revolt, as a lay movement determined that ordinary life — not just Temple ritual — should be lived by God's Law. The name probably comes from a word meaning 'separated ones.' They survived the Temple's destruction and their teaching shaped the Judaism that followed.
Beliefs
Pharisees believed the written Law and an oral tradition handed down alongside it, the resurrection of the dead, angels and spirits, divine providence working with human choice, and a coming judgment. They held that holiness belonged in kitchens and marketplaces, not only in the sanctuary.
Practices
They tithed garden herbs, washed hands before meals, kept Sabbath and purity rules with scrupulous care, fasted regularly, prayed publicly, and taught in synagogues. Their discipline won them wide respect among ordinary people, who saw them as the most serious about obeying God.
Role
Pharisees were the popular religious teachers of first-century Judaism — lay scholars, not priests, whose influence came from learning and piety rather than office. They sat on the Sanhedrin, ran schools, and were the group most Jews would have named if asked who truly lived the Law.
Relationship To Jesus
Jesus debated the Pharisees more than anyone. He shared much of their theology — resurrection, judgment, the Law's authority — and his fiercest words for them were aimed not at their doctrine but at pride, show, and using tradition to dodge mercy: tithing mint while neglecting justice. Yet not all Pharisees opposed him: Nicodemus defended him, Joseph's circle included sympathizers, and Paul remained proud to call himself a Pharisee even as an apostle.
Key Members
Nicodemus; Paul; Simon the Pharisee
Scripture
Matthew 23:1-39; Mark 7:1-13; Luke 18:9-14; John 3:1-2; Acts 23:6-10; Philippians 3:5-6
Summary
The Pharisees were the popular lay teachers of first-century Judaism — serious about the Law in everyday life, believers in resurrection and angels, widely respected, and Jesus' most frequent debate partners, who were neither all villains nor all heroes.
