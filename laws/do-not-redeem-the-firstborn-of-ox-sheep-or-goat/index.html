Do not redeem the firstborn of ox, sheep, or goat

The command: Do not redeem the firstborn of ox, sheep, or goat (Numbers 18:17). Clean firstborn animals belonged to the LORD outright and could not be bought back.

Commandment

Do not redeem the firstborn of ox, sheep, or goat.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Redemption of the firstborn remembered that God spared Israel's firstborn in Egypt. The New Testament speaks of Jesus as the firstborn and of believers as redeemed, but does not continue the redemption payment as a Christian rite (Colossians 1:15-18; 1 Peter 1:18-19).

Purpose

Scripture's stated reason: because the LORD slew all the firstborn in Egypt and spared Israel's, therefore the firstborn are his (Exodus 13:14-15).

Function

Clean firstborn animals belonged to the LORD outright and could not be bought back — a ceremonial law of unredeemable belonging. Its goal was to hold the firstborn of herd and flock as God's own, not as property the owner could reclaim at a price. It did not address a particular evil; its work was dedication and remembrance, not restraint. The firstborn stayed God's because God had spared Israel's firstborn in Egypt, and no payment could undo that claim.
