Do not work the firstborn ox

The command: Do not work the firstborn ox (Deuteronomy 15:19). The firstling ox might not be put to work, since it was sanctified to the LORD.

Commandment

Do not work the firstborn ox.

Application

It cannot be practised as written without the standing temple and Aaronic priesthood; its enduring lesson is God's holiness, the cost of atonement, and worship offered exactly as God appoints.

Purpose

Scripture's stated ground: the firstling was sanctified unto the LORD and might not be put to common work (Deuteronomy 15:19).

Function

The firstling ox might not be put to work, since it was sanctified to the LORD — a ceremonial law of set-apart animals. Its goal was to keep the firstborn from common labour, the beast claimed by God never yoked to the plough. It did not address a particular evil; its work was dedication, not restraint. The unworked ox said the firstborn belonged to God, and no profit was taken from what was his.
