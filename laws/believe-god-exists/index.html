Believe God exists

Exodus 20:2 opens the Ten Words with God's self-declaration: 'I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.' In the traditional enumeration this verse is counted as a positive command — the first mitzvah — even though it reads as a statement rather than an imperative, because it grounds every command that follows in who God is.

Commandment

Believe that God exists, and that he is the LORD who brought Israel out of Egypt.

Application

Every other command rests on this one: obedience follows from knowing who commands. The New Testament carries the same ground forward — without faith it is impossible to please God, for he that cometh to God must believe that he is (Hebrews 11:6).

Purpose

Scripture's stated ground is the Exodus itself: the God who redeemed Israel is the God who commands Israel (Exodus 20:2).

Function

Before any command comes the God who commands. This opening of the Ten Words — counted in the traditional enumeration as the first mitzvah, though it reads as a statement rather than an imperative — is foundational moral law in the same sense as the rest. Its goal was to ground every command that follows in who God is: the LORD who brought Israel out of Egypt. The evil it addressed was forgetfulness of the Redeemer, which leaves obedience with no one to obey. It restrained that evil by putting God's self-declaration first.
