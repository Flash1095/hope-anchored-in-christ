Write This Song and Teach It

Command
Write This Song and Teach It
Category
Ceremonial Law
Summary
Now therefore write ye this song for you, and teach it the children of Israel: put it in their mouths, that this song may be a witness for me against the children of Israel. The verse's plain command is the Song; the Torah-scroll mitzvah traditionally derived from it is stated as a derivation, not as the verse's wording.
Commandment
Write this song for yourselves, teach it to the children of Israel, and put it in their mouths, that it may be a witness against them.
Scripture
Deuteronomy 31:19
Application
The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Views differ on what the verse commands: the plain reading commands writing and teaching this Song (the Song of Moses, Deuteronomy 32), as a witness against Israel; rabbinic tradition derives from it the mitzvah for every person to write a Torah scroll for himself (or have one written). The derivation is traditional, not the verse's plain sense, and this draft says so rather than presenting the scroll mitzvah as the verse's direct command — see debated_mappings and flags.
