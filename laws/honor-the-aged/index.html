Honor the Aged

The respect law: rise before gray hair and honor the aged, treating age itself as worthy of deference.

Commandment

Stand up in the presence of gray hair and honor the aged.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as moral. Paul applies the same posture inside the church: treat older men as fathers and older women as mothers, correcting with gentleness.

Purpose

Leviticus 19:32 commands rising before gray hair as reverence for age itself; Paul applies the posture inside the church (1 Timothy 5:1-2).

Function

This is a moral law: it commands reverence for age itself, and it binds in every age. Its goal was a community that honours the old rather than discarding them. The evil it addressed was contempt for the aged — treating grey hair as worthless. It restrained that evil by making respect a duty, not a courtesy: you rise before grey hair whether you feel like it or not.
