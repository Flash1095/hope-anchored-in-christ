The Sinew That Shrank

Because Jacob's hip was struck as he wrestled, Israel does not eat the sinew on the hollow of the thigh. The verse states this as an explanation of an existing practice, not as a direct imperative — see flags.

Commandment

Do not eat the sinew which shrank, on the hollow of the thigh.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. This is a memorial custom rooted in Jacob's wrestling: the practice remembers that night. Jewish tradition still observes it (nikkur, the removal of the sinew); Christians generally read dietary customs of this kind as covenant-bound rather than continuing obligations.

Purpose

Scripture's stated reason: it memorializes Jacob's wrestling, in which the hollow of his thigh was touched (Genesis 32:31-32).

Function

Because Jacob's hip was struck as he wrestled, Israel did not eat the sinew on the hollow of the thigh — a ceremonial law that is a memorial custom, not a direct command. Its goal was to remember Jacob's wrestling, in which the hollow of his thigh was touched. It addressed no particular evil — its work was remembrance, not restraint. The sinew left uneaten said the night at the Jabbok was never forgotten, and the practice itself was the memory.
