Do not offer leaven on the altar

The command: Do not offer leaven on the altar (Leviticus 2:11). No leaven might be burned as an offering made by fire, though leavened bread could be offered as firstfruits.

Commandment

Do not offer leaven on the altar.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Hebrews teaches these sacrifices could never perfect the worshiper; they pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering, which is why they ceased with the new covenant.

Purpose

To provide atonement, thanksgiving, and fellowship under the old covenant. Hebrews teaches these pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering (Hebrews 10:1-14), so the system ceased.

Function

No leaven might be burned as an offering made by fire — a ceremonial law about what belongs on the altar. Its goal was to keep the altar's fire free of leaven, holding the line between the feast's bread and the fire's offering. Scripture gives no particular evil this restrained beyond the guarding of the altar itself, and I will not invent one; its work was order and reverence, not restraint. The altar took only what God appointed, and leaven was not among it.
