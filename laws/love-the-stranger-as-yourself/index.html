Love the stranger as yourself

Leviticus 19:34 extends neighbour-love to the stranger dwelling among Israel: 'thou shalt love him as thyself; for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt: I am the LORD your God.' Israel's own memory of Egypt is the ground of the duty.

Commandment

Love the stranger who dwells with you as yourself.

Application

The church hears the same ground: those who were once strangers and aliens have been brought near (Ephesians 2:19), and love for the stranger is neighbour-love in its widest honest form.

Purpose

Scripture's stated reason: for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt (Leviticus 19:34).

Function

Moral law, as I read it: a duty that outlives Israel's courts. Leviticus 19:34 extends neighbour-love to the stranger dwelling among Israel: 'thou shalt love him as thyself.' Scripture gives the reason: for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt. The evil it addressed was contempt for the outsider — treating the stranger as less than a neighbour. It restrained that evil by making the stranger's standing equal to the native-born: the same love, the same law.
