Leaven shall not be seen in your borders during Passover

During Unleavened Bread, no leaven may be seen anywhere within Israel's borders, not only in the house.

Commandment

Let no leaven be seen in all your borders during the seven days.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The wider boundary extended the clean break from leaven beyond the house to the whole land's feast observance. Debated: some see the commanded observance fulfilled as shadow (Colossians 2:16-17); others keep feasts voluntarily as memorial, not as binding command. The site does not settle whether observance continues.

Purpose

Scripture's stated context: the meal is eaten in haste on the night of deliverance from Egypt, ready to depart (Exodus 12:11).

Function

No leaven was to be seen anywhere inside Israel's borders, not just in the house — a ceremonial law that stretched the memorial across the whole land. Its goal was a clean break from leaven for the whole feast week, the entire people keeping the same remembrance together. No particular evil is in view here; its work was remembrance, not restraint. The wider boundary made the point plain: this was a nation's memory, not one household's habit.
