Do no servile work on the eighth day of Tabernacles

On the eighth day, the solemn assembly after Tabernacles, no servile work may be done.

Commandment

On the eighth day of Tabernacles, do no servile work.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The eighth day's ban closed the feast season with a separate solemn assembly. Debated: some see the commanded observance fulfilled as shadow (Colossians 2:16-17); others keep feasts voluntarily as memorial, not as binding command. The site does not settle whether observance continues.

Purpose

To rehearse God's saving acts at appointed seasons (Leviticus 23); Paul calls Christ our Passover (1 Corinthians 5:7).

Function

On the eighth day, the solemn assembly after Tabernacles, no servile work might be done — a ceremonial law for the closing day. Its goal was to keep the eighth day distinct as its own assembly, the feast season's final gathering set apart from labour. It did not address a particular evil; its work was honouring holy time, not restraint. The ban closed the season the way the first day's ban had opened it, and the people ended the feasts as they began them: at rest.
