Priests shall wear the sacred garments for service

The command: Priests shall wear the sacred garments for service (Exodus 28:2). Aaron and his sons ministered in garments made for glory and for beauty, each piece prescribed in Exodus 28.

Commandment

Priests shall wear the sacred garments for service, as prescribed in Exodus 28:2.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Hebrews teaches the earthly tabernacle was a copy of the heavenly one, and Christ now serves as high priest in the true tabernacle.

Purpose

To give Israel a mediated approach to God through the tabernacle pattern (Exodus 25:40). Hebrews teaches the earthly copy gave way to Christ's priesthood in the true tabernacle (Hebrews 8:1-6).

Function

Garments made for glory and for beauty marked the priests for service, and that vesting is what makes this ceremonial: a rite of consecration. Its goal was to set Aaron and his sons visibly apart for the holy work, so that everyone who saw them knew they stood between God and the people. It did not address a particular evil; its work was holiness made visible, not restraint. The clothes were not decoration; they were the uniform of men appointed to handle holy things.
