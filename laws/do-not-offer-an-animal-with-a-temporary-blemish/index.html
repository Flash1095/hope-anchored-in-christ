Do not offer an animal with a temporary blemish

The command: Do not offer an animal with a temporary blemish (Deuteronomy 17:1). Even a temporary blemish, a passing defect, made an animal an abomination to the LORD as a sacrifice.

Commandment

Do not offer an animal with a temporary blemish.

Application

It cannot be practised as written without the standing temple and Aaronic priesthood; its enduring lesson is God's holiness, the cost of atonement, and worship offered exactly as God appoints.

Purpose

Scripture's stated ground: any blemish, even a passing one, was an abomination unto the LORD thy God (Deuteronomy 17:1).

Function

Even a temporary blemish, a passing defect, made an animal an abomination to the LORD as a sacrifice — a ceremonial law of the whole offering. Its goal was to keep the altar's standard absolute: any blemish, even one that would heal, disqualified the animal. The evil was offering God the damaged beast and hoping the passing defect would pass unnoticed. It restrained that by the word abomination: temporary or not, the blemish made the offering unacceptable, and the giver brought a whole animal or none.
