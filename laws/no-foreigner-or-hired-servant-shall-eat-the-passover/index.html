No foreigner or hired servant shall eat the Passover

A foreigner merely passing through, and a hired servant, may not eat the Passover unless brought into the covenant household.

Commandment

No foreigner or hired servant shall eat the Passover.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The rule guarded the covenant character of the meal; the stranger who joined the household by circumcision could eat (Exodus 12:48).

Purpose

Scripture's stated context: the meal is eaten in haste on the night of deliverance from Egypt, ready to depart (Exodus 12:11).

Function

This is a ceremonial law about who belongs at the table. Its goal was to guard the covenant character of the meal: the Passover belonged to God's household, not to any passer-by. The evil was treating a covenant meal as common food, open to anyone regardless of belonging. It restrained that by drawing a line at the table — though the line had a door in it, since the stranger who joined the household by circumcision could eat.
