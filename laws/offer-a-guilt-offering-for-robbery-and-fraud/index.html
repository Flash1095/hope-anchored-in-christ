Offer a guilt offering for robbery and fraud

The command: Offer a guilt offering for robbery and fraud (Leviticus 6:2). One who denied a deposit, dealt falsely, or robbed his neighbour restored the principal plus a fifth, and brought a ram as a trespass offering.

Commandment

Offer a guilt offering for robbery and fraud, as prescribed in Leviticus 6:2.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Hebrews teaches these sacrifices could never perfect the worshiper; they pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering, which is why they ceased with the new covenant.

Purpose

To provide atonement, thanksgiving, and fellowship under the old covenant. Hebrews teaches these pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering (Hebrews 10:1-14), so the system ceased.

Function

The robber restored the principal plus a fifth and brought a ram — a ceremonial law where atonement met restitution. Its goal was to make the wrong right in both directions: toward the neighbour who was defrauded, and toward God against whom the false dealing was also sin. The evil was robbery and fraud, denying a deposit or dealing falsely for gain. It restrained that evil by making the price of theft higher than the theft — repayment with interest, and blood on the altar besides.
