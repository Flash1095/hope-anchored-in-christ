Rest on the first day of Tabernacles

The first day of the Feast of Tabernacles is a holy convocation of rest, opening seven days of dwelling in booths.

Commandment

Keep the first day of Tabernacles as a holy convocation and rest on it.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The rest opened the harvest feast that remembered Israel's booths in the wilderness. Debated: some see the commanded observance fulfilled as shadow (Colossians 2:16-17); others keep feasts voluntarily as memorial, not as binding command. The site does not settle whether observance continues.

Purpose

To rehearse God's saving acts at appointed seasons (Leviticus 23); Paul calls Christ our Passover (1 Corinthians 5:7).

Function

The first day of Tabernacles was a holy convocation of rest, opening seven days of dwelling in booths — a ceremonial law of harvest joy. Its goal was to open the feast that remembered Israel's booths in the wilderness with a day set apart, worship before the week's dwelling began. It did not address a particular evil; its work was remembrance and rejoicing, not restraint. The rest said the wilderness memory was worth stopping work for, and the booths were worth living in.
