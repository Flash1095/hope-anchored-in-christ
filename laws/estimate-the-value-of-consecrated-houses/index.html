Estimate the value of consecrated houses

The command: Estimate the value of consecrated houses (Leviticus 27:14). A house sanctified to the LORD was valued by the priest, and his estimation stood.

Commandment

Estimate the value of consecrated houses, as prescribed in Leviticus 27:14.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Valuations and redemptions priced devotion in a fixed, public way, so that a vow could not be quietly abandoned or quietly inflated. The whole system of dedicated things ended with the temple system; Christians give freely and cheerfully, not by valuation tables (2 Corinthians 9:7).

Purpose

TRADITIONAL reading: fixed valuations kept vows honest and public, preventing both evasion and extravagant self-pricing. Scripture states the scale without a separate reason (Leviticus 27:1-29).

Function

A house sanctified to the LORD was valued by the priest, and his estimation stood — a ceremonial law of the priced house. Its goal, traditionally, was to keep vows honest and public: the house given to God was priced by the priest, not by its owner. The evil was the self-valued dedication, the giver naming his own price for what he gave. It restrained that by the priest's word: his estimation stood, and the house was worth what the priest said it was.
