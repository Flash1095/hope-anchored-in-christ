Do Not Speak Evil of One Another

James's speech command: speak not evil one of another, brethren; he that speaks evil of his brother judges the law.

Commandment

Speak not evil one of another, brethren. He that speaketh evil of his brother, and judgeth his brother, speaketh evil of the law, and judgeth the law.

Application

Distinct from the live judgment row, which governs hypocritical condemnation: this governs slanderous speech about a brother, which usurps the one Lawgiver who alone can save and destroy.

Purpose

Scripture's own reason: 'there is one lawgiver, who is able to save and to destroy' (James 4:12).

Function

Slander about a brother usurps the one Lawgiver. This one is apostolic, not Mosaic, so the civil, ceremonial, and moral division does not apply. Its goal was to stop brethren from judging the law by speaking evil of one another. The evil it addressed was slanderous speech — the tongue doing the Lawgiver's work without his authority. It restrained that evil with the reason Scripture gives: there is one lawgiver, who is able to save and to destroy, and he alone holds that seat.
