Do no servile work on the seventh day of Unleavened Bread

On the seventh day of Unleavened Bread, no servile work may be done; the day is a holy convocation.

Commandment

On the seventh day of Unleavened Bread, do no servile work.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The closing work ban matched the opening one, framing the whole feast week. Debated: some see the commanded observance fulfilled as shadow (Colossians 2:16-17); others keep feasts voluntarily as memorial, not as binding command. The site does not settle whether observance continues.

Purpose

To rehearse God's saving acts at appointed seasons (Leviticus 23); Paul calls Christ our Passover (1 Corinthians 5:7).

Function

The closing work ban matched the opening one, framing the whole feast week — a ceremonial law about holy time. Its goal was to keep the seventh day of Unleavened Bread set apart from ordinary labour, so the feast ended in worship. It addressed no particular evil — its work was remembrance, not restraint. Two bookends of rest held the week together, and the ban on servile work was what made those bookends visible in daily life.
