Carry out the laws of tzara'at

The whole tzara'at procedure is carried out as Leviticus 13–14 prescribes: the priest examines skin and house, the unclean cries 'Unclean, unclean,' the house is purified, the cleansed person shaves, washes, and brings offerings.

Commandment

Carry out the laws of tzara'at as prescribed: priestly examination, separation while unclean, and the offerings of cleansing.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The procedure kept the camp holy and gave the afflicted a road back to worship. Jesus healed the leper and sent him to the priest with the Mosaic offering (Mark 1:40-45), showing mercy reaching where ritual could only separate.

Purpose

To order who could approach worship while ceremonially unclean and how they were restored. TRADITIONAL reading: with the temple order ended, these washings no longer bind believers; Jesus' touch restored those the laws isolated (Mark 1:40-45; Luke 8:43-48).

Function

The whole tzara'at procedure was carried out as Leviticus 13–14 prescribes: the priest examined skin and house, the unclean cried 'Unclean, unclean,' the cleansed person shaved, washed, and brought offerings — a ceremonial law of examination and return. Its goal was to keep the camp holy and give the afflicted a road back to worship. The evil was the disease left unjudged, spreading through the camp with no priest to name it. It restrained that by the procedure: examine, separate, purify, and bring the cleansed one back.
