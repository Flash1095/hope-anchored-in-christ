Do not show favour to the poor in judgment either

In judgment, do not favour the poor either: pity for a poor litigant must not bend the verdict any more than respect for a rich one.

Commandment

Do not show favour to the poor in judgment either.

Application

Impartiality cuts both ways. Favouring the poor in court feels merciful, but it is still partiality — and it wrongs the other party while patronising the poor man, whose cause should win on its merits.

Purpose

TRADITIONAL reading: justice is blind to wealth and poverty alike, so favour shown to the poor in judgment is partiality no less than favour shown to the rich.

Function

In judgment, do not favour the poor either. Plain civil law: the Law laying down how a community handles this case.
