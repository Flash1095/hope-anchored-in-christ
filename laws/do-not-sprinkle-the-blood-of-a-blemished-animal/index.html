Do not sprinkle the blood of a blemished animal

The command: Do not sprinkle the blood of a blemished animal (Leviticus 22:22). The blood of a blemished animal might not be sprinkled on the altar.

Commandment

Do not sprinkle the blood of a blemished animal.

Application

It cannot be practised as written without the standing temple and Aaronic priesthood; its enduring lesson is God's holiness, the cost of atonement, and worship offered exactly as God appoints.

Purpose

Scripture's stated ground: blood sprinkled from a blemished animal could not make an acceptable offering (Leviticus 22:22).

Function

The blood of a blemished animal might not be sprinkled on the altar — a ceremonial law of the refused blood. Its goal was to keep the altar's blood acceptable: blood sprinkled from a blemished animal could not make an acceptable offering. The evil was trying to make a marred offering count by its blood, as if the sprinkling could fix the blemish. It restrained that by the ban: the blood stayed off the altar, and the blemished animal gave God nothing.
