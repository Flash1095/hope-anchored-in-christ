Do not eat leavened bread with the Passover on the afternoon of the fourteenth day

On the afternoon of the fourteenth day, when the Passover lamb is sacrificed, no leavened bread may be eaten with it.

Commandment

On the afternoon of the fourteenth day, eat no leavened bread with the Passover sacrifice.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The rule ties the leaven ban directly to the Passover sacrifice itself, not only to the seven-day feast. Debated: some see the commanded observance fulfilled as shadow (Colossians 2:16-17); others keep feasts voluntarily as memorial, not as binding command. The site does not settle whether observance continues.

Purpose

Scripture's stated context: the meal is eaten in haste on the night of deliverance from Egypt, ready to depart (Exodus 12:11).

Function

On the afternoon of the fourteenth day, when the Passover lamb was sacrificed, no leavened bread might be eaten with it — a ceremonial law tying the leaven ban to the sacrifice itself. Its goal was to join the unleavened memorial directly to the Passover offering, not only to the seven-day feast. It did not address a particular evil; its work was remembrance and order, not restraint. The leaven was gone before the lamb was offered, and the sacrifice and the bread were one observance.
