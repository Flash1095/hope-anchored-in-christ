The King Writes a Copy of the Law

Command
The King Writes a Copy of the Law
Category
Civil Law
Summary
And it shall be, when he sitteth upon the throne of his kingdom, that he shall write him a copy of this law in a book out of that which is before the priests the Levites: and it shall be with him, and he shall read therein all the days of his life. This royal-copy law is in no live row.
Commandment
When the king sits on his throne, he shall write for himself a copy of this law from the priests' book, keep it with him, and read it all the days of his life.
Scripture
Deuteronomy 17:18-20
Application
The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as civil. The picture is deliberately humbling: the king is not above the law but under it, reading it all his life. The New Testament universalizes the instinct — rulers are God's servants under God's law (Romans 13:4) — without continuing the royal copy rite.
Related Topics
Kingship; Scripture; Fear of God
Related Books
Deuteronomy
Related People
Moses
Purpose
Scripture's stated reason: that he may learn to fear the LORD his God, to keep all the words of this law and these statutes, to do them — that his heart be not lifted up above his brethren (Deuteronomy 17:19-20).
