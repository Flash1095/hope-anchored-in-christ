Burn the red heifer and use its ashes for purification

A red heifer without spot or blemish, never yoked, is slaughtered and burned outside the camp with cedar wood, hyssop, and scarlet, and its ashes are kept for the water of purification.

Commandment

Burn a red heifer without spot or blemish, never yoked, outside the camp, and keep its ashes for the water of purification.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Hebrews makes the contrast explicit: if the ashes of an heifer sprinkling the unclean sanctified to the purifying of the flesh, how much more shall the blood of Christ purge the conscience (Hebrews 9:13-14).

Purpose

Scripture's stated context: the ashes are for a purification for sin — the water of separation (Numbers 19:9). No further reason is given; tradition classes it among the chukim, statutes whose reason is not explained.

Function

A red heifer without spot, never yoked, was burned outside the camp with cedar, hyssop, and scarlet, and its ashes were kept for the water of purification — a ceremonial law Scripture leaves unexplained. Its goal, as far as Scripture states it, was purification for sin: the ashes made the water of separation. No further reason is given, so I will not invent an evil it restrained; its work was cleansing and restoration, not restraint.
