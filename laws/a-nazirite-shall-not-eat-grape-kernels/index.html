A Nazirite shall not eat grape kernels

Throughout the vow, the Nazirite eats nothing of the vine, not even the kernels or husks of grapes.

Commandment

A Nazirite shall eat no grape kernels or husks for all the days of his separation.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Acts 21:23-26 shows Paul joining men under a vow in Jerusalem, which shows the practice continued among Jewish believers, not that it is imposed on Christians.

Purpose

Scripture's stated context: the Nazirite separates himself unto the LORD (Numbers 6:2,5-6); no further reason is given, so the components' purposes are traditional — wine abstained from as a mark of consecration, extended even to kernels and husks.

Function

Not even the kernels or husks of grapes might the Nazirite eat — a ceremonial law of thoroughness. Its goal, traditionally, was to extend the vine's abstinence to its smallest parts, so the separation left no loophole. It did not address a particular evil; its work was consecration, not restraint. The kernel was the test of the vow's seriousness: a man who would not eat the husk had truly set the vine aside.
