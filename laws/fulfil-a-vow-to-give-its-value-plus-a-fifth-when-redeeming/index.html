Fulfil a vow to give its value plus a fifth when redeeming

The command: Fulfil a vow to give its value plus a fifth when redeeming (Leviticus 27:13). One who redeemed a vowed beast paid the priest's estimation plus a fifth part.

Commandment

Fulfil a vow to give its value plus a fifth when redeeming, as prescribed in Leviticus 27:13.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Valuations and redemptions priced devotion in a fixed, public way, so that a vow could not be quietly abandoned or quietly inflated. The whole system of dedicated things ended with the temple system; Christians give freely and cheerfully, not by valuation tables (2 Corinthians 9:7).

Purpose

TRADITIONAL reading: fixed valuations kept vows honest and public, preventing both evasion and extravagant self-pricing. Scripture states the scale without a separate reason (Leviticus 27:1-29).

Function

One who redeemed a vowed beast paid the priest's estimation plus a fifth part — a ceremonial law of costly redemption. Its goal, traditionally, was to keep vows honest and public: redeeming what was vowed cost more than its value, so the vow was not taken lightly. The evil was the cheap buy-back, vowing freely and redeeming cheaply. It restrained that by the extra fifth: the redemption stung a little, and the vow was weighed before it was made.
