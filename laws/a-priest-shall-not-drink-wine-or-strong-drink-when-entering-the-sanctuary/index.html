A priest shall not drink wine or strong drink when entering the sanctuary

The command: A priest shall not drink wine or strong drink when entering the sanctuary (Leviticus 10:9). Priests on duty were to drink no wine or strong drink when they went into the tent of meeting.

Commandment

A priest shall not drink wine or strong drink when entering the sanctuary.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Hebrews teaches the earthly tabernacle was a copy of the heavenly one, and Christ now serves as high priest in the true tabernacle.

Purpose

To give Israel a mediated approach to God through the tabernacle pattern (Exodus 25:40). Hebrews teaches the earthly copy gave way to Christ's priesthood in the true tabernacle (Hebrews 8:1-6).

Function

Priests on duty were to drink no wine or strong drink when they went into the tent of meeting — a ceremonial law of the sober priest. Its goal was to keep the priests clear-headed in holy service, the wine left outside when they entered the sanctuary. The evil was dulled judgment at the altar, a priest handling holy things with his senses clouded. It restrained that by the ban: no wine on duty, so the priest who served God did so with a clear head.
