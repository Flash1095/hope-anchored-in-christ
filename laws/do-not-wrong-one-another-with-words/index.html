Do not wrong one another with words

One man must not wrong another with words — no speech that grieves, shames, or puts a neighbour down, including reminding him of a past he has left behind.

Commandment

Do not wrong one another with words.

Application

Words can wrong as surely as prices can: the taunt, the reminder of a convert's past, the remark meant to sting. The command guards the neighbour's dignity in speech, a duty that outlives the Jubilee calendar entirely.

Purpose

TRADITIONAL reading: words that wound a neighbour wrong him as truly as a bad bargain does, so the Law forbids wronging with words alongside wronging in trade (Leviticus 25:17).

Function

This governed neighbours, not priests — it belongs to the ordering of Israel's common life. One man must not wrong another with words — no speech that grieves, shames, or puts a neighbour down.
