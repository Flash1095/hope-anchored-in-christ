Offer a sin offering for a ruler

The command: Offer a sin offering for a ruler (Leviticus 4:22). When a ruler sinned through ignorance, he brought a male goat without blemish as his sin offering.

Commandment

Offer a sin offering for a ruler, as prescribed in Leviticus 4:22.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Hebrews teaches these sacrifices could never perfect the worshiper; they pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering, which is why they ceased with the new covenant.

Purpose

To provide atonement, thanksgiving, and fellowship under the old covenant. Hebrews teaches these pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering (Hebrews 10:1-14), so the system ceased.

Function

When a ruler sinned through ignorance, he brought a male goat without blemish — a ceremonial law for the man in power. Its goal was to atone for the ruler's sin, the leader standing before God like any other sinner, with his own offering. The evil was the ruler's sin treated as above atonement, power excusing guilt. It restrained that by the goat: the ruler brought his offering like the common man brought his, and the throne did not exempt him from the altar.
