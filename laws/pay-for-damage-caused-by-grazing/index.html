Pay for damage caused by grazing

If a man lets his beast loose into another man's field or vineyard so that it grazes there, he repays from the best of his own field and vineyard.

Commandment

Pay for damage caused by grazing on another's land.

Application

Careless pasturing is not a trifle: the owner whose beast ate his neighbour's crop pays from his best, not his leftovers. The measure teaches that damage is repaid at full value, not at a discount.

Purpose

To make wrongs right: grazing damage repaid from the best of the offender's own field.

Function
