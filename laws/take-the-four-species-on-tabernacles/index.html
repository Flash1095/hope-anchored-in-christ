Take the four species on Tabernacles

On the first day of Tabernacles, Israel takes the fruit of goodly trees, palm branches, boughs of thick trees, and willows of the brook, and rejoices before the LORD.

Commandment

On the first day of Tabernacles, take the four species — goodly fruit, palm branches, thick boughs, and brook willows — and rejoice before the LORD.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The branches in hand made the harvest joy visible and tangible through the feast week. Debated: some see the commanded observance fulfilled as shadow (Colossians 2:16-17); others keep feasts voluntarily as memorial, not as binding command. The site does not settle whether observance continues.

Purpose

To rehearse God's saving acts at appointed seasons (Leviticus 23); Paul calls Christ our Passover (1 Corinthians 5:7).

Function

On the first day of Tabernacles, Israel took the fruit of goodly trees, palm branches, boughs of thick trees, and willows of the brook, and rejoiced before the LORD — a ceremonial law of harvest joy in the hand. Its goal was to make the rejoicing visible and tangible, the branches carried through the feast week. It did not address a particular evil; its work was thanksgiving, not restraint. The four species said the harvest was God's gift in every kind, and the hands that held them were giving thanks.
