Do no servile work on the Feast of Weeks

On the Feast of Weeks, no servile work may be done; the day is a holy convocation.

Commandment

On the Feast of Weeks, do no servile work.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The work ban set the harvest thanksgiving apart from the harvest labor itself. Debated: some see the commanded observance fulfilled as shadow (Colossians 2:16-17); others keep feasts voluntarily as memorial, not as binding command. The site does not settle whether observance continues.

Purpose

To rehearse God's saving acts at appointed seasons (Leviticus 23); Paul calls Christ our Passover (1 Corinthians 5:7).

Function

On the Feast of Weeks, no servile work might be done — a ceremonial law setting the harvest thanksgiving apart. Its goal was to separate the day of rejoicing from the harvest labour itself, so the thanksgiving was not just another working day with a prayer attached. It did not address a particular evil; its work was honouring holy time, not restraint. The work ban made the feast visible: the fields waited, and the people kept the day to the LORD.
