Court shall not execute on a majority of one in a capital case

In a capital case the court must not execute on a majority of one; a death sentence requires a stronger majority than a single vote.

Commandment

The court shall not execute on a majority of one in a capital case.

Application

Life is weighed more heavily than other verdicts: a bare majority is not enough to put a man to death. The extra margin is a deliberate brake on irreversible judgment.

Purpose

TRADITIONAL reading: because a death sentence cannot be undone, a majority of one is insufficient to execute in a capital case. Scripture states the prohibition without a separate reason (Exodus 23:2).

Function

A courtroom law at heart: it gave judges something firm to stand on. In a capital case the court must not execute on a majority of one.
