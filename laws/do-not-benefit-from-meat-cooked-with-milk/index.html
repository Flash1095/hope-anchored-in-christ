Do not benefit from meat cooked with milk

No benefit may be taken from meat cooked with milk: the mixture may not be used, sold, or turned to any gain.

Commandment

Take no benefit from meat cooked with milk.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The threefold form — not to cook, not to eat, not to benefit — closes every use of the mixture. The New Testament records the food distinctions set aside (Mark 7:19; Acts 10).

Purpose

TRADITIONAL reading: the prohibition keeps what gives life (milk) from being mixed with what takes life (meat) in cooking and eating, and keeps Israel's table distinct; Scripture states no reason for this rule.

Function

No benefit might be taken from meat cooked with milk: the mixture might not be used, sold, or turned to any gain — a ceremonial law closing the last loophole. Its goal, traditionally, was to keep what gives life from being mixed with what takes life, and to keep Israel's table distinct. The evil it addressed, on that same traditional reading, was profiting from the forbidden mixture. It restrained that by forbidding the benefit itself — though I hold this as a traditional reading, not as Scripture's own stated reason.
