Do Not Forget to Entertain Strangers

Hebrews' hospitality command: be not forgetful to entertain strangers, for thereby some have entertained angels unawares.

Commandment

Be not forgetful to entertain strangers: for thereby some have entertained angels unawares.

Application

Hospitality is commanded as remembrance, not convenience: strangers are easily forgotten, and the precedent is Abraham's tent. The 'angels unawares' is the motive, not a promise of reward for every guest.

Purpose

Scripture's own reason: 'for thereby some have entertained angels unawares' (Hebrews 13:2).

Function

Strangers are easily forgotten, and this command says not to forget them. An apostolic command, not a Mosaic one — the civil, ceremonial, and moral division does not sort it. Its goal was hospitality as remembrance: be not forgetful to entertain strangers, for thereby some have entertained angels unawares. The evil it addressed was a closed door — the stranger left outside while the household looks inward. It restrained that evil by the precedent itself: Abraham's tent, and angels unawares.
