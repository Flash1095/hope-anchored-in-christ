Do not eat unclean birds

The birds listed in Leviticus 11 — birds of prey and scavengers such as the eagle, vulture, raven, and owl — are unclean and may not be eaten.

Commandment

Do not eat the unclean birds listed in Leviticus 11.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The list names carrion and predatory birds as unclean. The New Testament records these food distinctions set aside (Mark 7:19; Acts 10).

Purpose

To mark Israel's table as distinct (Leviticus 11). The New Testament records the distinctions set aside: Jesus declares all foods clean (Mark 7:19) and Peter's vision confirms it (Acts 10).

Function

The eagle, vulture, raven, and owl — birds of prey and scavengers — were unclean and might not be eaten, a ceremonial law given as a list. Its goal was to mark Israel's table as distinct, the named birds kept off it by name. The evil was eating the carrion birds, creatures that fed on death, as if they were ordinary food. It restrained that by naming them: the list left no guessing, and the birds that ate the dead were not eaten in turn.
