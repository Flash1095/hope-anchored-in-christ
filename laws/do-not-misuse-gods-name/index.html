Do Not Misuse God's Name

The third commandment: God's name must be treated with reverence, never used emptily, falsely, or as a cover for wrongdoing.

Commandment

Do not use God's name in a worthless, false, or irreverent way.

Application

Bearing God's name is about more than speech: his people carry his name before the world, so their words and conduct must match it.

Purpose

Scripture states the sanction: the LORD will not hold him guiltless who takes his name in vain (Exodus 20:7). His name carries his character, so it must not be used emptily.

Function

God's name carries his character, and this third of the Ten Words guards it. It is foundational moral law, traditionally classed moral, binding in every age. Its goal was reverence — a people who bear God's name before the world must match it in word and conduct. The evil it addressed was empty, false, or self-serving use of his name, turning it into a cover for wrongdoing. It restrained that evil with a plain sanction: the LORD will not hold him guiltless who takes his name in vain.
