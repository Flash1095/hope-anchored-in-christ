Do not eat creeping things

Creeping things that creep upon the earth — worms, snails, moles, and the like — are unclean and may not be eaten.

Commandment

Do not eat any creeping thing that creeps upon the earth.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The creeping kinds were wholly excluded from Israel's table. The New Testament records these food distinctions set aside (Mark 7:19; Acts 10).

Purpose

To mark Israel's table as distinct (Leviticus 11). The New Testament records the distinctions set aside: Jesus declares all foods clean (Mark 7:19) and Peter's vision confirms it (Acts 10).

Function

Creeping things that creep upon the earth — worms, snails, moles, and the like — were wholly excluded from Israel's table, a ceremonial law of total exclusion. Its goal was to mark the table as distinct, the lowest creatures kept off it entirely. The evil was the same blurring as the other food laws: a people whose eating showed nothing of their God. It restrained that by a flat ban on the creeping kinds, so even the smallest food was chosen by God's rule, not by appetite.
