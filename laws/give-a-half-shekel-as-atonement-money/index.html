Give a half-shekel as atonement money

The command: Give a half-shekel as atonement money (Exodus 30:13). Every numbered Israelite, rich and poor alike, gave half a shekel as atonement money for the service of the tabernacle.

Commandment

Give a half-shekel as atonement money, as prescribed in Exodus 30:13.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Hebrews teaches the earthly tabernacle was a copy of the heavenly one, and Christ now serves as high priest in the true tabernacle.

Purpose

To give Israel a mediated approach to God through the tabernacle pattern (Exodus 25:40). Hebrews teaches the earthly copy gave way to Christ's priesthood in the true tabernacle (Hebrews 8:1-6).

Function

Every numbered Israelite, rich and poor alike, gave half a shekel as atonement money for the service of the tabernacle — a ceremonial law of equal ransom. Its goal was to tie the census itself to atonement: each man counted gave the same half shekel, the rich no more and the poor no less. It did not address a particular evil; its work was atonement and equality, not restraint. The half shekel said every soul cost the same before God, and the tabernacle was built on equal giving.
