Do not return an escaped servant to his master

Command
Do not return an escaped servant to his master
Category
Civil Law
Summary
A servant who has escaped from his master to you must not be delivered back to his master: Israel is his refuge.
Commandment
Do not return an escaped servant to his master.
Scripture
Deuteronomy 23:15
Application
The runaway was not a thief to be returned but a person seeking refuge, and the law made Israel his refuge. Paul's letter to Philemon moves in the same direction for a later age: Onesimus returns no longer as a slave but as a beloved brother. The institution has ended; the refuge principle — do not hand the fleeing back to their oppressor — remains a moral warning.
Related Topics
Mercy; Justice; Compassion
Related Books
Deuteronomy
Related People
Moses
Purpose
Scripture states the command without a separate reason beyond the servant's refuge itself (Deuteronomy 23:15-16); TRADITIONAL reading: the escaped servant is treated as a person seeking protection, not property to be restored.
