Do Not Shed Human Blood

The Noahic life law: whoever sheds man's blood, by man shall his blood be shed, because man is made in God's image.

Commandment

Do not shed human blood; the shedder's blood is required in return.

Application

Distinct from the live Sinai row (Do Not Murder): this is the earlier, pre-Mosaic command, given to all Noah's descendants, not only to Israel. Its stated ground is the image of God, and that ground is why the prohibition is treated as enduring even though its penalty procedure belonged to the ancient order.

Purpose

To guard human life on the stated ground that man is made in the image of God (Genesis 9:6).

Function

Whoever sheds man's blood, by man shall his blood be shed — because man is made in God's image. This command predates Sinai, given to all Noah's descendants, not only to Israel, so the civil, ceremonial, and moral division does not sort it. Its goal was to guard human life on the stated ground of the image of God. The evil it addressed was the shedding of human blood, murder treated as a light thing. It restrained that evil by the sentence itself, and that ground — the image of God — is why the prohibition is treated as enduring.
