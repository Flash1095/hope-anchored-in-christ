Give the foreleg, cheeks, and maw to the priest

The command: Give the foreleg, cheeks, and maw to the priest (Deuteronomy 18:3). From every animal slaughtered for food, the shoulder, the two cheeks, and the maw belonged to the priest.

Commandment

Give the foreleg, cheeks, and maw to the priest, as prescribed in Deuteronomy 18:3.

Application

It cannot be practised as written without the standing temple and Aaronic priesthood; its enduring lesson is God's holiness, the cost of atonement, and worship offered exactly as God appoints.

Purpose

Scripture's stated ground: these were the priest's due from the people who offered sacrifice (Deuteronomy 18:3).

Function

From every animal slaughtered for food, the shoulder, the two cheeks, and the maw belonged to the priest — a ceremonial law of the priest's due. Its goal was to feed the priesthood from ordinary slaughter, not only from sacrifices, so the men who served God ate from the people's tables too. It did not address a particular evil; its work was provision, not restraint. The foreleg and the cheeks were the priest's share of every meal, and the people who ate remembered who served God for them.
