The Silver Trumpets

Make thee two trumpets of silver; of a whole piece shalt thou make them, that thou mayest use them for the calling of the assembly, and for the journeying of the camps — and if ye go to war, ye shall blow an alarm with the trumpets, and ye shall be remembered before the LORD; also in the day of gladness, and in solemn days, and in the beginnings of months, ye shall blow with the trumpets over burnt offerings and peace offerings.

Commandment

Make two silver trumpets, to call the assembly, to order the camps' journeys, to sound the alarm in war, and to sound over offerings and feasts.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The trumpets gathered, ordered, and warned: one instrument did the work of assembly, march, war-cry, and festival. The New Testament keeps the trumpet as the sound of gathering and of the last day (1 Thessalonians 4:16; 1 Corinthians 15:52), but the silver trumpets of the camp are not a Christian liturgical requirement.

Purpose

Scripture's stated reason: that ye may be remembered before the LORD your God (Numbers 10:9-10) — the trumpet sound in war and in joy is a memorial before God.

Function

Make thee two trumpets of silver, to call the assembly, to order the camps' journeys, to sound the alarm in war, and to sound over offerings and feasts — a ceremonial law of the silver sound. Its goal was remembrance before God: blown in war, the people would be remembered before the LORD, and blown in joy, the feasts were remembered too. It did not address a particular evil; its work was gathering, ordering, and memorial, not restraint. One instrument did the work of assembly, march, war-cry, and festival.
