Do Not Eat from the Tree of Knowledge

The one prohibition in Eden: every tree may be eaten from except the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, on pain of death.

Commandment

Do not eat of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, for in the day you eat of it you shall surely die.

Application

This is the test command of Eden: abundance (every tree) joined to one boundary, with the consequence stated in the command itself. It was broken at the Fall and the tree became inaccessible (Genesis 3:22-24); it does not bind anyone today as an Eden prohibition, though its lesson about boundaries and trust is preached from it.

Purpose

To test trust and obedience within abundance: one stated boundary, with death as the stated consequence (Genesis 2:17).

Function

One boundary inside abundance: every tree may be eaten from except one. This command predates Sinai, so the civil, ceremonial, and moral division does not sort it. Its goal was to test trust and obedience within abundance — one stated boundary, with death as the stated consequence. The evil it addressed was disobedience itself, the reaching for what God had withheld. It restrained that evil by the boundary and its consequence: in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die. It was broken at the Fall, and the tree became inaccessible.
