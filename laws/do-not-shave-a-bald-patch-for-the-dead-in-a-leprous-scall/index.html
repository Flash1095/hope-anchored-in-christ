Do not shave a bald patch for the dead in a leprous scall

In examining a scall, the priest must not have the bald patch shaved away: the scall is left so its spreading or healing can be judged after seven days.

Commandment

Do not shave the bald patch in a leprous scall; let the priest examine it unshaved.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The unshaved patch preserved the evidence the priest needed to judge the disease. Jesus' touch restored those the purity laws isolated (Mark 1:40-45).

Purpose

To order who could approach worship while ceremonially unclean and how they were restored. TRADITIONAL reading: with the temple order ended, these washings no longer bind believers; Jesus' touch restored those the laws isolated (Mark 1:40-45; Luke 8:43-48).

Function

In examining a scall, the priest must not have the bald patch shaved away: the scall was left so its spreading or healing could be judged after seven days — a ceremonial law of preserving the evidence. Its goal was to keep the priest's judgment honest, the patch left unshaved so the disease could be seen truly. The evil was tampering with the signs. It restrained that by forbidding the shave: the patch stayed, and the priest judged what was really there.
