Go Up to Bethel

God's command to Jacob: arise, go up to Bethel, dwell there, and make an altar to the God who appeared when he fled from Esau.

Commandment

Arise, go up to Beth-el, and dwell there: and make there an altar unto God.

Application

A return to the place of the dream (Genesis 28) now commanded as an act of worship: Jacob first put away his household's strange gods (Genesis 35:2 — Jacob's own household order, not a separate divine command, and not rowed separately). God blessed him at Bethel and renamed the ground of the promise (Genesis 35:9-15).

Purpose

To bring Jacob back to Bethel in worship, fulfilling the vow-ground of his first flight and re-establishing the covenant there (Genesis 35:1, 35:9-12).

Function

A return to the place of the dream, now commanded as an act of worship. This was a one-time command, given once to Jacob for a specific moment, so the civil, ceremonial, and moral division does not apply to it. Its goal was to bring Jacob back to Bethel in worship, fulfilling the vow-ground of his first flight and re-establishing the covenant there. The evil or restraint angle does not apply here; this was a pilgrimage of worship, not a law against a recurring evil. Jacob first put away his household's strange gods, and God blessed him at Bethel.
