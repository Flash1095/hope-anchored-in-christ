Keep the second Passover if unclean or on a journey

A man unclean by a dead body, or on a journey far off, at the first Passover, shall keep the Passover in the second month, on the fourteenth day at even.

Commandment

If unclean by a dead body or on a journey at Passover, keep the Passover in the second month on the fourteenth day at even.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The second Passover is mercy built into the calendar: uncleanness or a journey did not mean a year of exclusion. The defiled are not kept from the table for a month but are cleansed and welcomed (1 John 1:7-9).

Purpose

Scripture's stated context: the men defiled by a dead body asked why they should be kept back from offering the LORD's offering — and God answered with a second date, not a refusal (Numbers 9:6-11).

Function

Here is mercy built into the calendar, and that is what makes this a ceremonial law with a different accent: a rite that makes room. Its goal was to keep no one excluded for a year — the man unclean by a dead body, or far off on a journey, kept the Passover a month later. The evil was exclusion itself: being shut out of the memorial for a whole year over uncleanness or distance. It restrained that by giving a second date.
