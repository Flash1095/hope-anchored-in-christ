Aaron shall not enter the holy place at all times

The command: Aaron shall not enter the holy place at all times (Leviticus 16:2). After the deaths of Nadab and Abihu, Aaron might not enter the most holy place whenever he chose, but only as appointed, lest he die.

Commandment

Aaron shall not enter the holy place at all times.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Hebrews reads the Day as a shadow: Christ entered the true Most Holy Place with his own blood, securing an eternal redemption the yearly rite could only picture. (Distinct from the Jewish Cultural Backgrounds entry on the Day of Atonement as a feast; this entry covers the commanded rite as law.)

Purpose

Annual cleansing for the people's sins (Leviticus 16). Hebrews reads it as shadow of Christ's entry with his own blood (Hebrews 9:11-14).

Function

After the deaths of Nadab and Abihu, Aaron might not enter the most holy place whenever he chose, but only as appointed, lest he die — a ceremonial law of the closed veil. Its goal was to keep the holiest room entered only on God's terms. The evil was casual entry into the presence, treating the holy of holies as an open room. It restrained that by the appointment itself: not at all times, but at the time God set, and with the blood God required.
