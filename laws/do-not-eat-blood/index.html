Do not eat blood

No manner of blood may be eaten, whether of fowl or beast, in any dwelling of Israel.

Commandment

Do not eat any manner of blood, of fowl or of beast, in any of your dwellings.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The blood was given on the altar to make atonement, not given as food. Hebrews teaches the sacrificial system pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering (Hebrews 10:1-14).

Purpose

To provide atonement, thanksgiving, and fellowship under the old covenant. Hebrews teaches these pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering (Hebrews 10:1-14), so the system ceased.

Function

No manner of blood might be eaten, of fowl or beast, in any dwelling — a ceremonial law rooted in the life itself. Its goal was to honour the blood as God's: given on the altar to make atonement, not given as food. The evil was treating life as meat, eating the very thing that carried life as if it were common. It restrained that by a total ban, and it was never temporary — the life in the blood was not a temporary idea.
