Do not eat peace-offering flesh while unclean

The command: Do not eat peace-offering flesh while unclean (Leviticus 7:20). One who was unclean and ate of the peace offering's flesh was cut off from his people.

Commandment

Do not eat peace-offering flesh while unclean.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Hebrews teaches these sacrifices could never perfect the worshiper; they pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering, which is why they ceased with the new covenant.

Purpose

To provide atonement, thanksgiving, and fellowship under the old covenant. Hebrews teaches these pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering (Hebrews 10:1-14), so the system ceased.

Function

One who was unclean and ate of the peace offering's flesh was cut off from his people — a ceremonial law with teeth. Its goal was to keep the fellowship meal holy: the table of peace with God was not for those in a state of uncleanness. The evil was approaching God's table while defiled, treating fellowship as if purity did not matter. It restrained that with the sharpest penalty the law knew — being cut off — so the meal stayed clean.
