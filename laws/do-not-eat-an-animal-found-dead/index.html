Do not eat an animal found dead

An animal that dies of itself, not slaughtered, may not be eaten by Israel; it may be given to the stranger, but Israel, as a holy people, shall not eat it.

Commandment

Do not eat any animal found dead of itself.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The rule kept Israel from eating carrion, whose blood was not properly shed and whose death was unwitnessed. The New Testament records these food distinctions set aside (Mark 7:19; Acts 10).

Purpose

To mark Israel's table as distinct (Leviticus 11). The New Testament records the distinctions set aside: Jesus declares all foods clean (Mark 7:19) and Peter's vision confirms it (Acts 10).

Function

An animal that died of itself, not slaughtered, might not be eaten by Israel — a ceremonial law about how meat is taken. Its goal was to keep Israel from eating carrion, whose blood was not properly shed and whose death was unwitnessed. The evil was eating what died on its own, treating a carcass as food as the nations did. It restrained that by the ban, and Scripture notes the stranger might take it — Israel, as a holy people, would not.
