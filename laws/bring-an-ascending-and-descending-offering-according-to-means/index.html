Bring an ascending and descending offering according to means

The command: Bring an ascending and descending offering according to means (Leviticus 5:7). The offering slid with the sinner's means: lamb or goat, two birds, or fine flour for the very poor, so no one was shut out from atonement.

Commandment

Bring an ascending and descending offering according to means, as prescribed in Leviticus 5:7.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Hebrews teaches these sacrifices could never perfect the worshiper; they pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering, which is why they ceased with the new covenant.

Purpose

To provide atonement, thanksgiving, and fellowship under the old covenant. Hebrews teaches these pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering (Hebrews 10:1-14), so the system ceased.

Function

The offering slid with the sinner's means: lamb or goat, two birds, or fine flour for the very poor, so no one was shut out from atonement — a ceremonial law of the open door. Its goal was to make atonement reachable at every level of wealth. The evil was exclusion by poverty, the poor sinner left without a sacrifice he could afford. It restrained that by the sliding scale: the offering descended to meet the giver, and no one was priced out of forgiveness.
