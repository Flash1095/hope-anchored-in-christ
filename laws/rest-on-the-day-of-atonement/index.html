Rest on the Day of Atonement

The Day of Atonement is kept as a sabbath of rest, from evening to evening.

Commandment

Keep the Day of Atonement as a sabbath of rest, from even unto even.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The evening-to-evening rest framed the whole day of atonement in stillness. Debated: some see the commanded observance fulfilled as shadow (Colossians 2:16-17); others keep feasts voluntarily as memorial, not as binding command. The site does not settle whether observance continues.

Purpose

To rehearse God's saving acts at appointed seasons (Leviticus 23); Paul calls Christ our Passover (1 Corinthians 5:7).

Function

The Day of Atonement was kept as a sabbath of rest, from evening to evening — a ceremonial law of stillness. Its goal was to frame the whole day of atonement in rest, the people still while the high priest worked inside the veil. It did not address a particular evil; its work was solemnity and reverence, not restraint. The evening-to-evening rest said this day was unlike any other: the nation waited, and the atonement was made for them.
