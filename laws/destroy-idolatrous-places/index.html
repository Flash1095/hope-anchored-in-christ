Destroy Idolatrous Places of Worship

Command
Destroy Idolatrous Places of Worship
Category
Moral Law
Summary
When Israel enters the land, the places where the nations served their gods — on high mountains, hills, and under green trees — are to be utterly destroyed, with their altars, images, and groves broken down. This is the conquest-era removal command, distinct from the live No Idols row (personal idolatry) and from the Warfare row (Deuteronomy 20).
Commandment
Destroy the places of idolatrous worship in the land, breaking down their altars, images, and groves.
Scripture
Deuteronomy 12:2-3; Deuteronomy 7:5; Exodus 34:13
Application
The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as moral. This was a conquest-era command to Israel entering the land, not a standing mandate for Christians: the New Testament never commands believers to destroy other religions' places of worship, but it does command believers themselves to flee idolatry (1 Corinthians 10:14) and to pull down arguments raised against God (2 Corinthians 10:5). The procedure is temporary; the refusal of idolatry endures.
Related Topics
Idolatry; Worship; Conquest
Related Books
Deuteronomy; Exodus
Related People
Moses
Purpose
Scripture's stated context: the land is given to Israel, and its idolatrous worship must not remain alongside the LORD's worship there (Deuteronomy 12:2-4).
