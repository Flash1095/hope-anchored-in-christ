Do not eat a limb torn from a living animal

A limb torn from a living animal may not be eaten; the animal's life must be duly taken and its blood shed before its flesh is eaten.

Commandment

Do not eat a limb torn from a living animal, for the blood is the life.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The rule is bound to the blood itself: flesh eaten while the creature still lives treats its life as food. The Jerusalem council likewise told Gentile believers to abstain from things strangled and from blood (Acts 15:29).

Purpose

Scripture's stated reason: only be sure that thou eat not the blood, for the blood is the life (Deuteronomy 12:23).

Function

A limb torn from a living animal might not be eaten; the animal's life had to be duly taken and its blood shed before its flesh was eaten — a ceremonial law of the blood. Its goal was to honour the life in the blood: flesh eaten while the creature still lived treated its life as food. The evil was cruelty dressed as eating, tearing from the living what belonged to a life properly taken. It restrained that by the ban, and it was never temporary.
