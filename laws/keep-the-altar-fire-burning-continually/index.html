Keep the altar fire burning continually

The command: Keep the altar fire burning continually (Leviticus 6:13). The burnt offering burned on the altar all night until morning, and the fire burned continually upon it.

Commandment

Keep the altar fire burning continually, as prescribed in Leviticus 6:13.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Hebrews teaches these sacrifices could never perfect the worshiper; they pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering, which is why they ceased with the new covenant.

Purpose

To provide atonement, thanksgiving, and fellowship under the old covenant. Hebrews teaches these pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering (Hebrews 10:1-14), so the system ceased.

Function

The burnt offering burned all night until morning, and the fire never went out — a ceremonial law of unbroken worship. Its goal was to keep the altar alive around the clock, so atonement and offering never stopped in Israel. It did not address a particular evil; its work was constancy, not restraint. The fire said God's readiness to receive was not a daytime matter; it burned through the night while the camp slept.
