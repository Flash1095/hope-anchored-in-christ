Estimate the value of consecrated animals

The command: Estimate the value of consecrated animals (Leviticus 27:12). A beast vowed to the LORD was set before the priest, who estimated its value, and that estimation stood.

Commandment

Estimate the value of consecrated animals, as prescribed in Leviticus 27:12.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Valuations and redemptions priced devotion in a fixed, public way, so that a vow could not be quietly abandoned or quietly inflated. The whole system of dedicated things ended with the temple system; Christians give freely and cheerfully, not by valuation tables (2 Corinthians 9:7).

Purpose

TRADITIONAL reading: fixed valuations kept vows honest and public, preventing both evasion and extravagant self-pricing. Scripture states the scale without a separate reason (Leviticus 27:1-29).

Function

A beast vowed to the LORD was set before the priest, who estimated its value, and that estimation stood — a ceremonial law of the priest's price. Its goal, traditionally, was to price devotion in a fixed, public way, so a vow could not be quietly abandoned or quietly inflated. The evil was the self-priced vow, the giver setting his own value on what he gave God. It restrained that by the priest's estimation: his word stood, and the giver paid what the priest said.
