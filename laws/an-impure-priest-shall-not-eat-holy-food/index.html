An impure priest shall not eat holy food

The holy gifts (terumah) belonged to the priests, but a priest in impurity — from leprosy, a discharge, or touching uncleanness — was barred from eating them until cleansed (Leviticus 22:4-7).

Commandment

A priest unclean by leprosy, a discharge, or contact with uncleanness shall not eat of the holy things until he is clean.

Application

Holiness and impurity did not mix at the LORD's table: the priest's own cleansing came before his portion, and eating holy food in impurity profaned the holy name.

Purpose

Holy food was not to be eaten in impurity, keeping what is holy to the LORD distinct from uncleanness (Leviticus 22:2-4).

Function

A priest unclean by leprosy, a discharge, or contact with uncleanness was barred from eating the holy things until he was clean — a ceremonial law of the clean eater. Its goal was to keep what is holy to the LORD distinct from uncleanness: the priest's own cleansing came before his portion. The evil was holy food eaten in impurity, the priest profaning the holy name at his own table. It restrained that by the bar: the impure priest did not eat until cleansed, and the holy food stayed holy.
