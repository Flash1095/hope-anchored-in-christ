Admonish the Idle, Encourage the Fainthearted, Help the Weak

Paul's pastoral-care command: warn the unruly, comfort the feebleminded, support the weak, and be patient toward all.

Commandment

Now we exhort you, brethren, warn them that are unruly, comfort the feebleminded, support the weak, be patient toward all men.

Application

One community, three different treatments: the idle need warning, the fainthearted comfort, the weak support — and all of it needs patience. Undifferentiated care misfires; this command differentiates.

Purpose

TRADITIONAL reading: to fit the care to the condition, as a physician fits the remedy.

Function

One community, three different treatments — and the wrong remedy misfires. This one is apostolic, not Mosaic, so the civil, ceremonial, and moral division does not apply. Its goal, on the TRADITIONAL reading, was to fit the care to the condition, as a physician fits the remedy. The evil it addressed was undifferentiated care: warning the fainthearted, comforting the idle, and helping no one rightly. It restrained that evil by the differentiation itself: warn the unruly, comfort the feebleminded, support the weak, and be patient toward all.
