A Nazirite shall not drink wine or strong drink

Throughout the vow, the Nazirite drinks no wine or strong drink, and takes no vinegar of wine or of strong drink.

Commandment

A Nazirite shall drink no wine or strong drink for all the days of his separation.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Acts 21:23-26 shows Paul joining men under a vow in Jerusalem, which shows the practice continued among Jewish believers, not that it is imposed on Christians.

Purpose

Scripture's stated context: the Nazirite separates himself unto the LORD (Numbers 6:2,5-6); no further reason is given, so the components' purposes are traditional — wine abstained from as a mark of consecration.

Function

Throughout the vow, the Nazirite drank no wine or strong drink — a ceremonial law of voluntary separation. Its goal was consecration: the man who separated himself unto the LORD marked it by refusing what loosens and gladdens, staying clear-headed for God. It did not address a particular evil; its work was devotion, not restraint. The abstinence was the visible sign of a vow freely taken, and the cup left untouched said the Nazirite belonged to the LORD for the days of his separation.
