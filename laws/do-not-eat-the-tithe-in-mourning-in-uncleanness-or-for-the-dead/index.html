Do not eat the tithe in mourning, in uncleanness, or for the dead

The command: Do not eat the tithe in mourning, in uncleanness, or for the dead (Deuteronomy 26:14). The tithe confession declared that none of it had been eaten in mourning, in uncleanness, or given for the dead.

Commandment

Do not eat the tithe in mourning, in uncleanness, or for the dead.

Application

It cannot be practised as written without the standing temple and Aaronic priesthood; its enduring lesson is God's holiness, the cost of atonement, and worship offered exactly as God appoints.

Purpose

TRADITIONAL reading: holy gifts were not to be spent in mourning rites or for the dead, keeping worship of the LORD distinct from death customs.

Function

The tithe confession declared that none of it had been eaten in mourning, in uncleanness, or given for the dead — a ceremonial law keeping holy gifts from death customs. Its goal, traditionally, was to keep worship of the LORD distinct from death customs, the holy tithe never spent in mourning rites. The evil was mixing the tithe with the dead, the holy gift turned to funeral use. It restrained that by the confession: the giver declared what he had not done, and the declaration held him to it.
