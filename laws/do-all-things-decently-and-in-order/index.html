Do All Things Decently and in Order

Paul's order command for worship: let all things be done decently and in order.

Commandment

Let all things be done decently and in order.

Application

The closing rule of the long worship-order chapter: God is not the author of confusion but of peace. Decency and order are the frame within which gifts — tongues, prophecy, singing — are exercised, not a ban on them.

Purpose

Scripture's own reason: 'For God is not the author of confusion, but of peace' (1 Corinthians 14:33).

Function

God is not the author of confusion, but of peace — and worship must show it. Apostolic, not Mosaic — the civil, ceremonial, and moral division does not sort this one. Its goal was the closing rule of the long worship-order chapter: let all things be done decently and in order. The evil it addressed was confusion in the assembly — gifts exercised without order until no one could follow. It restrained that evil by the frame itself: decency and order within which tongues, prophecy, and singing are exercised, not banned.
