Do not shear the firstborn sheep

The command: Do not shear the firstborn sheep (Deuteronomy 15:19). The firstling sheep might not be shorn, nor might the firstling ox be worked; both were holy to the LORD.

Commandment

Do not shear the firstborn sheep.

Application

It cannot be practised as written without the standing temple and Aaronic priesthood; its enduring lesson is God's holiness, the cost of atonement, and worship offered exactly as God appoints.

Purpose

Scripture's stated ground: the firstling was holy unto the LORD and might not be used for common profit (Deuteronomy 15:19).

Function

The firstling sheep might not be shorn, nor might the firstling ox be worked; both were holy to the LORD — a ceremonial law of the untouched firstborn. Its goal was to keep the firstling from common profit, the wool and the work withheld because the animal was God's. It did not address a particular evil; its work was dedication, not restraint. The unsheared sheep said the firstborn was not a resource to be used, but a belonging to be honoured.
