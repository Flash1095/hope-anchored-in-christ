Burn sacrifices left over past their eating time

The command: Burn sacrifices left over past their eating time (Leviticus 7:17). Flesh of a peace offering remaining on the third day was burned with fire.

Commandment

Burn sacrifices left over past their eating time, as prescribed in Leviticus 7:17.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Hebrews teaches these sacrifices could never perfect the worshiper; they pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering, which is why they ceased with the new covenant.

Purpose

To provide atonement, thanksgiving, and fellowship under the old covenant. Hebrews teaches these pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering (Hebrews 10:1-14), so the system ceased.

Function

Flesh of a peace offering remaining on the third day was burned with fire — a ceremonial law of the deadline. Its goal was to keep holy food inside its appointed time, the leftovers burned rather than eaten late or treated as common. The evil was the stale sacrifice, holy flesh kept past its day and eaten as if the time did not matter. It restrained that by the fire: what remained past the eating time was burned, and the holy meal was never stretched into an ordinary one.
