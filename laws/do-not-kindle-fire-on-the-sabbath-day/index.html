Do not kindle fire on the Sabbath day

No fire may be kindled in Israel's dwellings on the Sabbath day, setting the day apart from ordinary work.

Commandment

Kindle no fire in any of your dwellings on the Sabbath day.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The fire ban made the Sabbath rest visible in every home: even the day's ordinary preparations stopped. Debated: some Christians keep the Sabbath day as a binding command; others see its rest fulfilled in Christ (Hebrews 4; Colossians 2:16-17). The site does not settle the question.

Purpose

Scripture's stated context: the Sabbath is the LORD's day of rest, kept holy in remembrance of creation and of deliverance from Egypt (Exodus 20:8-11; Deuteronomy 5:12-15).

Function

No fire might be kindled in Israel's dwellings on the Sabbath day — a ceremonial law that made the rest visible in every home. Its goal was to set the day apart from ordinary work, even the day's ordinary preparations stopping. The evil was the slow erosion of the Sabbath, work creeping back in through the kitchen fire. It restrained that by the ban: no fire in any dwelling, and the rest was visible in every house, not only in the sanctuary.
