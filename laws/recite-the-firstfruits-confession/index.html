Recite the firstfruits confession

The command: Recite the firstfruits confession (Deuteronomy 26:5). Bringing the basket of firstfruits, Israel confessed: a Syrian ready to perish was my father, and the LORD brought us out of Egypt into this land.

Commandment

Recite the firstfruits confession, as prescribed in Deuteronomy 26:5.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The New Testament keeps the principle in a new key: Christ is the firstfruits of them that slept (1 Corinthians 15:20), and Christians are urged to give first and cheerfully, not by the old basket rite (2 Corinthians 9:7). The rite belongs to the land and the sanctuary; the first-giving heart it taught does not expire with it.

Purpose

Scripture's stated context: the confession itself gives the reason — 'a Syrian ready to perish was my father,' and God brought Israel out of Egypt and gave them this land (Deuteronomy 26:5-10).

Function

Bringing the basket of firstfruits, Israel confessed: a Syrian ready to perish was my father, and the LORD brought us out of Egypt into this land — a ceremonial law of spoken memory. Its goal was to make the giver say the story aloud, so the offering came with the history that explained it. No particular evil is in view here; its work was remembrance, not restraint. The confession kept the gift from becoming a mere tax: every basket carried the Exodus with it, told in the giver's own voice.
