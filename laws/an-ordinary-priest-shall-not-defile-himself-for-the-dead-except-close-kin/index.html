An ordinary priest shall not defile himself for the dead except close kin

The command: An ordinary priest shall not defile himself for the dead except close kin (Leviticus 21:1). An ordinary priest might defile himself only for his nearest kin: mother, father, son, daughter, brother, and unmarried sister.

Commandment

An ordinary priest shall not defile himself for the dead except close kin.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The priest's holiness had a cost: because he handled holy things, his contact with death was restricted beyond the people's. The high priest's restriction was stricter still (Leviticus 21:10-12). The New Testament's great High Priest, by contrast, touches the dead and raises them — the restriction pointed to a holiness he embodies differently (Hebrews 4:14-16).

Purpose

Scripture's stated reason: they shall be holy unto their God, and not profane the name of their God (Leviticus 21:6).

Function

An ordinary priest might defile himself only for his nearest kin — mother, father, son, daughter, brother, and unmarried sister — a ceremonial law of measured separation. Its goal was to hold the priest apart from death beyond the closest family, because he handled holy things and death defiled. The evil was the casual mixing of death and holiness, a priest carrying defilement into the sanctuary. It restrained that by drawing the line at near kin: grief had its place, but the holy things came first.
