Do not gather the gleanings of your harvest

When reaping the harvest, the gleanings — what the reapers drop or leave — are not to be gathered up by the owner.

Commandment

Do not gather the gleanings of your harvest.

Application

The harvester's dropped sheaf is not waste to be recovered but provision left in the field. Gleaning law builds charity into the harvest itself, so the poor gather with dignity rather than begging for a dole.

Purpose

God's stated care for those without a defender — widow, orphan, stranger — carried by James into the church as pure religion (James 1:27). In the harvest, that care takes the form of leaving the gleanings ungathered.

Function

Here the Law works as Israel's civil code, ordering community life rather than temple worship. When reaping the harvest, the gleanings — what the reapers drop or leave behind — stay in the field for the poor. The care God states for those without a defender — widow, orphan, stranger — James carried into the church as pure religion. The evil it addressed was a harvest kept entirely for its owner while the poor went hungry. It restrained that evil by making the dropped sheaf provision, not waste: the harvester's loss was the poor man's right.
