Do no servile work on the first day of Tabernacles

On the first day of Tabernacles, no servile work may be done; the day is a holy convocation.

Commandment

On the first day of Tabernacles, do no servile work.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The work ban set the feast's opening day apart from the harvest labor it celebrated. Debated: some see the commanded observance fulfilled as shadow (Colossians 2:16-17); others keep feasts voluntarily as memorial, not as binding command. The site does not settle whether observance continues.

Purpose

To rehearse God's saving acts at appointed seasons (Leviticus 23); Paul calls Christ our Passover (1 Corinthians 5:7).

Function

On the first day of Tabernacles, no servile work might be done — a ceremonial law setting the feast's opening apart. Its goal was to separate the first day of booths from the harvest labour it celebrated, so the feast began in worship, not in work. It did not address a particular evil; its work was honouring holy time, not restraint. The work ban cleared the day for the booths and the rejoicing, and the harvest waited while Israel remembered the wilderness.
