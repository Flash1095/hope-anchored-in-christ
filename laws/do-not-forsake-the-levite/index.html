Do not forsake the Levite

The command: Do not forsake the Levite (Deuteronomy 12:19). The Levite, having no portion or inheritance with Israel, was not to be forsaken as long as Israel lived in the land.

Commandment

Do not forsake the Levite.

Application

Debated: some hold the Levite's support continues as support for those who minister, while others hold the Levitical order itself has ceased and giving is now governed by New Testament generosity. The site does not settle the form; the duty not to abandon those who serve is the enduring point.

Purpose

Scripture's stated ground: the Levite had no part nor inheritance with Israel, and depended on the people's gifts (Deuteronomy 12:19; Deuteronomy 14:27).

Function

The Levite, having no portion or inheritance with Israel, was not to be forsaken as long as Israel lived in the land — a ceremonial law of remembering the servant. Its goal was to keep the Levites provided for by the people they served, since they had no land of their own to live on. The evil was neglect of the men who served God. It restrained that by the command itself: do not forsake the Levite — the duty was named, and the forgetting was forbidden.
