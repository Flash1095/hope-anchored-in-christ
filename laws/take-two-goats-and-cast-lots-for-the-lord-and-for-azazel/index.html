Take two goats and cast lots for the LORD and for Azazel

The command: Take two goats and cast lots for the LORD and for Azazel (Leviticus 16:7). Two goats were set before the LORD at the door of the tabernacle, and lots cast: one for the LORD as a sin offering, one for Azazel.

Commandment

Take two goats and cast lots for the LORD and for Azazel, as prescribed in Leviticus 16:7.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Hebrews reads the Day as a shadow: Christ entered the true Most Holy Place with his own blood, securing an eternal redemption the yearly rite could only picture. (Distinct from the Jewish Cultural Backgrounds entry on the Day of Atonement as a feast; this entry covers the commanded rite as law.)

Purpose

Annual cleansing for the people's sins (Leviticus 16). Hebrews reads it as shadow of Christ's entry with his own blood (Hebrews 9:11-14).

Function

Two goats were set before the LORD at the door of the tabernacle, and lots were cast: one for the LORD as a sin offering, one for Azazel — a ceremonial law of the two goats. Its goal was to carry the Day of Atonement's work in two animals, one slain and one sent away. It did not address a particular evil beyond the sins the day covered; its work was atonement, not restraint. The lots decided which goat did which work, and God, not the priest, made the choice.
