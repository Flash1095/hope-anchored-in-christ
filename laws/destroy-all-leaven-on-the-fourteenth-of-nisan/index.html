Destroy all leaven on the fourteenth of Nisan

On the fourteenth day of Nisan, all leaven must be put away from Israel's houses before the Passover meal.

Commandment

On the fourteenth day of Nisan, put away all leaven from your houses.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The removal of leaven pictured leaving Egypt in haste, with no time for bread to rise, and a clean break from the old life there. Debated: some see the commanded observance fulfilled as shadow (Colossians 2:16-17); others keep feasts voluntarily as memorial, not as binding command. The site does not settle whether observance continues.

Purpose

Scripture's stated context: the meal is eaten in haste on the night of deliverance from Egypt, ready to depart (Exodus 12:11).

Function

On the fourteenth day of Nisan, all leaven had to be put away from Israel's houses before the Passover meal — a ceremonial law of the clean house. Its goal was to make the break from Egypt visible before the meal began, the leaven gone before the lamb was eaten. Its work was remembrance, not restraint; no particular evil is addressed. The empty house said the departure was real: nothing of Egypt's leaven stayed for the night of deliverance.
