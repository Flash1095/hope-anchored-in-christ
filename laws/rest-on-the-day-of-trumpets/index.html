Rest on the Day of Trumpets

The Day of Trumpets is kept as a sabbath of rest, a holy convocation on the first day of the seventh month.

Commandment

Keep the first day of the seventh month as a sabbath of rest and a holy convocation.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The new-year rest opened the seventh month's season of repentance and rejoicing. Debated: some see the commanded observance fulfilled as shadow (Colossians 2:16-17); others keep feasts voluntarily as memorial, not as binding command. The site does not settle whether observance continues.

Purpose

To rehearse God's saving acts at appointed seasons (Leviticus 23); Paul calls Christ our Passover (1 Corinthians 5:7).

Function

The first day of the seventh month was kept as a sabbath of rest, a holy convocation — a ceremonial law opening the solemn season. Its goal was to open the seventh month in rest and trumpet sound, the new-year rest waking the people to the season of Atonement and Tabernacles ahead. It did not address a particular evil; its work was remembrance and preparation, not restraint. The rest said the season mattered: the year turned, and Israel stopped to hear it.
