Do Not Covet

The tenth commandment: desire itself is addressed — not only acts, but the grasping heart that wants what belongs to another.

Commandment

Do not set your heart on possessing what belongs to your neighbor.

Application

This is the commandment that reaches inward, which is why Paul calls covetousness idolatry. It exposes that sin begins in desire before it becomes action.

Purpose

To reach desire itself, not only acts; Paul calls covetousness idolatry (Colossians 3:5).

Function

The tenth of the Ten Words reaches further inward than any of the rest — not the act, but the grasping heart behind it. It is foundational moral law, traditionally classed moral, binding in every age. Its goal was to address desire itself, since sin begins in wanting what belongs to another before it becomes action. The evil it addressed was covetousness, which Paul calls idolatry. It restrained that evil by naming the desire as sin, not merely its outcome.
