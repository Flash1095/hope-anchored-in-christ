Court shall not inflict punishment on the Sabbath

No judicial punishment is to be inflicted on the Sabbath day; the day of rest is not a day of penalties.

Commandment

Let no punishment be inflicted by the court on the Sabbath day.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Even the work of justice paused, so that the Sabbath was wholly a day of rest. Debated: some Christians keep the Sabbath day as a binding command; others see its rest fulfilled in Christ (Hebrews 4; Colossians 2:16-17). The site does not settle the question.

Purpose

Scripture's stated context: the Sabbath is the LORD's day of rest, kept holy in remembrance of creation and of deliverance from Egypt (Exodus 20:8-11; Deuteronomy 5:12-15).

Function

No judicial punishment was to be inflicted on the Sabbath day; the day of rest was not a day of penalties — a ceremonial law that paused even justice. Its goal was to keep the Sabbath wholly a day of rest, the work of the court stopping with all other work. The evil was the Sabbath broken by the court's own hand, penalties inflicted on the day God set apart for rest. It restrained that by the pause: even justice waited.
