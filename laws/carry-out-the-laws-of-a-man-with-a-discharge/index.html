Carry out the laws of a man with a discharge

Command
Carry out the laws of a man with a discharge
Category
Ceremonial Law
Summary
A man with a running issue counts seven days for his cleansing after the discharge stops, washes his clothes, bathes in running water, and on the eighth day brings two turtledoves or pigeons to the priest.
Commandment
A man with a discharge shall count seven days for cleansing, wash, bathe, and bring his offering on the eighth day.
Scripture
Leviticus 15:13
Application
The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The counting and washing gave the unclean man a definite road back to worship. Jesus' touch restored those the purity laws isolated (Luke 8:43-48).
Related Topics
Purity; Holiness
Related Books
Leviticus; Mark; Luke
Related People
Moses
Purpose
To order who could approach worship while ceremonially unclean and how they were restored. TRADITIONAL reading: with the temple order ended, these washings no longer bind believers; Jesus' touch restored those the laws isolated (Mark 1:40-45; Luke 8:43-48).
