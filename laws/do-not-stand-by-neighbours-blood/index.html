Do Not Stand Idly By Your Neighbour's Blood

Neither shalt thou stand against the blood of thy neighbour. This is Leviticus 19:16b — the duty to intervene, warn, or rescue when a neighbour's life is at stake — distinct from the tale-bearing clause in the same verse, drafted separately.

Commandment

Do not stand idly by while your neighbour's life is in danger.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as moral. The New Testament gives the same principle in story form: the priest and Levite who passed by on the other side failed exactly this command (Luke 10:30-37), and John makes it concrete — whoever has this world's goods and shuts his heart against a brother in need, how does God's love abide in him (1 John 3:17-18)?

Purpose

TRADITIONAL reading: the verse pairs tale-bearing with standing against a neighbour's blood because both are ways of letting a neighbour be destroyed — one by speech, one by silence. Scripture states the prohibition without a separate reason (Leviticus 19:16).

Function

Neither shalt thou stand against the blood of thy neighbour. This is the Law shaping character: a moral duty that outlives Israel's civil arrangements.
