Do not eat animals that do not chew the cud or lack split hooves

A land animal is clean only if it both chews the cud and divides the hoof; an animal lacking either sign is unclean and may not be eaten.

Commandment

Do not eat any land animal that does not both chew the cud and divide the hoof.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The two-sign test gave Israel a plain rule for the herds and wild animals. The New Testament records these food distinctions set aside (Mark 7:19; Acts 10).

Purpose

To mark Israel's table as distinct (Leviticus 11). The New Testament records the distinctions set aside: Jesus declares all foods clean (Mark 7:19) and Peter's vision confirms it (Acts 10).

Function

A land animal was clean only if it both chewed the cud and divided the hoof — a ceremonial law of the table. Its goal was to mark Israel's table as distinct, the two-sign test giving a plain rule for herds and wild animals. The evil was the blurring of Israel into the nations, eating as the nations ate with no difference at the table. It restrained that by the signs themselves: every meal asked the question, and the answer kept Israel separate.
