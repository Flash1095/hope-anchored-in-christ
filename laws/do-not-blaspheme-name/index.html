Do Not Blaspheme the Name of the LORD

Whoever blasphemes the name of the LORD shall surely be put to death — the whole congregation stones him. The command itself is distinct from the live Do Not Misuse God's Name row (Exodus 20:7, vain use); this is direct cursing and reviling of God's name, given after the blasphemer's case in Leviticus 24.

Commandment

Do not blaspheme — curse or revile — the name of the LORD.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as moral. The penalty belonged to Israel's theocratic court and is not carried into Christian church discipline, which answers blasphemy with witness and, where needed, exclusion from fellowship — but the prohibition itself is repeated: blasphemy against the Holy Spirit is the one sin Jesus says will not be forgiven (Matthew 12:31-32), and reverence for God's name remains the first petition of the Lord's Prayer.

Purpose

TRADITIONAL reading: the name is God's own self-disclosure, so cursing it strikes at God himself, not merely at a word. Scripture states the prohibition and penalty without a separate reason (Leviticus 24:16).

Function

This law names a duty that does not expire: it is about character, not procedure. Whoever blasphemes the name of the LORD shall surely be put to death — the whole congregation stones him.
