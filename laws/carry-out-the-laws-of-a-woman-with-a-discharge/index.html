Carry out the laws of a woman with a discharge

A woman with a discharge beyond her monthly course counts seven days from when it stops, and on the eighth day brings two turtles or young pigeons to the priest for atonement.

Commandment

A woman with a discharge shall count seven days for cleansing and bring her offering on the eighth day.

Application

The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. The counting and offering gave the afflicted woman a definite road back to worship. The bleeding woman who touched Jesus was healed and told her faith had made her whole (Luke 8:43-48).

Purpose

To order who could approach worship while ceremonially unclean and how they were restored. TRADITIONAL reading: with the temple order ended, these washings no longer bind believers; Jesus' touch restored those the laws isolated (Mark 1:40-45; Luke 8:43-48).

Function

A woman with a discharge beyond her monthly course counted seven days from when it stopped, and on the eighth day brought two turtles or young pigeons to the priest for atonement — a ceremonial law of return. Its goal was to give the afflicted woman a definite road back to worship. The evil was uncleanness with no way back, a woman left outside worship with no path in. It restrained that by the eighth day: the offering was brought, and she was restored.
