An impure priest shall not serve in the sanctuary

The command: An impure priest shall not serve in the sanctuary (Leviticus 22:2). A priest in a state of uncleanness was to be separated from the holy things and not approach them in his uncleanness.

Commandment

An impure priest shall not serve in the sanctuary.

Application

It cannot be practised as written without the standing temple and Aaronic priesthood; its enduring lesson is God's holiness, the cost of atonement, and worship offered exactly as God appoints.

Purpose

Scripture's stated ground: the holy things are the LORD's, and uncleanness profanes them (Leviticus 22:2-3).

Function

A priest in a state of uncleanness was to be separated from the holy things and not approach them in his uncleanness — a ceremonial law of the clean priest. Its goal was to keep the sanctuary served only by clean hands, the impure priest stepping back until he was cleansed. The evil was unclean service, a priest handling holy things while defiled. It restrained that by the separation itself: the impure priest did not serve, and the holy things were not touched by unclean hands.
