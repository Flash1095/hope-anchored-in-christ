Offer a guilt offering for doubtful sin

The command: Offer a guilt offering for doubtful sin (Leviticus 5:17). One who was unsure whether he had sinned brought a ram without blemish as a trespass offering for his doubt.

Commandment

Offer a guilt offering for doubtful sin, as prescribed in Leviticus 5:17.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Hebrews teaches these sacrifices could never perfect the worshiper; they pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering, which is why they ceased with the new covenant.

Purpose

To provide atonement, thanksgiving, and fellowship under the old covenant. Hebrews teaches these pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering (Hebrews 10:1-14), so the system ceased.

Function

A man unsure whether he had sinned still brought a ram — a ceremonial law for the doubtful conscience. Its goal was to give the uncertain sinner a way back to peace: doubt did not have to harden into either carelessness or despair, because atonement was provided. The evil was guilt left unresolved, festering in uncertainty. It restrained that evil by putting a sacrifice in reach of the doubter — the offering settled what the conscience could not settle alone.
