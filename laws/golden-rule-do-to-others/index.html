Do to Others as You Would Have Them Do to You

The Golden Rule: whatever you would want others to do to you, do the same to them — Jesus' summary of the Law and the Prophets in action.

Commandment

Therefore all things whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them: for this is the law and the prophets.

Application

Distinct from the love commands as a practical rule: put yourself in the other's place before you act, and treat them as you would wish to be treated.

Purpose

Scripture's own reason: 'for this is the law and the prophets' (Matthew 7:12).

Function

Put yourself in the other's place before you act — that is the whole of it. A New Testament command, not a Mosaic one — the civil, ceremonial, and moral division does not sort it. Its goal was to turn the Law and the Prophets into a practical rule: whatever you would want others to do to you, do the same to them. The evil it addressed was self-centred dealing, treating others as we would never accept being treated. It restrained that evil by making our own wishes the measure of our treatment of others.
