Do not eat sacrifices offered with improper intention

The command: Do not eat sacrifices offered with improper intention (Leviticus 7:18). An offering eaten with the intention of eating it beyond its appointed day was an abomination and brought iniquity on the eater.

Commandment

Do not eat sacrifices offered with improper intention.

Application

This row narrows the parent group command to this one mitzvah. The moral/ceremonial/civil division is a traditional classification, not Scripture's own division; this law is traditionally classed as ceremonial. Hebrews teaches these sacrifices could never perfect the worshiper; they pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering, which is why they ceased with the new covenant.

Purpose

To provide atonement, thanksgiving, and fellowship under the old covenant. Hebrews teaches these pointed forward to Christ's once-for-all offering (Hebrews 10:1-14), so the system ceased.

Function

An offering eaten with the intention of eating it beyond its appointed day was an abomination and brought iniquity on the eater — a ceremonial law of the heart's intent. Its goal was to keep the offering honest from the inside: even the intention to eat it late made it unacceptable. The evil was the scheming offerer, bringing the sacrifice while already planning to stretch its time. It restrained that by judging the intention itself: the abomination was in the plan.
