Destroy an idolatrous city

One of the Deuteronomy 13 safeguards against idolatry: destroy an idolatrous city. The chapter treats enticement to idolatry as a public danger to the whole community, not a private opinion.

Commandment

Destroy an idolatrous city

Application

Scripture's stated reason: the city that has gone after other gods must be destroyed utterly, its spoil burned wholly for the LORD, so that nothing of the cursed thing cleaves to Israel's hand and the LORD turns from the fierceness of his anger (Deuteronomy 13:15-17). These civil penalties belonged to ancient Israel's theocracy; the refusal to follow idolatrous leading endures.

Purpose

Scripture's stated reason: the city that has gone after other gods must be destroyed utterly, its spoil burned wholly for the LORD, so that nothing of the cursed thing cleaves to Israel's hand and the LORD turns from the fierceness of his anger (Deuteronomy 13:15-17).

Function

A moral law in its traditional classing: it addressed character and conduct within Israel. One of the Deuteronomy 13 safeguards against idolatry: destroy an idolatrous city. Scripture gives the reason: the city that has gone after other gods must be destroyed utterly, its spoil burned wholly for the LORD, so that nothing of the cursed thing cleaves to Israel's hand and the LORD turns from the fierceness of his anger. The evil it addressed was idolatry turned public — a whole city gone after other gods, a danger to the whole community, not a private opinion. It restrained that evil by purging it completely: the city, its spoil, and everything in it. This penalty belonged to ancient Israel's theocracy; the refusal to follow idolatrous leading endures.
