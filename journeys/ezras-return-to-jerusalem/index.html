Summary
In the seventh year of Artaxerxes, Ezra the scribe led a second group of exiles from Babylon to Jerusalem, gathering at the river Ahava for fasting and prayer before the journey (Ahava has no place entry, so it is named here and not listed as a stage). Ezra carried silver, gold, and vessels for the temple and delivered the king's commissions. The text gives the journey's length as about four months but names no intermediate stops.
Significance
Ezra's return is a journey of the Book: a scribe who has set his heart to study, practice, and teach the law leads God's people home.
