Grammatical Context

Principle
Grammatical Context
Category
Context
Summary
Grammatical context is the level of words in sentences: what a word means depends on its grammar, its syntax, and the sentence it stands in. The same word can carry different senses in different sentences, and verb tense, voice, and word order all shape meaning.
How To Apply
Read words inside their sentences, not as isolated dictionary entries. Check how the author uses the word elsewhere, watch for key connectors (for, therefore, but, so that), and be cautious with English-only word studies — the original languages often draw distinctions English hides.
Scripture Example
John 1:14 — 'the Word became flesh' uses flesh (sarx) for genuine humanity, while Galatians 5 uses flesh for the fallen human nature in conflict with the Spirit. Only the sentence, not the word alone, tells you which sense is in play.
Common Mistake
Word-study fallacies: assuming a word's root determines its meaning, that an English word equals its Greek or Hebrew counterpart, or that a word always carries its full theological freight wherever it appears.
Scripture
John 1:14; Galatians 5:16-25; 2 Timothy 2:15
