Prayer

Summary

Recorded prayers — individual, communal, intercessory, and even imprecatory — are a distinct literary form in Scripture. They reveal the theology of the one praying and model how God's people speak to him: in praise, confession, petition, thanksgiving, and complaint.

How To Apply

Read a prayer for what it assumes about God and about the prayer's situation, and let its form shape your own praying. Distinguish prayers that model an attitude (dependence, honesty) from prayers tied to a specific covenant moment (such as imprecations against Israel's enemies) that cannot simply be copied onto personal enemies.

Common Mistake

Copying imprecatory prayers as personal curses, or treating recorded prayers as formulas that guarantee a result when the right words are repeated.

Details

Category: Genre
Scripture Example: Nehemiah 9 — the Levites' great prayer retells Israel's whole history as confession, showing that biblical prayer is often rehearsed history turned into worship. The Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9-13) does the same in miniature: God's name, kingdom, provision, forgiveness, and deliverance.
Scripture: Nehemiah 9:5-38; Matthew 6:9-13; 1 Samuel 2:1-10
