Typology

Principle
Typology
Category
Device
Summary
Typology recognizes that Old Testament persons, events, and institutions can prefigure Christ and the gospel — real history that also points forward. Unlike free allegory, a type is grounded in a genuine historical correspondence the New Testament itself often confirms.
How To Apply
Look for patterns Scripture itself draws: Passover, exodus, temple, priesthood, sacrifice. Let the New Testament authorize the connections, and keep the correspondence to the pattern's real point rather than every detail.
Scripture Example
1 Corinthians 5:7 — 'Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed.' Paul himself draws the line from the Passover lamb to Jesus, showing typology at work: the old deliverance pictures the greater one.
Common Mistake
Finding types everywhere — every piece of wood becomes the cross, every meal becomes the Lord's Supper — until typology becomes allegory with no anchor in the text.
Scripture
1 Corinthians 5:7; Hebrews 10:1; Luke 24:27
