Parable

Summary

A parable is a story with a punch: told to reveal truth to open hearts and conceal it from closed ones. Most parables press one main point (sometimes two or three) — they are not allegories where every detail stands for something else.

How To Apply

Find the story's twist or surprise, and ask what single point it drives home to its original hearers. Resist assigning a hidden meaning to every character, object, and number in the story.

Common Mistake

Allegorizing every detail — the oil becomes the Holy Spirit, the inn becomes the church, the coins become sacraments — until the parable's plain punch is buried under invented meanings.

Details

Category: Genre
Scripture Example: Luke 10:25-37 — the Good Samaritan's point is the answer to 'who is my neighbor?': mercy crosses ethnic and religious lines. The innkeeper, the donkey, and the two coins are story furniture, not symbols to decode.
