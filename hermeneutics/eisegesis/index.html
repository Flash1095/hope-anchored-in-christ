Eisegesis

Principle
Eisegesis
Category
Term
Summary
Eisegesis is reading meaning into a text rather than drawing it out: starting with a conclusion and then finding verses to support it. It is the opposite of exegesis, and it is the root error behind proof-texting and most misreadings.
How To Apply
Guard against it by letting the text set the agenda: read the whole passage before deciding what it proves, and welcome the discovery that a favorite verse does not say what you assumed. If your reading needs the verse to ignore its context, it is eisegesis.
Scripture Example
Matthew 4:5-7 — Satan quotes Psalm 91:11-12 to Jesus, accurately in wording but against the psalm's context of trusting God rather than testing him. Jesus answers with another Scripture read in context: the episode is the Bible's own portrait of eisegesis at work.
Common Mistake
Starting with a conclusion and hunting for verses, so that the Bible becomes a mirror for opinions formed elsewhere instead of a voice that can correct them.
Scripture
Matthew 4:5-7; 2 Peter 3:16; Psalm 91:11-12
