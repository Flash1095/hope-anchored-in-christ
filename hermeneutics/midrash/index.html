Midrash

Principle
Midrash
Category
Method
Summary
Midrash is the Jewish interpretive tradition of expounding Scripture: rabbinic teachers filled narrative gaps, drew moral lessons, and linked texts by shared words and themes. It is background, not background noise — the New Testament writers, steeped in this tradition, sometimes use its techniques when they handle the Old Testament.
How To Apply
Recognize midrashic moves when they appear: a gap filled, a word linked to another passage, a story retold with a lesson drawn. Treat midrash as the interpretive world the biblical writers inhabited, and distinguish a midrashic expansion from the plain sense of the passage it expounds.
Scripture Example
Acts 7 — Stephen retells Israel's story with expansions and emphases drawn from the tradition, shaping the history into an argument about Israel's resistance. Matthew's fulfillment citations work similarly: they link Jesus to the Old Testament by shared patterns and phrases, in ways a modern reader must learn to recognize.
Common Mistake
Treating a midrashic expansion as if it were the plain meaning of the passage it expounds, or dismissing the Jewish interpretive tradition as irrelevant to understanding the New Testament.
Scripture
Matthew 2:15; Acts 7:1-53; Nehemiah 8:8
