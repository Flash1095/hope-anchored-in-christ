Narrative

Principle
Narrative
Category
Genre
Summary
Much of the Bible is story, and stories teach mainly by showing rather than telling: through plot, character, dialogue, and the narrator's emphasis. Narrative invites us to follow the arc and notice what the storyteller highlights or leaves unsaid.
How To Apply
Read the whole story in one sitting when you can. Track the characters' choices and consequences, notice repeated words, and ask what the narrator seems to approve or expose — rather than moralizing every detail.
Scripture Example
2 Samuel 11-12 — the spare account of David and Bathsheba, followed by Nathan's confrontation, teaches through the arc itself: sin concealed, sin exposed, repentance offered. The lesson comes from the story's shape, not from a stated rule.
Common Mistake
Treating every character as a direct role model, or mining each detail for a moral, so that even villains' actions get copied because 'it's in the Bible.'
Scripture
2 Samuel 11:1-12:25; Genesis 37:1-36; Luke 15:11-32
