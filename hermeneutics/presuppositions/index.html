Presuppositions

Principle
Presuppositions
Category
Term
Summary
Presuppositions are the beliefs and assumptions a reader brings to the text before interpretation begins: about whether God exists and speaks, whether miracles can happen, whether the text is trustworthy, and what counts as evidence. No reader comes to the Bible with a blank mind. Presuppositions do not make interpretation impossible, but unexamined ones quietly decide outcomes in advance — the same passage can yield opposite readings from readers who started from opposite assumptions.
How To Apply
Name your own starting points honestly before you study: what do you already assume about this passage, and what would change your mind? When two careful readers disagree, check whether the disagreement is really about the text or about a prior assumption each brought to it. Test your presuppositions against Scripture itself rather than letting them silently filter what Scripture is allowed to say.
Scripture Example
Acts 17:11 commends the Bereans for receiving the message eagerly and examining the Scriptures daily to see whether what Paul said was true — a model of holding teaching accountable to the text rather than to prior loyalty. 1 Thessalonians 5:21 gives the same posture in one line: test everything, hold fast to what is good.
Common Mistake
Believing you have no presuppositions while spotting everyone else's, or treating the recognition of presuppositions as permission for relativism — as if every reading were equally valid. The goal is examined assumptions, not abandoned ones.
Scripture
Acts 17:11; 1 Thessalonians 5:21
