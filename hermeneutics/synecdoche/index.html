Synecdoche

Summary

Synecdoche lets a part stand for the whole: 'bread' means food and provision generally, 'sword' means warfare, 'flesh' means humanity. The named part is a concrete handle for a larger reality.

How To Apply

When a concrete part appears where a larger whole is meant, widen the reading: ask what whole the part represents. The device is economical — one vivid word carrying a whole category.

Common Mistake

Narrowing the part to itself — reading 'bread' as only bread, or 'flesh' as only skin — and so shrinking a broad promise or statement into a small one.

Details

Category: Device
Scripture Example: Matthew 6:11 — 'Give us this day our daily bread' asks for daily provision, not only for baked bread. Joel 2:28 — 'all flesh' means all humanity, not merely human skin. In both cases the part is the doorway to the whole.
Scripture: Matthew 6:11; Joel 2:28; Luke 3:6
