Immediate Context

Principle
Immediate Context
Category
Context
Summary
Immediate context is the paragraph around a verse: the sentences directly before and after it, the argument they form, and the situation they address. A verse means what it means inside its paragraph first, and only then anywhere else.
How To Apply
Read at least the whole paragraph — ideally the whole section — before applying a verse. Ask what problem the paragraph is solving and what the verse contributes to that paragraph's point. If an application needs the verse to mean something its paragraph never says, it is the wrong application.
Scripture Example
Jeremiah 29:11 — 'plans to prosper you' is spoken in a letter to exiles facing seventy years in Babylon, promising restoration after judgment, not instant success. Its comfort is real, but it is the comfort of a long exile with a promised end, not a general prosperity pledge.
Common Mistake
Applying a verse to a situation its paragraph never addressed — turning a promise to a specific people in a specific crisis into a universal guarantee for any wish.
Scripture
Jeremiah 29:1-14; Philippians 4:10-13; Matthew 18:15-20
