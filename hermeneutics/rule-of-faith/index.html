The Rule of Faith

Principle
The Rule of Faith
Category
Principle
Summary
The rule of faith is the early church's summary of apostolic teaching — creation by God, the incarnation, death, and resurrection of Christ, and the hope of new life — used as a guardrail for interpreting Scripture. Writers of the second and third centuries appealed to it when rival readings twisted passages: an interpretation that contradicted the church's received summary of the gospel was judged to have gone wrong, however clever its handling of individual verses. Christians differ on how much authority to give this summary today: some traditions treat it as a binding guide alongside Scripture, while others treat it as a helpful summary that must itself be tested by Scripture.
How To Apply
Use the core summary of the gospel as a sanity check on novel readings: if your interpretation of a difficult passage overturns the plain teaching of the whole canon about who God is and what Christ has done, the interpretation is the problem, not the summary. At the same time, do not let any summary replace the text — the rule of faith guards interpretation, it does not substitute for reading Scripture itself.
Scripture Example
Jude 3 urges contending for the faith that was once for all delivered to the saints, pointing to a received body of teaching that predates later disputes. 2 Timothy 1:13 tells Timothy to follow the pattern of sound words he heard, and 1 Corinthians 15:3-5 preserves one of the earliest summaries itself: Christ died for sins, was buried, and was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures.
Common Mistake
Either ignoring the rule entirely, so that any inventive reading counts as valid, or elevating a later creed or tradition above Scripture so that the text is never allowed to correct it. Both errors abandon the balance the early church itself practiced.
Scripture
Jude 3; 2 Timothy 1:13; 1 Corinthians 15:3-5
