Apocalyptic

Principle
Apocalyptic
Category
Genre
Summary
Apocalyptic writing — Daniel and Revelation — unveils spiritual reality through highly symbolic visions: beasts, colors, numbers, and cosmic battles. It was written to encourage believers under pressure, not to provide a puzzle for decoding the news.
How To Apply
Read apocalyptic for its big picture: God reigns, evil is judged, and the faithful are kept. Treat symbols as symbols, let the book's own interpretations guide you, and resist matching each image to a modern person or event.
Scripture Example
Revelation 13 — the beast rising from the sea symbolizes oppressive imperial power demanding worship, a picture of every empire that sets itself against God. Read symbolically, it comforts persecuted believers; read literally, it becomes a monster hunt.
Common Mistake
Literalistic decoding — identifying the beast, the mark, or the dates with specific modern figures and technologies, and building fear or speculation on the guesses.
Scripture
Revelation 13:1-18; Daniel 7:1-28; Revelation 1:1-3
