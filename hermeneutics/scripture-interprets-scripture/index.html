Scripture Interprets Scripture

Principle
Scripture Interprets Scripture
Category
Principle
Summary
The Bible is its own best commentary: clear passages illuminate unclear ones, and the whole canon keeps any single text in check. When a passage is obscure, the answer usually lies in letting other Scripture speak first.
How To Apply
When you hit a difficult verse, gather what the rest of Scripture says on the same subject before settling a meaning. Let the clear interpret the unclear, and never build a doctrine on a passage whose meaning is genuinely uncertain.
Scripture Example
1 Corinthians 15:29 — 'baptism for the dead' appears once, with no explanation, and no other passage teaches the practice. Sound interpretation refuses to build a doctrine on a single obscure verse when the rest of Scripture is silent.
Common Mistake
Building an entire doctrine on one obscure or isolated text, or quoting a verse against the grain of everything else Scripture says on the subject.
Scripture
1 Corinthians 15:29; 2 Peter 3:15-16; Acts 17:11
