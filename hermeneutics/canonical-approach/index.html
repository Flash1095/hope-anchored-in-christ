Canonical Approach

Principle
Canonical Approach
Category
Method
Summary
The canonical approach, associated with Brevard Childs, reads each text in light of the whole canon: its final form, its place among the books, and the community that received it as Scripture. The question is not only what a text meant in its original setting but what it means as part of the church's Bible.
How To Apply
Read a passage with its canonical neighbors in view: how does its book function within its section, how does the whole canon frame it, and what does the final form emphasize? Keep historical context in the conversation rather than replacing it — the approach's debated point is how much weight the final form carries against the history behind the text.
Scripture Example
Luke 24:44 — Jesus reads 'the Law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms' as a single story about himself, modeling a canonical reading: the parts cohere as one witness. Reading Jonah as part of the Twelve, or the Psalms as an ordered book, follows the same instinct.
Common Mistake
Ignoring historical context entirely in favor of the final form, or treating the canon as flat — as if every book spoke with the same voice on every subject.
Scripture
Luke 24:44; 2 Timothy 3:16; Romans 15:4
