Poetry

Principle
Poetry
Category
Genre
Summary
Poetry speaks in imagery, emotion, and rhythm rather than precision prose. The Psalms include laments, praises, thanksgivings, royal psalms, and wisdom psalms, each with its own mood and purpose — all of them meant to be felt, not flattened into literal statements.
How To Apply
Read poetry slowly and aloud when possible. Ask what image or emotion the poet is painting, and let the feeling carry the theology. Do not press every metaphor into a doctrinal definition.
Scripture Example
Psalm 23 — 'the valley of the shadow of death' paints danger and God's shepherding presence in imagery; it is not a literal valley on a map. Read as poetry, it comforts far more than a literal reading ever could.
Common Mistake
Reading poetic exaggeration as literal promise — for example, turning Psalm 91's imagery of protection into a guarantee that no harm will ever touch a believer.
Scripture
Psalm 23:1-6; Psalm 91:1-16; Song of Solomon 2:7
