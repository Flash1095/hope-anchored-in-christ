Anthropomorphism

Principle
Anthropomorphism
Category
Device
Summary
Anthropomorphism describes God with human features and actions — his hand, his arm, his eyes, his grief — so that infinite truth can be grasped in human terms. The device conveys real truth about God without implying that God has a body or changes like a human.
How To Apply
Read anthropomorphic language as true description in human dress: God's 'arm' speaks of his power, his 'eyes' of his watchful care, his 'grief' of his real displeasure at sin. Hold both sides — the language is meaningful, and it is not literal anatomy.
Scripture Example
Exodus 15:6 — 'Your right hand, O LORD, glorious in power, shattered the enemy.' No reader is meant to picture a physical hand; the image says God's power acted decisively at the sea. Numbers 23:19 guards the other side: God 'is not man, that he should lie.'
Common Mistake
Literalizing the image (God has a physical form) or dismissing it (denying that God truly acts, sees, and cares) — both errors miss that the device is how Scripture truthfully speaks of God.
Scripture
Exodus 15:6; Psalm 17:8; Numbers 23:19
