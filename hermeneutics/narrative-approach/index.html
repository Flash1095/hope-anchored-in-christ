Narrative Approach

Principle
Narrative Approach
Category
Method
Summary
The narrative approach reads Scripture as story: plot, character, narrator, and the reader's place inside the story. Associated with narrative theology, it holds that identity and doctrine are carried by the biblical story itself — Israel's exodus, Jesus' life, death, and resurrection — rather than existing only as abstract propositions.
How To Apply
Follow the story's shape: who are the characters, where does the plot turn, what does the narrator emphasize, and where do you stand inside the story? Let the story do its work on imagination and identity before reducing it to principles.
Scripture Example
Luke 24:27 — the risen Jesus interprets 'all the Scriptures' as a story about himself, from Moses through the Prophets. Narrative reading takes that cue: the Old Testament is not a set of disconnected lessons but one unfolding plot that reaches its climax in Christ.
Common Mistake
Reducing doctrine to story only, as if propositions never mattered, or moralizing characters (be like David, don't be like Saul) instead of following the plot to Christ.
Scripture
Luke 24:27; Exodus 14:1-31; Mark 1:1-15
