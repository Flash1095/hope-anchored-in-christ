Summary
The four Gospels are four portraits of Jesus, each with its own emphasis, audience, and arrangement of material. They are theological biographies: historically rooted, but selective and shaped to proclaim who Jesus is.
How To Apply
Read each Gospel on its own terms first: notice what its author emphasizes, how he arranges events, and whom he addresses. Compare Gospels to enrich the picture, not to flatten their differences into contradictions.
Scripture Example
Matthew 1:1-17 — Matthew opens with a genealogy tracing Jesus to Abraham and David, framing him as the fulfillment of Israel's story for a Jewish audience. Luke, writing more broadly, traces the line back to Adam. Both are true; each serves its Gospel's purpose.
Common Mistake
Forcing the four Gospels into one flat harmony and calling every difference a contradiction, or ignoring an author's emphasis and reading all four as if they were identical.
