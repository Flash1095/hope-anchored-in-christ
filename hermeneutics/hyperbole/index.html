Hyperbole

Principle
Hyperbole
Category
Device
Summary
Hyperbole is deliberate exaggeration for emphasis — a common and accepted way of speaking in the Bible's world. It is meant to shock the hearer into taking something seriously, not to be obeyed woodenly or dismissed as a joke.
How To Apply
When a statement sounds extreme, ask whether the author is using exaggeration to underline urgency or seriousness. Let the overstatement make its point, then apply the point — not the exaggerated form.
Scripture Example
Matthew 5:29 — 'if your right eye causes you to sin, tear it out' uses shocking language to show how radical the fight against sin must be. Jesus is not commanding self-mutilation; he is commanding seriousness about holiness.
Common Mistake
Obeying hyperbole literally (and harming oneself or others) or, at the other extreme, shrugging it off as mere colorful talk and missing the urgent point entirely.
Scripture
Matthew 5:27-30; Luke 14:26; 1 Corinthians 13:1-3
