Summary
Acts is the second volume of Luke's history, narrating the spread of the gospel from Jerusalem to Rome through the apostles' witness in the power of the Holy Spirit. Like the Gospels it is theological history: real events, selected and shaped to show how God's plan advanced.
How To Apply
Follow Luke's geographic and theological arc (Acts 1:8) and read each episode in the book's unfolding story. Distinguish description from prescription: Luke records what happened, which is not always a command for every church in every age. Let the speeches carry the theology — they are Luke's summaries of what the gospel meant.
Scripture Example
Acts 2 — Pentecost: the Spirit falls, Peter preaches Christ crucified and risen, and three thousand are added. The chapter is not a formula for every conversion but the founding moment of the church's witness, showing that the gospel crosses from Jerusalem toward the ends of the earth.
Common Mistake
Treating every narrative detail as normative — demanding communal living, tongues at every conversion, or an identical church order — or else reading Acts as a mere church manual instead of history with a theological purpose.
Scripture
Acts 1:8; Acts 2:1-47; Acts 28:30-31
