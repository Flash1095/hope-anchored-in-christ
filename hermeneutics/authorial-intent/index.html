Authorial Intent

Principle
Authorial Intent
Category
Context
Summary
Meaning is anchored in what the human author, carried along by the Holy Spirit, intended to communicate to the original readers. Authorial intent is the guardrail: a text means what its author meant, not whatever a later reader wishes it meant.
How To Apply
Ask what the author was trying to do: to comfort, to warn, to persuade, to record, to worship? Look for the author's own purpose statements, repeated themes, and the response the text seems designed to produce.
Scripture Example
John 20:30-31 — John states his purpose plainly: these signs 'are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ.' Reading John with that intent in view keeps the focus on belief in Jesus rather than on curiosity about the signs themselves.
Common Mistake
Replacing the author's meaning with 'what this verse means to me,' which makes every reader's feelings the authority and lets one text mean opposite things to different people.
Scripture
John 20:30-31; 2 Peter 1:20-21; Nehemiah 8:8
