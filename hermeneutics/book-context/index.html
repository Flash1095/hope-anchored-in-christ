Book Context

Principle
Book Context
Category
Context
Summary
Every passage sits inside a whole book with its own purpose, argument, and themes. Book context asks how a passage functions in that larger flow: what came before it, what it prepares for, and what the author's stated purpose says it is doing.
How To Apply
Before interpreting a passage, sketch the book: read its opening and closing, note repeated words and turning points, and locate your passage on that map. A verse that seems to contradict the book's drift is usually being misread, not the book contradicting itself.
Scripture Example
Romans 8:28 — 'all things work together for good' lands inside Romans' argument about suffering, the Spirit, and future glory (Romans 8:18-30), not as a blanket promise that every event is good. The book's flow keeps the verse from becoming a platitude about bad things.
Common Mistake
Interpreting a verse against the book that contains it — for instance, using a verse from a book of lament to cancel that book's own teaching, or ignoring an author's purpose statement.
Scripture
Luke 1:1-4; John 20:30-31; Romans 8:18-30
