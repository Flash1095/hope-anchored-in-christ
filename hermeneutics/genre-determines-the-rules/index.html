Genre Determines the Rules

Principle
Genre Determines the Rules
Category
Principle
Summary
How you read depends on what you are reading: poetry is not read like law, apocalyptic is not read like epistle, and proverb is not read like promise. Genre sets the rules of interpretation, and ignoring it is the fastest way to misread a passage.
How To Apply
Before interpreting, name the genre: narrative, law, poetry, wisdom, prophecy, apocalyptic, Gospel, parable, or epistle. Then read by that genre's rules — imagery for poetry, argument for epistle, symbol for apocalyptic.
Scripture Example
Revelation 1:1-3 and Romans 1:16-17 — Revelation announces itself as prophecy in symbols, while Romans argues a theological case step by step. Reading Revelation's dragons with Romans' rules, or Romans' argument with Revelation's symbols, misreads both books.
Common Mistake
Reading every genre by the same rules: treating Proverbs as promises, Revelation as a newspaper, or a parable as a history report — and then wondering why the Bible seems to contradict itself.
Scripture
Revelation 1:1-3; Romans 1:16-17; Proverbs 26:4-5
