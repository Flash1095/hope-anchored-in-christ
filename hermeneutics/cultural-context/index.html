Cultural Context

Principle
Cultural Context
Category
Context
Summary
The Bible's world had customs very different from ours: honor and shame, patronage, hospitality codes, purity laws, and family structures. Cultural context asks what a behavior or image would have meant inside that world before judging it by today's norms.
How To Apply
When something in a passage feels strange or harsh, pause and ask what it meant in its culture: consult a Bible dictionary or study notes on the custom, then separate the timeless principle from the cultural form it took.
Scripture Example
Luke 15:11-32 — a son demanding his inheritance while his father lived was, in that culture, effectively wishing his father dead; and a respected father running to meet him was shameful and undignified. Knowing that, the father's run becomes the shocking heart of the grace in the story.
Common Mistake
Judging ancient customs by modern standards, or assuming a culturally shaped practice (like a greeting, a garment, or a seating custom) carries exactly the same meaning today.
Scripture
Luke 15:11-32; Ruth 3:1-13; 1 Corinthians 11:2-16
