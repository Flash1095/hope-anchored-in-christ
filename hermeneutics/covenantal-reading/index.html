Covenantal Reading

Principle
Covenantal Reading
Category
Method
Summary
Covenantal reading (covenant theology) sees one covenant of grace running through redemptive history: the promises to Abraham are fulfilled in Christ and extend to the church, and the Old and New Testaments form one people of God. Continuity is emphasized: what God promised Israel finds its yes in Christ. Dispensational readers disagree, holding that national Israel retains distinct promises yet to be fulfilled literally — the two approaches differ chiefly on Israel and the church, not on the gospel itself.
How To Apply
Trace promises through their covenant stages: what was promised, to whom, under which covenant, and how Christ fulfills it. Let the New Testament's own use of the Old Testament (Galatians 3, Romans 11) set the pattern for continuity and fulfillment.
Scripture Example
Galatians 3:7-9 — Paul argues that those of faith are sons of Abraham, so the blessing promised to Abraham reaches the nations through Christ. Covenantal reading takes this as the pattern: promise given to Israel, fulfilled in Christ, shared by the church.
Common Mistake
Flattening the distinctions between covenants — treating every Old Testament promise as if it applied unchanged to the church — or, on the other side, denying the real continuity the New Testament itself claims.
Scripture
Genesis 12:1-3; Galatians 3:7-9; Romans 11:1-32
