Read the Whole Counsel

Principle
Read the Whole Counsel
Category
Principle
Summary
God's whole Word — not just favorite parts — is the church's diet. Reading the whole counsel means letting every doctrine be shaped by all of Scripture, so that one emphasis never crowds out the truths that balance it.
How To Apply
Read broadly and systematically, not only in familiar passages. When forming a belief, ask what the whole Bible says: the hard passages and the comforting ones, the Old Testament and the New, the Gospels and the Epistles.
Scripture Example
Acts 20:27 — Paul tells the Ephesian elders he declared 'the whole counsel of God,' holding nothing back. A church fed on the whole counsel knows both God's love and his holiness, both grace and judgment, because it has heard all of it.
Common Mistake
Keeping a canon within the canon — only Paul, only the red letters, only the passages that comfort — until a lopsided Bible produces a lopsided faith.
Scripture
Acts 20:27; 2 Timothy 3:16; Psalm 119:160
