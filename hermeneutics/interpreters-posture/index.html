The Interpreter's Posture

Principle
The Interpreter's Posture
Category
Principle
Summary
The interpreter's posture is the character a reader brings to the text: humility about one's own understanding, prayer for light, patience with difficulty, and a willingness to read in community rather than alone. Technique matters, but technique in the hands of a hasty, proud, or isolated reader still produces hasty, proud, isolated readings. The posture does not replace study — it is what keeps study honest, because it admits that the reader, not only the text, is part of every interpretation.
How To Apply
Slow down on passages you are sure about, and slower still on passages you want to use against someone. Pray before you study and expect to be corrected by what you read, not merely confirmed. Check your reading with other believers, living and dead — a private interpretation that no faithful reader in any age has seen deserves extra scrutiny. And let reading lead to doing: an interpretation that never reaches obedience is unfinished.
Scripture Example
James 1:19-22 pairs the posture with the practice: be quick to listen and slow to speak, receive the implanted word with meekness, and be doers of the word rather than hearers only. Psalm 119:18 prays the posture in one line — open my eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of your law — and Acts 2:42 shows it communally: the first believers devoted themselves together to the apostles' teaching.
Common Mistake
Treating interpretation as pure technique, so that character drops out of the picture, or treating posture as a substitute for study, so that sincerity excuses sloppy reading. Humility without diligence is sentiment; diligence without humility is pride with footnotes.
Scripture
James 1:19-22; Psalm 119:18; Acts 2:42
