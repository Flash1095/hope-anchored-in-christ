Descriptive vs. Prescriptive

Principle
Descriptive vs. Prescriptive
Category
Principle
Summary
Narrative describes what happened; it does not always prescribe what should happen. Much of the Bible records human behavior — good and bad — without endorsing it, and wisdom lies in telling description apart from command.
How To Apply
When reading a story, ask whether the text commends, condemns, or merely reports the action. Look for the narrator's signals and for explicit commands elsewhere before copying a character's behavior.
Scripture Example
Judges 6:36-40 — Gideon's fleece is a description of one man's hesitant request for assurance, not a prescribed method for decision-making. Treating it as a technique turns a story about God's patience into a formula for demanding signs.
Common Mistake
Copying everything Bible characters did — polygamy, deception, vengeance — simply because it appears in the narrative, as if description were endorsement.
Scripture
Judges 6:36-40; Genesis 16:1-16; Acts 2:44-45
