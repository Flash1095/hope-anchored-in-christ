Clarity of Scripture

Principle
Clarity of Scripture
Category
Principle
Summary
The clarity (perspicuity) of Scripture holds that the Bible's central message is clear enough for ordinary readers to understand and obey: the gospel, God's character, and the life he requires do not hide behind specialist knowledge. Obscure passages exist, but they do not block the main road.
How To Apply
Read with confidence that the main point is within reach, and with humility about genuinely difficult passages. Use study tools for the hard places without concluding that the whole book is a puzzle only experts can open.
Scripture Example
Deuteronomy 30:11-14 — Moses tells Israel the commandment 'is not too hard for you' nor far off in heaven or across the sea; it is 'very near you, in your mouth and in your heart.' Psalm 119:105 makes the same claim in miniature: God's word is a lamp for ordinary feet on an ordinary path.
Common Mistake
Claiming the Bible is too obscure for anyone to understand (an excuse for disobedience), or the opposite error — assuming every passage is equally plain and needing no study at all.
Scripture
Deuteronomy 30:11-14; Psalm 119:105; 2 Timothy 3:15
