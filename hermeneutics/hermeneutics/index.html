Hermeneutics

Summary

Hermeneutics is the discipline of interpretation itself: the principles and methods by which texts — above all Scripture — are understood, explained, and applied. The name comes from the Greek hermeneuein, to interpret or explain. Everyone who reads the Bible practices hermeneutics; the only question is whether they practice it well.

How To Apply

Approach every text with method rather than instinct: identify its genre, set it in context, ask what the author intended, and only then apply it. Studying hermeneutics is learning to do consciously what every reader already does unconsciously.

Common Mistake

Assuming hermeneutics is optional — as if reading without method meant reading without interpretation — when in fact an unexamined method is still a method, only a worse one.

Details

Category: Term
Scripture Example: Acts 8:30-31 — Philip asks the Ethiopian eunuch, 'Do you understand what you are reading?' and the eunuch answers, 'How can I, unless someone guides me?' The whole discipline exists in that exchange: understanding requires guidance, method, and humility.
