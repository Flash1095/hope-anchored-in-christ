Dual Authorship

Principle
Dual Authorship
Category
Principle
Summary
Dual authorship is the conviction that Scripture has two authors at once: God, who breathed it out, and the human writers, who wrote as real people in real times with real vocabularies, styles, and purposes. Neither side cancels the other. The divine authorship is why the text carries God's authority and a unity across centuries; the human authorship is why grammar, history, culture, and genre are legitimate and necessary tools for understanding it.
How To Apply
Read every passage with both authorships in view: ask what the human writer was saying to the original audience through words, genre, and history, and ask how that fits the whole counsel of God across the canon. If an interpretation honors the human writer's intent but contradicts the rest of Scripture, or honors a theological system but flattens what the writer actually wrote, something has gone wrong on one side of the pair.
Scripture Example
2 Timothy 3:16 states that all Scripture is God-breathed, while 2 Peter 1:21 adds that men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit — divine origin through human agency. Luke 1:1-4 shows the human side plainly: Luke investigated eyewitness accounts and wrote an orderly account, using ordinary historical method in service of a true report.
Common Mistake
Collapsing to one side: treating the Bible as if it dropped from heaven without human authors (so genre, culture, and wording stop mattering), or treating it as merely human literature (so its divine authority and unity disappear). Sound interpretation holds both together.
Scripture
2 Timothy 3:16-17; 2 Peter 1:20-21; Luke 1:1-4
