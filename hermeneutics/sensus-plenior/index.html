Sensus Plenior

Principle
Sensus Plenior
Category
Term
Summary
Sensus plenior — Latin for 'fuller sense' — is the meaning intended by God that goes beyond what the human author consciously grasped, seen in light of later revelation. It is a contested concept: evangelicals differ on how far it extends. The site's position, stated plainly, is to affirm it carefully where the New Testament authors themselves draw it, and to resist using it as a license for uncontrolled hidden meanings.
How To Apply
Use sensus plenior only where the New Testament leads: when an apostolic writer finds a deeper fulfillment in an Old Testament text, receive it as God's fuller sense. Do not extend the principle by analogy to meanings no apostolic writer drew.
Scripture Example
Hosea 11:1 — 'out of Egypt I called my son' speaks of Israel in Hosea's own context, yet Matthew 2:15 finds its fuller sense in Jesus, the true Son who relives Israel's story. The fuller sense does not replace Hosea's meaning; it completes it in light of Christ.
Common Mistake
Using sensus plenior to license any hidden meaning an interpreter prefers, so that the human author's meaning disappears and the text can be made to say anything.
Scripture
Hosea 11:1; Matthew 2:15; 1 Peter 1:10-12
