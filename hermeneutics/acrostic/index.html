Acrostic

Principle
Acrostic
Category
Device
Summary
An acrostic is a poem whose lines or stanzas begin with successive letters of the Hebrew alphabet, from aleph to tav. The form signals completeness — a subject covered from A to Z — and aids memorization.
How To Apply
Notice the acrostic structure when you meet it: it marks a poem as a complete, crafted meditation rather than a spontaneous outburst, and it explains why some psalms feel comprehensive rather than narrowly focused.
Scripture Example
Psalm 119 — twenty-two stanzas of eight verses, each stanza built on one Hebrew letter in order, covering the psalmist's love for God's law from every angle. Lamentations 1-4 use the same device for grief, and Proverbs 31:10-31 for the portrait of a worthy wife.
Common Mistake
Hunting for hidden codes in the acrostic order, or missing that the form itself is the message: this subject is being treated completely, from first letter to last.
Scripture
Psalm 119:1-176; Lamentations 1:1-22; Proverbs 31:10-31
