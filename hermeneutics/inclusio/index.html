Inclusio

Principle
Inclusio
Category
Device
Summary
Inclusio (bookending) opens and closes a passage or section with the same word or phrase, marking its boundaries and naming its theme. The repeated frame tells the reader where the unit begins and ends and what it is about.
How To Apply
Watch for repeated opening and closing lines: they outline the section for you and prevent importing context from outside the frame. The theme named by the frame is usually the passage's controlling idea.
Scripture Example
Psalm 8 opens and closes with the same line — 'O LORD, our Lord, how majestic is your name in all the earth!' (verses 1 and 9) — framing a meditation on human smallness and dignity inside God's majesty. Matthew does the same on a gospel scale: 'God with us' at 1:23 and 'I am with you always' at 28:20.
Common Mistake
Missing section boundaries and dragging in context from outside the frame, or treating the repeated line as filler instead of the passage's own outline.
Scripture
Psalm 8:1, 9; Matthew 1:23; Matthew 28:20
