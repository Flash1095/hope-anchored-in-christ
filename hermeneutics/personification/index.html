Personification

Principle
Personification
Category
Device
Summary
Personification gives human traits to non-human things: Wisdom cries aloud in the streets, creation groans in labor pains, sin crouches at the door. The device makes abstract realities vivid and relational without claiming the figure is a person.
How To Apply
Read personified figures as vivid presentations of real truths: Wisdom's speeches teach what wisdom is and does; creation's groaning pictures the world's real bondage to decay. Ask what truth the figure embodies rather than what person it might be.
Scripture Example
Proverbs 8 — Wisdom speaks as a woman calling in the streets, inviting the simple to learn. Romans 8:19-22 picks up the same device: creation itself 'groans' awaiting redemption, a picture of the real brokenness of the world, not a claim that trees have feelings.
Common Mistake
Treating a personified figure as a separate divine person (as some do with Wisdom in Proverbs 8), or reducing the figure to mere poetry and losing the real truth it embodies.
Scripture
Proverbs 8:1-36; Romans 8:19-22; Genesis 4:7
