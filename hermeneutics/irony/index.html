Irony

Principle
Irony
Category
Device
Summary
Irony says or shows the opposite of what is expected, and its force depends on the reader noticing the gap. Dramatic irony goes further: the reader knows something the characters do not, so their confident words cut against them.
How To Apply
Read for the gap between appearance and reality: when a scene's outcome reverses its setup, or a character's words mean more than they know, the author is using irony. Let the reversal carry the point instead of flattening it into a straight statement.
Scripture Example
Esther 7:9-10 — Haman, who built a gallows for Mordecai, is hanged on it himself; the reversal is the point. John 9 works the same way in reverse: the blind man comes to see while the sighted Pharisees prove blind. Even the placard 'King of the Jews' (Mark 15:26) is true in a way its writers never meant.
Common Mistake
Flattening irony into a straight statement and missing the reversal, or the opposite error — detecting irony everywhere until no statement can be taken at face value.
Scripture
Esther 7:9-10; John 9:1-41; Mark 15:26
