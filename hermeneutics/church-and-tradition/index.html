The Church and Tradition

Principle
The Church and Tradition
Category
Principle
Summary
Interpretation happens in community, not alone: the historic creeds, confessions, and the church's long reading of Scripture guard against private novelty. Tradition serves Scripture as a guide and a check; Christians differ on how much weight it carries — Catholic and Orthodox traditions give it greater authority, while Protestant traditions hold Scripture alone as the final rule — but all agree that lone-ranger interpretation is dangerous.
How To Apply
Test a new reading against the church's historic understanding: if no faithful reader in two thousand years saw it, be suspicious. Use creeds and commentaries as conversation partners, and let Scripture remain the judge when tradition and text appear to conflict.
Scripture Example
Acts 17:11 — the Bereans are praised for examining the Scriptures daily to see whether Paul's teaching was true. They neither accepted a new teacher uncritically nor dismissed him without checking: community, tradition, and the text itself worked together.
Common Mistake
Ignoring two thousand years of faithful reading and assuming a private insight is self-validating, or the opposite error — elevating tradition above Scripture so that the text can no longer correct the tradition.
Scripture
Acts 17:11; 2 Thessalonians 2:15; 1 Timothy 3:15
