Exegesis

Principle
Exegesis
Category
Term
Summary
Exegesis (from the Greek for 'to lead out') is the careful work of drawing meaning out of a text: studying its language, context, genre, and history in order to explain what it says. It is the disciplined opposite of eisegesis.
How To Apply
Practice exegesis in order: read the passage whole, study its words and grammar, set it in its historical and literary context, identify its genre, and state its meaning in a sentence before asking what it requires of you.
Scripture Example
Nehemiah 8:8 — the Levites 'read from the Book of the Law of God, clearly, and they gave the sense, so that the people understood the reading.' That verse is exegesis in miniature: clear reading plus explained sense producing understanding.
Common Mistake
Exegesis without application — knowledge that never reaches obedience — or exegesis that skips context and mistakes word studies for understanding.
Scripture
Nehemiah 8:8; 2 Timothy 2:15; Acts 17:11
