Canonical Context

Principle
Canonical Context
Category
Context
Summary
Each book belongs to one unfolding story — creation, fall, redemption, and new creation — and later Scripture often interprets earlier Scripture. Canonical context asks where a passage sits in that whole story and how the rest of the Bible fills it out.
How To Apply
Place the passage on the Bible's storyline and ask what comes before and after it in redemptive history. Check how later biblical authors quote or use it, and let the whole canon keep any single passage in balance.
Scripture Example
Isaiah 53 — the Suffering Servant songs land differently when read within Isaiah's exile-and-restoration storyline and then fulfilled in Jesus, whom the New Testament presents as the Servant who bears his people's griefs.
Common Mistake
Treating a passage as an isolated island, so that an Old Testament promise or command is applied without any reference to how Christ and the apostles later handle it.
Scripture
Luke 24:27; Isaiah 53:1-12; Romans 15:4
