Intertextual Context

Principle
Intertextual Context
Category
Context
Summary
Scripture constantly echoes Scripture: later writers quote, allude to, and rework earlier passages, and their meaning deepens when the source is known. Intertextual context asks what earlier text is being invoked and what its original setting adds.
How To Apply
When a passage quotes or echoes an earlier one, read the source passage too. Ask why the author chose that text, whether the quotation follows or redirects its original sense, and what the echo adds that a plain statement would not.
Scripture Example
Matthew 2:15 — 'Out of Egypt I called my son' quotes Hosea 11:1, where Israel is God's son called out of Egypt. Matthew is not claiming Hosea predicted Jesus' flight; he is presenting Jesus as the true Son who relives Israel's story faithfully — a meaning visible only with Hosea open beside Matthew.
Common Mistake
Ignoring the quoted context entirely, so that a quotation is read as if it were an isolated proof, or else assuming every quotation must be a direct prediction rather than a pattern fulfilled.
Scripture
Matthew 2:15; Hosea 11:1; Romans 4:1-25
