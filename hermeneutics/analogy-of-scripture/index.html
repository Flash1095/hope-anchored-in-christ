Analogy of Scripture

Principle
Analogy of Scripture
Category
Principle
Summary
The analogy of Scripture (analogia scripturae) holds that Scripture is consistent with itself, so no interpretation may contradict the clear teaching of Scripture elsewhere. Obscure passages yield to clear ones, and no doctrine may rest on a verse read against the whole.
How To Apply
When a passage seems to teach something at odds with the rest of Scripture, treat that as a signal to re-read, not as a license to build a doctrine. Let the clear passages set the boundaries inside which the obscure passage is understood.
Scripture Example
1 Corinthians 15:29 — 'baptized for the dead' is a famously obscure verse. Whatever it means, the analogy of Scripture forbids building a doctrine of post-mortem baptism on it against the clear teaching of salvation elsewhere; the obscure verse is interpreted within the clear gospel, not above it.
Common Mistake
Building a doctrine on an obscure or isolated verse against the whole counsel of Scripture, or resolving every tension by flattening one passage to fit a preferred system.
Scripture
1 Corinthians 15:29; 2 Peter 3:15-16; Acts 17:11
