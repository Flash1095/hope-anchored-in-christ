Literary Context

Summary

No verse stands alone: every passage sits inside a paragraph, a chapter, and a book's argument. Literary context means reading the verses around a text — what comes before and after — because the surrounding flow usually controls what a single verse means.

How To Apply

Always read at least the paragraph before and after a verse, and preferably the whole chapter. Ask: what argument is the author making here, and how does this verse serve it? Let the flow, not a single line, carry the meaning.

Common Mistake

Cherry-picking a favorite verse and building a meaning on it that the surrounding passage never intended — the classic source of slogans the Bible never actually teaches.

Details

Category: Context
Scripture Example: Philippians 4:13 — 'I can do all things through him who strengthens me' sits inside Paul's discussion of contentment in plenty and in want (4:11-12). In context it is about enduring any circumstance with Christ's strength, not a promise of success at any goal.
Scripture: Philippians 4:10-20; 2 Timothy 2:15; Luke 10:38-42
