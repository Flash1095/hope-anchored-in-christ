Lament

Principle
Lament
Category
Genre
Summary
Lament is the Bible's form for bringing grief, confusion, and protest to God honestly. Its usual shape moves from address and complaint, through petition, to an expression of trust and often a vow of praise — but the praise never cancels the pain that came first.
How To Apply
Let a lament take its full course: name the trouble plainly, ask God to act, and notice where trust appears — sometimes only as a single line at the end. Lament is faith speaking under pressure, not faith failing, and it gives words to sufferers who cannot yet praise.
Scripture Example
Psalm 13 — David asks 'How long, O LORD?' four times, pleads for God to look and answer, and ends, 'I will sing to the LORD, for he has been good to me.' The movement from complaint to trust is the form itself, and it is the same shape behind Lamentations and Jesus' cry in Gethsemane.
Common Mistake
Treating lament as faithlessness and rushing sufferers to praise, or else treating the complaint as the final word and missing the trust the form deliberately preserves.
Scripture
Psalm 13:1-6; Lamentations 3:19-26; Mark 14:32-36
