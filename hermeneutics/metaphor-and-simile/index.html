Metaphor and Simile

Principle
Metaphor and Simile
Category
Device
Summary
Metaphors and similes describe one thing in terms of another: 'I am the vine,' 'like a lion,' 'the Lord is my shepherd.' They reveal truth by comparison, and their power lies in the point of likeness — not in every possible way the two things compare.
How To Apply
Identify the point of comparison the author intends, and stop there. Ask what the image illuminates about God or his people, and do not press the image beyond its purpose.
Scripture Example
John 15:1-8 — 'I am the true vine' pictures union with Christ and fruitfulness: branches live only by abiding in the vine. The point is dependence and fruit, not botany, pruning schedules, or vineyard management.
Common Mistake
Literalizing a metaphor ('Jesus is a door, so what are the hinges?') or pressing every detail of an image until the comparison collapses under weight it was never meant to bear.
Scripture
John 15:1-8; Psalm 23:1; Matthew 5:13-14
