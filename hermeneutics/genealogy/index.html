Genealogy

Principle
Genealogy
Category
Genre
Summary
Genealogies are family lists that trace lineage, and in Scripture they are never mere filler. They establish legitimacy, trace the line of promise, structure history into eras, and show God's faithfulness across generations.
How To Apply
Ask why names are included, omitted, or arranged: Matthew's three sets of fourteen and his inclusion of Tamar, Rahab, Ruth, and Bathsheba are theological choices, not accidents. Read a genealogy for what its shape says about God's plan, and allow for telescoping — ancient lists often skip generations for structure.
Scripture Example
Matthew 1:1-17 — Jesus' genealogy traces him to David and Abraham in three structured movements, deliberately including Gentile women and scandalous stories, so that the Messiah's ancestry itself announces grace reaching beyond Israel.
Common Mistake
Skipping genealogies as boring data, or forcing modern chronological precision onto lists that were shaped for theological and literary purposes.
Scripture
Matthew 1:1-17; Genesis 5:1-32; 1 Chronicles 1:1-9:44
