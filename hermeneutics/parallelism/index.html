Parallelism

Summary

Hebrew poetry builds meaning in paired lines: the second line may restate the first (synonymous), contrast it (antithetic), or advance it (synthetic). Parallelism is the engine of biblical poetry — the pair, not the single line, carries the thought.

How To Apply

Read poetic lines in pairs, not one at a time. Ask how the second line relates to the first: does it echo, contrast, or complete it? The relationship between the lines is where the meaning lives.

Common Mistake

Reading each poetic line as a separate new idea, so that restatements get counted as additional promises or contrasts get missed entirely.

Details

Category: Device
Scripture Example: Psalm 1:1-2 — 'walk... stand... sit' pairs with 'delight... meditate': the first pair traces a progression into sin, the second a progression into God's law. The parallel structure itself teaches the contrast between two ways of life.
Scripture: Psalm 1:1-6; Proverbs 10:1; Isaiah 55:8-9
