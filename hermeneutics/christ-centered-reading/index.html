Christ-Centered Reading

Principle
Christ-Centered Reading
Category
Principle
Summary
All of Scripture points to Christ: the Old Testament prepares for him, the Gospels proclaim him, and the Epistles apply him. Reading with Christ at the center means asking how each passage fits the story that culminates in Jesus — without forcing him into every verse artificially.
How To Apply
After establishing a passage's own meaning, ask how it relates to Christ: does it promise, picture, prepare for, or flow from him? Let Luke 24 set the pattern, but let each passage speak in its own voice rather than stamping Jesus' name on every detail.
Scripture Example
Luke 24:27 — on the Emmaus road, Jesus himself showed how Moses, the Prophets, and the Psalms all testify to him. The Old Testament is not a collection of moral tales waiting for a Christian ending; it is a story heading toward Christ.
Common Mistake
Moralistic reading that never reaches Christ — reducing every passage to 'be brave like David' — or fanciful allegory that finds Jesus hiding in every detail of every story.
Scripture
Luke 24:27; John 5:39; Colossians 1:15-20
