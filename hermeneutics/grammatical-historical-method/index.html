Grammatical-Historical Method

Principle
Grammatical-Historical Method
Category
Method
Summary
The grammatical-historical method is the standard evangelical approach: a text means what its author intended, recovered through grammar, language, history, culture, and genre. 'Literal' here means the literal sense — the sense the author intended, which may itself be poetic, symbolic, or figurative — not literalistic woodenness.
How To Apply
Work the steps in order: establish the text, study its words and grammar, set it in its historical and literary context, identify its genre, and only then apply it. Let each step check the others: grammar without history goes thin, and history without grammar goes speculative.
Scripture Example
John 3:3-5 — 'born again' is read inside its Jewish context of conversion and renewal, where 'water and Spirit' echoes Ezekiel 36. The grammatical-historical reading refuses both a bare literalism (physical rebirth) and a free spiritualization, because the context fixes the sense.
Common Mistake
Reducing interpretation to an academic exercise that never reaches application, or letting a reconstructed 'history behind the text' override what the text itself actually says.
Scripture
Nehemiah 8:8; Luke 1:1-4; 2 Timothy 2:15
