Pesher

Principle
Pesher
Category
Method
Summary
Pesher is the Qumran community's style of interpretation: an ancient prophetic text is quoted and then applied directly to the community's present — 'this is that.' The Dead Sea Scrolls' Habakkuk Commentary is the classic example, reading Habakkuk's oracles as descriptions of the community's own time.
How To Apply
Recognize pesher moves in the scrolls and, in milder form, in the New Testament's fulfillment language ('this is what was spoken by the prophet Joel'). Distinguish the apostolic use — grounded in Christ's resurrection as the decisive fulfillment — from the Qumran use, which keyed prophecy to the sect's own history.
Scripture Example
Acts 2:16-21 — Peter introduces Joel 2 with 'this is what was spoken,' reading the outpouring of the Spirit at Pentecost as Joel's promised event. The form echoes pesher's 'this is that,' while its content rests on the resurrection, not on a sect's self-understanding.
Common Mistake
Copying pesher's direct 'this is that' for every modern event — turning each news headline into a fulfilled prophecy — without the apostolic grounding that made the New Testament's use legitimate.
Scripture
Acts 2:16-21; Joel 2:28-32; Habakkuk 1:1-2:20
