Avoid Proof-Texting

Principle
Avoid Proof-Texting
Category
Principle
Summary
Proof-texting lifts a verse out of context to win an argument the verse was never making. It treats Scripture as a supply of slogans rather than as God's Word speaking in whole passages, and it almost always distorts the verse it claims to honor.
How To Apply
When someone (including yourself) quotes a single verse as decisive, stop and read its paragraph. Ask what the verse meant where it stands before asking what it proves. If the context does not support the claim, the verse does not either.
Scripture Example
Matthew 18:20 — 'where two or three are gathered in my name, there am I among them' is spoken inside Jesus' teaching on church discipline (18:15-20), assuring a church handling sin that Christ stands behind its judgment — not promising a special presence only in small groups.
Common Mistake
Verse-sniping in debates, where isolated lines get deployed as weapons without any regard for what their passages actually say — and listeners walk away believing the Bible said something it never did.
Scripture
Matthew 18:15-20; 2 Timothy 2:15; Acts 17:11
