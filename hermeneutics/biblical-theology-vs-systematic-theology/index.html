Biblical Theology vs Systematic Theology

Principle
Biblical Theology vs Systematic Theology
Category
Term
Summary
Biblical theology traces the story as it unfolds: what God revealed at each stage of redemptive history, in the order and terms Scripture itself uses. Systematic theology gathers what the whole canon teaches on a topic — God, humanity, salvation, the church — and arranges it into an ordered, coherent account. Both are legitimate, and both are needed: biblical theology keeps doctrine rooted in the story, while systematic theology keeps the story's claims clear, consistent, and applicable. They are two ways of putting Scripture together, not two rival Bibles.
How To Apply
When studying a doctrine, do both moves in order: first trace it through the story (what did this mean for Abraham, for Israel, for the first Christians?), then gather the whole-canon teaching into a summary. Be suspicious of either move done alone: a system built without the story flattens development into timeless abstractions, and a story told without ever drawing conclusions leaves the reader with narrative but no doctrine.
Scripture Example
Luke 24:27 models the biblical-theological move: Jesus explains from Moses and all the Prophets, in sequence, the things concerning himself. Romans models the systematic move: Paul gathers teaching on sin, justification, Israel, and Christian living into a sustained, ordered argument that later readers can summarize topic by topic.
Common Mistake
Confusing the two: reading a later systematic category back into an early passage as if the original audience held it, or refusing to systematize at all on the grounds that 'the Bible is a story, not a textbook.' The first anachronizes; the second leaves the church unable to state what it believes.
Scripture
Luke 24:27; Romans 1:16-17; Romans 9:1-5
