Literal vs. Literalistic

Principle
Literal vs. Literalistic
Category
Principle
Summary
Reading literally means reading according to the author's intended sense — which includes recognizing figures of speech as figures. Reading literalistically means flattening everything into wooden literalism, so that poetry becomes science and metaphor becomes mechanics.
How To Apply
Ask what kind of language the author is using before deciding how literally to take it. Poetry, parable, and apocalyptic are meant to be read literally — that is, as poetry, parable, and apocalyptic — not as newspaper prose.
Scripture Example
Psalm 91:4 — 'He will cover you with his pinions, and under his wings you will find refuge.' The literal sense is poetic shelter in God's care, not a claim that God has feathers. Recognizing the figure is reading literally; demanding feathers is literalism.
Common Mistake
Flattening figures into facts (God has wings, Jesus is a wooden door) or, at the other extreme, dissolving plain historical claims into mere symbolism whenever they are inconvenient.
Scripture
Psalm 91:4; John 6:35; Galatians 4:24-26
