Sermon and Discourse

Principle
Sermon and Discourse
Category
Genre
Summary
Extended teaching blocks — the Sermon on the Mount, the farewell discourse, the sermons of Hebrews, the speeches of Acts — are structured arguments, not collections of loose sayings. Each follows a line of reasoning from its opening claim to its conclusion.
How To Apply
Follow the discourse's argument before lifting a line from it: trace the opening, the turns, and the conclusion, and notice repeated words and rhetorical questions that mark the structure. A single verse inside a sermon means what it means as a step in that argument.
Scripture Example
John 13-17 — Jesus' farewell discourse moves from foot-washing servanthood through promises of the Spirit to his high-priestly prayer, and every part (including 'I am the way') only lands inside that farewell logic of departure, comfort, and mission.
Common Mistake
Cherry-picking single lines from a discourse as slogans while ignoring the argument they belong to — the classic source of misread beatitudes, promises, and warnings alike.
Scripture
Matthew 5:1-7:29; John 13:1-17:26; Hebrews 1:1-4:16
