Chiasm

Principle
Chiasm
Category
Device
Summary
A chiasm is a mirrored structure — A, B, C, B', A' — that draws attention to the center. Biblical authors used it to highlight the turning point of a story or argument, placing the main point at the middle of the pattern.
How To Apply
When a passage seems to repeat itself in reverse order, map the mirror: list the matching sections and look at the center. The center is usually the author's emphasis, the hinge the whole passage turns on.
Scripture Example
Genesis 8:1 — the Flood narrative is arranged chiastically around a single center: 'God remembered Noah.' At the story's darkest point, the turning point is God's remembering — the hinge from judgment to new beginning.
Common Mistake
Missing the center and treating a chiastic passage as a flat list, so that the author's highlighted turning point goes unnoticed.
Scripture
Genesis 8:1; John 1:1-18; Matthew 6:9-13
