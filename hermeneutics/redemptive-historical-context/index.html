Redemptive-Historical Context

Summary

Redemptive-historical context places a passage in the unfolding story of redemption: creation, fall, patriarchs, Israel, Christ, church, and consummation. Every text belongs to a chapter of that story, and its force depends on where in the story it stands.

How To Apply

Locate a passage on the story's timeline before applying it: what has God already done, what is still promised, and how does Christ's coming change this text's role? Passover, temple, sacrifice, and law all mean something different once the story reaches the cross and resurrection.

Common Mistake

Flattening all eras — applying Mosaic civil law directly to a modern state, or treating Old Testament saints as if they possessed the full New Testament revelation — or ignoring the story arc altogether.

Details

Category: Context
Scripture Example: Hebrews 10:1 — the law has 'but a shadow of the good things to come.' Read inside the story, the sacrificial system is a God-given shadow pointing to Christ's once-for-all sacrifice, not a permanent institution nor a failed experiment.
Scripture: Luke 24:27; Galatians 3:15-29; Hebrews 10:1-18
