Prophecy

Principle
Prophecy
Category
Genre
Summary
Prophets spoke God's word into their own time — forthtelling covenant warnings and comforts — and sometimes foretold what was to come. Their oracles are usually poetic, often with a near horizon and a farther one, and they aim first at repentance and hope.
How To Apply
Read a prophecy in its historical crisis first, then ask how it points forward. Expect layered fulfillment rather than a single coded prediction, and let the prophet's call to justice and faithfulness hit home before hunting for timelines.
Scripture Example
Isaiah 7:14 — the sign of Immanuel was first a sign to King Ahaz in his crisis, and Matthew later shows its fuller fulfillment in Jesus' birth. Both horizons matter; neither cancels the other.
Common Mistake
Reading every prophecy as a coded newspaper prediction about current events, so that ancient oracles get forced onto modern headlines they were never addressing.
Scripture
Isaiah 7:14; Matthew 1:22-23; Amos 5:24
