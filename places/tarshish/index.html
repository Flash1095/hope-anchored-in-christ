Description
A distant maritime land, reached by ships, famous for its trade: the ships of Tarshish brought Solomon gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks, and the kings of Tarshish are pictured bringing presents to the king. Jonah fled toward Tarshish when God sent him to Nineveh, boarding a ship at Joppa to get as far from his calling as the sea would take him. No modern location is given here, because Tarshish's location is debated: Tartessos in Spain is commonly proposed, while Sardinia and Tarsus have also been proposed, and none is asserted here as settled.
Biblical Significance
Tarshish stands in Scripture for the far edge of the known world, the place you go when you want to be out of reach. Jonah chose it precisely because it was far from Nineveh and, as he imagined, far from the LORD's presence, yet the storm found him on the way and the fish brought him back. At the same time Tarshish's ships and tribute point to a world wider than Israel, whose distant kings would one day bring gifts to God's king.
