Description
The valley where David's forces defeated Edom and where Amaziah, king of Judah, later slew ten thousand Edomites. The title of Psalm 60 associates that psalm with the same campaign, when Joab returned and smote Edom in the valley of salt. The valley's location is debated and is left blank here: the Arabah south of the Dead Sea is commonly proposed, but that identification is not asserted as settled.
Biblical Significance
The Valley of Salt is remembered as a place of hard-won victory over Edom, first under David and again under Amaziah, and it gave David a name for his fame in war. Yet the psalm tied to the campaign is also a prayer of a people who felt cast off and broken, asking God to turn to them again. Victory and dependence sit side by side: the battle was won, and the victory still had to be prayed for.
