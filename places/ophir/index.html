Description
The gold land of Solomon's voyages, reached by ships sent with Hiram's servants, which brought back gold, almug trees, and precious stones, and whose gold became a byword for wealth. Ophir is also named in the Table of Nations as a son of Joktan, and Job pictures a man laying his gold in the dust, even the gold of Ophir. No modern location is given here, because Ophir's location is debated: Arabia, India, and Africa have all been proposed, and none is asserted here as settled.
Biblical Significance
Ophir is Scripture's picture of wealth at its most distant and dazzling, gold that had to be sailed for and waited three years to return. Solomon's Ophir voyages mark the height of his kingdom's reach and riches, the same riches that the prophets and wisdom writers would later warn could not buy what God alone gives. Job's use of Ophir gold makes the point plainly: even the finest gold of the farthest land is dust beside submission to God.
