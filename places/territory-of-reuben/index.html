Conquest and Judges
Description
The tribe takes its name from Reuben, son of Jacob and Leah, Jacob's firstborn (Genesis 29:32; 49:3-4). The southernmost Transjordan allotment of the tribe Reuben, whose people profile is the Reuben entry in Ancient Civilizations, east of the Jordan River and the Dead Sea, as described in general terms in Joshua 13:15-23. Its territory lay south of Gad, with the Dead Sea and the Jordan valley as its western edge, reaching south toward Moab; Joshua gives its boundary by neighbouring territories and named towns, not by a precise surveyed line, and no precise border is claimed here. Its key cities among existing place entries include Heshbon, Dibon, Aroer and Medeba. Like Gad, Reuben settled east of the Jordan before the crossing, on condition that its fighting men would help conquer the land west of the river. In Judges, Reuben is noted for staying among the sheepfolds while other tribes went to war.
Biblical Significance
Reuben, the firstborn's tribe, settled first and furthest east, and its later hesitation in Judges stands in contrast to the urgency of its original bargain to fight for its brothers.
