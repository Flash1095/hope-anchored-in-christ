Description
The tribe takes its name from Dan, son of Jacob and Bilhah, Rachel's servant (Genesis 30:6; 49:16-17). The original allotment of the tribe Dan, whose people profile is the Dan entry in Ancient Civilizations, on the coastal plain west of Benjamin and Ephraim, as described in general terms in Joshua 19:40-48. This entry is the TERRITORY, not the city: the existing Dan place entry is the CITY in the far north, the place the tribe renamed Dan after capturing Laish. Joshua gives the territory's boundary by neighbouring territories and named towns, not by a precise surveyed line, and no precise border is claimed here. Its key city among existing place entries is Joppa, on its coastal edge. Finding its original allotment too hard to hold, part of the tribe later migrated north to Laish, which it renamed Dan, so the tribe's name came to mark Israel's northern edge as well as its original coastal home.
Biblical Significance
Dan's territory tells two stories at once: an allotment on the coast that the tribe could not hold, and a migration north that gave Israel its proverbial northern boundary, from Dan to Beersheba.
