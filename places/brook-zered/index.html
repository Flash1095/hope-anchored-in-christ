Brook Zered

View on map →

Name
Brook Zered
Modern Location
Wadi el-Hasa, Jordan
Testament
Old
Timeline Era
Exodus and Wilderness
Description
The brook or valley that Israel crossed on the way from Kadesh toward the land of Moab, marking the border of Moab. Deuteronomy says the crossing came after thirty-eight years, when the generation of the men of war had passed away from the camp, so that the wandering generation's time in the wilderness was over. Wadi el-Hasa in Jordan is the commonly accepted identification of the brook; it is stated here as the standard identification, not as certain.
Biblical Significance
Zered is a quiet turning point: a stream crossed, and a generation gone. Israel's long wandering ends not with a battle but with a boundary crossed, as the people God had disciplined step toward the land their children would enter. The brook also marks the edge of Moab, land Israel was told not to take, so the crossing is as much about obedience to God's limits as it is about progress.
