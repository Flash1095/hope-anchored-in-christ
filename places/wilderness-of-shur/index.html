Exodus and Wilderness
Description
The desert east of Egypt, on Egypt's eastern borderland, where Hagar fled from Sarah and was found by the angel of the LORD at a fountain in the way to Shur, and where Israel entered after crossing the Red Sea and went three days finding no water. The same wilderness later marked the edge of Saul's campaign against Amalek and of David's raids. Its modern placement is given only in general, approximate terms: the northwestern Sinai and Egypt's eastern borderland.
Biblical Significance
Shur is the wilderness of both flight and first steps. Hagar, alone and fleeing, met God there and was told to return, with a promise about the son she carried. Israel, newly delivered, walked into the same desert and within three days was thirsty, learning that freedom still meant daily dependence on God for water. Shur therefore stands at the start of the wilderness story in both generations: a place where God meets people who have nowhere else to go.
