Conquest and Judges
Description
The tribe takes its name from Gad, son of Jacob and Zilpah, Leah's servant (Genesis 30:11; 49:19). The Transjordan allotment of the tribe Gad, whose people profile is the Gad entry in Ancient Civilizations, east of the Jordan River, as described in general terms in Joshua 13:24-28. Its territory lay in the Gilead region, between Reuben to the south and the eastern half of Manasseh to the north, with the Jordan valley as its western edge; Joshua gives its boundary by neighbouring territories and named towns, not by a precise surveyed line, and no precise border is claimed here. Its key cities among existing place entries include Ramoth-gilead, Mahanaim and Jazer. Gad was one of the tribes that settled east of the Jordan before the rest of Israel crossed over, on condition that its fighting men would help conquer the land west of the river.
Biblical Significance
Gad's Transjordan home shows Israel's inheritance reaching beyond the Jordan itself, and the tension that came with it: settled early, yet expected to fight alongside its brothers for land it would not itself occupy.
