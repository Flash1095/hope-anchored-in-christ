Territory of Manasseh

Name
Territory of Manasseh
Modern Location
Northern Israel and Jordan, on both sides of the Jordan River
Testament
Old
Timeline Era
Conquest and Judges
Description
The tribe takes its name from Manasseh, son of Joseph, adopted by Jacob as a full tribal ancestor (Genesis 41:51; 48:5, 14). The allotment of the tribe Manasseh, whose people profile is the Manasseh entry in Ancient Civilizations, divided between two half-tribes on opposite sides of the Jordan, as described in general terms in Joshua 13:29-31 and Joshua 17:1-13. The eastern half lay in the Transjordan, in the Bashan region north of Gad, and the western half lay in the northern hill country and coastal plain west of the Jordan, between Ephraim to the south and Issachar and Asher to the north; Joshua gives its boundaries by neighbouring territories and named towns, not by precise surveyed lines, and no precise border is claimed here. Its key cities among existing place entries include Bethshan and Megiddo. In Judges, Manasseh's story continues with Gideon, who was of Manasseh.
Biblical Significance
Manasseh is the only tribe with an inheritance on both sides of the Jordan, a reminder that the promise was never confined to one bank of the river, and its western towns long remained partly unconquered.
