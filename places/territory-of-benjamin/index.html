Description
The tribe takes its name from Benjamin, son of Jacob and Rachel, who died bearing him (Genesis 35:16-18). The allotment of the tribe Benjamin, whose people profile is the Benjamin entry in Ancient Civilizations, lying between Judah to the south and Ephraim to the north, as described in general terms in Joshua 18:11-28. Its border ran from the Jordan valley in the east, through the central hill country, toward the western approaches; Joshua gives its boundary by neighbouring territories and named towns, not by a precise surveyed line, and no precise border is claimed here. Its key cities among existing place entries include Jericho, Gibeon, Ramah and Mizpah, with Jerusalem on its southern border. In Judges, Benjamin's story continues in the civil war of Judges 19-21, after which the tribe survived greatly reduced.
Biblical Significance
Benjamin's small central allotment became a hinge between north and south: it held Jerusalem's border, produced Israel's first king, and nearly vanished in civil war before being restored.
