Territory of Ephraim

Name
Territory of Ephraim

Testament
Old
Timeline Era
Conquest and Judges
Description
The tribe takes its name from Ephraim, son of Joseph, adopted by Jacob as a full tribal ancestor (Genesis 41:52; 48:5, 14). The hill-country allotment of the tribe Ephraim, whose people profile is the Ephraim entry in Ancient Civilizations, lying between Benjamin to the south and Manasseh to the north, as described in general terms in Joshua 16:5-10. Its border ran from the Jordan valley in the east across the central hills toward the Mediterranean coast in the west; Joshua gives its boundary by neighbouring territories and named towns, not by a precise surveyed line, and no precise border is claimed here. Its key cities among existing place entries include Shechem, Shiloh and Bethel. In Judges, Ephraim appears as a proud and prominent tribe, disputing with Gideon and later with Jephthah.
Biblical Significance
Ephraim's central hill country held the tabernacle at Shiloh and the covenant gathering place at Shechem, and later gave its name to the northern kingdom as a whole.
