Description
The tribe takes its name from Naphtali, son of Jacob and Bilhah, Rachel's servant (Genesis 30:8; 49:21). The northern allotment of the tribe Naphtali, whose people profile is the Naphtali entry in Ancient Civilizations, in the hill country of the far north, as described in general terms in Joshua 19:32-39. Its territory lay between Asher to the west, Zebulun and Issachar to the south, and the upper Jordan valley to the east; Joshua gives its boundary by neighbouring territories and named towns, not by a precise surveyed line, and no precise border is claimed here. Its key cities among existing place entries include the fortified cities Hazor and Kedesh. In Judges, Barak of Naphtali led Israel's army with Deborah against Sisera.
Biblical Significance
Naphtali's northern hill country guarded Israel's frontier toward Syria, and its people are remembered in Deborah's song as risking their lives on the heights of the battlefield.
