Description
The tribe takes its name from Judah, son of Jacob and Leah (Genesis 29:35; 49:8-10). The large southern allotment of the tribe Judah, whose people profile is the Judah entry in Ancient Civilizations, as described in general terms in Joshua 15:1-63. Its border ran from the Dead Sea and the southern wilderness in the south and east, across the southern hill country, to the Mediterranean coast in the west, with Benjamin to its north; Joshua gives its boundary by neighbouring territories and named towns, not by a precise surveyed line, and no precise border is claimed here. Its key cities among existing place entries include Jerusalem, Hebron, Bethlehem and Beersheba. This entry is the Joshua allotment, not the later region: the existing Judaea place entry is the later Roman-era region covering the south in the time of Christ. Simeon's allotment lay inside Judah's territory. Judah later became the southern kingdom, and the tribe from which David and, in the New Testament, Jesus came.
Biblical Significance
Judah's allotment became the heartland of the southern kingdom and of David's dynasty, holding Jerusalem, Hebron and Bethlehem within a single tribal inheritance.
