Appeal to Emotion

Fallacy
Appeal to Emotion
Category
Relevance
Definition
Trying to win agreement by stirring strong feelings — fear, pity, anger, or guilt — instead of offering reasons and evidence.
Example
An advert says, 'If you really loved your family, you would buy this product today,' using guilt rather than facts about the product.
Faith Discussion Example
Someone says, 'How could a loving person ever believe in judgment? That idea just feels cruel,' letting a strong feeling settle a theological question by itself.
How To Respond
Take feelings seriously without letting them decide alone: 'I understand why that feels hard — I feel it too. Could we look slowly at what Scripture teaches and why?'
