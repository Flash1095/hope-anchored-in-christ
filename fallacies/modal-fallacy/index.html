Modal Fallacy

Category
Formal
Definition
Confusing the necessity of a whole conditional with the necessity of its consequent. 'Necessarily, if P then Q' does not mean 'if P, then necessarily Q'.
Example
Someone argues, 'It is necessarily true that if I am sitting, I am seated — so if I am sitting, I am necessarily seated.'
Faith Discussion Example
Someone argues, 'If God knows what I will do, then I must necessarily do it' — but the 'must' belongs to the whole statement, not to the person choosing.
Fallacy
Modal Fallacy
How To Respond
Move the 'must' back where it belongs: 'Knowledge of what someone freely chooses doesn't force the choice.'
