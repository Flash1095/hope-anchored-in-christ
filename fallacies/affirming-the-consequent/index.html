Affirming the Consequent

An invalid deductive form: if P then Q; Q is true; therefore P must be true. The consequent being true does not prove the antecedent, since something else could cause Q.

Example

Someone argues, 'If it rains, the grass is wet. The grass is wet, so it must have rained' — forgetting the sprinkler could explain it.

Faith Discussion Example

Someone argues, 'If God is real, people experience peace. I feel peace, so God must be real' — the feeling is real, but other causes of peace exist too.

How To Respond

Honor the experience, then ask for independent support: 'That peace matters — could we also look for reasons that point the same way on their own?'
