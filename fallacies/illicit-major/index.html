Illicit Major

Category
Formal
Definition
An invalid syllogism: all A are B; no C is A; therefore no C is B. The conclusion reaches further than the premises allow, because C could still be B another way.
Example
Someone argues, 'All dogs are mammals. No cats are dogs, so no cats are mammals.'
Faith Discussion Example
Someone argues, 'All prophets spoke for God. No poet is a prophet, so no poet speaks for God' — though poets can still speak truthfully about God.
Fallacy
Illicit Major
How To Respond
Show the overreach: 'The conclusion goes further than the premises allow — let's see what actually follows.'
