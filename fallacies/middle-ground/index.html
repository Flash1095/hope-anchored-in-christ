Middle Ground

Fallacy
Middle Ground
Category
Weak Induction
Definition
Assuming the truth must lie halfway between two opposing positions simply because it is the compromise. Sometimes one side is right, sometimes the middle is, but splitting the difference is not proof.
Example
Two people disagree on a factual bill amount — one says $100, the other $0 despite a receipt for $100 — and someone insists the fair answer must be $50.
Faith Discussion Example
In a doctrinal disagreement, someone says, 'One side says Jesus rose bodily and the other says He did not, so the truth is probably somewhere in between,' treating a factual claim as a compromise.
How To Respond
Seek truth over symmetry: 'Compromise can be kind in preferences, but for factual claims could we follow the evidence, even if it lands closer to one side?'
