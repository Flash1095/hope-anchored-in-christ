Affirmative Conclusion from a Negative Premise

Category
Formal
Definition
An invalid syllogism: a negative premise ('no A is B') combined with another premise cannot yield an affirmative conclusion ('some C is B').
Example
Someone argues, 'No fish are mammals. Some pets are fish, so some pets are mammals.'
Faith Discussion Example
Someone argues, 'No work earns salvation. Some church activities are works, so some church activities earn salvation' — the form cannot carry that conclusion.
Fallacy
Affirmative Conclusion from a Negative Premise
How To Respond
Point to the form, not the faith: 'A negative premise can't yield that positive conclusion — let's rebuild the argument.'
