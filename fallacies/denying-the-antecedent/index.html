Denying the Antecedent

Category
Formal
Definition
An invalid deductive form: if P then Q; P is false; therefore Q must be false. Denying the antecedent does not deny the consequent, since Q could hold for other reasons.
Example
Someone argues, 'If you study, you will pass. You did not study, so you will fail' — though you might pass anyway.
Faith Discussion Example
Someone argues, 'If someone truly believes, they never doubt. She doubts, so she does not believe' — though doubt and faith often live together.
Fallacy
Denying the Antecedent
How To Respond
Test the first claim itself: 'Doubt and faith often live together — let's check whether never doubting is really the mark of belief.'
