Negative Conclusion from Affirmative Premises

Category
Formal
Definition
An invalid syllogism: two affirmative premises cannot yield a negative conclusion. If all A are B and all C are A, then all C are B — not none.
Example
Someone argues, 'All cats are mammals. All Siamese are cats, so no Siamese are mammals.'
Faith Discussion Example
Someone argues, 'All believers are forgiven. All new believers are believers, so no new believers are forgiven' — the premises actually point the other way.
Fallacy
Negative Conclusion from Affirmative Premises
How To Respond
Follow the premises to their real conclusion: 'Affirmative premises can't prove a negative — they actually point the other way here.'
