The Necessary Attributes of God

Argument
The Necessary Attributes of God
Category
Philosophy
Summary
Suppose, as the argument from contingency concludes, that a necessary Being stands behind everything that exists. What must that Being be like? The answer can be read off what we observe. A cause of time itself cannot be bound by time, so it must be timeless and eternal. A cause of space cannot be located inside space, so it must be spaceless, not confined to any place. A cause of matter cannot itself be material, so it must be immaterial spirit. A cause that produced a universe of staggering power and precise, intelligible order must be immensely powerful and intelligent. And a cause that produced conscious persons who know right from wrong must itself be personal and good, since an impersonal force does not explain minds or morals. Scripture names the same profile: the 'I AM' who inhabits eternity, who is Spirit, and who is love. The limits are stated honestly: philosophers debate how tightly some of these steps follow, and this argument on its own establishes the classical profile of God rather than the full Christian doctrine of the Trinity or the Incarnation — it tells us what kind of God the rest of the Christian case must then identify.
Key Points
A cause of time must stand outside time: timeless and eternal; A cause of space is not contained by space: spaceless, not confined to any place; A cause of matter is not made of matter: immaterial spirit; A cause of a powerful, ordered universe must be immensely powerful and intelligent; A cause of conscious, moral persons must itself be personal and good; Limits stated honestly: some steps are debated, and this argument alone does not prove the Trinity or the Incarnation
