Christianity by Elimination

Argument
Christianity by Elimination
Category
Philosophy
Summary
This argument works in four steps, and it applies the same standard at every step: evidence, logical coherence, and explanatory scope. First, God's existence is established logically, not assumed — the collection's arguments from Contingency, Degrees, the Transcendental Argument, Abstract Objects, and Necessary Attributes converge on a necessary, intelligent, moral ground of reality. Second, the major rival worldviews are tested against that same standard, each stated as its own adherents hold it: atheism and naturalism, pantheism, polytheism, deism, Buddhism, Hinduism, Judaism, and Islam. Each is found, in this site's reasoned assessment, to fall short somewhere — unable to explain existence itself, or moral obligation, or consciousness, or evil, or history — and where scholars genuinely debate a point, that debate is named rather than hidden. Third, Christianity's own positive evidence is weighed: the resurrection of Jesus, fulfilled prophecy, the reliability of the New Testament manuscripts, and a church that began in Jerusalem under persecution. Fourth, the conclusion: Christianity is the worldview that survives the scrutiny applied to the others and explains the most — one God, a moral order, a rational universe, a historical redemption, and an answer to evil that neither denies suffering nor leaves it meaningless. The verdict is offered as a reasoned judgment for the reader to weigh step by step, not as a claim everyone already concedes.
Key Points
First establish God's existence: the collection's arguments from Contingency, Degrees, the Transcendental Argument, Abstract Objects, and Necessary Attributes converge on a necessary, intelligent, moral ground of reality; Test every rival by the same standard: evidence, logical coherence, and explanatory scope; Atheism and naturalism, fairly stated, struggle to explain existence itself, an intelligible universe, objective moral obligation, and reason that reliably tracks truth; Pantheism, fairly stated, struggles with personal moral accountability, evil as more than illusion, and a God personal enough to act in history; Polytheism, fairly stated, struggles with one ordered universe, one moral order, and a first cause needing no cause; Deism keeps a Creator but gives up revelation, miracles, and a God who acts, leaving history's claims unevaluated; Buddhism and Hinduism, in their classical forms and with real internal diversity, speak powerfully to suffering but set aside a personal Creator and a once-for-all historical redemption; Judaism shares Christianity's God and Scriptures — the dividing question is historical — whether Jesus is the promised Messiah; Islam shares strict monotheism — the dividing questions are historical and theological — whether Jesus died and rose, and whether God is triune and incarnate; Christianity's positive evidence: the resurrection, fulfilled prophecy, manuscript reliability, and a church born under persecution in Jerusalem; Conclusion, as this site's reasoned assessment: Christianity survives the same scrutiny and explains the most — one God, moral order, a rational universe, historical redemption, and an answer to evil
