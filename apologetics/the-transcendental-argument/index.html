The Transcendental Argument

Argument
The Transcendental Argument
Category
Philosophy
Key Points
Logic, science, and morality all assume an orderly, rational world; An atheist worldview borrows these tools without explaining them; A rational Creator grounds the preconditions of thought; Denying God while using reason argues against its own foundation
Related Books
Proverbs; Romans; Colossians
Related Topics
Apologetics; Truth; Reason
Scripture
Proverbs 1:7; Romans 1:19; Colossians 2:3
Summary
Every argument — including arguments against God — borrows tools it cannot account for: the laws of logic, the uniformity of nature, and objective moral standards. These preconditions of intelligible thought only make sense if the universe is the product of a rational, orderly Mind. The transcendental argument turns the tables: it is not that God is one explanation among others, but that without God, reasoning itself has no foundation.
