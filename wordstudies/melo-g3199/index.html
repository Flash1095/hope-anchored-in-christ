mélō

a primary verb; to be of interest to, i.e. to concern (only third person singular present indicative used impersonally, it matters):--(take) care.

Biblical Usage

Melo means to care, to be of interest to someone, and it is almost always used of God or of Jesus caring when no one else does. Matthew 22:16 has the Pharisees admit that Jesus carest for no man, and Mark 4:38 has the disciples cry, Master, carest thou not that we perish? 1 Peter 5:7 answers them both: cast all your care upon Him, for He careth for you.

Theological Significance

I believe the question the disciples asked in the storm is the question every frightened heart asks, and Peter's answer is the one that holds.
