miçpachath

scruf (as spreading over the surface)

Biblical Usage

The priest looks again on the seventh day, and if the plague is somewhat dark and has not spread, it is but a scab, and the man is clean. But if the scab spreads much abroad after the cleansing, the priest sees it again, and if it spreads in the skin, he pronounces the man unclean. The word is the scab itself, and Leviticus watches it day by day.

Theological Significance

I believe the scab is a picture of sin in miniature: it looks small on the seventh day, and if it spreads, it is leprosy, and the priest must call it what it is. The priest must call it what it is, and leprosy by any other name still spreads.
