maṭṭeh

a branch (as extending); figuratively, a tribe; also a rod, whether for chastising (figuratively, correction), ruling (a sceptre), throwing (a lance), or walking (a staff; figuratively, a support of life, e.g. bread)

Biblical Usage

A rod is a branch, a staff, and a tribe, and the word covers all three in Israel's story. Moses' rod becomes a serpent before Pharaoh and opens the sea when it is lifted over the waters, and Aaron's rod buds and blossoms to prove whom God has chosen. The same word names the tribes of Israel, and the psalm promises a rod of strength out of Zion.

Theological Significance

I believe the rod is the picture of authority that comes from God and not from men: a shepherd's stick in Moses' hand did what Pharaoh's army could not. And a dead stick that buds is the sign of the priesthood itself — life where there should be none, because God chose it. I hold that the rod of strength out of Zion is Christ, ruling in the midst of His enemies.
