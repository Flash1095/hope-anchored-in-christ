gángraina

from γραίνω (to gnaw); an ulcer ("gangrene"):--canker.

Biblical Usage

Gangraina is gangrene, an eating ulcer, and Paul uses it once for false teaching in 2 Timothy 2:17. Their word will eat as a canker does, he says, of whom is Hymenaeus and Philetus. The word paints error as a wound that spreads through the body.

Theological Significance

I believe false teaching spreads like gangrene because it eats what is healthy; Paul names the men and the disease together, and I hold that the cure is the truth that cuts out the rot before it takes the limb.
