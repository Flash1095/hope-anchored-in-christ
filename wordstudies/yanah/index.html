yânâh

to rage or be violent; by implication, to suppress, to maltreat

Biblical Usage

Yânâh is the word for vexing and oppressing, for pressing down on someone who cannot fight back. God forbids His people to vex or oppress a stranger, because they were strangers in Egypt themselves, and the same command covers the servant who flees to Israel for shelter. Ezekiel describes the righteous man as one who has not oppressed anyone but has given bread to the hungry and covered the naked.

Theological Significance

I believe God stands with the oppressed because He was the stranger's God in Egypt, and He has not changed. My position is that how I treat the weakest person at my gate is the truest measure of my religion. And I hold that a righteous man is known not by his prayers alone but by the bread he gives and the coat he shares.
