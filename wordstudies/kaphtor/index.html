kaphtôr

a chaplet; but used only in an architectonic sense, i.e. the capital of a column, or a wreath-like button or disk on the candelabrum

Biblical Usage

Kaphtor is the chaplet — the knob or capital worked into the golden lampstand, a bud and a flower of beaten gold. The lampstand was made with its shaft and branches, its bowls and its knops and its flowers, all of one beaten work; and Amos sees the Lord standing upon the altar, commanding, smite the lintel of the door, that the posts may shake.

Theological Significance

I believe the knop on the lampstand and the lintel of the temple both say the same thing: God's house is made beautiful down to the smallest knob, and the same ornament that adorned the lampstand stood above the door where judgment fell. Beauty and judgment stood in the same house, and both were His.
