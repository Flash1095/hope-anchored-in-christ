hormáō

from G3730; to start, spur or urge on, i.e. (reflexively) to dash or plunge:--run (violently), rush.

Biblical Usage

I take hormáō to mean run (violently), rush, nothing more and nothing less. It occurs 5 times, and Matthew and Mark show it doing ordinary work in ordinary sentences. The Bible uses plain words plainly, and this is one of them.

Theological Significance

I hold that hormáō matters because rushing is what men do when they refuse to listen — the swine ran to their death, and the crowd ran at Stephen. My position is that Scripture does not hide its meaning from honest readers.
