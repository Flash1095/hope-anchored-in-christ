ʼashpôth

a heap of rubbish or filth

Biblical Usage

The dunghill is the heap of filth outside the city, and the Bible uses it for the lowest place a man can be, and the highest lift God gives. Hannah sings that He raiseth up the poor out of the dust, and lifteth up the beggar from the dunghill, to set them among princes; Nehemiah rebuilds the dung gate and the wall by it; and the psalm sings the same song, that He lifteth the needy out of the dunghill.

Theological Significance

I believe the dunghill is the gospel in one word: the poor are raised out of the dust, the beggar is lifted from the filth, and set among princes. God does not wait for men to climb out of the heap, He lifts them out of it Himself, and I hold that the dung gate Nehemiah builds is the gate we all come in by.
