nâchash

properly, to hiss, i.e. whisper a (magic) spell; generally, to prognosticate

Biblical Usage

Nachash as a verb means to practise divination or enchantment, to whisper a spell and watch for signs, and the law forbids it outright. In Genesis 30:27 Laban says he has learned by experience that the Lord blessed him for Jacob's sake, and in Genesis 44:5 Joseph's steward asks if his lord does not divine with the cup. In Leviticus 19:26 the people are told not to use enchantment nor observe times, and in Deuteronomy 18:10 no one among them shall use divination or be an observer of times.

Theological Significance

I believe divination is the sin of wanting to know the future without trusting the God who holds it. Laban learned by experience and called it wisdom, but the law calls it forbidden, and I hold that the difference matters. God's people do not need spells; they have His word.
