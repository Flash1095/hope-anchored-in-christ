hýsteros

comparative from G5259 (in the sense of behind); later:--latter.

Biblical Usage

The latter one is the one who comes last, and Jesus uses that order to turn expectations upside down. In Matthew 21:29 the son who first refused afterward repented and went, in Matthew 21:32 the publicans and harlots believed John before the chief priests did, in Matthew 21:37 the householder sends his own son last of all, and in Matthew 25:11 the foolish virgins come afterward saying, Lord, Lord, open to us.

Theological Significance

I hold that being last is no small thing in the kingdom: the son who repented late still went, and the door that shut late stayed shut. I want to be among those who answer while the door is still open.
