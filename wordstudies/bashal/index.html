bâshal

properly, to boil up; hence, to be done in cooking; figuratively to ripen

Biblical Usage

Bashal means to boil, to cook. It is the word for the Passover lamb that must be roasted, not boiled, for the kid that must not be boiled in its mother's milk, for the pot of pottage Elisha's servant sets before the sons of the prophets, and for the priests' servants who come with a fleshhook while the meat is boiling.

Theological Significance

I believe the cooking pot is where God's law meets the daily table, because He tells His people not only what to eat but how to cook it. The rules about boiling look small until you see what they guard, and I hold that a God who cares about the pot cares about the people who eat from it.
