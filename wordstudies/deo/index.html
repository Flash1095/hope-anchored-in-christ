déō

a primary verb; to bind (in various applications, literally or figuratively):--bind, be in bonds, knit, tie, wind. See also G1163, G1189.

Biblical Usage

To bind is to tie fast, and Scripture uses it for ropes and chains, for grave clothes, and for the dragon himself. Jesus gives His church the keys that bind and loose on earth, and Lazarus walks out of the tomb still bound hand and foot until Jesus says to loose him.

Theological Significance

I believe binding and loosing is authority given, not taken. What the church binds on earth is bound in heaven because Christ stands behind it, and the same power that bound death in the grave will one day bind the devil for good.
