hâthal

to deride; by implication, to cheat

Biblical Usage

Hathal means to mock, to cheat, to make a fool of someone, and it runs through the stories of deceivers and the deceived. In Genesis 31:7 Laban changed Jacob's wages ten times, in Judges 16:10 and 16:13 Delilah says Samson has mocked her and told her lies, and in 1 Kings 18:27 Elijah mocks the prophets of Baal when their god does not answer.

Theological Significance

I believe the word cuts both ways, and I need to know which side I am on: Laban's mockery was theft, Delilah's was a trap, and Elijah's was the truth told with a sharp edge. Mockery is only righteous when the lie is the thing being mocked.
