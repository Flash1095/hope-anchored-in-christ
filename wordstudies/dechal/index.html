dᵉchal

to slink, i.e. (by implication) to fear, or (causatively) be formidable

Biblical Usage

To fear, in the Aramaic of Daniel, is the dread that falls on kings and peoples before the God of heaven. Nebuchadnezzar's dream makes him afraid, Darius trembles when Daniel is thrown to the lions, and the beasts of the vision make Daniel himself shake. The word also names the formidable power that makes men afraid.

Theological Significance

I believe the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, and the fear of man is its ruin: Daniel feared God and did not fear the lions, and the kings who feared men lost their kingdoms. The kings who feared men fell, and the man who feared God stood, and the lions knew the difference.
