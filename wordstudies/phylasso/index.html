phylássō

probably from G5443 through the idea of isolation; to watch, i.e. be on guard (literally of figuratively); by implication, to preserve, obey, avoid:--beward, keep (self), observe, save. Compare G5083.

Biblical Usage

Phylasso means to keep, to guard, to watch over what has been entrusted to you. Jesus blesses those who hear the word of God and keep it in Luke 11:28, and He says He kept His disciples in the Father's name in John 17:12. Paul charges Timothy to keep the good thing committed to him in 2 Timothy 1:14, and Jude ends by praising the God who is able to keep us from falling in Jude 1:24.

Theological Significance

I believe keeping is a two-way promise: I keep His word, and He keeps me. My position is that obedience is not the price of His keeping — it is the proof of it.
