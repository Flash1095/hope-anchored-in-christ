agnoéō

from G1 (as a negative particle) and G3539; not to know (through lack of information or intelligence); by implication, to ignore (through disinclination):--(be) ignorant(-ly), not know, not understand, unknown.

Biblical Usage

Agnoeo is not to know — through lack of information, or through wilful ignorance. The disciples did not understand the saying about the resurrection and were afraid to ask; the Athenians worshipped in ignorance the God Paul proclaimed; Paul warns the Romans not to be ignorant of the mystery of Israel; and he says of the man who is ignorant, let him be ignorant. The word covers honest not-knowing and the kind that refuses to learn — and Scripture treats them very differently.

Theological Significance

What this word teaches me is that ignorance is not one thing. The disciples' fear and the Athenians' altar are met with patience and preaching — but the ignorance that refuses to know is left alone, and I hold that the difference is the willingness to ask.
