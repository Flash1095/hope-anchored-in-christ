patéō

from a derivative probably of G3817 (meaning a "path"); to trample (literally or figuratively):--tread (down, under foot).

Biblical Usage

Pateo means to tread, to trample under foot. Jesus gives His disciples power to tread on serpents and scorpions in Luke 10:19, and says Jerusalem shall be trodden down of the Gentiles in Luke 21:24. Revelation 11:2 says the holy city shall they tread under foot forty and two months, and Revelation 19:15 says Christ treads the winepress of the fierceness and wrath of Almighty God.

Theological Significance

I believe treading is the Bible's picture of victory: the serpent under the heel, the city under the nations, and the winepress under the King. My position is that the treading that matters most is His, because the victory He wins is the one His people walk in.
