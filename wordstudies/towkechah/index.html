tôwkêchâh

chastisement; figuratively (by words) correction, refutation, proof (even in defence)

Biblical Usage

Rebuke is the word for the day of trouble and the answer that waits for it. Hezekiah calls his day a day of trouble and of rebuke, Ezekiel says Jerusalem shall be an instruction and an astonishment by furious rebukes, Habakkuk waits to see what he shall answer when he is reproved, and Hosea says Ephraim shall be desolate in the day of rebuke.

Theological Significance

I hold that rebuke is God's mercy wearing a stern face: it is the correction that comes before the desolation, and the watchman who waits for his answer is the model for how to receive it. I believe a rebuke heard is better than a rebuke ignored, and the prophets are the proof.
