châmêts

ferment, (figuratively) extortion

Biblical Usage

Chamets is leaven, the fermented lump that makes bread rise, and the law is blunt about it: for seven days no leavened bread is to be eaten, and no leaven at all is to be burned in any offering made by fire. The Passover bread is unleavened because Israel left in haste, and Amos later turns the word back on a people who offer leavened thanksgiving while their hearts are far from God.

Theological Significance

I believe leaven is the Bible's picture of how a little spreads through the whole lump, for good or for ill. God commanded it out of the Passover house so that Israel would remember the haste of their deliverance. I hold that what I allow to ferment in my own life will not stay small, and that is why the command is so absolute.
