ʻâdan

to be soft or pleasant; figuratively and reflexively, to live voluptuously

Biblical Usage

To live softly, to delight oneself — adan appears once, in the Levites' great prayer: Israel took the fat land, "and became fat, and delighted themselves in thy great goodness." One word for the joy of the gift — and the prayer goes on to tell how they repaid it with rebellion.

Theological Significance

One rare word for delighting in God's goodness is a small peg, and the prayer it sits in remembers both the delight and the ingratitude that followed.
