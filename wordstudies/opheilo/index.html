opheílō

, or (in certain tenses), its prolonged form ὀφειλέω probably from the base of G3786 (through the idea of accruing); to owe (pecuniarily); figuratively, to be under obligation (ought, must, should); morally, to fail in duty:--behove, be bound, (be) debt(-or), (be) due(-ty), be guilty (indebted), (must) need(-s), ought, owe, should. See also G3785.

Biblical Usage

To owe is to be bound, and the word stretches from money to duty. Paul says children ought not to lay up for parents, but parents for children, and John says the one who claims to abide in Christ ought to walk as He walked, while Paul tells the Thessalonians that we are bound to thank God always for them.

Theological Significance

I hold that the Christian life is full of oughts, but they are not chains: owing love, owing thanks, owing a life that walks like Jesus — these are the debts that make me free. The heaviest of these debts is also the simplest: to love my neighbour as myself.
