katallássō

from G2596 and G236; to change mutually, i.e. (figuratively) to compound a difference:--reconcile.

Biblical Usage

To be reconciled is to have the difference compounded, and Paul says while we were enemies we were reconciled to God by the death of His Son, and now we are ambassadors begging men to be reconciled to God. The same word is used for a wife returning to her husband, because reconciliation always means coming back to the one you left. It is never God who moves first in anger, but God who moves first in grace.

Theological Significance

I believe I was reconciled while I was still an enemy, and that is the measure of His love. I hold that the ambassador's plea is the most urgent work in the world, because men are still at war with the God who has already made peace.
