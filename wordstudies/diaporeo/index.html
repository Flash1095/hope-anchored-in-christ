diaporéō

from G1223 and G639; to be thoroughly nonplussed:--(be in) doubt, be (much) perplexed.

Biblical Usage

To be perplexed is to be at a loss, standing before something you cannot explain. The crowd is perplexed at Pentecost, Herod is perplexed at the report of Jesus, the rulers doubt what the apostles' escape will come to, and Peter doubts what his vision means.

Theological Significance

I believe perplexity is often the beginning of understanding. The crowd at Pentecost did not know what to make of the sound, and the wondering that followed is what opened their ears to Peter's sermon.
