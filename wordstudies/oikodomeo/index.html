oikodoméō

from the same as G3619; to be a house-builder, i.e. construct or (figuratively) confirm:--(be in) build(-er, -ing, up), edify, embolden.

Biblical Usage

Oikodomeo means to build, to construct a house, or to build up a person. The wise man builds his house on the rock in Matthew 7:24, and a man builds a tower in his vineyard in Mark 12:1. The same word turns dark in Luke 4:29, where the crowd leads Jesus to the brow of the hill where their city was built, to cast Him down, and in John 2:20 the Jews say the temple was forty-six years in building.

Theological Significance

I believe building is the Bible's test of foundations: the wise man builds on the rock, and the foolish on the sand. My position is that the temple the Jews took forty-six years to build was the shadow, and Christ is the true temple raised in three days.
