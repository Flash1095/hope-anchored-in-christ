metáthesis

from G3346; transposition, i.e. transferral (to heaven), disestablishment (of a law):--change, removing, translation.

Biblical Usage

Hebrews uses this word three times for three great changes: the priesthood being changed brings a change of the law, Enoch was translated so he did not see death, and the shaking removes what can be shaken so the unshakable remains. Removal and transfer are the same word, and Hebrews makes them the shape of God's new covenant.

Theological Significance

I believe Hebrews is telling us that God removes what is temporary on purpose: the old priesthood gives way, Enoch is taken, the shaken things fall, and what cannot be shaken — His kingdom — is what remains. What cannot be shaken is what remains, and that is the kingdom itself.
