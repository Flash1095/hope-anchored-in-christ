eikē

probably from G1502 (through the idea of failure); idly, i.e. without reason (or effect):--without a cause, (in) vain(-ly).

Biblical Usage

This word means idly, without reason, or in vain, and the Greek text spells it with a long vowel at the end, where Strong's lists it with a short one. Paul says the ruler does not bear the sword in vain, tells the Corinthians they are saved by the gospel if they keep it in memory, unless they believed in vain, and asks the Galatians whether they suffered so many things in vain. The word asks whether anything we do has a reason behind it.

Theological Significance

I believe nothing God does is in vain, and my suffering is not wasted when it is His hand that allows it.
