machăneh

an encampment (of travellers or troops); hence, an army, whether literal (of soldiers) or figurative (of dancers, angels, cattle, locusts, stars; or even the sacred courts)

Biblical Usage

The word names a camp, an encampment of travellers or troops, and it is one of the Bible's great words. Jacob saw the host of God and called the place Mahanaim, the angel of God went before the camp of Israel at the Red Sea, and the tribes pitched their camps around the tabernacle in order. Gideon crept down to the camp of Midian by night, and the camp of the Syrians was struck by the angel of the LORD.

Theological Significance

I believe the camp is the picture of God's people on the move, because Israel never built a city in the wilderness, only a camp. I hold that the God who camps with His people in the desert is the same God who pitches His tent among us now, and the camp is holy because He is in it.
