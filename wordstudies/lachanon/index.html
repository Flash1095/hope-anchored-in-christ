láchanon

from λαχαίνω (to dig); a vegetable:--herb.

Biblical Usage

Herbs are the garden plants, and the word appears in the parable and in the rebuke. In Mark 4:32 the mustard seed "becometh greater than all herbs," and in Luke 11:42 Jesus rebukes the Pharisees, who "tithe mint and rue and all manner of herbs, and pass over judgment and the love of God."

Theological Significance

What I take from this is that this herbs preach the same sermon twice: the kingdom grows from the smallest seed to the greatest of herbs, and the Pharisees measured the smallest herb and missed the love of God.
