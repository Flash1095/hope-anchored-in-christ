châram

to seclude; specifically (by a ban) to devote to religious uses (especially destruction); physical and reflexive, to be blunt as to the nose

Biblical Usage

Charam is devoting to destruction under the ban, and it is the hardest word in the Old Testament. God commanded Saul to smite Amalek and utterly destroy all that they have, and then sent him to utterly destroy the sinners the Amalekites. Solomon laid tribute on the Canaanites Israel could not utterly destroy, and the Simeonites smote the tents of Ham and destroyed them utterly in the days of Hezekiah.

Theological Significance

I believe this word must be read with its whole weight, not softened away: God devoted Amalek to destruction, and Saul's failure to do it cost him the kingdom. I hold that the ban belongs to God's judgment, not to man's anger, and the difference is everything. I do not get to devote what God has not devoted.
