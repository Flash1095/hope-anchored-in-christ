Gáïos

of Latin origin; Gaius (i.e. Caius), a Christian:--Gaius.

Biblical Usage

Gaius is a name carried by four different men in the New Testament, and three of them are named here. Gaius of Macedonia was seized by the Ephesian mob with Paul in Acts 19:29, Gaius of Corinth was Paul's host and host of the whole church in Romans 16:23, and the wellbeloved Gaius received John's third letter in 3 John 1:1. One name, four men, each known for standing close to an apostle.

Theological Significance

I believe the four men named Gaius show how the gospel makes strangers into hosts and friends. A Macedonian seized in a riot, a Corinthian who opened his house, and a beloved brother who walked in the truth — I hold that hospitality and truth are the marks of the name.
