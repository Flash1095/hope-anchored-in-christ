katageláō

to laugh down, i.e. deride:--laugh to scorn.

Biblical Usage

They laughed Jesus to scorn when He said the damsel was not dead but sleeping, and the same laughter met Him in every house where grief had already decided how the story ended. The mourners knew death when they saw it, and they were sure He was wrong. Then He took her by the hand, and the laughter stopped.

Theological Significance

I believe the world's scorn is often just grief that has given up, and Jesus walks into that room anyway. I hold that the One they laughed at is the One who gets the last word over death, and the scoffers always go quiet when He speaks.
