aḗr

from ἄημι (to breathe unconsciously, i.e. respire; by analogy, to blow); "air" (as naturally circumambient):--air. Compare G5594.

Biblical Usage

Aer is the air — the dust the crowd throws into the air, the air into which unintelligible speech is spoken, the realm of the prince of the power of the air, and the air where the saints will meet the Lord at His coming. The word moves from the ordinary sky to the spiritual battleground to the meeting place of the church.

Theological Significance

I believe this word reminds me that the air is not empty. An enemy rules there for now, but the Lord will come through it, and His people will meet Him in it — so the same air that carries dust and empty words will carry my hope.
