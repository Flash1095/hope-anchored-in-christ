eán

from G1487 and G302; a conditional particle; in case that, provided, etc.; often used in connection with other particles to denote indefiniteness or uncertainty:--before, but, except, (and) if, (if) so, (what-, whither-)soever, though, when (-soever), whether (or), to whom, (who-)so(-ever). See G3361.

Biblical Usage

If, the great conditional word of the New Testament, the hinge on which the promises turn. If you forgive men their trespasses, your Father will forgive you, and if you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus, you shall be saved. If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us, and if I have all faith but have not charity, I am nothing.

Theological Significance

I believe the ifs of Scripture are not God's uncertainty but my responsibility, because His promises are sure and my part is to meet the condition. The if does not make God doubtful; it makes me answerable, and I answer by believing.
