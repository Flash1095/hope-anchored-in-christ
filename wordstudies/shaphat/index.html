shâphaṭ

to judge, i.e. pronounce sentence (for or against); by implication, to vindicate or punish; by extenssion, to govern; passively, to litigate (literally or figuratively)

Biblical Usage

Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right? Abraham asked it of God before Sodom, and Moses sat to judge the people from morning unto evening. The LORD raised up judges who delivered Israel out of the hand of those that spoiled them, Isaiah promises that with righteousness He shall judge the poor, and the psalm says God standeth in the congregation of the mighty and judgeth among the gods.

Theological Significance

I hold that the Judge of all the earth must do right, because His own name is at stake in every judgment. Moses sat all day to judge because God's people needed a judge they could reach, and Isaiah's promise is that the poor will finally be judged with righteousness. My position is that I can trust a judgment I cannot yet see, because the Judge is righteous and His verdicts deliver His people.
