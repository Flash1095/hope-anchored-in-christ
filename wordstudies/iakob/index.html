Iakṓb

of Hebrew origin (H3290); Jacob (i.e. Ja`akob), the progenitor of the Israelites:--also an Israelite:--Jacob.

Biblical Usage

Jacob is the patriarch whose name became a nation. Matthew 1:2 places him in the line of Christ, Acts 7:8 tells how God gave him the covenant of circumcision and he begat the twelve patriarchs, and Hebrews 11:9 names him among the heirs of the same promise, dwelling in tabernacles with Abraham and Isaac by faith.

Theological Significance

I hold that Jacob's life is proof God keeps his promises to unlikely people: the younger son who grabbed at the blessing became the father of Israel.
