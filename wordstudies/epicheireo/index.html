epicheiréō

from G1909 and G5495; to put the hand upon, i.e. undertake:--go about, take in hand (upon).

Biblical Usage

Epicheireo means to take in hand, to undertake — to put the hand to a work. Many have taken in hand to set forth the gospel in order; Paul disputes with the Grecians, who go about to slay him; and the vagabond Jews take upon them to call over the name of the Lord Jesus.

Theological Significance

Luke's careful history, Paul's bold dispute, and the exorcists' empty imitation all took something in hand, but the authority was not the same. I believe the word separates the three undertakings: the hand is the same, and only the Name makes the difference.
