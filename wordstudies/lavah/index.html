lâvâh

properly, to twine, i.e. (by implication) to unite, to remain; also to borrow (as a form of obligation) or (caus.) to lend

Biblical Usage

lâvâh means to twine, and it is one of those words that quietly holds a whole theme together. In Deuteronomy 28:44, "and thou shalt not lend to him: he shall be the head"; and in Exodus 22:25, "If thou lend money to [any of] my people [that is] poor by thee". In Esther 9:27, "and upon all such as joined themselves unto them"; in Daniel 11:34, "they shall be holpen with a little help: but many shall cleave to them with flatteries."; and in Zechariah 2:11, "And many nations shall be joined to the LORD in that day".

Theological Significance

I believe joining is a word with two edges — Ruth joined herself to Israel, and a borrower joins himself to a lender. I hold that God warned Israel about both: join the right people, and never let debt make you a servant. My position is that I want to be joined to God's people and free of every other master.
