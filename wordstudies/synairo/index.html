synaírō

from G4862 and G142; to make up together, i.e. (figuratively) to compute (an account):--reckon, take.

Biblical Usage

In the parable of the talents the lord comes after a long time and reckons with his servants, and the kingdom of heaven is like a king who would take account of his servants. The reckoning is not a surprise inspection but the settling of accounts after a long trust, and every servant must show what he did with what was given.

Theological Significance

I believe a reckoning is coming after a long trust, and I hold that I should live now as one who will give account gladly.
