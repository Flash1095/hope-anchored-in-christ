paraitéomai

from G3844 and the middle voice of G154; to beg off, i.e. deprecate, decline, shun:--avoid, (make) excuse, intreat, refuse, reject.

Biblical Usage

Paraiteomai means to beg off, to refuse, to make excuse. The guests at the great supper all begin to make excuse — I have bought a piece of ground, I have bought five yoke of oxen — and Hebrews warns, See that ye refuse not Him that speaketh.

Theological Significance

I hold that excuses are refusals with good manners: the ground and the oxen sound reasonable, but they keep a man from the supper, and refusing Him who speaks from heaven has no escape.
