ainéō

from G136; to praise (God):--praise.

Biblical Usage

The angels praise God over Bethlehem's fields, the shepherds go back glorifying and praising God, the crowds praise Him as Jesus rides into Jerusalem, and the lame man who has just been healed walks into the temple leaping, and praising God. Every use is praise that bursts out — angels, shepherds, crowds, and one healed man.

Theological Significance

Praise here is never polite applause; it is the sound of people who have just seen God act. I believe that is the pattern for us too: praise is the right answer to a God who shows up, whether in a manger, on a road, or in a healed pair of legs.
