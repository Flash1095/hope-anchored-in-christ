migdâl

a tower (from its size or height); by analogy, a rostrum; figuratively, a (pyramidal) bed of flowers

Biblical Usage

Men built a city and a tower whose top would reach to heaven, and the Lord came down to see the city and the tower. Gideon threatened to break down the tower of Penuel, the psalmist tells Zion to count her towers, and the name of the Lord is a strong tower the righteous run into and are safe. The word is the tower itself, and it stands for man's pride and God's refuge at once.

Theological Significance

I hold that the two towers in this word are the choice every man faces: Babel's tower, built to make a name, and the name of the Lord, the strong tower where the righteous run and are safe. One tower fell by confusion, the other stands forever, and every man must choose his tower.
