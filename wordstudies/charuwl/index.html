chârûwl

properly, pointed, i.e. a bramble or other thorny weed

Biblical Usage

Charuwl is the bramble, the thorny weed, the nettle, and it grows where men are broken or judged. The outcasts bray among the bushes and gather under the nettles, the sluggard's field is grown over with thorns and nettles have covered it, and Moab shall become the breeding of nettles and saltpits and a perpetual desolation.

Theological Significance

I believe nettles are the Bible's picture of neglect: a field left alone grows thorns, and a nation left to itself becomes a nettle-bed.
