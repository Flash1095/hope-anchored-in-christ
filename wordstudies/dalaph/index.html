dâlaph

to drip; by implication, to weep

Biblical Usage

The word pictures water dripping through a roof and a heart weeping itself away. The Preacher warns that by slothfulness the building decays and the roof drips, Job's friends weep for him, and the psalmist's soul melts for heaviness. Drip by drip, neglect and sorrow wear a house down.

Theological Significance

I believe small leaks sink great houses, and the verse warns us to mend the roof of our lives before the rain comes through. The drip is slow, but it never stops, and the house that ignores it will one day find the roof gone.
