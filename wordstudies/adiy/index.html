ʻădîy

finery; generally an outfit; specifically, a headstall

Biblical Usage

Adiy is finery — the ornaments and jewellery people wore, and the word carries both beauty and danger. At Sinai the people stripped off their ornaments in mourning after the golden calf; David's lament remembers the daughters of Israel clothed in scarlet with ornaments of gold; Isaiah promises Zion will wear her children as ornaments; and Jeremiah asks how a bride can forget her ornaments — or a virgin her attire — yet Israel forgot God.

Theological Significance

This word does a small, honest job in Scripture, and I won't dress it up as a doctrine — the verse it sits in carries the meaning; in this case, the verse is Exodus 33:4.
