maʻăbâr

a crossing-place (of a river, a ford; of a mountain, a pass); abstractly, a transit, i.e. (figuratively) overwhelming

Biblical Usage

The word names a crossing place, a ford where a river can be passed or a pass through the mountains, and it marks the places where journeys turn. Jacob passed over the ford of Jabbok on the night he wrestled with God, the men of Gilead held the passages of Jordan against Ephraim, and the spies' pursuers went out after them to the fords.

Theological Significance

I believe the crossing places of Scripture are where God meets His people between one life and the next, and no one crosses alone. I hold that the ford is a picture of every hard passage in life: God goes before us through the water, and what waits on the other side is His promise.
