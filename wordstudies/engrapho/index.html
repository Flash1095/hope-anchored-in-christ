engráphō

from G1722 and G1125; to "engrave", i.e. inscribe:--write (in).

Biblical Usage

To engrave, to inscribe. Paul tells the Corinthians they are his epistle written in his heart in 2 Corinthians 3:2, and the epistle of Christ written not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God, not on stone but on the heart, in 2 Corinthians 3:3. Jesus tells His disciples to rejoice because their names are written in heaven in Luke 10:20.

Theological Significance

I believe God writes in two places — His Spirit on my heart and my name in heaven — and both writings are permanent. I hold that the heart is the only tablet that matters, because stone breaks and ink fades, but what the Spirit writes stays.
