epitássō

from G1909 and G5021; to arrange upon, i.e. order:--charge, command, injoin.

Biblical Usage

Epitasso means to command, to charge, to arrange upon. The people are amazed: with authority He commands even the unclean spirits, and they obey Him; He commands the winds and water, and they cease — yet Paul, who might boldly enjoin Philemon, chooses for love's sake to beseech instead.

Theological Significance

Demons obey, winds obey — and the apostle who could command chooses to beseech, because love outranks command. I believe the authority is the point, and the restraint is the greater point: power that could compel, choosing to persuade.
