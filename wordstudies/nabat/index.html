nâbaṭ

to scan, i.e. look intently at; by implication, to regard with pleasure, favor or care

Biblical Usage

Nabat means to look intently, to scan or regard with care, and it is used for God looking down and for men looking up. In Genesis 15:5 the Lord tells Abram to look toward heaven and count the stars, and in Exodus 3:6 Moses hides his face because he is afraid to look upon God. In Psalm 33:13 the Lord looketh from heaven and beholds all the sons of men, and in Isaiah 5:12 the people regard not the work of the Lord.

Theological Significance

I believe looking is never neutral in Scripture, because what we look at shapes what we become. Abram looked at the stars and believed a promise, Moses feared to look at God and was sent, and the Lord looks at all of us and sees truly. I hold that I want my looking to be the kind that notices the work of the Lord.
