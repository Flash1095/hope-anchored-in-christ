ʻâlam

to veil from sight, i.e. conceal (literally or figuratively)

Biblical Usage

To hide, to veil from sight, and the Bible uses it both ways. The law forbids hiding yourself from your brother's lost ox, and commands you not to look away. Isaiah turns it on God: when your hands are full of blood, He hides His eyes and will not hear. And the psalm knows the other side, that our secret sins stand in the light of His countenance, hidden from everyone but Him.

Theological Significance

I believe hiding is the oldest human instinct after the fall, and God answers it both ways: He will not be hidden from, and He will not hide from the one who comes honestly. The same God who hides His face from bloodied hands is the one who sees the secret sin and still makes a way back, and I hold that is why confession works — nothing I hide is hidden from Him, and nothing I bring Him is turned away.
