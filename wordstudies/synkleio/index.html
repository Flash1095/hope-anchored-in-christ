synkleíō

from G4862 and G2808; to shut together, i.e. include or (figuratively) embrace in a common subjection to:--conclude, inclose, shut up.

Biblical Usage

God has concluded them all in unbelief, that He might have mercy upon all, and the Scripture has concluded all under sin, that the promise by faith of Jesus Christ might be given to them that believe. Before faith came, we were kept under the law, shut up unto the faith which should afterwards be revealed, and the same shutting together fills a net with fishes until it breaks. The concluding is a shutting in, and God shuts us in so that mercy can find us with nowhere else to go.

Theological Significance

What grips me is that God shuts all men up under sin so that mercy is the only door left, and I hold that this is good news, because the same shutting that condemns is the shutting that saves. I take the net as my picture: the fishes are inclosed, the net breaks, and still the catch is His.
