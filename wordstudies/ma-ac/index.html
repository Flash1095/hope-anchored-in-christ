mâʼaç

to spurn; also (intransitively) to disappear

Biblical Usage

The word means to spurn or reject, and it is one of Scripture's saddest words, because it is used of God being rejected and of His people rejecting Him. When Israel asked for a king, God told Samuel they had not rejected him but had rejected God from reigning over them, and Samuel told Saul that because he had rejected the word of the LORD, the LORD had rejected him from being king. Hosea says God's people rejected knowledge, and the psalm says the stone the builders refused has become the head stone of the corner.

Theological Significance

I believe rejection is the heart of sin: we do not only break God's law, we refuse God Himself. I hold that the stone the builders rejected became the cornerstone, so God's answer to our rejection is not to give up on us but to build His house on the One we threw away.
