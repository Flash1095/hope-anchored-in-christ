chará

from G5463; cheerfulness, i.e. calm delight:--gladness, X greatly, (X be exceeding) joy(-ful, -fully, -fulness, -ous).

Biblical Usage

Chara is joy, and the New Testament ties it to fellowship and to faith that holds. John writes so that our joy may be full, Peter says believers rejoice with joy unspeakable and full of glory though they do not see Christ, Paul tells the Thessalonians they are his glory and joy, and he calls himself a helper of the Corinthians' joy, not a lord over their faith.

Theological Significance

I believe joy in the New Testament is never a private mood but a shared possession: John's joy is full when his readers walk in the light, and Paul's joy is a church standing firm. I hold that joy is the fruit of faith, not its precondition, and it grows precisely where it is shared.
