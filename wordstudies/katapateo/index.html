katapatéō

from G2596 and G3961; to trample down; figuratively, to reject with disdain:--trample, tread (down, underfoot).

Biblical Usage

To trample down is to walk over what is precious, and Jesus says salt that has lost its savour is trodden under foot, pearls are not to be cast before swine lest they trample them, and the seed fell by the wayside and was trodden down. Hebrews warns of the one who has trodden under foot the Son of God and counted His blood unholy. The word is a picture of contempt made visible.

Theological Significance

I believe trampling is what contempt looks like when it puts on boots, and the Son of God will not be walked over forever. I hold that the blood they count common is the blood that saves, and the day is coming when the tramplers will wish they had knelt instead.
