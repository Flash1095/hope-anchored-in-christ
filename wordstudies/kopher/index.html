kôpher

properly, a cover, i.e. (literally) a village (as covered in); (specifically) bitumen (as used for coating), and the henna plant (as used for dyeing); figuratively, a redemption-price

Biblical Usage

Kopher is a cover, and the word carries three senses: pitch, a village, and a ransom. Genesis 6:14 tells Noah to pitch the ark within and without with pitch, 1 Samuel 6:18 counts the country villages of the Philistines, Exodus 21:30 lays a sum of money for the ransom of a man's life, Numbers 35:31 forbids taking any satisfaction for the life of a murderer, Job 33:24 says, deliver him from going down to the pit, I have found a ransom, and Psalms 49:7 says none can give to God a ransom for his brother.

Theological Significance

I believe pitch, village, and ransom are one truth: the pitch covers the ark, the ransom covers the life, and the price no man can pay for his brother is the price God paid Himself. I hold that Psalms 49:7 is the question and Job 33:24 is the answer, because the ransom is found, not bought.
