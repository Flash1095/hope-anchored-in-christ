gennáō

from a variation of G1085; to procreate (properly, of the father, but by extension of the mother); figuratively, to regenerate:--bear, beget, be born, bring forth, conceive, be delivered of, gender, make, spring.

Biblical Usage

I take gennáō to mean bear, beget, be born, bring forth, conceive, be delivered of, gender, make, spring, nothing more and nothing less. It occurs 65 times, and Matthew and Mark show it doing ordinary work in ordinary sentences. The Bible uses plain words plainly, and this is one of them.

Theological Significance

gennáō matters to me because new birth is God’s work from first to last. My position is that Scripture does not hide its meaning from honest readers.
