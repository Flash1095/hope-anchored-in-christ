phrýganon

neuter of a presumed derivative of φρύγω (to roast or parch; akin to the base of G5395); something desiccated, i.e. a dry twig:--stick.

Biblical Usage

Phryganon is a stick, a bundle of dry sticks for a fire, and it appears once, on Malta. Paul gathers a bundle of sticks and lays them on the fire, and a viper comes out of the heat and fastens on his hand.

Theological Significance

I hold that the bundle of sticks is Paul serving others after a shipwreck: the apostle gathers the firewood himself, and the serpent that bites him cannot kill him.
