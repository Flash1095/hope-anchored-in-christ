kenós

apparently a primary word; empty (literally or figuratively):--empty, (in) vain.

Biblical Usage

Kenos means empty, vain, to no purpose: 1 Corinthians 15:10 says his grace which was bestowed upon me was not in vain. 1 Corinthians 15:14 says if Christ be not risen, then is our preaching vain, and your faith is also vain. Ephesians 5:6 says let no man deceive you with vain words. Philippians 2:16 says hold forth the word of life, that I have not run in vain, neither laboured in vain.

Theological Significance

I believe empty is the word that decides everything: if Christ is not risen, our preaching is vain, and if he is, no labour in him is vain. I hold that grace was not bestowed on Paul in vain, and it is not bestowed on me in vain either.
