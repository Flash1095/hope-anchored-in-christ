maʻăleh

an elevation, i.e. (concretely) acclivity or platform; abstractly (the relation or state) a rise or (figuratively) priority

Biblical Usage

The word names an ascent, a going up, and it is used for the steep ways of the land and for the going up to God's house. David went up the ascent of the Mount of Olives weeping, with his head covered, when he fled from Absalom, and Saul met young women going up to the city to draw water, who told him the seer was just ahead. Isaiah pictures the fugitives of Moab going up to Luhith with weeping, and Nehemiah's procession went up the stairs of the city of David.

Theological Significance

I believe the ascents of Scripture are climbed weeping as often as singing, and God walks the steep road with His people. I hold that David's weeping ascent and the singers' going up are the same road, and both end in the house of the LORD.
