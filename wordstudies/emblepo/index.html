emblépō

from G1722 and G991; to look on, i.e. (relatively) to observe fixedly, or (absolutely) to discern clearly:--behold, gaze up, look upon, (could) see.

Biblical Usage

The word emblépō carries the sense of to look intently at. In Matthew 6:26, the text says "Behold the fowls of the air." In Mark 8:25, the text says "After that he put [his] hands again upon his eyes."

Theological Significance

I believe emblépō shows us that faith is not a feeling but a holding-on — trusting what God has said when nothing else makes sense. That shapes how I read the passages where it appears: not as filler, but as God's own wording for what He is doing.
