râkak

to soften (intransitively or transitively), used figuratively

Biblical Usage

Rakak is to soften — the heart melting, the words going down smooth. Josiah's heart was tender, and he humbled himself and wept before the LORD; God told Ahaz, 'Take heed, and be quiet; fear not, neither be fainthearted for the two tails of these smoking firebrands'; and the psalm says the words of his mouth 'were softer than oil, yet were they drawn swords.'

Theological Significance

What I see here is that softness cuts both ways: a tender heart hears God, and smooth words can hide a sword — and the difference is whether the softening is before God or before men.
