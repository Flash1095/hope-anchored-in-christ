katḗgoros

from G2596 and G58; against one in the assembly, i.e. a complainant at law; specially, Satan:--accuser.

Biblical Usage

The accuser is the plaintiff who stands against a man in the assembly, and Paul's accusers were told to come to Caesarea and say what they had against him, and Festus said it was not the Roman manner to deliver any man to die before the accused had his accusers face to face. The word is the courtroom word for the one who brings the charge, and Paul met his accusers again and again.

Theological Significance

I believe the Roman rule was just, because a man should face his accusers and answer them. I hold that Paul's courage before his accusers is the pattern for mine, because the truth does not fear a hearing.
