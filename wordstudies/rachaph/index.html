râchaph

to brood; by implication, to be relaxed

Biblical Usage

Rachap is to brood, to hover over — as a bird over its young. The Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters at creation; Moses sang that the LORD, 'as an eagle stirreth up her nest, fluttereth over her young, spreadeth abroad her wings, taketh them, beareth them on her wings'; and Jeremiah says his bones shake, his heart is broken within him.

Theological Significance

I take it that the Spirit still broods: He hovered over the waters before there was light, and He hovers over His people now, warming what is cold and formless into life. And my position is that the eagle's flutter is the same love — God does not only watch from above, He spreads His wings and carries.
