shâbaʻ

to seven oneself, i.e. swear (as if by repeating a declaration seven times)

Biblical Usage

Shaba' is swearing, sevening oneself, and the oath binds both God and men. David swore that Solomon should reign after him, and Bathsheba held him to it. The LORD swore unto the house of Eli that its iniquity shall not be purged with sacrifice nor offering for ever. And all Judah sware unto the LORD with a loud voice, and with shouting, and with trumpets — and all Judah rejoiced at the oath, for they had sworn with all their heart, and sought Him with their whole desire, and He was found of them.

Theological Significance

I believe an oath is only as good as the heart behind it: David's oath stood, God's oath against Eli stood, and Judah's oath brought rest because it was sworn with the whole heart. An oath sworn lightly is a snare, and an oath sworn truly is a joy. And I hold that God's own swearing is the surest thing in the Bible — when He swears, it is done.
