syntēréō

from G4862 and G5083; to keep closely together, i.e. (by implication) to conserve (from ruin); mentally, to remember (and obey):--keep, observe, preserve.

Biblical Usage

Mary keeps all these things and ponders them in her heart, and Herod fears John, knowing he is a just man and holy, and observes him, and when he hears him he does many things and hears him gladly. Men put new wine into new bottles, and both are preserved. Keeping can be a mother's treasuring, a king's watching, or a wineskin's holding, and in each case what is kept is kept safe together.

Theological Significance

I believe Mary kept the right things in her heart, and I hold that treasuring God's words is the way to keep them. I take the wineskin as my warning too: new wine needs new bottles, and I must be a vessel that preserves what God pours in.
