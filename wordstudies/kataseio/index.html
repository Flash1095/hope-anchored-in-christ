kataseíō

from G2596 and G4579; to sway downward, i.e. make a signal:--beckon.

Biblical Usage

To beckon is to sway the hand downward, and Peter beckoned to the house of Mary to hold their peace, and Paul beckoned with his hand in the synagogue and began to preach, and Alexander beckoned to make his defence, and Paul stood on the stairs and beckoned to the people. The word is the gesture before the speech, the hand that asks for silence so the voice can be heard.

Theological Significance

I believe the beckon matters, because the preacher asks for quiet before he speaks of Christ. I hold that Peter's beckon from prison's door and Paul's beckon in the synagogue are the same gesture: listen, because what comes next is the gospel.
