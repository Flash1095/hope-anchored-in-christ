nᵉzaq

to suffer (causatively, inflict) loss

Biblical Usage

Loss in Ezra is the king's loss: the enemies warn that a rebuilt Jerusalem will endamage the king's revenue, that the city is hurtful unto kings, and that damage will grow to the hurt of the kings. Daniel is set over the princes so the king should have no damage.

Theological Significance

I believe the enemies' argument is always the same — the king's purse — and God answers it by putting Daniel where no loss can touch the throne. The enemies counted every shekel of damage, and Daniel was set over the accounts so the king would lose nothing.
