tâphêl

plaster (as gummy) or slime; (figuratively) frivolity

Biblical Usage

The word is tasteless, unsavoury, foolish, whitewash that hides a weak wall. Job asks whether there is any taste in the white of an egg, and whether his words can be eaten without salt. Ezekiel says the false prophets daubed the wall with untempered mortar, and Lamentations says the prophets saw false and foolish things for Jerusalem.

Theological Significance

I hold that foolish words are like whitewash on a cracked wall: they cover nothing, and the rain will find them out.
