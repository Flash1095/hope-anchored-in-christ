lâmad

properly, to goad, i.e. (by implication) to teach (the rod being an Oriental incentive)

Biblical Usage

To teach or to learn is the verb of the rod and the classroom, used eighty times for instructing in war, in song, and in the law. In Deuteronomy 4:1 Moses says, "hearken, O Israel, unto the statutes and unto the judgments, which I teach you," in Psalms 25:4 David prays, "Teach me thy paths," and the title of Psalms 60 says the psalm was written "to teach."

Theological Significance

What convinces me is that teaching in Scripture is never only information: Moses teaches statutes to be done, David asks to be taught the paths to walk, and the psalm itself was written to teach, because the truth must be learned to be lived. I want to be taught that way: statutes to do, and paths to walk.
