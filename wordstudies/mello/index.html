méllō

a strengthened form of G3199 (through the idea of expectation); to intend, i.e. be about to be, do, or suffer something (of persons or things, especially events; in the sense of purpose, duty, necessity, probability, possibility, or hesitation):--about, after that, be (almost), (that which is, things, + which was for) to come, intend, was to (be), mean, mind, be at the point, (be) ready, + return, shall (begin), (which, that) should (after, afterwards, hereafter) tarry, which was for, will, would, be yet.

Biblical Usage

Mello means to be about to do a thing, to intend it, to have it coming. 1 Corinthians 3:22 counts things to come among all the things that are yours in Christ, and 1 Peter 5:1 looks to the glory that shall be revealed. 1 Thessalonians 3:4 recalls that Paul told the church beforehand that they should suffer tribulation, and 1 Timothy 1:16 says Christ showed longsuffering in Paul as a pattern to them which should hereafter believe.

Theological Significance

I believe 'about to' is the tense of hope and of warning together: glory is about to be revealed, and suffering is about to come, and both are certain. My position is that the Christian lives between two certainties, and the word mello names them both.
