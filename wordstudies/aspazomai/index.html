aspázomai

from G1 (as a particle of union) and a presumed form of G4685; to enfold in the arms, i.e. (by implication) to salute, (figuratively) to welcome:--embrace, greet, salute, take leave.

Biblical Usage

To greet, in the New Testament, is to embrace, to welcome, and to take leave, all in one word. Paul ends his letters with a long roll of greetings by name, the believers of old greeted the promises from afar and embraced them, and John closes his short letter hoping to greet his friends face to face.

Theological Significance

I believe the greetings at the ends of the letters are not filler, they are the church being the church, naming names and holding people close. Faith greets the promises before it ever holds them, and I hold that heaven will feel like that final greeting, face to face at last.
