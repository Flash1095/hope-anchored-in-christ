ʼâdôwn

sovereign, i.e. controller (human or divine)

Biblical Usage

Adown is 'lord' or 'master' — the word Sarah uses of Abraham, the word for the master a servant may choose to serve forever, and the word the psalmist uses when the LORD says to 'my Lord,' sit at My right hand. It covers human masters and the divine Master alike, and the difference is always in who is speaking and who is being addressed.

Theological Significance

I hold that adown matters because it teaches me what lordship means: a master who is obeyed, trusted, and loved. And when David's Lord is seated at God's right hand, I know my Master is not only above me but for me — ruling until every enemy is under His feet.
