yôwnath ʼêlem rᵉchôqîym

dove of (the) silence (i.e. dumb Israel) of (i.e. among) distances (i.e. strangers); the title of a ditty (used for a name of its melody)

Biblical Usage

This entry is not a word used in a verse at all: it is the title of Psalm 56. The Hebrew title reads "upon Jonath-elem-rechokim," the dove of the silence of distant places, and its words are the dove of H3123, the silence of H482, and the distant ones of H7350, all sitting in the title verse of the psalm. The psalm itself begins, in Psalms 56:1, "Be merciful unto me, O God: for man would swallow me up."

Theological Significance

I believe God gave this psalm a title that is itself a picture, a dove, silent and far from home, which is David hiding from the Philistines in Gath, and I hold that the title belongs in the study even though the phrase never occurs in a verse.
