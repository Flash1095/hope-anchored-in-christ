mystḗrion

from a derivative of μύω (to shut the mouth); a secret or "mystery" (through the idea of silence imposed by initiation into religious rites):--mystery.

Biblical Usage

Mysterion is a secret that has been kept hidden and is now made known by God, and the New Testament uses it for the deepest things of the faith. Jesus says the mysteries of the kingdom are given to His disciples in Matthew 13:11, and Paul writes of the mystery of His will made known to us in Ephesians 1:9. The wisdom of God in a mystery is spoken in 1 Corinthians 2:7, and the mystery of Christ is revealed to Paul in Ephesians 3:3.

Theological Significance

I believe a mystery in Scripture is not a puzzle God hides from us, but a truth He kept until the right time to tell it. The cross, the church, the calling of the Gentiles — all of it was hidden in God and is now spoken plainly in Christ. That is why I never treat the gospel as my discovery; I treat it as His gift, revealed.
