apodēméō

from G590; to go abroad, i.e. visit a foreign land:--go (travel) into a far country, journey.

Biblical Usage

To go abroad, to travel into a far country — and in Jesus' parables it is always the master who leaves. The younger son takes his journey into a far country in Luke 15:13 and wastes his substance with riotous living; the householder plants a vineyard, lets it out, and goes into a far country in Matthew 21:33 and Mark 12:1; and the man travelling into a far country in Matthew 25:14 entrusts his goods to his servants.

Theological Significance

I believe the far country is where the story is decided. The master is away, the servants are unsupervised, and what they do with what was left in their hands is the whole test. The son squanders, the husbandmen rebel, the servants trade — and the master always comes back to settle accounts. I live in the far country now, and the question is what I am doing with what He left me.
