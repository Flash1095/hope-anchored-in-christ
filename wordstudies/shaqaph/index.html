shâqaph

properly, to lean out (of a window), i.e. (by implication) peep or gaze (passively, be a spectacle)

Biblical Usage

To look down from heaven is God's posture toward men, and to look out at a window is man's posture toward the world. The LORD looked unto the host of the Egyptians through the pillar of fire and of the cloud, and the psalm says the LORD looked down from heaven upon the children of men, to see if any did understand. Michal looked through a window and saw David leaping and dancing, and the mother of Sisera looked out at a window for a chariot that never came.

Theological Significance

I believe God looks down to bless and to judge, and the difference is whether men are looking up or looking out. The Egyptians were troubled by His look, the children of men were searched by it, and Michal despised what she saw through her window. My position is that I want to be seen by the God who looks down, not to hide from Him behind a lattice.
