enkaléō

from G1722 and G2564; to call in (as a debt or demand), i.e. bring to account (charge, criminate, etc.):--accuse, call in question, implead, lay to the charge.

Biblical Usage

To bring a charge, to call to account. The town clerk tells the Ephesians to implead one another in the open courts in Acts 19:38, and Paul asks who shall lay anything to the charge of God's elect in Romans 8:33. Lysias brought Paul before the council to learn the cause wherefore he was accused in Acts 23:28.

Theological Significance

I believe the charge-book against God's elect is empty — God justifies, so no accusation stands. I hold that every charge men bring will be answered the same way: it is God who justifies, and Christ who died.
