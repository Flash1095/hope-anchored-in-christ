nâçâh

to test; by implication, to attempt

Biblical Usage

Nasah means to test or try, and it is used for God testing His people and for people testing God. In Genesis 22:1 God did tempt Abraham, asking for Isaac, and in Exodus 15:25 He proved Israel at the bitter waters of Marah. In Deuteronomy 8:2 the forty years in the wilderness were to humble them and prove them, to know what was in their heart, and in Psalm 26:2 David asks the Lord to examine him and prove him.

Theological Significance

I believe God's testing is not a trap but a revealing, because He already knows what is in the heart and wants us to know it too. Abraham was tested and trusted, Israel was proved and grumbled, and David asked for the test himself. I hold that a test passed is worth more than a test avoided.
