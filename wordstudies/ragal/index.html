râgal

to walk along; but only in specifically, applications, to reconnoiter, to be a tale-bearer (i.e. slander); to lead about

Biblical Usage

Ragal is to walk along — and, in its specific uses, to spy out a land, or to be a tale-bearer, going about with slander. Joseph accused his brothers, 'Ye are spies; to see the nakedness of the land ye are come'; Moses sent men to spy out Jaazer; and Joshua sent two men to spy secretly out of Shittim, saying, 'Go view the land, even Jericho.'

Theological Significance

I am convinced that the word holds two kinds of going about: the spies who went to see the land God promised, and the tale-bearer who goes to see his neighbour's shame — and only the first is blessed.
