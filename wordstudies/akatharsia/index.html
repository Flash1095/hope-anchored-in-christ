akatharsía

from G169; impurity (the quality), physically or morally:--uncleanness.

Biblical Usage

The word is impurity in every sense — physical, moral, and spiritual. Jesus calls the Pharisees whitewashed tombs full of dead men's bones, and of all uncleanness; Paul says God gave men up to uncleanness through the lusts of their own hearts; it heads the works of the flesh in Galatians; and Ephesians says it should not even be named among the saints. The same word covers a dirty tomb and a dirty heart.

Theological Significance

I believe the word's force is in the contrast: uncleanness is what God gives people up to when they refuse Him, and it is the very thing the saints are told to leave unnamed. The gospel does not manage impurity; it washes it away.
