ʼĕlôhîym

gods in the ordinary sense; but specifically used (in the plural thus, especially with the article) of the supreme God; occasionally applied by way of deference to magistrates; and sometimes as a superlative

Biblical Usage

ʼĕlôhîym means God. In Genesis 1:1, the text says "In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth." In Exodus 1:17, the text says "But the midwives feared God."

Theological Significance

I hold that ʼĕlôhîym matters because God is who He says He is, and His name carries everything He is. That is why I keep this word in mind when I read those passages — it carries more than its size suggests.
