katágō

from G2596 and G71; to lead down; specially, to moor a vessel:--bring (down, forth), (bring to) land, touch.

Biblical Usage

To bring down a ship is to make land, and Luke uses the word for Paul's voyage to Rome, for the landing at Syracuse, and for the fishermen who brought their ships to land and left everything to follow Jesus. Paul borrows it too, asking who shall descend into the deep to bring Christ up again. The word moves from harbours to heaven and back.

Theological Significance

I believe following Jesus always costs a landing, because the fishermen had to bring the boats in before they could walk away. I hold that Christ has already made the only descent that matters, and I do not need to fetch Him down again.
