talaipōréō

from G5005; to be wretched, i.e. realize one's own misery:--be afflicted.

Biblical Usage

James tells his readers to be afflicted, and mourn, and weep, and to let their laughter be turned to mourning and their joy to heaviness. The word is to realise one's own misery, to feel the weight of it. James is not praising gloom; he is calling for honest sorrow over sin before God.

Theological Significance

I believe James is right that sorrow over sin comes before lifting up: the humble are the ones God raises, and the mourning is the road there.
