dâbaq

properly, to impinge, i.e. cling or adhere; figuratively, to catch by pursuit

Biblical Usage

To cleave is to stick fast, and a man leaves his father and mother and cleaves to his wife. The inheritance must cleave to its tribe, and Israel is told to cleave to the LORD and to serve Him. Joshua repeats it at his farewell: cleave to the LORD your God, and keep His commandments. The word is the picture of a bond that will not let go.

Theological Significance

I believe cleaving is the Bible's picture of covenant: a man to his wife, a tribe to its land, and a people to their God. The same word holds them all, and the holding is the point. My position is that a faith that does not cleave is not faith at all — it is acquaintance, and acquaintance does not save.
