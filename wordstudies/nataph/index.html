nâṭaph

to ooze, i.e. distil gradually; by implication, to fall in drops; figuratively, to speak by inspiration

Biblical Usage

The false prophets are told not to drop their word on the people, and Ezekiel is told to drop his word toward the holy places in judgment. The mountains drop new wine in the day of restoration, and the hills drop sweet wine when God brings His people back.

Theological Significance

I hold that preaching is like rain: it falls drop by drop, and it either softens the ground or runs off the hard heart. The same word that judges in Ezekiel's day brings wine to the mountains in Joel's.
