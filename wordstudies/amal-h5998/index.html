ʻâmal

to toil, i.e. work severely and with irksomeness

Biblical Usage

To toil, to work severely and with irksomeness, and the Preacher makes it his question: What profit hath a man of all his labour which he taketh under the sun? He looks on all the works that his hands have wrought, and on the labour that he has laboured to do, and behold, all is vanity and vexation of spirit. Jonah is told he had pity on a gourd for which he had not laboured, and the psalm says except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it.

Theological Significance

I believe the toil is real and the question is honest: what profit is there in work that ends in vanity? The Preacher's answer is not to stop working but to see whose house it is — the labour is vain when it is mine alone, and it is not vain when the LORD builds with me. I hold that Jonah's gourd is the warning, that I can pity what I did not labour for and resent what God did not give me, and the cure is the same: the work is His, and so is the profit.
