systratiṓtēs

from G4862 and G4757; a co-campaigner, i.e. (figuratively) an associate in Christian toil:--fellowsoldier.

Biblical Usage

Paul calls Epaphroditus his brother, companion in labour, and fellow-soldier, and he gives Archippus the same title in his letter to Philemon. The word is for someone who campaigns alongside you, sharing the danger and the work. Paul does not fight alone, and he is glad to say so.

Theological Significance

Something plain is honoured by this word, I believe: the Christian life is a campaign fought shoulder to shoulder, and the fellow-soldiers deserve naming.
