Tâmâr

Tamar, the name of three women and a place

Biblical Usage

Tamar is the name of three women and a place: the first is Judah's daughter-in-law, who bears him Perez and Zerah when justice is denied her, and the second is David's daughter, the sister of Absalom, whom Amnon violates, and for whom Absalom takes revenge. The third is Absalom's daughter, named for his sister, and the place is Tamar in the wilderness, on the border of the promised land. One name carries a widow's justice, a sister's sorrow, and a border town.

Theological Significance

I believe Tamar's name carries more sorrow than almost any other in Scripture. I hold that the first Tamar's story ends in the line of Christ, and that is the hope in it: God writes justice into a genealogy that begins with a wronged widow.
