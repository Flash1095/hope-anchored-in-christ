chûwd

properly, to tie a knot, i.e. (figuratively) to propound a riddle

Biblical Usage

To pose a riddle is to tie a knot in words, and Samson ties one for the Philistines at his wedding feast: out of the eater came forth meat. Ezekiel is told to put forth a riddle to the house of Israel, and the riddle is a parable of eagles and vines. The word is used for a puzzle that hides a truth until it is untied.

Theological Significance

I believe riddles in Scripture are not games; they are truths wrapped so that only the willing will unwrap them. Samson's riddle cost him his wife and his secret, and Ezekiel's riddle carried a judgment. I hold that God speaks in riddles to those who will listen closely, and plainly to those who will not.
