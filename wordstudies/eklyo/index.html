eklýō

from G1537 and G3089; to relax (literally or figuratively):--faint.

Biblical Usage

This word means to relax, to grow faint, and the Greek text carries it in the middle voice, as fainting or being wearied, where Strong's lists the plain active form. Jesus says the multitude has been with Him three days and has nothing to eat, and He will not send them away fasting lest they faint by the way, while Paul warns not to be weary in well doing and Hebrews tells us to consider Him who endured, lest we be wearied and faint in our minds. The word is the body's warning and the soul's.

Theological Significance

I believe Jesus cares for fainting bodies as well as fainting hearts, because He fed the multitude before He sent them home, and He feeds me still.
