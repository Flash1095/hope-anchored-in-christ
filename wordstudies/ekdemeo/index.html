ekdēméō

from a compound of G1537 and G1218; to emigrate, i.e. (figuratively) vacate or quit:--be absent.

Biblical Usage

This word means to be away from home, to live abroad, and Paul uses it for the body and the Lord. While we are at home in the body, he says, we are absent from the Lord, and we are willing rather to be absent from the body and present with the Lord. The word makes this life a foreign country and heaven the home we are travelling toward.

Theological Significance

I believe heaven is home and this world is the journey, because Paul calls the body a tent we live in while we are away. I hold that to be absent from the body and present with the Lord is the Christian's hope, and it makes death a homecoming.
