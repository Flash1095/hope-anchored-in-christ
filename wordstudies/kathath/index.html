kâthath

to bruise or violently strike

Biblical Usage

To bruise and strike is what the nations will do to their swords when they beat them into plowshares, and what Joel reverses when he says beat your plowshares into swords, and what Hezekiah did when he brake in pieces the brasen serpent Moses had made. The word holds the beating of swords into ploughs and the breaking of idols together: one is the promise of peace, the other is the price of it.

Theological Significance

I believe the swords will become plowshares when the Lord judges, and the idols must be broken before that day comes. I hold that Hezekiah's hammer is the picture of repentance, because what was once lifted up for healing had become an idol, and it had to come down.
