chârar

to glow, i.e. literally (to melt, burn, dry up) or figuratively (to show or incite passion)

Biblical Usage

Charar is burning, glowing, drying up, and it is the heat of judgment and of sickness. The vine wood is cast into the fire, both ends devoured and the midst burned, and the pot is set to boil until the bones are burned. The bellows are burned and the founder melteth in vain, and Job says his skin is black upon him and his bones are burned with heat.

Theological Significance

I believe the burning in these verses is thorough: fire takes the ends and the middle, the bones and the skin. Jeremiah's founder melts in vain because the wicked are not plucked away. I hold that fire in the Bible always tests what it touches, and what is left after it is what was real.
