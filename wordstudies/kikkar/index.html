kikkâr

a circle, i.e. (by implication) a circumjacent tract or region, especially the Ghor or valley of the Jordan; also a (round) loaf; also a talent (or large [round] coin)

Biblical Usage

Kikkar is a circle, and the word stretches to cover a plain, a loaf, and a talent. Genesis 13:10 shows Lot beholding all the plain of Jordan, well watered like the garden of the LORD, and Deuteronomy 34:3 calls it the plain of the valley of Jericho. Exodus 25:39 measures the lampstand's gold by a talent of pure gold, and 2 Samuel 12:30 weighs a king's crown at a talent of gold with precious stones. Judges 8:5 asks for loaves of bread for Gideon's faint men, and 1 Samuel 2:36 has the fallen house of Eli begging for a morsel of bread.

Theological Significance

I believe the round word teaches a round truth: the plain that tempted Lot, the talent that crowned a king, and the loaf that fed a faint man are all measured by God. I hold that what is round in Scripture comes back to where it began, and the plain of Jordan ends in judgment while the bread of God ends in life.
