tôhûw

a desolation (of surface), i.e. desert; figuratively, a worthless thing; adverbially, in vain

Biblical Usage

Without form and void — the words open the Bible. The earth was tohu before God spoke, Israel was found in a waste howling wilderness, Samuel warns against turning aside after vain things that cannot profit, and Isaiah says the city of confusion is broken down.

Theological Significance

I hold that tohu is the Bible's word for what is empty of God: the unformed earth, the desert, the idol that cannot save, the city that has lost its order. I believe the hope in the word is that God speaks into tohu — the formless earth became a garden, and the wilderness became a people. What is void is never the end of the story when God is in it.
