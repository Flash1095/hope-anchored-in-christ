epaúrion

from G1909 and G839; occurring on the succeeding day, i.e. (G2250 being implied) to-morrow:--day following, morrow, next day (after).

Biblical Usage

Epaurion simply means the next day, the morrow — and the Gospels and Acts are full of it. The next day John sees Jesus coming and says, Behold the Lamb of God; the next day Peter goes with Cornelius's men; the next day the disciples break bread at Troas. Ordinary days, carrying extraordinary things.

Theological Significance

God works on plain tomorrows, not only on feast days, and the next day is often when the promise arrives. I hold that the Gospels' habit of saying 'the next day' is itself a sermon: keep walking, because tomorrow may be the day you meet the Lamb of God.
