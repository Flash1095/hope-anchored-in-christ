phármakos

the same as 5332:--sorcerer.

Biblical Usage

Pharmakos is a sorcerer, and John names him twice, both times at the gates of the New Jerusalem. In the first, the sorcerers are among those whose part is in the lake of fire, and in the second, they are outside the city, with the dogs and the whoremongers and the murderers and the idolaters. Inside the city are the tree of life and the river of water of life; outside are those who loved the lie.

Theological Significance

I believe the sorcerer is shut out because sorcery is a lie about power: it offers what only God can give, and it takes what only God can keep. My position is that the gates of the city are not cruel; they are honest, and the one who will not leave his spells behind cannot enter where no lie enters.
