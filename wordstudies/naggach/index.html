naggâch

butting, i.e. vicious

Biblical Usage

Naggach means butting or vicious, an ox that is known to push with its horn, and it is used for the law of the goring ox. In Exodus 21:29 if the ox were wont to push with his horn in time past, and it has been testified to the owner, and he has not kept him in, and the ox kills a man or a woman, the ox shall be stoned and the owner also put to death, and in Exodus 21:36 if it is known the ox has used to push, the owner shall surely pay ox for ox.

Theological Significance

I hold that the law of the butting ox is a law about responsibility, not about cattle; a known danger left unguarded is the owner's fault, and God holds the owner to it.
