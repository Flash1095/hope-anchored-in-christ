gâmal

to treat a person (well or ill), i.e. benefit or requite; by implication (of toil), to ripen, i.e. (specifically) to wean

Biblical Usage

Gamal means to deal with a person, to benefit or to requite — and it covers both weaning a child and rewarding a man. Hannah would not go up until the child was weaned in 1 Samuel 1:22, and Tahpenes weaned Genubath in Pharaoh's house in 1 Kings 11:20. David sings that the LORD rewarded him according to his righteousness in 2 Samuel 22:21, and the enemies of Judah say, Behold, how they reward us, in 2 Chronicles 20:11.

Theological Significance

I believe God deals with us as a mother weans a child and as a judge rewards the righteous. He brings us from milk to meat and repays us according to our ways, and I hold that His dealing is always just and always kind.
