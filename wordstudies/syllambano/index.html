syllambánō

from G4862 and G2983; to clasp, i.e. seize (arrest, capture); specially, to conceive (literally or figuratively); by implication, to aid:--catch, conceive, help, take.

Biblical Usage

The angel tells Mary she will conceive a son and call His name Jesus, and Elizabeth conceives in her old age, so the word opens the gospel with God taking hold of human life from the inside. The same word seizes: the band takes Jesus in the garden, and the officers take Him to the high priest's house. And it helps: Paul asks his true yokefellow to help the women who laboured with him in the gospel, while James warns that when lust has conceived it brings forth sin.

Theological Significance

I believe this word holds the whole gospel in one grip, that God lays hold of us to give us life, and men laid hold of Jesus to take His. I hold that being seized by God is the best thing that can happen to a person, because His grip gives life and never lets go. And I take James's warning seriously: what I let conceive in my heart will be born, so I want Christ conceived in me, not sin.
