nâshaq

to kiss, literally or figuratively (touch); also (as a mode of attachment), to equip with weapons

Biblical Usage

Isaac calls his son near to kiss him, and Jacob kisses Rachel at the well when he first sees her. David and Jonathan kiss at their parting, and Absalom kisses every man who bows to him, stealing their hearts with the gesture.

Theological Significance

I believe a kiss means whatever the heart means by it. Jonathan's kiss is loyalty, Absalom's is treason, and God tells me to watch the heart behind the gesture, not the gesture itself.
