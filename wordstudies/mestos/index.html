mestós

of uncertain derivation; replete (literally or figuratively):--full.

Biblical Usage

Mestos is full, replete, filled to the brim, and it is used for eyes, tongues, and hearts alike. 2 Peter 2:14 has eyes full of adultery, and James 3:8 has a tongue full of deadly poison. John 19:29 sets a vessel full of vinegar at the cross, and Matthew 23:28 says the Pharisees outwardly appear righteous, but within are full of hypocrisy and iniquity.

Theological Significance

I believe 'full' is the most honest word in these verses: what fills a man is what comes out of him. My position is that the cure is not to be emptied but to be filled with something better, because a full vessel is never neutral.
