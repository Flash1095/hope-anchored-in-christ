philóneikos

from G5384 and νεῖκος (a quarrel; probably akin to G3534); fond of strife, i.e. disputatious:--contentious.

Biblical Usage

Philoneikos means fond of strife, contentious, and it appears once, in Paul's teaching on head coverings. He says that if any man seems to be contentious, we have no such custom, neither the churches of God.

Theological Significance

I hold that Paul's answer to contention is custom and peace: the churches do not fight over what the churches do not do.
