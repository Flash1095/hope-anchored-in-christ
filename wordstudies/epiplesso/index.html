epiplḗssō

from G1909 and G4141; to chastise, i.e. (with words) to upbraid:--rebuke.

Biblical Usage

Epiplesso means to rebuke, to upbraid — and Paul tells Timothy, rebuke not an elder, but intreat him as a father, and the younger men as brethren.

Theological Significance

The manner of correction matters as much as the correction, I believe: an elder is entreated as a father, not scolded as a servant.
