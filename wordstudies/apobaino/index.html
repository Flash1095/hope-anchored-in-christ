apobaínō

from G575 and the base of G939; literally, to disembark; figuratively, to eventuate:--become, go out, turn.

Biblical Usage

Literally to step off a boat onto the shore, and then, by figure, for a thing to turn out a certain way. The disciples come to land in John 21:9 and find a fire of coals with fish and bread already waiting, and the fishermen in Luke 5:2 have gone out of their boats to wash their nets when Jesus steps in. Jesus promises the persecuted in Luke 21:13 that it shall turn to them for a testimony, and Paul is sure in Philippians 1:19 that his imprisonment shall turn to his salvation.

Theological Significance

I love how this word moves from feet on a beach to the whole shape of a life. What looks like an ending — a night of empty nets, a prison cell, a trial before rulers — turns, in God's hands, into provision, testimony, and salvation. The landing is never the end of the story.
