nâbâl

stupid; wicked (especially impious)

Biblical Usage

Nabal as an adjective means foolish or stupid, and in its worst sense wicked and impious. In Psalm 14:1 and Psalm 53:1 the fool says in his heart, There is no God, and they are corrupt and have done abominable works. In Proverbs 17:21 the father of a fool has no joy, and in Isaiah 32:6 the vile person speaks villany and works iniquity against the Lord.

Theological Significance

I believe the fool in the Bible is not a person with a low score on a test, but a person who has decided God does not matter. Saying there is no God in the heart is where the corruption starts, and everything else follows from it. I hold that wisdom begins where folly ends, with the fear of the Lord.
