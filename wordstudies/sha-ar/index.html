shâʼar

to remain, be left over

Biblical Usage

Sha'ar is being left, remaining, and Scripture uses it for what judgment leaves and what God keeps. Baasha smote all the house of Jeroboam and left not any that breathed; and of Dagon only the stump was left to him when his head and hands were cut off. But David sent abroad to the brethren that are left in all the land of Israel, to gather them to the ark; and Samuel set the shoulder before Saul, saying, behold that which is left, set it before thee, and eat, for unto this time hath it been kept for thee.

Theological Significance

The remnant is the whole hope of Israel in one word. Judgment leaves a stump — Dagon's stump, Jeroboam's nothing — and then God keeps a shoulder for Saul and brethren for David. The one who is left is not the one who was strong enough to live; he is the one the LORD left. And I hold that the kept portion is the promise: what is left is kept for a purpose, and the purpose is God's.
