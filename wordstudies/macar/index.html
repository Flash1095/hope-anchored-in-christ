mâçar

to sunder, i.e. (transitively) set apart, or (reflex.) apostatize

Biblical Usage

The word means to set apart or sunder, and it appears twice in the war against Midian. Twelve thousand were set apart from the thousands of Israel, a thousand of every tribe, armed for war, and the same chapter says the women of Midian, through the counsel of Balaam, caused Israel to commit trespass against the LORD in the matter of Peor.

Theological Significance

I hold that being set apart cuts both ways: Israel was set apart for war, and the women who caused Israel to sin were set apart for judgment.
