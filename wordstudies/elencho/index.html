elénchō

of uncertain affinity; to confute, admonish:--convict, convince, tell a fault, rebuke, reprove.

Biblical Usage

The word elénchō carries the sense of convict, convince, tell a fault, rebuke, reprove. In Matthew 18:15, the text says "go and tell him his fault between thee and him alone." In Luke 3:19, the text says "being reproved by him for Herodias his brother Philip's wife."

Theological Significance

For me, elénchō is a reminder that discernment is learning to tell the true from the almost-true, by God's Word. That shapes how I read the passages where it appears: not as filler, but as God's own wording for what He is doing.
