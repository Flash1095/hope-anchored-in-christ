aschēmonéō

from G809; to be (i.e. act) unbecoming:--behave self uncomely (unseemly).

Biblical Usage

To behave unseemly is to act in a way that shames, and Paul uses it twice, for a father and for love. If a man thinks he behaves himself uncomely toward his virgin, let him do what he will, he sinneth not; and charity doth not behave itself unseemly, seeketh not her own, is not easily provoked.

Theological Significance

One word for unseemly behaviour carries no doctrine of its own; its weight is the contrast, that love never acts the way the word describes, and the man who loves well has nothing to be ashamed of.
