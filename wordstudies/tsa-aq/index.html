tsâʻaq

to shriek; (by implication) to proclaim (an assembly)

Biblical Usage

Tsaaq is the cry of a person in trouble, raised to the only One who can help. Israel cried to the LORD, and God heard; the widow in 2 Kings cried to Elisha when the creditor came for her sons; and the psalmist says the redeemed cried to the LORD in their trouble and He delivered them. It is not a polite request — it is the sound of need reaching for God.

Theological Significance

I believe God hears the cry of His people — that is the promise running through every one of these passages. My position is that prayer is not a formality for the comfortable; it is the cry of the needy, and God has bound Himself to answer it. When I cry to Him, I am doing what Israel did, and He is the same God who heard them.
