dâyêq

a battering-tower

Biblical Usage

The battering tower is the engine of siege, built against the walls of Jerusalem and set up by the king of Babylon. Ezekiel pictures the ramps and the towers rising against the city, and Jeremiah records the siege that ended it. The tower is the slow, certain work of judgment.

Theological Significance

I believe the tower warns the proud city: walls that are trusted instead of God will be battered down. The tower rises slowly and falls quickly, and the city that trusts its walls has trusted the wrong thing.
