shâchar

properly, to dawn, i.e. (figuratively) be (up) early at any task (with the implication of earnestness); by extension, to search for (with painstaking)

Biblical Usage

Shachar is to rise at dawn and seek, to look for something earnestly and early. David sings that early will I seek thee in a dry and thirsty land, and Wisdom promises that those who seek her early shall find her. The prophets use the same word both ways, for God waiting until His people seek Him early in their affliction, and for the soul that seeks Him in the night.

Theological Significance

I believe seeking God early is not about the clock but about the heart, putting Him first before the day fills up. The promise stands on both sides of it, that those who seek Him early shall find Him, and that He waits to be found. That is why I come to Him before anything else has had its say.
