dâqar

to stab; by analogy, to starve; figuratively, to revile

Biblical Usage

To pierce is the wound of the sword and the thrust of the spear, and the word gathers the battlefield and the prophecy. Saul falls on his own sword, the slain lie pierced in the streets, and the prophet says they shall look on Him whom they have pierced. The wound that kills is also the wound that saves.

Theological Significance

I believe the pierced One is the centre of the word: the spear that opened His side opened the way for us. The spear that pierced Him is the proof that He was really dead, and really raised.
