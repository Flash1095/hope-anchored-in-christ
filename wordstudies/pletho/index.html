plḗthō

; a prolonged form of a primary πλέω (which appears only as an alternate in certain tenses and in the reduplicated form πίμπλημι); to "fill" (literally or figuratively (imbue, influence, supply)); specially, to fulfil (time):--accomplish, full (…come), furnish.

Biblical Usage

Pletho means to be filled, filled up to the full. John the Baptist would be filled with the Holy Ghost from his mother's womb, in Luke 1:15, and Zacharias was filled with the Holy Ghost and prophesied in Luke 1:67. On Pentecost they were all filled with the Holy Ghost in Acts 2:4, and Peter, filled with the Holy Ghost, spoke boldly in Acts 4:8. When they prayed, the place was shaken, and they were all filled again in Acts 4:31.

Theological Significance

I believe being filled is not a once-for-all event but an again-and-again grace — the disciples were filled, and filled again. That is why I hold that I should keep asking, because God keeps filling.
