ʻâshêsh

probably to shrink, i.e. fail

Biblical Usage

To waste away is what grief does to the eyes and the strength, and David uses it three times in his sorrow. Mine eye is consumed because of grief, it waxeth old because of all mine enemies; mine eye is consumed with grief, yea, my soul and my belly; and my strength faileth because of mine iniquity, and my bones are consumed.

Theological Significance

I believe wasting away is the honest language of a man who knows his sin has cost him his strength: the eye dims, the bones are consumed, and the grief is not only sorrow but iniquity. And I hold that David's answer is the same in both psalms, to cry for mercy, and the Lord who hears is the Lord who restores.
