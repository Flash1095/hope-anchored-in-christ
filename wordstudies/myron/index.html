mýron

probably of foreign origin (compare H4753, G4666); "myrrh", i.e. (by implication) perfumed oil:--ointment.

Biblical Usage

Myron is the perfumed ointment poured out in love and in grief, and all four Gospels bring it to the same table. A woman in Bethany breaks her alabaster box over Jesus in Mark 14:3, and Mary anoints His feet in John 12:3 until the house is filled with the smell. The sinful woman in Luke 7:37 brings the same costly ointment, and in Matthew 26:12 Jesus says it was poured for His burial.

Theological Significance

I believe the ointment matters because it was poured before anyone understood the cross, and Jesus received it as worship anyway. Costly love does not wait until it can explain itself, and what looks like waste to onlookers smells like devotion to Him. My position is simple: pour out the best you have on Jesus now, because the moment to honour Him is the moment you are in.
