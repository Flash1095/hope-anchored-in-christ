ʻânan

figuratively, to act covertly, i.e. practise magic

Biblical Usage

The word means to practise hidden arts — to observe times, to read the clouds for omens, to deal in what God has forbidden. The law says there shall not be found among you an observer of times, and Leviticus says neither shall you use enchantment nor observe times; Manasseh did exactly what the law forbade, observing times and using enchantments and dealing with familiar spirits; and Micah promises a day when God will cut off witchcrafts and there will be no more soothsayers. The root is the same as the word for cloud — the arts of reading the sky for what only God knows.

Theological Significance

I hold that the ban on these arts is a mercy, not a restriction: God forbids them because they are a shortcut around trusting Him, and a shortcut that ends in serving what is not God. Manasseh's reign shows where the shortcut leads, and Micah's promise shows where it ends — a day when no one needs a soothsayer, because God Himself speaks plainly to His people.
