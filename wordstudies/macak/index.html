mâçak

to mix, especially wine (with spices)

Biblical Usage

The word means to mix, especially wine with spices, and it is used for Wisdom's feast and for the drunkard's boast alike. Wisdom has mingled her wine and calls the simple to come and eat of her bread, while Isaiah says the mighty men of Israel are strong to mingle strong drink. The psalmist says he has mingled his drink with weeping, because his tears fall into the cup.

Theological Significance

I believe the mingled cup shows what we put into our lives: Wisdom mingles wine for a feast, and the drunkard mingles it for ruin. I hold that the tears mingled with drink are the truest cup of all, because God counts the tears we cannot swallow.
