ʼăman

{properly, to build up or support; to foster as a parent or nurse; figuratively to render (or be) firm or faithful, to trust or believe, to be permanent or quiet; morally to be true or certain};

Biblical Usage

Certain, true, faithful — and in Daniel's Aramaic it is the word for what can be trusted. The dream is certain, and the interpretation thereof sure, Daniel tells the king of the stone cut out without hands. And the presidents could find no fault in Daniel, forasmuch as he was faithful, neither was there any error found in him. And no hurt was found on him in the den, because he believed in his God.

Theological Significance

I believe this word is the spine of Daniel's story: the dream is certain, the man is faithful, and the God he believed is sure. Faithfulness is not a feeling but a record — the presidents searched for a fault and found none, and the lions found a man whose trust had already been tested. I hold that the God who makes dreams certain is the same God who keeps faithful men, and believing Him is not a risk but the one sure thing in a foreign court.
