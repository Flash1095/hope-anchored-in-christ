sákkos

of Hebrew origin (H8242); "sack"-cloth, i.e. mohair (the material or garments made of it, worn as a sign of grief):-- sackcloth.

Biblical Usage

Sákkos is the word for "sack"-cloth, that is mohair (the material or garments made of it, worn as a sign of grief). In Luke 10:13, we read, "…a great while ago repented, sitting in sackcloth…"; in Matthew 11:21, "…have repented long ago in sackcloth and ashes". You hear it again in Revelation 6:12 — "…had opened the sixth seal, and, lo, there…" — and in Revelation 11:3, "…prophesy a thousand two hundred threescore days, clothed…".

Theological Significance

I hold that sákkos is no accident in the text, and it names something real, and God does not use empty words. I hold that sákkos still speaks, because the God who spoke then has not changed.
