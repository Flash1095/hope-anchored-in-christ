kataskēnóō

from G2596 and G4637; to camp down, i.e. haunt; figuratively, to remain:--lodge, rest.

Biblical Usage

To camp down is what the birds do in the branches of the mustard tree, lodging there when the smallest seed has grown greater than the herbs, and David says his flesh shall rest in hope because God will not leave his soul in hell. The word holds the bird in the branch and the body in the grave together: both are resting in a place they did not build, waiting for what comes next.

Theological Significance

I believe the kingdom is the tree the birds lodge in, and my flesh rests in hope for the same reason David's did. I hold that God will not leave His Holy One in the grave, and He will not leave me there either.
