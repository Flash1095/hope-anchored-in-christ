sâkâr

payment of contract; concretely, salary, fare, maintenance; by implication, compensation, benefit

Biblical Usage

Here is sâkâr: it means payment of contract; concretely, salary, fare, maintenance; by implication, compensation, benefit. In 1 Kings 5:6, we read, "…skill to hew timber like unto the Sidonians"; in 2 Chronicles 15:7, "…ye strong therefore, and let not your hands…". You hear it again in Deuteronomy 15:18 — "…double hired servant, in serving thee six years…" — and in Ecclesiastes 4:9, "…better than one; because they have a good…".

Theological Significance

My position is simple: sâkâr is in the Bible for a reason, and it belongs to the story God is telling from beginning to end. And I believe sâkâr still speaks to us today, because God's word does not go out of date.
