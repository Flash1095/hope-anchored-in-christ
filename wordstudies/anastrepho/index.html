anastréphō

from G303 and G4762; to overturn; also to return; by implication, to busy oneself, i.e. remain, live:--abide, behave self, have conversation, live, overthrow, pass, return, be used.

Biblical Usage

Paul says his rejoicing is the testimony of his conscience, that in simplicity and godly sincerity he has had his conversation — his whole way of life — in the world, and more abundantly toward Corinth; and he reminds the Ephesians that they all once had their conversation in the lusts of the flesh. Peter tells the scattered believers to pass the time of their sojourning in fear, and Hebrews says the writers trust they have a good conscience, in all things willing to live honestly. The word is a life lived, day after day, in the world and before God.

Theological Significance

I believe this word is the test of everything else I claim: my conversation — the way I actually live — is the evidence, and the conscience knows whether it is simplicity and godly sincerity or not. I once lived in the lusts of the flesh like everyone else, and now I am to pass my sojourning in fear, living honestly in all things. That is the life I want my own to be, and I hold that God judges the walk, not just the words.
