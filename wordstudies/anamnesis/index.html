anámnēsis

from G363; recollection:--remembrance (again).

Biblical Usage

Jesus took bread, gave thanks, broke it, and said, 'This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me' — and Paul repeats the words to Corinth, adding that the cup is the same. But Hebrews turns the word the other way: in the old sacrifices there is a remembrance again made of sins every year. The same word is the remembering that saves and the remembering that accuses — the supper that remembers Christ, and the sacrifices that only remembered sin.

Theological Significance

I believe the difference between the two remembrances is the whole difference between the covenants: the old sacrifices remembered sins every year and never took them away, but the bread and the cup remember Christ, who took them away once for all. That is why I come to the table — not to remember my sins, but to remember the One who bore them.
