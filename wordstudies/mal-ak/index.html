mălʼâk

a messenger; specifically, of God, i.e. an angel (also a prophet, priest or teacher)

Biblical Usage

Malak is the messenger, the angel, and it is the most frequent heavenly word in the Old Testament. Genesis 16:7 has the angel of the LORD finding Hagar by the fountain in the wilderness, and Exodus 3:2 has the angel of the LORD appearing to Moses in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush. Judges 6:11 has the angel of the LORD sitting under an oak in Ophrah when Gideon was threshing wheat, and Psalms 91:11 says He shall give His angels charge over thee, to keep thee in all thy ways, while Malachi 3:1 promises the messenger of the covenant, whom ye delight in.

Theological Significance

I believe this word is the proof that God does not send His messages by accident: an angel finds Hagar, calls Moses, and sits down with Gideon, each one on purpose. My position is that Psalms 91:11 is the promise that makes the others personal, because the charge given to the angels is over me too, to keep me in all my ways.
