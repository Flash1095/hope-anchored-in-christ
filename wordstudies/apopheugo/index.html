apopheúgō

from G575 and G5343; (figuratively) to escape:--escape.

Biblical Usage

To escape — three times, all in 2 Peter, and all about the same snare. By the promises we are partakers of the divine nature, having escaped the corruption that is in the world through lust, in 2 Peter 1:4. But the false teachers allure those that were clean escaped from them who live in error in 2 Peter 2:18, and if after they have escaped the pollutions of the world they are again entangled, the latter end is worse with them than the beginning, in 2 Peter 2:20.

Theological Significance

I hold that Peter's warning is the sober side of every escape. God has given me a way out of corruption, and it is real — but the escape is not a fence I can never climb back over. Those who return to the pollution they escaped find the second captivity worse than the first, and I am to guard my escape like the gift it is.
