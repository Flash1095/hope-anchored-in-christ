aichmálōtos

from αἰχμή (a spear) and a derivative of the same as G259; properly, a prisoner of war, i.e. (genitive case) a captive:--captive.

Biblical Usage

The word appears once, on Jesus' own lips in the Nazareth synagogue, reading Isaiah: He has come to preach deliverance to the captives. The captives are not only prisoners in cells; in Isaiah's world they are exiles and the oppressed, and Jesus claims the whole promise as His mission.

Theological Significance

The word itself is only a captive's label, and its weight lies in the lips that speak it — Jesus in Nazareth, claiming Isaiah's promise as His own mission.
