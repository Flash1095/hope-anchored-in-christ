parangéllō

from G3844 and the base of G32; to transmit a message, i.e. (by implication) to enjoin:--(give in) charge, (give) command(-ment), declare.

Biblical Usage

Parangello is a command given with authority, not a suggestion. Jesus charges the twelve before He sends them out in Matthew 10:5, Peter says God commanded the apostles to preach in Acts 10:42, and Paul charges Timothy again and again in 1 Timothy 1:3 and 4:11. The same word carries church discipline in 2 Thessalonians 3:6, where believers are commanded to withdraw from a brother who walks disorderly.

Theological Significance

I believe a command from God is not advice to weigh, it is an order to obey. That is why I hold that preaching, teaching, and even discipline all rest on the same ground: Christ has spoken, and His servants pass His charge on unchanged.
