hâbal

to be vain in act, word, or expectation; specifically to lead astray

Biblical Usage

The word hâbal carries the sense of to act vainly, to lead astray. In 2 Kings 17:15, the text says "and became vain." In Job 27:12, the text says "why then are ye thus altogether vain?"

Theological Significance

My position is simple: hâbal tells me that wisdom begins with fearing the Lord, and it shows in how we live, not just what we know. That shapes how I read the passages where it appears: not as filler, but as God's own wording for what He is doing.
