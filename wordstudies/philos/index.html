phílos

properly, dear, i.e. a friend; actively, fond, i.e. friendly (still as a noun, an associate, neighbor, etc.):--friend.

Biblical Usage

Philos means a friend, dear, fond, and it is one of the warmest words in the New Testament. Jesus says greater love has no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends, and He says He no longer calls His disciples servants but friends. Abraham is called the friend of God, and John closes his little letter by greeting the friends by name.

Theological Significance

I believe philos is the gospel's most personal word: God does not only save me, He befriends me. Abraham believed and was called the friend of God, the disciples were told they were friends, and the friend's love lays down its life. My position is that friendship with God is not presumption but promise: Christ called me friend, and I am to live like one who is known by name.
