symbállō

from G4862 and G906; to combine, i.e. (in speaking) to converse, consult, dispute, (mentally) to consider, (by implication) to aid, (personally) to join, attack:--confer, encounter, help, make, meet with, ponder.

Biblical Usage

Mary kept all the things she heard about her Son and pondered them in her heart, turning them over until they fit together, and in Athens the Epicurean and Stoic philosophers encountered Paul and demanded to know what he was saying. The word joins pondering and disputing, the quiet turning of truth in the heart and the open clash of it in the marketplace.

Theological Significance

I hold that truth is worth pondering in private and defending in public, and I believe Mary got the order right: ponder first, then speak.
