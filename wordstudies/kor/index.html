kôr

properly, a deep round vessel, i.e. (specifically) a cor or measure for things dry

Biblical Usage

Kor is a cor, a deep round measure for dry things. 1 Kings 4:22 counts Solomon's daily provision at thirty measures of fine flour and threescore of meal, 1 Kings 5:11 has Solomon giving Hiram twenty thousand measures of wheat year by year, 2 Chronicles 2:10 promises the hewers twenty thousand measures of beaten wheat and barley, 2 Chronicles 27:5 takes ten thousand measures of wheat and barley as tribute from Ammon, Ezra 7:22 allows an hundred measures of wheat for the house of God, and Ezekiel 45:14 sets the ordinance of oil at the tenth part of a bath out of the cor.

Theological Significance

I believe the cor is the measure of a kingdom's daily bread: Solomon's table, Hiram's wages, the temple's store, and the tribute of Ammon are all counted in cors. I hold that the measure in Ezekiel 45:14 is the same measure in 1 Kings 4:22, because God's house and the king's house are both fed by measure, and the measure is God's.
