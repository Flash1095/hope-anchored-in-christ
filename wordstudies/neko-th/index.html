nᵉkôʼth

properly, a smiting, i.e. (concretely) an aromatic gum (perhaps styrax) (as powdered)

Biblical Usage

The Ishmaelites come down from Gilead with camels bearing spicery and balm and myrrh, and Jacob sends down spices and balm and honey as a present to the ruler of Egypt.

Theological Significance

I believe the spices carry the story: the traders who buy Joseph carry spicery, and the father who lost him sends spices to Egypt, and the same fragrance marks both the selling and the seeking.
