anaítios

from G1 (as a negative particle) and G159 (in the sense of G156); innocent:--blameless, guiltless.

Biblical Usage

The word appears twice, both times on Jesus' lips in the same argument: He reminds them that the priests in the temple profane the sabbath and are blameless, and that if they had known what 'I will have mercy, and not sacrifice' means, they would not have condemned the guiltless. It is the word for someone with no guilt on them — the priests doing their work, the disciples picking grain — and the Pharisees condemning them anyway.

Theological Significance

Two verses about the guiltless carry no doctrine of their own; their weight is Jesus' charge — mercy over sacrifice, and no condemnation for the innocent.
