synkoinōnós

from G4862 and G2844; a co-participant:--companion, partake(-r, -r with).

Biblical Usage

If some of the branches are broken off, and you, being a wild olive tree, were grafted in among them, and with them partake of the root and fatness of the olive tree, then you stand by faith. Paul does all for the gospel's sake that he might be a partaker of it with the Corinthians, and he tells the Philippians they are all partakers of his grace, in his bonds and in the defence and confirmation of the gospel. John calls himself their brother and companion in tribulation, and in the kingdom and patience of Jesus Christ, on the isle called Patmos for the word of God. The partaking is always with someone: with the root, with the gospel, with grace, with tribulation, and never alone.

Theological Significance

My position is that the wild branch partakes of the root only because it was grafted in, and I hold that my part in the gospel is a gift, not a wage. I take John's word as my own: companion in tribulation, and in the kingdom and patience of Jesus Christ, is the best company there is.
