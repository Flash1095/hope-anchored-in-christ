netsach

properly, a goal, i.e. the bright object at a distance travelled towards; hence (figuratively), splendor, or (subjectively) truthfulness, or (objectively) confidence; but usually (adverbially), continually (i.e. to the most distant point of view)

Biblical Usage

For ever is the reach of this word, and the Strength of Israel is its name. Samuel says the Strength of Israel will not lie nor repent, Abner asks whether the sword will devour for ever, David's prayer ascribes victory and majesty to the LORD, and Job watches men perish for ever without any regarding it.

Theological Significance

I believe for ever cuts both ways: God's strength endures for ever, and so does the ruin of those who refuse Him — the word is a promise and a warning in the same breath. David calls the LORD's victory His own, and Job watches the wicked perish for ever — the same for ever, two opposite ends.
