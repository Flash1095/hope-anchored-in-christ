peçach

a pretermission, i.e. exemption; used only technically of the Jewish Passover (the festival or the victim)

Biblical Usage

The Passover began the night God passed over the houses of Israel in Egypt, when the blood of the lamb on the doorposts marked the household for deliverance. Exodus calls it the LORD's passover, eaten in haste with loins girded and staff in hand, and Leviticus fixes it on the fourteenth day of the first month. Deuteronomy commands Israel to keep it every year, remembering the night God brought them out of Egypt.

Theological Significance

I believe the Passover is the gospel in miniature: a lamb dies so the firstborn lives, and deliverance comes by blood, not by merit. That is why the New Testament calls Christ our passover, sacrificed for us. Every year Israel ate the meal looking back at Egypt, and I eat it in memory looking back at the cross.
