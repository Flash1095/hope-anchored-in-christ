límnē

probably from G3040 (through the idea of nearness of shore); a pond (large or small):--lake.

Biblical Usage

límnē means a lake, and the Bible uses it the way ordinary people use ordinary words — for everything. In Luke 5:1, "he stood by the lake of Gennesaret"; and in Luke 8:23, "But as they sailed he fell asleep: and there came down a storm of wind on the lake". In Luke 8:33, "and entered into the swine: and the herd ran violently down a steep place into the lake"; and in Revelation 19:20, "These both were cast alive into a lake of fire burning with brimstone.".

Theological Significance

I believe the lake is where Jesus shows His power over wind and water and demons — the storm rises, the swine run down, and the beast is cast into the lake of fire. I hold that the same water that filled the disciples' boat is the picture of the final judgment. My position is that I will not fear the lake, because the One who walks on it is in my boat.
