nâdach

to push off; used in a great variety of applications, literally and figuratively (to expel, mislead, strike, inflict, etc.)

Biblical Usage

Nadach means to be driven away or pushed off, and it is the word for exile and for straying sheep. In Deuteronomy 30:1 the Lord says the people will call the blessing and the curse to mind among all the nations where He has driven them, and in Jeremiah 23:2 the pastors have scattered the flock and driven them away. In Ezekiel 34:4 the shepherds have not brought again that which was driven away, and in Deuteronomy 30:4 even if the driven ones are at the outmost parts of heaven, the Lord will gather them from there.

Theological Significance

I believe being driven away is the Bible's picture of what sin does to a people, but Deuteronomy 30:1 holds the way back in the same breath. The driven ones are told to call it all to mind, and remembering is the first step home. I hold that no exile is too far for the God who drove them to bring them back.
