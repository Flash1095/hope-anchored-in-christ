sᵉʼêth

an elevation or leprous scab; figuratively, elation or cheerfulness; exaltation in rank or character

Biblical Usage

Se'eth is lifting up, excellency, acceptance, and it opens on Cain: if thou doest well, shalt thou not be accepted? And Jacob gives it to Reuben, his firstborn, the excellency of dignity and the excellency of power.

Theological Significance

What I take from this is that acceptance is the question put to every man, and excellency is the birthright that can be lost by doing ill.
