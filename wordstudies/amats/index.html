ʼâmats

to be alert, physically (on foot) or mentally (in courage)

Biblical Usage

To be alert, to be strong and of good courage, and the Bible says it to men who are afraid. Be strong and of a good courage, fear not, nor be afraid of them, Moses tells Israel at the Jordan. Have not I commanded thee? Be strong and of a good courage, God tells Joshua at the same river; and David tells Solomon, Be strong and of good courage, and do it: fear not, nor be dismayed: for the LORD God, even my God, will be with thee.

Theological Significance

I believe courage in the Bible is not a temper but a command, given three times at three rivers of fear, and always with the same reason: God goes with you. Moses says it to a people about to fight, God says it to a man about to lead, and David says it to a son about to build. I hold that the courage is not in me but in the company — He will not fail thee, nor forsake thee, and that is the whole of it.
