maʻan

properly, heed, i.e. purpose; used only adverbially, on account of (as a motive or an aim), teleologically, in order that

Biblical Usage

The word means on account of, or for the sake of, and it is the hinge on which God's promises turn. Abram says it may be well with him for Sarai's sake, God gives His commandments that it may be well with His people, and He honours His father and mother commandment that their days may be long in the land. The word always points to the reason behind the blessing.

Theological Significance

I believe this small word carries the heart of the gospel: God blesses for His own name's sake, not because we have earned it.
