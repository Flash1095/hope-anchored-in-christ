akrobystía

from G206 and probably a modified form of πόσθη (the penis or male sexual organ); the prepuce; by implication, an uncircumcised (i.e. gentile, figuratively, unregenerate) state or person:--not circumcised, uncircumcised (with G2192), uncircumcision.

Biblical Usage

The word for the uncircumcised state — and, by extension, the Gentiles themselves. Paul argues that circumcision profits only if you keep the law, that Abraham was counted righteous while still in uncircumcision, that the called man should stay as he is, and that in Christ Jesus neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision, but faith which worketh by love. The word is the old dividing line, and Paul erases it.

Theological Significance

I hold that this word is where Paul's gospel to the nations turns. The mark in the flesh never saved anyone — Abraham believed before he was circumcised — and what counts now is faith working through love. The wall is down, and the Gentiles are in.
