pseúdomai

middle voice of an apparently primary verb; to utter an untruth or attempt to deceive by falsehood:--falsely, lie.

Biblical Usage

Pseudomai means to lie, to utter an untruth, to attempt to deceive by falsehood. Ananias lied to the Holy Ghost about the price of the land, and fell dead, and Paul says, Lie not one to another, seeing ye have put off the old man with his deeds. James says, if ye have bitter envying and strife in your hearts, glory not, and lie not against the truth, and Hebrews says it was impossible for God to lie, in the two immutable things wherein we have strong consolation.

Theological Significance

I believe lying is the old man's language: I put it off with him when I came to Christ, and every lie I tell now is a word from a life I left. I hold that God's inability to lie is my strong consolation, because the One who cannot lie is the One who promised me.
