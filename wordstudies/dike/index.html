díkē

probably from G1166; right (as self-evident), i.e. justice (the principle, a decision, or its execution):--judgment, punish, vengeance.

Biblical Usage

Dike is justice itself, the right sentence and the punishment that follows it. The islanders saw the viper fasten on Paul and assumed justice would not let a murderer live, and Jude holds up Sodom as an example of eternal fire. Paul warns that those who do not obey the gospel will be punished with everlasting destruction.

Theological Significance

I believe justice is real and terrible, which is why the cross matters so much. Christ bore the sentence I deserved, so that justice is satisfied and mercy can reach me.
