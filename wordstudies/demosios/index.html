dēmósios

from G1218; public; (feminine singular dative case as adverb) in public:--common, openly, publickly.

Biblical Usage

In Acts this word marks what happens out in the open, where everyone can see it. The apostles are put in the common prison, Paul is beaten openly and then demands a public release, Apollos refutes the Jews publicly, and Paul teaches openly from house to house.

Theological Significance

I believe the gospel was never meant to hide. When the apostles suffered in public and taught in public, the watching city could judge for itself whether their message was true, and that openness still commends the faith today.
