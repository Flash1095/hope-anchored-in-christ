diaginṓskō

from G1223 and G1097; to know thoroughly, i.e. ascertain exactly:--(would) enquire, know the uttermost.

Biblical Usage

To know thoroughly is to find out exactly, and Felix uses the word when he adjourns Paul's case, saying he will know the uttermost of the matter once Lysias comes down. He wants the full truth before he judges.

Theological Significance

I hold that Felix's delay is a warning to me; he knew enough to be afraid and never enough to decide, and a man can keep asking for more light while the light he already has is enough to act on.
