ʻâthaq

to remove (intransitive or transitive) figuratively, to grow old; specifically, to transcribe

Biblical Usage

To move away or grow old is the sense here, and the word covers a journey, a well, and a weary eye. Abram moved on from Bethel toward the south, Isaac moved away from the strife over the wells and found room at Rehoboth, the proverbs of Solomon were copied out by Hezekiah's men, and David says his eye grows old because of all his enemies.

Theological Significance

I believe the word holds two kinds of moving: the moving on of faith and the wearing down of sorrow. Abram and Isaac moved toward the promise, and I hold that God makes room for the ones who keep moving in trust.
