peíthō

a primary verb; to convince (by argument, true or false); by analogy, to pacify or conciliate (by other fair means); reflexively or passively, to assent (to evidence or authority), to rely (by inward certainty):--agree, assure, believe, have confidence, be (wax) conflent, make friend, obey, persuade, trust, yield.

Biblical Usage

Peitho covers persuading and being persuaded, believing and trusting. Some of the Thessalonians were persuaded by Paul's preaching and joined him, and Paul says he is persuaded by the Lord Jesus that nothing is unclean of itself. He asks the Galatians whether he seeks to persuade men or God, and the writer of Hebrews says he is persuaded better things of his readers, things that accompany salvation.

Theological Significance

What I believe is that persuasion is the work of the gospel: God does not drive His people, He convinces them. Paul's question still convicts me — am I trying to please men or God? — because a servant of Christ cannot do both. I hold that true conviction shows in a life, not just in agreement, and I am persuaded that God keeps those He has called.
