ampelṓn

from G288; a vineyard:--vineyard.

Biblical Usage

Jesus tells three vineyard stories, and the vineyard is always Israel, or the kingdom, in His hands. A householder hires labourers into his vineyard at every hour of the day and pays them all the same; another plants a vineyard, hedges it, builds a tower, and lets it to husbandmen who beat his servants and kill his son; and Mark's version ends with the same warning — the vineyard will be given to others. The vineyard is God's planting, and the question in every parable is what the tenants do with it.

Theological Significance

I believe the vineyard parables are about stewardship, not farming: God plants, God provides, and then He asks what we did with what He gave. The labourers who came late were paid in full because the master is generous, and the tenants who killed the son lost the vineyard because they forgot it was never theirs. Both stories end the same way — with the owner's right to do what He will with His own.
