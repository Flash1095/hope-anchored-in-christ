châgâb

a locust

Biblical Usage

Chagab is a locust, a grasshopper. The law allows the locust to be eaten, the spies said they were grasshoppers beside the giants, God warns that He may command the locusts to devour the land, and the preacher says in old age the grasshopper will be a burden.

Theological Significance

I believe the grasshopper is the Bible's picture of smallness: small enough to eat, small enough to fear giants, small enough to be a burden — and big enough, in God's hand, to strip a land bare. Smallness is not safety when God sends it, and it is not shame when He holds it.
