parazēlóō

from G3844 and G2206; to stimulate alongside, i.e. excite to rivalry:--provoke to emulation (jealousy).

Biblical Usage

Parazeloo means to provoke to jealousy, to stir up rivalry. God says through Moses in Romans 10:19 that He will provoke Israel to jealousy by a foolish nation, and Paul says in Romans 11:11 that salvation has come to the Gentiles to provoke them to jealousy. In Romans 11:14 Paul hopes to save some of his own flesh by that very provoking. The warning side stands in 1 Corinthians 10:22, asking whether we provoke the Lord to jealousy and whether we are stronger than He.

Theological Significance

I believe jealousy in God's hands is a tool of mercy: He uses the Gentiles to wake Israel, and Paul's whole ministry leans on that hope. But my position is that provoking the Lord Himself is the other edge of the word, and no one who tests it is stronger than He is.
