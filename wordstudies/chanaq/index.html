chânaq

to be narrow; by implication, to throttle, or (reflex.) to choke oneself to death (by a rope)

Biblical Usage

Chanaq is throttling, choking, and it appears twice, both times in death. Ahithophel, seeing his counsel was not followed, saddled his ass, went home, put his household in order, and hanged himself. Nahum pictures the lion that tore enough for his whelps and strangled for his lionesses, and then says God is against it.

Theological Significance

I believe the word is as blunt as the acts it names: counsel refused ends in a rope, and a lion's strength ends under God's judgment.
