synistáō

, or (strengthened) συνιστάνω, or συνίστημι from G4862 and G2476 (including its collateral forms); to set together, i.e. (by implication) to introduce (favorably), or (figuratively) to exhibit; intransitively, to stand near, or (figuratively) to constitute:--approve, commend, consist, make, stand (with).

Biblical Usage

Paul says God commends His love toward us, in that while we were yet sinners Christ died for us, and that by Christ all things consist, holding together in Him. He commends Phoebe to the Romans as a servant of the church, and in his letters he approves himself as a minister of God in much patience and affliction, and he warns that if he builds again the things he destroyed, he makes himself a transgressor. The word commends and establishes and proves, and at its heart it sets things together so they stand.

Theological Significance

I hold that God commended His love in the plainest way possible, by Christ dying for sinners, and I believe everything consists in Him, so nothing of mine stands apart from Him. I take Paul's warning to heart as well: I must not rebuild what God tore down, or I make myself a transgressor.
