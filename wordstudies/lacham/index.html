lâcham

to feed on; figuratively, to consume; by implication, to battle (as destruction)

Biblical Usage

To fight is one of the great verbs of the Old Testament, used a hundred and seventy-one times for war in every sense. In Exodus 14:14 Moses tells Israel, "The LORD shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace," in Joshua 10:25 Joshua tells the captains not to fear, for the Lord has done this to all their enemies, and in 1 Samuel 4:9 the Philistines urge one another to "quit yourselves like men, and fight."

Theological Significance

For me, this word's first use is the one that matters most: the Lord fights for His people, and my part is to hold my peace and watch Him do it. I hold my peace, and He fights.
