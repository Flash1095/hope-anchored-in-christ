tsᵉdâqâh

rightness (abstractly), subjectively (rectitude), objectively (justice), morally (virtue) or figuratively (prosperity)

Biblical Usage

Tsedaqah is righteousness itself — God's right way, and the right standing He gives. Abraham believed God and it was counted to him for righteousness; Moses told Israel their righteousness would be in keeping God's commandments; and Amos cries for righteousness to run down like a mighty stream. It is both who God is and what He counts as ours.

Theological Significance

I believe righteousness is the heart of the gospel in one word: God is righteous, and He makes sinners righteous by faith. My position is that Abraham's story is my story — belief counted as righteousness. And I hold that a faith that never runs down like a stream into justice for others is not the righteousness Amos preached.
