katoikéō

from G2596 and G3611; to house permanently, i.e. reside (literally or figuratively):--dwell(-er), inhabitant(-ter).

Biblical Usage

Katoikeo means to dwell permanently, to make a home and stay: Acts 22:12 speaks of the Jews which dwelt in Damascus, where Ananias lived. Ephesians 3:17 prays that Christ may dwell in your hearts by faith. 2 Peter 3:13 says we look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness. Revelation 17:8 says they that dwell on the earth shall wonder at the beast.

Theological Significance

I believe dwelling is the question of every life: Christ dwells in hearts by faith, righteousness dwells in the new earth, and the beast only impresses those who dwell on this earth. I hold that where I dwell decides what I worship, and I want Christ at home in me.
