pârar

to break up (usually figuratively), i.e. to violate, frustrate

Biblical Usage

Parar means to break, to frustrate, to make of none effect. Its heaviest use is covenant-breaking: the uncircumcised man has broken God's covenant in Genesis 17:14, and Moses warns in Deuteronomy 31:16 that Israel will break the covenant after his death. Leviticus 26:15 ties breaking the commandments to despising the statutes, and Jeremiah 11:10 says the house of Israel and the house of Judah have broken the covenant God made with their fathers.

Theological Significance

I believe a covenant is not broken by accident, it is broken by a choice, and the whole history of Israel turns on that choice. My position is that the same word that names man's breaking also names God's mercy, because He keeps His own word unbroken while the plans of men come to nothing.
