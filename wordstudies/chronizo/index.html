chronízō

from G5550; to take time, i.e. linger:--delay, tarry.

Biblical Usage

To delay is to take longer than expected, and Jesus warns of the servant who says his master delays his coming. The bridegroom tarries and all the virgins sleep, and the people wonder why Zacharias lingers so long in the temple. Hebrews answers the waiting: He who is coming will come, and will not tarry.

Theological Significance

I believe delay in Scripture is never God's absence; it is God's patience, and the waiting itself is part of the test. The servant who takes delay as permission to strike his fellows has misunderstood the master entirely. I hold that waiting well is one of the hardest and holiest things a believer does.
