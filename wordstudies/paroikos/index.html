pároikos

from G3844 and G3624; having a home near, i.e. (as noun) a by-dweller (alien resident):--foreigner, sojourn, stranger.

Biblical Usage

Paroikos is a stranger, a sojourner, one who dwells beside but does not belong. Stephen says in Acts 7:6 that Abraham's seed would sojourn in a strange land, and in Acts 7:29 Moses flees and becomes a stranger in the land of Midian. Paul turns the word to promise in Ephesians 2:19: now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints. Peter calls believers strangers and pilgrims in 1 Peter 2:11.

Theological Significance

I believe the gospel's great reversal is in this word: those who were strangers are made citizens, and those who were far off are brought near. That is why I hold that I should live like a stranger to the world and a citizen of heaven, because that is what I am.
