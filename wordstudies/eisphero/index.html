eisphérō

from G1519 and G5342; to carry inward (literally or figuratively):--bring (in), lead into.

Biblical Usage

To bring in is to carry inward, and the word appears in the prayer Jesus taught and in the sickbeds of Galilee. Lead us not into temptation, we pray, and the friends of a paralysed man carry him in on his bed to lay him before Jesus. Hebrews says the bodies of the animals whose blood is brought into the sanctuary are burned outside the camp, and the word holds both the prayer and the offering.

Theological Significance

I believe the prayer lead us not into temptation is the wisest prayer I can pray, because I know my own heart wanders and only God can keep me from the places I should not go. He brings us in; He also keeps us out.
