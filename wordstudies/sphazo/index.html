spházō

a primary verb; to butcher (especially an animal for food or in sacrifice) or (generally) to slaughter, or (specially), to maim (violently):--kill, slay, wound.

Biblical Usage

The slaying that matters most is the Lamb's. Cain slew his brother because his own works were evil and his brother's righteous, and in the midst of the throne stands a Lamb as it had been slain, and the elders cry, Worthy is the Lamb that was slain to receive power and riches and wisdom and strength, the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world.

Theological Significance

I believe the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world is the centre of the whole Bible, and I hold that His wounds are the reason the throne is a throne of grace. Cain's slaying shows the other side: evil kills its brother, and the Lamb was slain to end the killing.
