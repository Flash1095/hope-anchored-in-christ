lýō

a primary verb; to "loosen" (literally or figuratively):--break (up), destroy, dissolve, (un-)loose, melt, put off. Compare G4486.

Biblical Usage

The word means to loosen, and it stretches from untying a sandal to undoing the works of the devil. Jesus gives the keys of the kingdom with power to bind and loose, and He cries loose him at the tomb of Lazarus. Peter says God loosed the pains of death because it was not possible for death to hold Christ, and John says the Son of God appeared to destroy the works of the devil.

Theological Significance

I believe loosening is the work of Christ from first to last: He unties what sin has bound, and death itself could not hold Him. I hold that what He looses on earth is loosed in heaven, so His people need not stay tied to anything He has already undone.
