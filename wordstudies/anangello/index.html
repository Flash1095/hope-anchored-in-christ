anangéllō

from G303 and the base of G32; to announce (in detail):--declare, rehearse, report, show, speak, tell.

Biblical Usage

John says, 'This then is the message which we have heard of him, and declare unto you, that God is light, and in him is no darkness at all' — and Jesus promises the Spirit of truth will show His people things to come. Paul tells the Ephesian elders he has not shunned to declare all the counsel of God, and he tells Rome that those to whom Christ was not spoken of shall see. The word is declaring in full: the message heard, the counsel told, the future shown, and the name carried to those who never heard it.

Theological Significance

I believe declaring is the duty that follows hearing: John heard the message and declared it, Paul knew the counsel and would not shun it, and the Spirit shows things to come so that we can tell them. The gospel was never meant to stop with the hearer — it was heard so it could be declared, and I hold that is still the chain: hear it, then tell it.
