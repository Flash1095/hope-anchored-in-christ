hamartánō

perhaps from G1 (as a negative particle) and the base of G3313; properly, to miss the mark (and so not share in the prize), i.e. (figuratively) to err, especially (morally) to sin:--for your faults, offend, sin, trespass.

Biblical Usage

To sin is to miss the mark, and Paul states the case plainly in Romans 3:23: all have sinned and come short of the glory of God. Romans 5:12 traces it back to one man, and 1 John 2:1 gives the remedy, an advocate with the Father, while the prodigal in Luke 15:18 shows the way home, saying, I have sinned against heaven and before thee.

Theological Significance

I believe the doctrine only helps when it is personal: I have missed the mark, and I have an advocate. That is the whole gospel in one sentence, and it is the reason I can come back.
