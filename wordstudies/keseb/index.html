keseb

a young sheep

Biblical Usage

Keseb means a young sheep, a lamb: Genesis 30:40 says Jacob did separate the lambs, and set the faces of the flocks toward the ringstraked. Leviticus 7:23 says ye shall eat no manner of fat, of ox, or of sheep, or of goat. Numbers 18:17 says the firstling of a sheep thou shalt not redeem, for they are holy. Deuteronomy 14:4 lists the beasts which ye shall eat: the ox, the sheep, and the goat.

Theological Significance

I believe the lamb is the quiet centre of the whole law: separated by Jacob, kept holy as firstling, and eaten only as God commanded. I hold that every young sheep in these verses was a rehearsal for the Lamb who would be offered once.
