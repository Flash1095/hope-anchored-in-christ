ʼayil

properly, strength; hence, anything strong; specifically a chief (politically); also a ram (from his strength); a pilaster (as a strong support); an oak or other strong tree

Biblical Usage

Ayil is strength in several shapes — the ram of sacrifice, the oak, and the chief. God provided a ram caught in the thicket for Abraham in place of Isaac, Daniel saw a ram with two horns pushing westward, northward and southward, Isaiah says the oaks of righteousness are God's planting, and the ram's horns, the breast and the thigh belong to the priest in the consecration offering. Strength, offered back to God, becomes worship.

Theological Significance

I believe the ram in the thicket is the whole gospel in one picture: God provides the substitute himself. And I hold that the strength God gives me — like the ram's — is meant to be laid on his altar, not kept for myself.
