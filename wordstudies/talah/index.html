tâlâh

to suspend (especially to gibbet)

Biblical Usage

Moses says he that is hanged is accursed of God, and his body shall not remain all night upon the tree. Joseph tells the chief baker that Pharaoh will lift up his head from off him, and hang him on a tree, and Haman is hanged on the gallows he prepared for Mordecai. The word is to suspend, to hang, to gibbet: the baker, the blasphemer, and the plotter, all lifted up on wood.

Theological Significance

The hanging carries the weight of the curse in the law, I hold, and I believe that is why Paul can say Christ redeemed us from the curse, being made a curse for us: He was lifted up on the same kind of tree, and the curse fell on Him instead of me.
