yôwbêl

the blast of a horn (from its continuous sound); specifically, the signal of the silver trumpets; hence, the instrument itself and the festival thus introduced

Biblical Usage

Yobel is the horn blast that opens the jubilee. In Exodus 19:13 the long trumpet blast calls the people up the mountain, and in Leviticus 25:10 the fiftieth year is hallowed and liberty is proclaimed "throughout all the land unto all the inhabitants thereof." Numbers 36:4 shows the jubilee resetting inheritances, and in Joshua 6:4 the rams' horns go before the ark as Jericho falls. The same sound means release, return, and God's victory.

Theological Significance

I believe the jubilee is the sound of debts cancelled and slaves set free, and I hear the gospel in it. I hold that God built rest and return into Israel's calendar because he never meant his people to stay in bondage, and the trumpet that brought down Jericho's walls is the same kind of sound that announces freedom. My position is that yobel is good news, and it still is.
