ʼĔdôm

Edom, the elder twin-brother of Jacob; hence the region (Idumaea) occupied by him

Biblical Usage

Edom is Esau, the elder twin who sold his birthright for red stew and was called Edom, red, and it is also his descendants and their land south-east of Judah. Genesis traces the kings of Edom before Israel had a king, and Numbers tells how Edom refused Israel passage through their land. The name runs from one man's appetite to a nation's pride.

Theological Significance

I believe Esau's story warns me that a single careless choice can shape generations, because Edom's pride grew from his contempt for the birthright. God keeps His promises to Jacob's line, and He judges Edom's line for what it did with its own.
