ʻâsôwr

ten; by abbreviated form ten strings, and so a decachord

Biblical Usage

This word means ten, and it shows up in two places that could not be more different. It fixes the tenth day of the seventh month as the Day of Atonement, and it names the instrument of ten strings on which the psalms were sung. The same number counts a holy day and strings a song.

Theological Significance

I believe God gave His people both a calendar and a song, and the number ten ties them together. Atonement and praise belong in the same life, and I hold that the God who sets the day also tunes the instrument.
