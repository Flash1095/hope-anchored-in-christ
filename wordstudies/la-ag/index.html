lâʻag

to deride; by implication (as if imitating a foreigner) to speak unintelligibly

Biblical Usage

To mock is the verb of scorn, and it runs through the prophets and the wisdom books. Hezekiah's messengers were laughed to scorn in 2 Chronicles 30:10, Sennacherib was laughed to scorn by the daughter of Zion in 2 Kings 19:21, Jeremiah says in Jeremiah 20:7, "I am in derision daily, every one mocketh me," and Job's friends are told in Job 9:23 that God "will laugh at the trial of the innocent."

Theological Significance

I am convinced mockery is the weapon of the proud and the lot of the prophet: the messengers were mocked, Jeremiah was mocked daily, and God Himself laughs last at those who laugh at His people. Jeremiah was mocked daily, and the mockers did not have the last word.
