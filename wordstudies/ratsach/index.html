râtsach

properly, to dash in pieces, i.e. kill (a human being), especially to murder

Biblical Usage

Ratsach is to kill a human being — to dash in pieces, and by law, to murder. The commandment is short: 'Thou shalt not kill.' Moses set apart the cities of refuge, 'That the slayer might flee thither, which should kill his neighbour unawares, and hated him not in times past; and that fleeing unto one of these cities he might live'; and the law distinguishes the manslayer who killed unawares from the murderer who hated and lay in wait.

Theological Significance

I believe the sixth commandment draws the line the whole Bible honours: life is God's, and no man takes it but by His leave. And I hold that the cities of refuge show God's heart in the law — even the accidental killer had a place to run, because God makes room for mercy inside justice.
