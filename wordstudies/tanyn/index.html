tanŷn

or τὰ νῦν from neuter plural of G3588 and G3568; the things now, i.e. (adverbially) at present:--(but) now.

Biblical Usage

This is the little phrase for but now, the things now, the present moment; in the Greek it is two words, the things now, standing together as one phrase. The disciples pray, And now, Lord, behold their threatenings; Gamaliel says, And now I say unto you, Refrain from these men; and Paul tells Athens that God now commandeth all men every where to repent.

Theological Significance

I believe but now is where God turns the story: the threats are real, and now is the time He answers.
