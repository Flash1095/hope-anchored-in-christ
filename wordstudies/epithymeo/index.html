epithyméō

from G1909 and G2372; to set the heart upon, i.e. long for (rightfully or otherwise):--covet, desire, would fain, lust (after).

Biblical Usage

Epithymeo means to set the heart upon, to long for — rightly or wrongly. Whosoever looketh on a woman to lust after her hath committed adultery already in his heart; with desire I have desired to eat this passover with you; I had not known lust, except the law had said, Thou shalt not covet; and the flesh lusteth against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh.

Theological Significance

Set on Christ, desire is devotion; set on what is forbidden, it is adultery before the act. I believe desire is the hinge of the heart, and the law's job is to name it so I can fight it — before it becomes the deed.
