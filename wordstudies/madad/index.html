mâdad

properly, to stretch; by implication, to measure (as if by stretching a line); figuratively, to be extended

Biblical Usage

Madad means to measure, to stretch the line, and it belongs to God before it belongs to men. Isaiah 40:12 asks who hath measured the waters in the hollow of His hand, and Jeremiah 31:37 says if heaven above can be measured, then God will cast off Israel, which is to say He never will. Exodus 16:18 has the manna meted with an omer so that none had too much or too little, and Ezekiel 47:3 has the man measuring a thousand cubits and bringing the prophet through waters that rise to the ankles.

Theological Significance

I believe this word puts me in my place: God measures the seas in His hand, and I measure out my little lines and call them great. My position is that the manna in Exodus 16:18 is the comfort of it, because the God who measures all things measures out exactly enough for me.
