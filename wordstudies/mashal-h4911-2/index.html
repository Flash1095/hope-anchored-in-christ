mâshal

to liken, i.e. (transitively) to use figurative language (an allegory, adage, song or the like); in transitively, to resemble

Biblical Usage

mâshal is to liken, to speak in a proverb or parable, and it is also to rule. In Ezekiel 16:44 everyone who uses proverbs shall use this proverb against Jerusalem, as is the mother, so is her daughter, and in Ezekiel 17:2 the prophet is told to put forth a riddle and speak a parable. In Ezekiel 12:23 God says He will make the people's proverb cease, and in Ezekiel 18:2 He challenges the proverb about sour grapes. The word is truth carried in a picture.

Theological Significance

I believe mâshal teaches me that God speaks in pictures when plain words would be ignored. I hold that His proverbs are true even when I dislike them.
