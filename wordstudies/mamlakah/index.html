mamlâkâh

dominion, i.e. (abstractly) the estate (rule) or (concretely) the country (realm)

Biblical Usage

mamlâkâh is a kingdom, both the rule itself and the land ruled. In Exodus 19:6 Israel is promised as a kingdom of priests, and in 2 Samuel 7:16 David is told his house and his kingdom shall be established for ever. In 1 Samuel 24:20 Saul himself admits the kingdom of Israel will be established in David's hand, and Isaiah 9:7 looks to a kingdom with no end. The word stretches from earthly thrones to God's own rule.

Theological Significance

I believe mamlâkâh puts every earthly kingdom in its place beneath God's. Kings rise and fall, and I hold that only the kingdom promised to David's line, fulfilled in Christ, stands for ever. That is the kingdom I want to belong to.
