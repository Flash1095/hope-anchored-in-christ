châlam

properly, to bind firmly, i.e. (by implication) to be (causatively to make) plump; also (through the figurative sense of dumbness) to dream

Biblical Usage

Chalam means to dream — and, in another sense, to be strong and healthy. Jacob dreamed of a ladder reaching to heaven, the law warns of a prophet or a dreamer of dreams, Gideon's enemy told his fellow a dream of a barley cake, and Job says the young of the wild ass are in good liking and grow up with corn.

Theological Significance

I believe dreams and health share a root because both are gifts that come in the night: God speaks while men sleep, and young creatures grow strong while no one watches. The dreamer of Deuteronomy is the warning that not every night voice is His.
