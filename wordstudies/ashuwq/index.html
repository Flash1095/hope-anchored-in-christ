ʻâshûwq

used in plural masculine as abstractly, tyranny

Biblical Usage

Oppression is the cry of the pressed-down, and the prophets and the Preacher hear it together. Amos is told to publish in the palaces of Ashdod that there are great tumults and oppressions within her; the Preacher returns and considers all the oppressions done under the sun, and beholds the tears of such as were oppressed, and they had no comforter; and Elihu says men cry out by reason of the arm of the mighty.

Theological Significance

I believe oppression is the sin God hears first, because the oppressed cry and the cry goes up to Him: the tumults in the palaces, the tears with no comforter, the arm of the mighty pressing down. And I hold that the God who hears the cry is the God who will answer it, and the oppressor's arm is not the last word.
