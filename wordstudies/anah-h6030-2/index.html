ʻânâh

properly, to eye or (generally) to heed, i.e. pay attention; by implication, to respond; by extension to begin to speak; specifically to sing, shout, testify, announce

Biblical Usage

The word means to answer, and it is the whole back-and-forth of Scripture in one verb: in Hebrew it appears over three hundred times, from Abraham answering, 'Behold now, I have taken upon me to speak unto the Lord, which am but dust and ashes,' to the LORD answering Job out of the whirlwind. In Aramaic the same word answers in Daniel's court, where Daniel blessed the name of God and the three friends told the king they were not careful to answer him in the matter of the golden image. A prophet answering a king, a friend answering a friend, God answering man — it is the speaking and the hearing that runs through the Bible.

Theological Significance

A word for answering carries no doctrine of its own; its weight is the conversation — God speaks, and men answer, and God answers again, and I believe that is what prayer is.
