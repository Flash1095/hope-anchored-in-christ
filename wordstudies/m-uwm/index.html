mʼûwm

to stain; a blemish (physically or morally)

Biblical Usage

The word names a blemish, a stain or defect, and the law would not accept an offering with one: no animal with a blemish could be offered, and no priest with a blemish could come near to offer bread. The same word reaches beyond the body to the heart, for Deuteronomy calls a crooked generation a blemish upon themselves, yet the Song says of the beloved that there is no spot in her, and Absalom is praised as having no blemish from head to foot.

Theological Significance

I believe the blemish laws teach us that God is holy and His worship is not careless, yet the same word shows His grace, because He calls His beloved spotless. I hold that only Christ is truly without blemish, and He is the offering God accepts on our behalf.
