râchaq

to widen (in any direction), i.e. (intransitively) recede or (transitively) remove (literally or figuratively, of place or relation)

Biblical Usage

Rachaq is to widen, to recede, to remove far off — of place or of relation. Agur prayed, 'Remove far from me vanity and lies'; the psalm says, 'As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us'; and Isaiah confesses, 'Therefore is judgment far from us, neither doth justice overtake us.'

Theological Significance

This tells me that the distance runs both ways: God removes our sins far from us, and our sins remove His judgment far from us — and the prayer is that He would close the second distance and keep the first.
