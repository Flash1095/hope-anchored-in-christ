anaklínō

from G303 and G2827; to lean back:--lay, (make) sit down.

Biblical Usage

The word means to lay down, or to make to sit down: Mary brought forth her firstborn son and laid Him in a manger, because there was no room for them in the inn, and Jesus commanded the multitude to sit down on the grass before He broke the bread. It is used for a mother laying a baby down and for a host seating a crowd of five thousand.

Theological Significance

A word for laying down carries no doctrine of its own; its weight is the two layings — a baby in a manger, and five thousand seated on the grass to be fed.
