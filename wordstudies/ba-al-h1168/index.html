Baʻal

Baal, a Phoenician deity

Biblical Usage

Baal was the Phoenician god of storm and fertility, and Israel's great rival for a thousand years: Elijah stood against his prophets on Carmel and the fire fell, Jehu gathered his worshippers into the temple and destroyed them, and Hosea says Israel forgot God and went after Baal, while Gideon tore down his father's altar to Baal in the night. The name means lord, and the whole fight was over who is Lord.

Theological Significance

I believe the fight with Baal is the fight of the whole Old Testament, and Carmel settles it: the Lord, he is God, and Baal is nothing. Seven thousand knees never bowed to him, and I mean to be counted among them.
