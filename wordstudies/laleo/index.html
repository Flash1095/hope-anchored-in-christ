laléō

a prolonged form of an otherwise obsolete verb; to talk, i.e. utter words:--preach, say, speak (after), talk, tell, utter. Compare G3004.

Biblical Usage

To speak is the ordinary word for uttering words, used two hundred and seventy-three times, and it carries the whole weight of what is said. In John 3:11 Jesus says, "We speak that we do know, and testify that we have seen," in 1 Corinthians 2:6 Paul says, "we speak wisdom among them that are perfect," and in 1 Peter 3:10 the man who would love life must refrain his tongue from evil.

Theological Significance

What I take from this is that this word's lesson is in John 3:11: we speak what we know, and what we know is what we have seen, and the gospel is not opinion but testimony. Testimony, not opinion: that is what the word demands of my mouth.
