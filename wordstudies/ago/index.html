ágō

a primary verb; properly, to lead; by implication, to bring, drive, (reflexively) go, (specially) pass (time), or (figuratively) induce:--be, bring (forth), carry, (let) go, keep, lead away, be open.

Biblical Usage

Ago is to lead — to bring, to carry, to go. Jesus sends the disciples, 'Go into the village over against you'; He says, 'Let us go into Judaea again'; Paul says the goodness of God leadeth to repentance; and he tells the Galatians that if they are led by the Spirit they are not under the law. The word covers a journey, a command, and a life led by God — and the difference is always who is doing the leading.

Theological Significance

I hold that this word asks me a simple question: who is leading me? The goodness of God leads to repentance, the Spirit leads out from under the law, and the voice that says 'go' is the same voice that went to the cross — and I want to be led by Him.
