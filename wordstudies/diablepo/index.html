diablépō

from G1223 and G991; to look through, i.e. recover full vision:--see clearly.

Biblical Usage

To see clearly is what the blind man in Bethsaida receives and what the Pharisee in the parable lacks. The blind man looks up and sees every man clearly, while the man with the beam in his own eye is told to cast it out first, so that he can see clearly to help his brother.

Theological Significance

I believe clear sight begins at home. I cannot help my brother with his fault while my own beam blinds me, and the mercy of Christ is that He opens blind eyes — first mine, then through me, others.
