maṭṭârâʼ

a jail (as a guard-house); also an aim (as being closely watched)

Biblical Usage

The word is the prison and the mark, and Jeremiah knows both: he is shut up in the court of the prison while Jerusalem falls, and the LORD tells him the city is given into the enemy's hand. Job says God has set him up for His mark, and Lamentations says the same of Jerusalem, set as a mark for the arrow.

Theological Significance

I believe the word holds the two hardest pictures of the faithful life together: the prison cell and the target. Jeremiah was locked up for telling the truth, and Job was set up as a mark, and neither of them was abandoned — God was in the cell with Jeremiah and behind the shield with Job.
