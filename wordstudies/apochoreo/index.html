apochōréō

from G575 and G5562; to go away:--depart.

Biblical Usage

To go away, to depart. John departs from Paul and Barnabas in Acts 13:13 and returns to Jerusalem, and the spirit in Luke 9:39 hardly departs from the tormented boy. Jesus gives the word its heaviest use in Matthew 7:23, to those who worked iniquity in His name: ‘depart from me, ye that work iniquity.’

Theological Significance

I believe the same going-away can be a desertion, a deliverance, or a judgment, and only the direction tells which. John left the work; the spirit left the boy; the workers of iniquity will be told to leave Him. What matters is not that someone departs, but from whom, and to where.
