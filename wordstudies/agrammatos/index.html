agrámmatos

from G1 (as negative particle) and G1121; unlettered, i.e. illiterate:--unlearned.

Biblical Usage

Agrammatos means unlettered — illiterate, without formal schooling — and it appears once, in the council's reaction to Peter and John: 'they perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marvelled; and they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus.' The word is the council's insult and the story's proof: men without letters, speaking with a boldness no school could teach.

Theological Significance

Unlearned men stood bold before the council, and this word only says they had no schooling — their boldness said the rest.
