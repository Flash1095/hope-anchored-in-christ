oikéō

from G3624; to occupy a house, i.e. reside (figuratively, inhabit, remain, inhere); by implication, to cohabit:--dwell. See also G3625.

Biblical Usage

Oikeo means to dwell, to live in a house, and by figure, to inhabit a heart. Paul says sin dwells in him in Romans 7:17 and 7:18, in his flesh dwells no good thing, and then turns the word around in 1 Corinthians 3:16: the Spirit of God dwells in you. The same word lifts to heaven in 1 Timothy 6:16, where God dwells in the light no man can approach.

Theological Significance

I believe the question of the Christian life is who dwells in you, because sin and the Spirit both claim the same house. My position is that the Spirit's indwelling is the proof of the gospel: God does not visit His people, He moves in.
