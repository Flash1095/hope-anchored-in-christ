nēsteía

from G3522; abstinence (from lack of food, or voluntary and religious); specially, the fast of the Day of Atonement:-- fast(-ing).

Biblical Usage

Fasting in the New Testament is prayer with the body: Anna serves God with fastings and prayers night and day in the temple, the churches fast and pray before they lay hands on elders, and Paul lists fastings among the marks of his ministry. The one great fast of the year, the Day of Atonement, even sets the sailing season in Acts.

Theological Significance

I hold that fasting is not a way to earn God's ear but a way to clear it — the body goes without so the heart can listen. Anna fasted in the temple for decades, and the church fasted for a day — both of them were heard.
