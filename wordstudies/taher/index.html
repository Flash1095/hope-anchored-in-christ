ṭâhêr

to be pure (physical sound, clear, unadulterated; Levitically, uncontaminated; morally, innocent or holy)

Biblical Usage

The priest pronounces the leper clean, and sprinkles him seven times. God promises, I will sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean. Naaman dips seven times in Jordan, and his flesh comes again like a child's, and David prays, purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean; wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow. The word is to be clean, pure: washed by water, by blood, and by God Himself.

Theological Significance

I hold that cleansing is God's work before it is ours. I believe David's prayer says it best: purge me, wash me — and the promise that follows is just as sure: He will sprinkle clean water, and we shall be clean.
