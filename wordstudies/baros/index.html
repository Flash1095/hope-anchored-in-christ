báros

probably from the same as G939 (through the notion of going down; compare G899); weight; in the New Testament only, figuratively, a load, abundance, authority:--burden(-some), weight.

Biblical Usage

Baros is weight, a burden, a load to be carried. The labourers in the vineyard complain they have borne the burden of the day, Paul tells the Galatians to bear one another's burdens, the council at Jerusalem lays no greater burden on the Gentiles than what is necessary, and Paul says our light affliction works a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory.

Theological Significance

I believe this word teaches us to tell the weights apart, because some burdens are meant to be shared and some are meant to be laid down. The burden of the day is borne together, the burden of the law is lifted by the council, and I hold that the weight of glory is the one burden no believer will ever regret carrying.
