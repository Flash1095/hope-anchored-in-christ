layish

a lion (from his destructive blows)

Biblical Usage

Layish the lion is a different word from Laish the place, though they share the same letters — this one is the beast, the old lion of the proverbs. In Isaiah 30:6, "from whence [come] the young and old lion"; and in Job 4:11, "and the stout lion's whelps are scattered abroad.". In Proverbs 30:30, "A lion [which is] strongest among beasts".

Theological Significance

I believe the old lion teaches me what strength looks like when it is gone — the strongest beast perishes for lack of prey, and turns not away for any. I hold that human strength is the same: fearsome for a season, and then scattered. My position is that I will not put my trust in the lion's strength, but in the God who made the lion.
