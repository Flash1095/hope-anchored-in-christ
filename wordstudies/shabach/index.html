shâbach

properly, to address in a loud tone, i.e. (specifically) loud; figuratively, to pacify (as if by words)

Biblical Usage

Shabach is praising aloud, and also stilling, pacifying — the same loud word does both. David's song says it plainly: give thanks to Thy holy name, and glory in Thy praise; and my lips shall praise Thee, because Thy lovingkindness is better than life. The Preacher praises the dead more than the living. And the wise man does the other half of it: a fool uttereth all his mind, but a wise man keepeth it in till afterwards — he stills what the fool lets loose.

Theological Significance

I believe praise and stillness belong together: the lips that praise God are the lips that keep the mind in till afterwards. The fool utters all and praises nothing; the wise man praises God and stills himself. And I hold that the praise that is better than life is the praise that comes from a still heart.
