shâlach

to send away, for, or out (in a great variety of applications)

Biblical Usage

Shalach is to send, and it is one of the great verbs of the Bible, used for men, angels, and God Himself. The LORD sent Adam forth from the garden, and sent Moses to Pharaoh to bring His people out of Egypt. Isaiah heard the voice of the Lord asking whom shall I send, and answered, here am I, send me, and the psalm says God sends out His word and the waters flow.

Theological Significance

I believe sending is how God works, because He does not leave His people where they are. He sent Moses to Pharaoh, He sent Isaiah to Judah, and He sent His word to melt the ice. That is why I answer the way Isaiah did, here am I, send me.
