entrépō

from G1722 and the base of G5157; to invert, i.e. (figuratively and reflexively) in a good sense, to respect; or in a bad one, to confound:--regard, (give) reference, shame.

Biblical Usage

Entrepo turns two ways: to respect, and to shame. Paul writes not to shame the Corinthians but to warn them as beloved sons in 1 Corinthians 4:14, and the disobedient brother is to be noted that he may be ashamed in 2 Thessalonians 3:14. We gave reverence to our earthly fathers who corrected us in Hebrews 12:9, and the unjust judge in Luke 18:2 feared not God and regarded not man.

Theological Significance

I believe shame and reverence are two sides of the same coin — both come from knowing who stands before us. I hold that a father who corrects earns reverence, a brother who is shamed may be won back, and the Judge who neither fears God nor regards man is the warning of what I must never become.
