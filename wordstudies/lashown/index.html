lâshôwn

the tongue (of man or animals), used literally (as the instrument of licking, eating, or speech), and figuratively (speech, an ingot, a fork of flame, a cove of water)

Biblical Usage

lâshôwn means the tongue — and its range, from the ordinary to the holy, is the point. In Proverbs 18:21, "Death and life [are] in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof."; and in Psalms 12:4, "With our tongue will we prevail". In Isaiah 50:4, "The Lord GOD hath given me the tongue of the learned"; and in Proverbs 15:4, "A wholesome tongue [is] a tree of life: but perverseness therein [is] a breach in the spirit.".

Theological Significance

I believe the tongue is the smallest member with the largest reach — death and life hang on it, and no one tames it alone. I hold that God gave Isaiah a tongue that could speak a word in season to the weary, and that is what I want mine to do. My position is that every word I speak is either a gift or a wound, and I answer to God for which one it was.
