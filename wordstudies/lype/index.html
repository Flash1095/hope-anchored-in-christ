lýpē

apparently a primary word; sadness:--grief, grievous, + grudgingly, heaviness, sorrow.

Biblical Usage

The word names sorrow, grief and heaviness, and Scripture is honest about how much of it God's people carry. The disciples' hearts were filled with sorrow at Jesus' farewell words, and Paul says godly sorrow works repentance to salvation, while the sorrow of the world works death. Even chastening seems grievous for the present, yet afterward it yields the peaceable fruit of righteousness.

Theological Significance

I believe sorrow is not wasted in God's hands, because He tells the two sorrows apart and uses the godly one to bring us home. I hold that the grief that drives us to repentance is a gift, and the grief that only hardens us is the world's sorrow, which ends in death.
