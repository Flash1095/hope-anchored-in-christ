gânab

to thieve (literally or figuratively); by implication, to deceive

Biblical Usage

Ganab means to steal, and it steals things, hearts, and people alike. Rachel stole her father's images in Genesis 31:19, and Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel in 2 Samuel 15:6. The law commands that a man found stealing a brother shall die in Deuteronomy 24:7, and Jehosheba stole the child Joash from among the king's sons that were slain in 2 Chronicles 22:11, and hid him from Athaliah.

Theological Significance

I believe stealing is the same sin whether it takes an idol, a heart, or a child. Rachel stole what was not hers and Absalom stole what was not his, yet Jehosheba stole a child to save his life. I hold that the command against stealing stands, and the mercy that hides the innocent stands beside it.
