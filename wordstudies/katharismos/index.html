katharismós

from G2511; a washing off, i.e. (ceremonially) ablution, (morally) expiation:--cleansing, + purge, purification(-fying).

Biblical Usage

Purification is the washing off, and the Son purged our sins by Himself and sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high, and the days of Mary's purification were accomplished, and there arose a question between John's disciples and the Jews about purifying, and Peter warns of the one who has forgotten that he was purged from his old sins. The word holds the cleansing of the sinner and the ceremony of the law together, and both point to the same blood.

Theological Significance

I believe my purification was finished by the Son Himself, and that is why He sat down. I hold that forgetting I was purged is the first step back into the old sins, and I will remember what the blood has done.
