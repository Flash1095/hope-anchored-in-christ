kópos

from G2875; a cut, i.e. (by analogy) toil (as reducing the strength), literally or figuratively; by implication, pains:--labour, + trouble, weariness.

Biblical Usage

Kopos is labour, toil, weariness, and by extension trouble. Matthew 26:10 and Mark 14:6 have Jesus saying, why trouble ye the woman, for she hath wrought a good work upon me, Luke 11:7 has the man in bed saying, trouble me not, John 4:38 says other men laboured, and ye are entered into their labours, 1 Corinthians 3:8 says every man shall receive his own reward according to his own labour, and 2 Corinthians 6:5 lists labours among Paul's stripes and imprisonments and fastings.

Theological Significance

I believe the labour is honoured in every verse: the woman's good work, the planters' and waterers' reward, and Paul's labours in stripes. I hold that the trouble in Matthew 26:10 and the labour in 1 Corinthians 3:8 are the same word, because the work that troubles the critics is the work God rewards.
