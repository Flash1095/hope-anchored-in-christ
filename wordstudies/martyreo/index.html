martyréō

from G3144; to be a witness, i.e. testify (literally or figuratively):--charge, give (evidence), bear record, have (obtain, of) good (honest) report, be well reported of, testify, give (have) testimony, (be, bear, give, obtain) witness.

Biblical Usage

martyréō is to bear witness, to testify, and it carries the weight of the whole New Testament. In 1 John 1:2 the apostles have seen the life manifested and bear witness and show it, and in 1 John 4:14 they testify that the Father sent the Son to be the Saviour of the world. In 1 Corinthians 15:15 Paul says the apostles would be false witnesses of God if Christ were not raised, and in 1 John 5:6 the Spirit bears witness because the Spirit is truth. Witness here is sworn testimony, not opinion.

Theological Significance

I believe martyréō teaches me that a witness is not a debater but someone who has seen. I hold that my testimony stands or falls on what I have seen of Christ.
