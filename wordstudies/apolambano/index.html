apolambánō

from G575 and G2983; to receive (specially, in full, or as a host); also to take aside:--receive, take.

Biblical Usage

To receive in full — sometimes as a gift, sometimes as a reckoning. The father received his son safe and sound in Luke 15:27 and killed the fatted calf, and Abraham tells the rich man in Luke 16:25 that he received his good things in his lifetime, while Lazarus received evil things. Jesus promises in Luke 18:30 that whoever leaves all for His sake shall receive manifold more, and Paul warns in Romans 1:27 that men receive in themselves the recompence of their error.

Theological Significance

I believe the word is a ledger word: what I receive in full is what I chose in full. The rich man received his good things and found them spent; the disciple who leaves all receives manifold more, in this present time and in the world to come. I want to be sure of what I am receiving — and from whom.
