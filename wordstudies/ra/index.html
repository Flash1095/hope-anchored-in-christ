raʻ

bad or (as noun) evil (natural or moral)

Biblical Usage

Ra' is the Bible's ordinary word for bad and evil — bad in the natural sense, like bad figs or a sore disease, and bad in the moral sense, like an evil heart. It first appears at the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, and from there it covers everything that is wrong: wicked men, evil deeds, sore troubles, and the disasters God sends in judgment. Proverbs says the fear of the LORD is to hate evil, and Isaiah warns those who call evil good and good evil.

Theological Significance

My position is that ra' forces me to face that evil is real and not a misunderstanding: the tree was forbidden because evil is something to know by avoiding, not by tasting. And my position is that God is never the author of moral evil, even when He sends calamity in judgment — the same word covers both, and the difference is whose hand is doing the evil. That is why the Bible's answer to evil is not an explanation but a person: the One who was wounded for our transgressions.
