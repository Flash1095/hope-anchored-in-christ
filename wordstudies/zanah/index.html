zânâh

to commit adultery (usually of the female, and less often of simple fornication, rarely of involuntary ravishment); figuratively, to commit idolatry (the Jewish people being regarded as the spouse of Jehovah)

Biblical Usage

Zanah is to commit adultery, and Scripture uses it for the body and for the soul. After Dinah is defiled, Genesis 34:31 asks, "Should he deal with our sister as with an harlot?" Exodus 34:15 warns against covenants with the inhabitants of the land, lest they go a whoring after their gods. Leviticus 17:7 forbids sacrificing "unto devils, after whom they have gone a whoring," and Numbers 15:39 ties the fringes on the garments to remembering the commandments, "that ye seek not after your own heart and your own eyes, after which ye use to go a whoring."

Theological Significance

I believe God chose this word to shock me, because he calls idolatry what it is: unfaithfulness to a husband. I hold that Exodus 34:15 and Leviticus 17:7 are saying the same thing, that worshipping other gods is adultery against the Lord, and that Numbers 15:39 gives me the guard against it, remembering his commandments. My position is that zanah leaves no room for a divided heart.
