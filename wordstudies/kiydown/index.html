kîydôwn

properly, something to strike with, i.e. a dart

Biblical Usage

Kidown is a dart, something to strike with. Joshua 8:18 has the LORD telling Joshua to stretch out the spear toward Ai, for I will give it into thine hand, and Joshua 8:26 says he drew not his hand back until he had utterly destroyed all the inhabitants of Ai. 1 Samuel 17:6 arms Goliath with a target of brass between his shoulders, and 1 Samuel 17:45 has David answering, thou comest to me with a sword, and with a spear, and with a shield, but I come to thee in the name of the LORD of hosts. Job 39:23 hears the quiver rattle against the war horse, the glittering spear and the shield, and Jeremiah 6:23 arms the invader with bow and spear, cruel, and with no mercy.

Theological Significance

I believe the spear in these verses is always a signal, not just a weapon: Joshua's stretched spear is God's promise, and David's answer is God's name. I hold that the dart never decides the battle; the hand that stretches it, and the name it is stretched in, decide it.
