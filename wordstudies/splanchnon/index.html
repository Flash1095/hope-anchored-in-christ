splánchnon

probably strengthened from σπλήν (the "spleen"); an intestine (plural); figuratively, pity or sympathy:--bowels, inward affection, + tender mercy.

Biblical Usage

The bowels are the seat of pity, and the word covers the whole range from God's mercy to a man's hard heart. Through the tender mercy of our God the dayspring visits us, believers are to put on bowels of mercies and kindness, the bowels of the saints are refreshed by Philemon's love, and the man who shutteth up his bowels of compassion from his brother has no love of God in him.

Theological Significance

I believe pity lives in the gut before it reaches the hands, and I hold that a heart that will not feel for a brother in need has not yet learned the mercy of God. The tender mercy that visited us is the mercy we are to put on.
