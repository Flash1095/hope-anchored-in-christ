árrhōstos

from G1 (as a negative particle) and a presumed derivative of G4517; infirm:--sick (folk, -ly).

Biblical Usage

The sick are those without strength, and the word is used for the crowds Jesus healed and for the church at Corinth. Jesus went forth and saw a great multitude, and was moved with compassion toward them, and He healed their sick; and Paul says many at Corinth are weak and sickly because they eat and drink the Lord's supper unworthily.

Theological Significance

One word for the sick carries no doctrine of its own; its weight is the contrast, that Jesus heals the sick who come to Him, and that the same Lord will let His own people grow sick when they despise His table.
