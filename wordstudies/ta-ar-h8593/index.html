taʻar

a knife or razor (as making bare); also a scabbard (as being bare, i.e. empty)

Biblical Usage

Ezekiel is told to take a sharp sword as a barber's razor and shave his head and beard, a sign of the judgment coming on Jerusalem. The Nazirite vow forbids any razor on the head until the days are fulfilled, while David takes Goliath's sword and cuts off his head with it. The word is a razor, and a sheath: the blade that makes bare, and the case that holds it.

Theological Significance

I believe the razor cuts both ways in Scripture: a sign of judgment on Jerusalem, and a mark of consecration on the Nazirite, and the difference is whose hand holds it.
