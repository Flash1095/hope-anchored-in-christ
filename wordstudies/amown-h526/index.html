ʼÂmôwn

Amon, the name of three Israelites

Biblical Usage

Three men in Scripture carry the name Amon, and the king is the one most people meet first: Amon son of Manasseh took the throne of Judah at twenty-two and reigned only two years, doing evil as his father had done and sacrificing to the carved images Manasseh had made, until his own servants conspired against him and killed him, yet Matthew names him in the genealogy of Jesus — Manasseh begat Amon, and Amon begat Josiah — so the line of the promise runs straight through a wicked king and out the other side. The other two are smaller figures: Amon the governor of Samaria, who held the prophet Micaiah in prison, and Amon among the servants of Solomon whose children came back from exile.

Theological Significance

Amon's two years on the throne tell me that a king can undo nothing and build nothing — he only followed his father's idols and died for it, and his son Josiah had to tear it all down. Yet I believe the genealogy is the point: God's promise did not depend on Amon being good, and the line kept going through him anyway, which gives me hope for families where the middle of the story looks ruined.
