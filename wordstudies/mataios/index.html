mátaios

from the base of G3155; empty, i.e. (literally) profitless, or (specially), an idol:--vain, vanity.

Biblical Usage

Vain means empty, profitless, and the New Testament uses it for everything that promises life and delivers nothing. Paul says faith is vain if Christ was not raised, and Peter says we were redeemed from the empty way of life handed down by our fathers. Paul tells the crowd at Lystra to turn from these vanities to the living God, and James warns that a man who cannot bridle his tongue has a vain religion.

Theological Significance

I believe the word draws a line between what is real and what only looks real. An empty faith, an empty tradition, an empty religion all share the same flaw: they have no living God at the centre. That is why the gospel matters so much — Christ risen is the one thing that is not vain, and everything else is measured against Him.
