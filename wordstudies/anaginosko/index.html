anaginṓskō

from G303 and G1097; to know again, i.e. (by extension) to read:--read.

Biblical Usage

Jesus stood up to read in the synagogue at Nazareth, as His custom was; Philip ran to the Ethiopian's chariot and heard him read Isaiah, and asked whether he understood what he read; Jesus asked the chief priests whether they had never read in the Scriptures about the stone the builders rejected; and Revelation blesses the one who reads the words of the prophecy and keeps them. Reading in the Bible is never just sounding out words — it is hearing God speak, and being asked whether you understand.

Theological Significance

I hold that reading Scripture is the ordinary way God meets His people: Jesus read it, Philip explained it, and the blessing falls on the one who reads and keeps it. The question Philip asked is still the question for every reader — do you understand what you read? — and the Spirit who wrote it is the one who opens it.
