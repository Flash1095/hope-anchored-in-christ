kâshêr

by implication, to be acceptable; also to succeed or prosper

Biblical Usage

The word means to succeed, to please, to be right and proper, and it shows up where a dull axe needs sharpening, where a man sows his seed morning and evening not knowing which will prosper, and where Esther asks the king if it pleases him to undo Haman's letters. It ties success to doing the fitting thing at the fitting time rather than to luck.

Theological Significance

I believe success in Scripture is not grabbing what I want but doing what is right and leaving the outcome with God. I hold that Esther's question is the model: if it pleases the king, and if I have found favour, then let the good be done.
