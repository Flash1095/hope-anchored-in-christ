kathexēs

from G2596 and G1836; thereafter, i.e. consecutively; as a noun (by ellipsis of noun) a subsequent person or time:--after(-ward), by (in) order.

Biblical Usage

Thereafter is the word for in order, and Luke writes in order to Theophilus so he may know the certainty, and Peter rehearsed the matter from the beginning and expounded it by order, and Paul went over the country in order strengthening the disciples, and Samuel and the prophets after him foretold these days. The word is the order of the story told straight, from the beginning to the end, and the certainty is in the order.

Theological Significance

I believe the gospel is told in order because God works in order, and Luke's careful account is the proof of it. I hold that the prophets spoke in order too, each one adding to the last, and the story they told is the one I am living in.
