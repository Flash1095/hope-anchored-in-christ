bárbaros

of uncertain derivation; a foreigner (i.e. non-Greek):--barbarian(-rous).

Biblical Usage

Barbaros means a foreigner, someone whose speech sounds strange to a Greek ear. Paul says he is a debtor to Greeks and barbarians alike, that an unknown tongue makes men barbarians to one another, and that in Christ there is neither Greek nor Jew, circumcision nor uncircumcision, barbarian nor Scythian, while the barbarians of Malta show Paul and his companions uncommon kindness after the shipwreck.

Theological Significance

I believe this word exposes the pride of every people that thinks its own speech is the only human one. Paul owed the gospel to the barbarian as much as to the Greek, and I hold that the kindness of Malta's islanders shames every Christian who has ever looked down on a stranger.
