nēsteúō

from G3523; to abstain from food (religiously):--fast.

Biblical Usage

To fast is to go without food on purpose, and Jesus begins His ministry that way, forty days and forty nights in the wilderness. The disciples of John and the Pharisees fast and ask why Jesus' disciples do not, and the church at Antioch is fasting when the Holy Spirit separates Barnabas and Saul for the work.

Theological Significance

I believe fasting belongs with prayer and with sending: the church that fasts together is the church that hears God say, separate these two for Me. Jesus fasted before He preached, and the church fasted before it sent — the pattern has not changed.
