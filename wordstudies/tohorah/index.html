ṭohŏrâh

ceremonial purification; moral purity

Biblical Usage

Purification is the work that makes a person fit to come near. The Levites' office included the purifying of all holy things, Hezekiah's Passover allowed those whose hearts were prepared though they were not cleansed according to the purification of the sanctuary, the priest after cleansing reckons seven days, and the mother continues in the blood of her purifying.

Theological Significance

I hold that purification is God's way of saying the unclean can be made clean: it is not a single washing but a counted, ordered process, and the heart prepared to seek God is met halfway by His mercy. I believe this is why the New Testament can speak of being cleansed by the blood of Christ — the old pattern was always pointing at a washing that goes deeper than water.
