gᵉbûwl

properly, a cord (as twisted), i.e. (by implication) a boundary; by extension the territory inclosed

Biblical Usage

gᵉbûwl — border, territory. That is the whole of it, and it is enough: 196 occurrences, and in Genesis, Exodus, Numbers, and Deuteronomy the meaning never wavers. I keep coming back to the simple reading because the simple reading is the true one.

Theological Significance

What gᵉbûwl tells me is that God sets boundaries, and His boundaries are for our good. I hold that the simple reading is the true one, and I will not trade it for a clever one.
