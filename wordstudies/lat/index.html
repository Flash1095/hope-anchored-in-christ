lâṭ

properly, covered, i.e. secret; by implication, incantation; also secrecy or (adverb) covertly

Biblical Usage

lâṭ means secret arts, enchantment, and no one who reads the Bible can walk past it. In Exodus 7:22, "And the magicians of Egypt did so with their enchantments: and Pharaoh's heart was hardened"; and in Exodus 8:18, "And the magicians did so with their enchantments to bring forth lice". In Ruth 3:7, "he went to lie down at the end of the heap of corn: and she came softly"; and in 1 Samuel 18:22, "Commune with David secretly".

Theological Significance

I believe secret arts are real enough to fool a king, and weak enough to fail at lice. I hold that Pharaoh's magicians could copy the first signs but not the third, because God's power is not a trick to be learned. My position is plain: what is done in secret against God always runs out of power in the open.
