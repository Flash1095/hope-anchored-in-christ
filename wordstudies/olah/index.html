ʻôlâh

a step or (collectively, stairs, as ascending); usually a holocaust (as going up in smoke)

Biblical Usage

Olah is the burnt offering, the whole offering that goes up in smoke. Noah offers burnt offerings in Genesis 8:20, the first altar after the flood, and Moses demands sacrifices and burnt offerings in Exodus 10:25. Leviticus 1:3 sets the law: a male without blemish, offered of his own voluntary will, and Numbers 6:11 shows the Nazirite's burnt offering making atonement.

Theological Significance

I believe the burnt offering is the whole of worship in one act: everything given, nothing kept back, the smoke rising to God. That is why I hold that the burnt offering points to Christ, who offered Himself whole, and whose sacrifice needs no second offering.
