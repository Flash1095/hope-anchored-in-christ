makrothymía

from the same as G3116; longanimity, i.e. (objectively) forbearance or (subjectively) fortitude:--longsuffering, patience.

Biblical Usage

Makrothumia is longsuffering, patience, the long temper that does not flare, and it is both a fruit and a divine attribute. Galatians 5:22 lists longsuffering among the fruit of the Spirit, and Romans 2:4 says the riches of God's goodness and forbearance and longsuffering lead men to repentance. 2 Peter 3:15 says to account that the longsuffering of our Lord is salvation.

Theological Significance

I believe this word is the clearest proof that God's patience is not weakness: Romans 2:4 says it leads to repentance, and 2 Peter 3:15 says it is salvation itself. My position is that the fruit in Galatians 5:22 grows only where His longsuffering has already grown in me.
