eleuthería

from G1658; freedom (legitimate or licentious, chiefly moral or ceremonial):--liberty.

Biblical Usage

eleuthería is the word for liberty. In 1 Corinthians 10:29, the text says "for why is my liberty judged of another [man's] conscience?" In 2 Corinthians 3:17, the text says "there [is] liberty."

Theological Significance

I take eleuthería to mean that freedom in Christ is real freedom: not doing whatever we please, but being set free to live as God made us to live. That is why I keep this word in mind when I read those passages — it carries more than its size suggests.
