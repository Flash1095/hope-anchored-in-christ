ʼagmôwn

a bulrush (as growing there); collectively a rope of bulrushes

Biblical Usage

Agmown is a bulrush or reed — the rush that grows in the marsh, bending in the wind. Isaiah uses it three ways: the Lord will cut off head and tail, branch and rush, in one day; neither head nor tail, branch nor rush, will be able to do anything for Egypt; and true fasting is not bowing the head like a bulrush for a day.

Theological Significance

A bulrush bowing its head is the prophet's picture of a people brought low — the plant is the image, and Isaiah does the preaching.
