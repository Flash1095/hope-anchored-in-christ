dé

a primary particle (adversative or continuative); but, and, etc.:--also, and, but, moreover, now (often unexpressed in English).

Biblical Usage

This is the commonest little word in the Greek New Testament, the hinge on which sentences turn: and, but, moreover, now. It joins, it contrasts, it continues, and it appears thousands of times because every story needs it. Paul uses it to turn from argument to appeal, and the Gospels use it to move the narrative from one scene to the next.

Theological Significance

I believe the little word teaches us that Scripture is a story, not a list: the and and the but are where one thing leads to another, and the gospel itself turns on a but. The little word is the thread that holds the story together, and the story is the gospel.
