klínō

a primary verb; to slant or slope, i.e. incline or recline (literally or figuratively):--bow (down), be far spent, lay, turn to flight, wear away.

Biblical Usage

Klino means to bow down, to incline, to lay the head. Matthew 8:20 and Luke 9:58 both say the Son of man hath not where to lay his head, Luke 9:12 says the day began to wear away, Luke 24:5 has the women bowing down their faces to the earth at the empty tomb, John 19:30 says Jesus bowed his head and gave up the ghost, and Hebrews 11:34 says the faithful turned to flight the armies of the aliens.

Theological Significance

I believe the bowed head in John 19:30 is the centre of the word: the Son who had nowhere to lay His head laid it down on the cross, and finished it. I hold that the women bowing at the tomb and the armies turning to flight are the two ends of the same story, because the One who bowed His head rose again.
