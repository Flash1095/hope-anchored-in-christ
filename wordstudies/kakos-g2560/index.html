kakōs

from G2556; badly (physically or morally):--amiss, diseased, evil, grievously, miserably, sick, sore.

Biblical Usage

Kakos is the adverb 'badly' — to be ill, to fare badly, to be in evil case. They that be whole need not a physician, but they that are sick; and Jesus says, if I have spoken evil, bear witness of the evil.

Theological Significance

I believe this word puts sickness and sin side by side on purpose: both are a bad state, and both need the same Physician — and that is why Jesus came, for the sick and not for the whole. That is the gospel in one line: the Physician came for the sick, and I am glad He did.
