eíseimi

from G1519 and εἶμι (to go); to enter:--enter (go) into.

Biblical Usage

To enter is to go in, and the word appears in the temple and the city. The lame man asks Peter and John for alms as they enter the temple, Paul goes in to James and the elders at Jerusalem, and the priests enter the first tabernacle continually to accomplish the service of God. The word is plain, but every entrance in Acts opens a new chapter.

Theological Significance

I believe God still opens doors for His people to enter, because the temple, the city, and the council all had doors that led somewhere new. I want to walk through the doors He opens and not force the ones He shuts.
