bôqer

properly, dawn (as the break of day); generally, morning

Biblical Usage

Morning is the word for the break of day, and it appears nearly two hundred times. In Genesis 1:5 the evening and the morning were the first day. In Exodus 14:24 the Lord looked through the pillar of fire in the morning watch and troubled the Egyptians. In Psalms 30:5 weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning. And in Lamentations 3:23 God's mercies are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.

Theological Significance

I believe the morning in Lamentations is the promise I live on: his mercies are new every morning, and no night, however long, outlasts them.
