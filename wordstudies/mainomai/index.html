maínomai

middle voice from a primary μάω (to long for; through the idea of insensate craving); to rave as a "maniac":--be beside self (mad).

Biblical Usage

Mainomai means to be mad, beside oneself, raving, and it is the charge thrown at believers and at Christ Himself. 1 Corinthians 14:23 asks whether unbelievers coming into a church where all speak with tongues will say ye are mad, and Acts 12:15 has the household telling Rhoda she is mad when she says Peter is at the door. Acts 26:24 has Festus crying to Paul, thou art beside thyself, much learning doth make thee mad, and John 10:20 has the crowd saying of Jesus, He hath a devil, and is mad.

Theological Significance

I believe this word is the world's verdict on a faith it cannot explain: mad, beside oneself, possessed. My position is that Paul's answer in Acts 26:25 is the only answer worth giving, because he does not argue about his sanity, he speaks forth the words of truth and soberness.
