nâgash

to be or come (causatively, bring) near (for any purpose); euphemistically, to lie with a woman; as an enemy, to attack; religious to worship; causatively, to present; figuratively, to adduce an argument; by reversal, to stand back

Biblical Usage

Nagash means to draw near or come close, and it is the word for Abraham pleading and for Moses alone on the mountain. In Genesis 18:23 Abraham draws near and asks if God will destroy the righteous with the wicked, and in Exodus 24:2 Moses alone shall come near the Lord, but the people shall not come nigh. In Joshua 3:9 the people are told to come hither and hear the words of the Lord, and in Isaiah 41:1 the nations are called to come near together to judgment.

Theological Significance

I believe drawing near is the whole of prayer in one word, because Abraham drew near to argue for a city and Moses drew near alone to hear God. Nearness is not a privilege for the few; it is an invitation with a warning, since the people could not come nigh in Exodus 24:2. I hold that Christ has opened the way I could never open myself.
