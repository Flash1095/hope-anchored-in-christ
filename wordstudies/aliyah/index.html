ʻălîyâh

something lofty, i.e. a stair-way; also a second-story room (or even one on the roof); figuratively, the sky

Biblical Usage

The upper room, the chamber on the roof, the stairway up. Elijah carries the widow's dead son up into the loft where he abides and stretches himself on the child, and the child's soul comes into him again. And the Shunammite builds a little chamber on the wall for Elisha, with a bed and a table and a stool and a candlestick, so the man of God has a place to turn in; the upper room is where prophets stay, and where the dead come back.

Theological Significance

A word for an upstairs room carries no doctrine of its own; its weight is the two rooms, that a widow's son was raised in one and a prophet was sheltered in the other, and both were built by women who made room for God.
