ʻădar

to arrange as a battle, a vineyard (to hoe); hence, to muster and so to miss (or find wanting)

Biblical Usage

To be lacking, to fail, to miss — adar is the verb of the muster that comes up short. After the Amalekite raid, "there was nothing lacking to them, neither small nor great"; at the Jordan crossing, "there lacked not one of them"; and of the stars God calls by name, "not one faileth." The word counts what is missing — and finds nothing missing.

Theological Significance

A verb for coming up short is a counter's word, and the promise in these verses is God's — nothing of His is lost, and not one is missing.
