apokteínō

from G575 and κτείνω (to slay); to kill outright; figuratively, to destroy:--put to death, kill, slay.

Biblical Usage

To kill outright — seventy times, and almost always about the killing of the righteous. Jesus warns in Matthew 10:28 not to fear them which kill the body but cannot kill the soul, and in Luke 12:5 to fear Him which after He hath killed hath power to cast into hell. Peter charges the Jews in Acts 3:15 with killing the Prince of life, and Jesus tells His disciples in John 16:2 that the time cometh when whosoever killeth them will think that he doeth God service.

Theological Significance

I believe the word strips every excuse from religious murder. The killers of the Prince of life thought they were serving God; the killers of the disciples will think the same. Killing the body is the worst men can do, and it is not the worst thing that can happen — the soul is beyond their reach, and the Judge beyond their appeal.
