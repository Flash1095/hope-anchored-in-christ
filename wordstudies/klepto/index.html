kléptō

a primary verb; to filch:--steal.

Biblical Usage

Klepto means to steal, to filch. Matthew 6:19 has thieves breaking through to steal, Mark 10:19 and Luke 18:20 both carry the commandment, do not steal, John 10:10 says the thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy, Romans 2:21 asks the teacher who preaches a man should not steal, dost thou steal, and Ephesians 4:28 turns the thief around: let him that stole steal no more, but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.

Theological Significance

I believe Ephesians 4:28 is the gospel of the thief in one verse: the hands that took are to work, and the work is to give. I hold that stealing is never only about the thing taken, because the thief in John 10:10 comes to steal and to kill and to destroy, and only the Good Shepherd gives life.
