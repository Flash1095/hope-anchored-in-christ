mâchats

to dash asunder; by implication, to crush, smash or violently plunge; figuratively, to subdue or destroy

Biblical Usage

Machats means to crush, to smash, to wound with a shattering blow. Deuteronomy 32:39 puts it in God's own mouth: I wound, and I heal, and no one delivers out of His hand. Job 5:18 says the same thing from the sufferer's side: He maketh sore, and bindeth up. Judges 5:26 shows the human side, Jael smiting Sisera through the temples, and Psalms 110:5 looks to the end, when the Lord at God's right hand shall strike through kings in the day of His wrath.

Theological Significance

I believe this word holds judgment and mercy in one hand: the God who wounds is the God who heals, and no wound He gives is ever without a purpose. My position is that I would rather be wounded by Him than struck by anyone else, because His wounds are the ones that make whole.
