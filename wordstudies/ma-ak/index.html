mâʻak

to press, i.e. to pierce, emasculate, handle

Biblical Usage

The word means to press or crush, and its three uses show how one word can serve worship, lust and war. The law forbids offering an animal that is bruised or crushed or broken, Ezekiel says the breasts of Oholah and Oholibah were pressed in the sins of their youth, and Saul's spear was stuck in the ground at his bolster when David crept into the camp by night.

Theological Significance

I believe the word warns us that what is crushed cannot be offered to God, and what is pressed in secret will be named in the open. I hold that God alone can take what is broken and make it whole, and He does not accept a bruised offering in place of a whole heart.
