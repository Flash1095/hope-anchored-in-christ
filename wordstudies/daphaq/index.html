dâphaq

to knock; by analogy, to press severely

Biblical Usage

To knock is the sound of a visitor at the door and of a man pressed hard in flight. The Shunammite's son is dead and the mother knocks at Elisha's door; the men of Gibeah beat at the old man's door demanding the guest; and the Song pictures the beloved knocking, his head wet with the dew of the night. The knock is a demand to be let in.

Theological Significance

I believe the knock is also the Lord's own image: He stands at the door and knocks, and the one who opens finds a guest, not an intruder. The knock that is answered is the knock of the one who keeps knocking, and the Lord hears it.
