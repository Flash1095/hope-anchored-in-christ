anthýpatos

from G473 and a superlative of G5228; instead of the highest officer, i.e. (specially) a Roman proconsul:--deputy.

Biblical Usage

The proconsul is the Roman governor of a province, and Luke names two: Sergius Paulus in Cyprus, who believes, and Gallio in Corinth, who refuses to judge Paul. The word is the office of the highest Roman authority in the province.

Theological Significance

A word for a Roman governor carries no teaching of its own; its interest is the story, that the gospel reached the highest seats of Rome and found both belief and indifference there.
