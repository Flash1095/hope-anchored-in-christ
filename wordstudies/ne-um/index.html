nᵉʼum

an oracle

Biblical Usage

David's last words open with the oracle of the man raised up on high, and Isaiah opens his charge with the oracle of the LORD of hosts, the mighty One of Israel. Jeremiah asks whether God's word is not like a fire, says the LORD, and Amos closes his warning with the oracle of the LORD.

Theological Significance

I believe the oracle is the weight of the word: when the LORD speaks, it is not advice but decree, and the prophet's 'saith the LORD' is the seal on every promise and every warning. The oracle does not ask my opinion: it declares what the LORD has said, and my part is to hear and obey.
