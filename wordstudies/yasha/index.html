yâshaʻ

properly, to be open, wide or free, i.e. (by implication) to be safe; causatively, to free or succor

Biblical Usage

Yâsha is the word for saving, for delivering out of the hand of the enemy. The LORD saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians, and raised up judges who delivered them from those who spoiled them. The poor man cried and the LORD heard him and saved him out of all his troubles, and Isaiah declares that beside the LORD there is no saviour.

Theological Significance

I believe salvation belongs to the LORD alone, and every deliverance in the Old Testament is a rehearsal of the one in Christ. My position is that no judge, no king, and no army saves — God saves, and beside Him there is no saviour. And I hold that the poor man who cries is heard, because the God who saved Israel at the sea still saves today.
