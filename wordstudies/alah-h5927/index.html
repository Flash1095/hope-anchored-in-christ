ʻâlâh

to ascend, in transitively (be high) or actively (mount); used in a great variety of senses, primary and secondary, literal and figurative

Biblical Usage

This is the ordinary Hebrew word for going up, and it carries everything from climbing a hill to Elijah's whirlwind ride into heaven. Jacob watches angels ascending and descending on the ladder at Bethel; Moses goes up the mountain to meet God; Elijah is taken up and Elisha is left staring after him. The word does not change its meaning — what changes is who is going up, and where.

Theological Significance

I believe the going up matters because God is the one who calls us upward. He comes down to us first, in the ladder, in the burning bush, in Christ, and then He lifts us up with Him. Isaiah's promise still stands: those who wait on the LORD mount up with wings as eagles, and I hold that this is not poetry about escape but about strength — God gives tired people the lift they cannot give themselves.
