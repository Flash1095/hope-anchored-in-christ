châgar

to gird on (as a belt, armor, etc.)

Biblical Usage

Chagar means to gird on — a belt, armour, a sword. Israel ate the Passover with loins girded and staff in hand, Moses girded Aaron with the girdle of the ephod, the spies girded themselves to go up and fight, and Ehud girded a dagger under his raiment on his right thigh.

Theological Significance

I believe girding is the posture of a people about to move: Passover girded for the road, Aaron girded for service, Ehud girded for the deed. God does not call ungirded men.
