asebéō

from G765; to be (by implied act) impious or wicked:--commit (live, that after should live) ungodly.

Biblical Usage

To commit ungodly deeds is used once, in Enoch's prophecy quoted by Jude. The Lord cometh with ten thousands of His saints, to execute judgment upon all, and to convince all that are ungodly among them of all their ungodly deeds which they have ungodly committed.

Theological Significance

One word for ungodly deeds carries no doctrine of its own; its weight is the courtroom in it, that the deeds will be named, and the ungodly convinced of them, when the Lord comes with His saints.
