shᵉʼôwl

Hades or the world of the dead (as if a subterranean retreat), including its accessories and inmates

Biblical Usage

Sheol is the grave, the pit, the dark place under the earth where the dead go down. David tells Solomon not to let Joab's hoar head go down to the grave in peace, Job looks for rest together in the dust at the bars of the pit, and Habakkuk says the proud man enlarges his desire as hell and cannot be satisfied. Yet the Song sets love against it, strong as death, with jealousy cruel as the grave.

Theological Significance

I hold that Sheol is the honest end of every life lived apart from God, and the reason the hope of resurrection matters so much. Death is strong, but love is set against it, and I believe the grave does not have the last word for those God keeps.
