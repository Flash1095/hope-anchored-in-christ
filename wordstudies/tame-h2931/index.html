ṭâmêʼ

foul in a religious sense

Biblical Usage

The swine is unclean to you, though it divides the hoof, and the leper must cry, unclean, unclean. Isaiah cries, woe is me, for I am undone, because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell among a people of unclean lips. And Haggai asks whether bread touched by one unclean by a dead body is unclean, and the priests answer that it shall be unclean. The word is foul in a religious sense: the animal, the leper, the lips, and the bread, all touched by uncleanness.

Theological Significance

I hold that uncleanness is the Bible's word for what separates us from God, and the law says it spreads by touch. I believe Isaiah shows the way through: the coal touches the unclean lips, and the iniquity is taken away. What the law says spreads, grace says can be cleansed.
