Kasday

a Chaldaean or inhabitant of Chaldaea; by implication, a Magian or professional astrologer

Biblical Usage

The Chaldeans were the wise men of Babylon, the ones the kings called on to read dreams and signs, and they stood before Nebuchadnezzar and Belshazzar with all their learning and could not answer what only God could reveal. Daniel stood among them yet apart from them, and the king himself said the spirit of the holy gods was in him. Their wisdom was real as far as it went, but it stopped where God's wisdom begins.

Theological Significance

I believe this is the difference between man's wisdom and God's: the Chaldeans could describe the dream only if the king told it first, while God both knows and reveals. I hold that no amount of learning replaces a God who speaks, and that is why Daniel's God, not Babylon's wise men, gets the last word.
