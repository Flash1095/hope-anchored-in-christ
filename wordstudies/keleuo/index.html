keleúō

from a primary κέλλω (to urge on); "hail"; to incite by word, i.e. order:--bid, (at, give) command(-ment).

Biblical Usage

Keleuo means to command, to order, to bid. Matthew 27:64 has the chief priests saying, command therefore that the sepulchre be made sure until the third day. Luke 18:40 says Jesus stood, and commanded the blind man to be brought unto him. Acts 4:15 says the council commanded Peter and John to go aside, and Acts 5:34 says Gamaliel commanded to put the apostles forth a little space.

Theological Significance

I believe every command in these verses is answered by a greater one: the priests command the tomb shut, and Jesus commands the blind to come. I hold that the council's commands fade, but the Lord's command brings the blind near.
