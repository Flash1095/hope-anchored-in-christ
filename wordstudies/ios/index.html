iós

perhaps from εἶμι (to go) or ἵημι (to send); rust (as if emitted by metals); also venom (as emitted by serpents):--poison, rust.

Biblical Usage

Poison and rust are the two faces of this word, and both eat away at what they touch. In James 3:8 the tongue is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison, in James 5:3 the rust of the rich men's gold shall witness against them and eat their flesh as fire, and in Romans 3:13 the poison of asps is under the lips of men whose mouths are full of cursing.

Theological Significance

I believe the word is a warning about what corrodes: a tongue full of poison and gold eaten with rust are the same decay. Both testify against their owners.
