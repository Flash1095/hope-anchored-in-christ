leitourgéō

from G3011; to be a public servant, i.e. (by analogy) to perform religious or charitable functions (worship, obey, relieve):--minister.

Biblical Usage

leitourgéō means minister, and it is one of those words that quietly holds a whole theme together. In Acts 13:2, "As they ministered to the Lord"; and in Hebrews 10:11, "And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices". In Romans 15:27, "their duty is also to minister unto them in carnal things.".

Theological Significance

I believe ministering to the Lord is the highest public service — fasting, praying, and waiting until the Holy Ghost speaks. I hold that the priests who stood daily offering sacrifices that could never take away sins show me what my service is not, and Paul's gift to Jerusalem shows me what it is. My position is that my service must cost me something and carry Christ to someone.
