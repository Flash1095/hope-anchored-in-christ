bârak

to kneel; by implication to bless God (as an act of adoration), and (vice-versa) man (as a benefit); also (by euphemism) to curse (God or the king, as treason)

Biblical Usage

Barak means to kneel, and so to bless, and it is the word for God's blessing from the first promise to Abraham all the way to the priestly blessing. God tells Abram He will make him a blessing and bless those who bless him, the priests are told to bless Israel with the Lord's name upon them, and Moses promises blessing in the city and in the field to those who obey.

Theological Significance

I believe blessing in Scripture is God stooping to give, because the word itself begins on bended knee. He blesses Abraham so Abraham can bless the world, and I hold that every blessing we receive is meant to travel onward to someone else.
