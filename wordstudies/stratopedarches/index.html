stratopedárchēs

from G4760 and G757; a ruler of an army, i.e. (specially), a Prætorian præfect:--captain of the guard.

Biblical Usage

The captain of the guard is the prefect who receives the prisoners at Rome. When we came to Rome, the centurion delivered the prisoners to the captain of the guard, but Paul was suffered to dwell by himself with a soldier that kept him. The older Greek text has the title; the newer text leaves the clause out, and Paul's separate lodging is the point either way.

Theological Significance

I believe Paul's lodging shows the Lord's care in chains: the prisoners go to the prefect, and the apostle dwells by himself, still preaching.
