miaínō

perhaps a primary verb; to sully or taint, i.e. contaminate (ceremonially or morally):--defile.

Biblical Usage

To the pure all things are pure, but to the defiled and unbelieving nothing is pure, and even their mind and conscience is defiled. Hebrews warns that a root of bitterness springing up defiles many, and Jude says the filthy dreamers defile the flesh. The word is the stain itself, and Scripture traces it from the conscience to the flesh.

Theological Significance

I believe defilement spreads the way Hebrews says it does: a root of bitterness springs up, and many are defiled by it, because no sin stays where it starts. No sin stays where it starts, and a bitter root defiles many.
