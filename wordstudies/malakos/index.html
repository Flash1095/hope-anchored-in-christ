malakós

of uncertain affinity; soft, i.e. fine (clothing); figuratively, a catamite:--effeminate, soft.

Biblical Usage

Malakos means soft, fine, delicate, and it covers both clothing and character. Matthew 11:8 and Luke 7:25 both ask, what went ye out to see, a man clothed in soft raiment, and answer that they who wear soft clothing are in kings' houses. 1 Corinthians 6:9 turns the word toward conduct: neither fornicators, nor idolaters, nor adulterers, nor effeminate shall inherit the kingdom of God.

Theological Significance

I take the two uses of this word as one warning: soft living in Matthew 11:8 and soft morals in 1 Corinthians 6:9 are the same drift, from comfort to compromise. My position is that John the Baptist in Matthew 11:8 is the contrast God holds up, because he wore camel's hair and told the truth.
