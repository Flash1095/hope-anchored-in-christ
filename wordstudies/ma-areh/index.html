maʻăreh

a nude place, i.e. a common

Biblical Usage

The word names an open place, a common or meadow, and it appears once, when the liers in wait of Israel came forth out of their places, even out of the meadows of Gibeah, in the war against Benjamin. The meadow hid the ambush until the moment it sprang.

Theological Significance

I hold that the meadow of Gibeah warns us how quickly a quiet field can turn into a battlefield, and how one sin can draw a whole tribe into war.
