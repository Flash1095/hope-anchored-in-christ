Naʻămân

Naaman, the name of an Israelite and of a Damascene

Biblical Usage

Naaman is the Syrian commander who came to Israel a leper and went home clean. In 2 Kings 5:1 he is introduced as a great man and a mighty man of valour, but a leper, and in 2 Kings 5:11 he is angry at first because Elisha will not come out to him. In 2 Kings 5:17 Naaman asks for two mules' burden of earth, so that he may worship the Lord on Israel's soil from then on.

Theological Significance

I believe Naaman's healing came the moment he stopped dictating how God should heal him. He wanted a ceremony and got a command to wash, and the washing did what the ceremony never could; my position is that obedience in the plain thing is where the miracle lives, and another Naaman, a son of Benjamin, carries the same name in the genealogies.
