mâkar

to sell, literally (as merchandise, a daughter in marriage, into slavery), or figuratively (to surrender)

Biblical Usage

Makar means to sell, and it sells everything: a birthright, a brother, a people, and the truth itself. Genesis 25:31 has Jacob saying sell me this day thy birthright, and Genesis 37:28 has Joseph's brothers selling him to the Ishmeelites for twenty pieces of silver. Isaiah 52:3 says ye have sold yourselves for nought, and ye shall be redeemed without money, while Proverbs 23:23 commands, buy the truth, and sell it not.

Theological Significance

I believe this word is about what a man will trade away when the price looks right: Esau's birthright, Joseph's freedom, a whole people's future. My position is that Isaiah 52:3 is the gospel in one verse, because we sold ourselves for nothing and God redeems us without money.
