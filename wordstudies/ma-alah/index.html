maʻălâh

elevation, i.e. the act (literally, a journey to a higher place, figuratively, a thought arising), or (concretely) the condition (literally, a step or grademark, figuratively, a superiority of station); specifically a climactic progression (in certain Psalms)

Biblical Usage

The word names a going up, a flight of steps, and it marks the way into God's presence and the signs He gives in time. Solomon's throne had six steps with lions standing on either side, and the steps of the temple gates rose toward the house of the LORD. Hezekiah asked for a sign, and the shadow went back ten degrees on the dial of Ahaz, and Ezra's company went up from Babylon to Jerusalem in the first month.

Theological Significance

I believe the steps of Scripture teach us that the way to God is a going up, and He meets us on the climb. I hold that the shadow going backward on the dial is God's own sign that He can turn time itself for the sake of a praying king.
