ʼezrâch

a spontaneous growth, i.e. native (tree or persons)

Biblical Usage

Ezrach means one born in the land, a native, set against the stranger who sojourns among Israel. The Passover law makes the rule plain in Exodus 12:19, where leaven is forbidden whether the eater is a stranger or born in the land, and the Day of Atonement binds the native and the stranger alike in Leviticus 16:29. When Israel stands to bless the people in Joshua 8:33, the stranger and he that was born among them stand together before the ark.

Theological Significance

I believe the native-born and the stranger standing together before God is a picture of His welcome. The law drew a line between them in some things, yet in worship and in atonement they stood side by side, and I hold that the gospel has now made that welcome full.
