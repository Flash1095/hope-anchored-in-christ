nâzar

to hold aloof, i.e. (intransitivey) abstain (from food and drink, from impurity, and even from divine worship (i.e. apostatize)); specifically, to set apart (to sacred purposes), i.e. devote

Biblical Usage

Aaron and his sons are told to separate themselves from the holy things so they will not profane God's name, and a man or woman may separate themselves with the vow of a Nazarite to the LORD. The stranger who separates himself from the LORD is cut off, and Israel separates herself unto shame at Baal-peor.

Theological Significance

I believe separation cuts both ways: set apart to the LORD it is holiness, and set apart from the LORD it is apostasy. The vow and the defection use the same word, and the direction decides everything.
