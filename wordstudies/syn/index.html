sýn

a primary preposition denoting union; with or together (but much closer than G3326 or G3844), i.e. by association, companionship, process, resemblance, possession, instrumentality, addition, etc.:--beside, with. In composition it has similar applications, including completeness.

Biblical Usage

This little word carries the whole gospel: if we are dead with Christ we shall also live with Him, and our life is hid with Christ in God. Paul says we were crucified with Christ, we shall live with Him by the power of God, and those who are alive will be caught up together to meet the Lord and so shall we ever be with the Lord. The two on the road to Emmaus beg Him to abide with them, because being with Jesus is the whole of it.

Theological Significance

What grips me is that the Christian life is a with-life, with Christ in His death and His rising and His coming again. I hold that nothing in the gospel is mine apart from Him, because every blessing is a with-blessing. And I take the Emmaus prayer as my own: abide with us, Lord, for the day is far spent.
