râchâm

a kind of vulture (supposed to be tender towards its young)

Biblical Usage

Racham is a kind of vulture — the carrion bird, named, some say, for the tenderness it shows its young. Moses lists it among the unclean birds Israel must not eat, with the swan and the pelican and the gier eagle.

Theological Significance

My position is that even the unclean list teaches: God told His people what to touch and what to leave alone, and holiness began at the table.
