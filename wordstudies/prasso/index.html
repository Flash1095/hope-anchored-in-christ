prássō

a primary verb; to "practise", i.e. perform repeatedly or habitually (thus differing from G4160, which properly refers to a single act); by implication, to execute, accomplish, etc.; specially, to collect (dues), fare (personally):--commit, deeds, do, exact, keep, require, use arts.

Biblical Usage

Prasso means to practise, to do repeatedly, to carry on a business of doing. Paul asks if he does his apostleship willingly, and tells the Thessalonians to study to be quiet and to do their own business.

Theological Significance

I hold that what I practise is what I become, and the Christian life is a practice, not a single act.
