pheúgō

apparently a primary verb; to run away (literally or figuratively); by implication, to shun; by analogy, to vanish:--escape, flee (away).

Biblical Usage

Pheugo means to flee, to run away, and Scripture commands it as often as it warns against it. Paul tells believers to flee fornication and to flee from idolatry, and to flee youthful lusts and follow righteousness, faith, and love. And Joseph is told in a dream to flee into Egypt with the child, because Herod will seek Him to destroy Him.

Theological Significance

I believe fleeing is faith in motion: the same legs that run from sin carry the Saviour to safety. God does not tell me to stand and fight every temptation; some enemies are beaten only by running. My position is that flight is not cowardice when God commands it: Joseph fled and saved the Christ child, and I flee and save my soul.
