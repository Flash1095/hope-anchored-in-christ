páscha

of Chaldee origin (compare H6453); the Passover (the meal, the day, the festival or the special sacrifices connected with it):--Easter, Passover.

Biblical Usage

Pascha is the Passover, the meal, the day, the festival, and the Lamb. John sets the cleansing of the temple at the Jews' passover in John 2:13, and Jesus says in Luke 22:15 that He has desired to eat this passover with His disciples before He suffers. Matthew 26:2 says the Son of man is betrayed to be crucified at the passover. Paul says in 1 Corinthians 5:7 that Christ our passover is sacrificed for us, and Hebrews 11:28 says Moses kept the passover and the sprinkling of blood, that the destroyer of the firstborn should not touch them.

Theological Significance

I believe the Passover is the feast that explains the cross: the lamb slain, the blood on the door, the firstborn spared, and then the Lamb of God at the same feast, on the same day. That is why I hold that Christ our passover is not a symbol, He is the fulfilment, and the meal He desired to eat was the one He would become.
