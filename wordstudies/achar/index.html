ʼâchar

to loiter (i.e. be behind); by implication to procrastinate

Biblical Usage

To loiter, to delay, to tarry — that is achar as a verb. Eliezer pleads, "Hinder me not"; Sisera's mother asks why his chariot tarries; Amasa "tarried longer than the set time" and it cost him his life; and Habakkuk is told the vision "will not tarry." The word shows up wherever waiting turns into danger.

Theological Significance

Delay is a plain human fault, and the pattern in these verses is what preaches — men who tarry come to grief, while God's promise never tarries.
