Héllēn

from G1671; a Hellen (Grecian) or inhabitant of Hellas; by extension a Greek-speaking person, especially a non-Jew:-- Gentile, Greek.

Biblical Usage

Hellen means a Greek, and in the New Testament it comes to mean the whole Gentile world. In Romans 1:16 the gospel is the power of God unto salvation, to the Jew first and also to the Greek, in Romans 10:12 there is no difference between the Jew and the Greek, in Galatians 3:28 there is neither Jew nor Greek in Christ, and in 1 Corinthians 1:22 the Greeks seek after wisdom.

Theological Significance

I believe the word is the door the gospel walked through: the Greek who sought wisdom found a crucified Christ, and the Jew who kept the law found the same Lord. There is no difference now, and that is the miracle.
