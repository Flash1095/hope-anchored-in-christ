shâchath

to decay, i.e. (causatively) ruin (literally or figuratively)

Biblical Usage

Shachath covers the whole slide from corrupt to destroy, and it is used for people, cities, and wisdom alike. Israel corrupted themselves at Sinai while Moses was still on the mountain, and the generation of the wilderness corrupted themselves until Moses sang that their spot was not the spot of His children. Yet the same word is held back by mercy, for when the angel stretched out his hand to destroy Jerusalem the LORD repented of the evil, and Ezekiel heard God seeking a man to stand in the gap so He would not destroy the land.

Theological Significance

I hold that corruption starts small and ends in ruin, and Scripture never pretends otherwise. God destroys what is corrupt, but He also stays His hand when His people humble themselves, and He looks for one person to stand in the gap. That is why I take both the warning and the mercy seriously, because they come from the same God.
