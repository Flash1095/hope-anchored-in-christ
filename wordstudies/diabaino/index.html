diabaínō

from G1223 and the base of G939; to cross:--come over, pass (through).

Biblical Usage

To cross over is to go from one side to the other, and the three crossings here could not be more different. Paul crosses to Macedonia in answer to a vision, Israel crosses the Red Sea on dry ground, and the rich man in the parable learns no one can cross the great gulf fixed between him and Lazarus.

Theological Significance

I believe every crossing in Scripture asks the same question: will I go where God calls me to go? Paul went, Israel went, and the gulf in the parable warns me that some crossings cannot be made once the day of choice is past.
