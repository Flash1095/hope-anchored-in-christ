gymnázō

from G1131; to practise naked (in the games), i.e. train (figuratively):--exercise.

Biblical Usage

In the New Testament, gymnázō is the word for exercise. In 1 Timothy 4:7, the text says "and exercise thyself [rather] unto godliness." In Hebrews 5:14, the text says "even] those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil."

Theological Significance

Reading gymnázō this way, I believe faithfulness is staying when staying is costly — the way God stays with us. That is why I keep this word in mind when I read those passages — it carries more than its size suggests.
