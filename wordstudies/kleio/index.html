kleíō

a primary verb; to close (literally or figuratively):--shut (up).

Biblical Usage

Kleio means to shut, to close. Matthew 6:6 says when thou prayest, shut thy door and pray to thy Father in secret, Luke 4:25 recalls the heaven shut up three years and six months in the days of Elias, and John 20:19 has Jesus standing in the midst though the doors were shut for fear of the Jews. Acts 5:23 finds the prison shut with all safety and the keepers standing without, yet no man within, 1 John 3:17 warns the man who shutteth up his bowels of compassion from his brother, and Revelation 3:7 praises Him that openeth, and no man shutteth, and shutteth, and no man openeth.

Theological Significance

I believe shutting is the test of the heart: the door shut for prayer is holy, the heart shut against a brother is not. I hold that the doors shut against Jesus in John 20:19 could not keep Him out, and the prison shut in Acts 5:23 could not keep His apostles in.
