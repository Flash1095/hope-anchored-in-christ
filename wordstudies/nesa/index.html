nᵉsâʼ

{to lift}

Biblical Usage

To lift in Daniel's Aramaic is to take up and carry — the vessels carried back into the temple, the broken pieces of the great image carried away by the wind like chaff, and rebellion itself lifted up in a city of old. The same word lifts a cup to a king's lips and lifts a revolt against him.

Theological Significance

I hold that what is lifted decides the story: God lifts His vessels home, and the wind lifts away everything that stood against Him. The vessels go home by lifting, and the image goes away by the same word — lifted, and gone.
