philéō

from G5384; to be a friend to (fond of (an individual or an object)), i.e. have affection for (denoting personal attachment, as a matter of sentiment or feeling; while G25 is wider, embracing especially the judgment and the deliberate assent of the will as a matter of principle, duty and propriety: the two thus stand related very much as G2309 and G1014, or as G2372 and G3563 respectively; the former being chiefly of the heart and the latter of the head); specially, to kiss (as a mark of tenderness):--kiss, love.

Biblical Usage

Phileo is to love as a friend, with fondness and affection, and it is the word Jesus uses when He restores Peter. Three times He asks, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou Me, and three times Peter answers that the Lord knows he loves Him, and each time Jesus gives him sheep to feed. The Father Himself loves the disciples, Jesus says, because they have loved Him and believed that He came out from God.

Theological Significance

I believe phileo is the love of friendship, and friendship is the heart of the gospel: Christ calls His people friends, not servants, and the Father loves those who love His Son. Peter's threefold love answers his threefold denial, and the feeding of the sheep is the proof of the love. My position is that love for Christ is not a feeling to be confessed once but a friendship to be lived, and it shows itself in feeding His sheep.
