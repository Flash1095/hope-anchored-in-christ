kenóō

from G2756; to make empty, i.e. (figuratively) to abase, neutralize, falsify:--make (of none effect, of no reputation, void), be in vain.

Biblical Usage

Kenoo means to make empty, to make void, to make of none effect: Romans 4:14 says if they which are of the law be heirs, faith is made void, and the promise made of none effect. 1 Corinthians 1:17 says Christ sent me to preach the gospel, not with wisdom of words, lest the cross of Christ should be made of none effect. 2 Corinthians 9:3 says Paul sent the brethren, lest our boasting of you should be in vain. Philippians 2:7 says Christ made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant.

Theological Significance

I believe emptiness is the word of the gospel two ways: men empty the cross when they trust the law or wisdom of words, and Christ emptied himself when he took the servant's form. I hold that his emptying is what fills us, and their emptying is what leaves men with nothing.
