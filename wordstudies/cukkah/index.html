çukkâh

a hut or lair

Biblical Usage

A booth is a hut of branches, and Jacob builds booths for his cattle at Succoth and names the place for them. The feast of booths remembers the wilderness, when Israel lived in booths, and Uriah refuses to go home to his wife while the ark dwells in booths. The word is shelter that is temporary, and the feast makes the temporary holy.

Theological Significance

I believe the booth teaches that all our shelter is temporary, and God means us to know it. Israel lived in booths so they would remember the wilderness, and Uriah stayed out while the ark dwelt in booths, while his king slept in sin. My position is that a life lived in booths is a life that trusts God for tomorrow.
