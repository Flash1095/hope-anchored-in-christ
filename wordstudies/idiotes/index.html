idiṓtēs

from G2398; a private person, i.e. (by implication) an ignoramus (compare "idiot"):--ignorant, rude, unlearned.

Biblical Usage

An unlearned man is one without schooling, and the New Testament turns that label inside out. In Acts 4:13 the rulers see that Peter and John are unlearned and ignorant men, and marvel that they had been with Jesus, in 1 Corinthians 14:16 Paul asks how the unlearned will say Amen to a prayer they cannot understand, and in 2 Corinthians 11:6 he admits he is rude in speech, yet not in knowledge.

Theological Significance

I hold that being unlearned never stopped God: the rulers could tell Peter and John had been with Jesus. That was the only schooling that mattered.
