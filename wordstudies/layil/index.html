layil

properly, a twist (away of the light), i.e. night; figuratively, adversity

Biblical Usage

layil means night, and it does large work in the story. In Genesis 1:5, "and the darkness he called Night."; and in Psalms 19:2, "and night unto night sheweth knowledge.". In Psalms 91:5, "Thou shalt not be afraid for the terror by night"; in Isaiah 21:11, "what of the night?"; and in Jeremiah 9:1, "that I might weep day and night for the slain of the daughter of my people!".

Theological Significance

I believe night belongs to God as much as day — He made the darkness, He speaks in it, and He watches through it. I hold that the terrors of the night lose their power when I remember who is awake: the same God who divided light from darkness never sleeps. My position is that I do not have to fear the dark, because the One who made it walks in it with me.
