krázō

a primary verb; properly, to "croak" (as a raven) or scream, i.e. (genitive case) to call aloud (shriek, exclaim, intreat):--cry (out).

Biblical Usage

Krazo is to cry aloud, to shriek or call out, and it runs through the whole New Testament as the sound of people in extremity. Stephen cries with a loud voice in Acts 7:57 and again in Acts 7:60, "Lord, lay not this sin to their charge"; the Galatians passage says the Spirit cries "Abba, Father" in our hearts in Galatians 4:6; the cries of the reapers enter the ears of the Lord in James 5:4.

Theological Significance

I take it as given that God hears every cry, whether it is Stephen's dying prayer, a child's Abba, or the wages of the oppressed: none of it is lost on Him, and that is both comfort and warning. Stephen's cry, a child's Abba, and the reapers' cry all reached the same ears.
