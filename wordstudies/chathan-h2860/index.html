châthân

a relative by marriage (especially through the bride); figuratively, a circumcised child (as a species of religious espousal)

Biblical Usage

This word is the son-in-law, the bridegroom, the husband joined by marriage. Lot warns his sons-in-law to flee Sodom, Zipporah calls Moses a bloody husband at the circumcision, and Isaiah and Joel both dress the bridegroom in joy for the wedding day.

Theological Significance

I believe marriage language runs deeper than the wedding day: God uses the bridegroom to picture His own joy over His people. The husband in these verses is bound by covenant, not by convenience, and that is how I understand His love for us.
