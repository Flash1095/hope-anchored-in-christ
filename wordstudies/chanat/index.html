chânaṭ

to spice; by implication, to embalm; also to ripen

Biblical Usage

Chanat is ripening and embalming, and it spans a burial and a springtime. Joseph commanded the physicians to embalm his father, and they fulfilled forty days for him while Egypt mourned. At the last, Joseph himself was embalmed and put in a coffin in Egypt. In the Song the same word ripens figs: the fig tree puts forth her green figs, and the vines with the tender grape give a good smell.

Theological Significance

I believe one word holding both embalming and ripening says something true about the whole Bible: death is prepared for, and life ripens after it. Joseph's body was kept for the exodus, and the fig tree ripens for the summer. I hold that God ripens what He has kept.
