tâmîyd

properly, continuance (as indefinite extension); but used only (attributively as adjective) constant (or adverbially, constantly); ellipt. the regular (daily) sacrifice

Biblical Usage

The continual burnt offering is two lambs day by day continually, and the fire shall ever be burning upon the altar; it shall never go out. The daily offering is a lamb of the first year without spot, day by day for a continual burnt offering. David says, I have set the Lord always before me, and Daniel sees the daily sacrifice taken away. The word is continuance, constantly, always: the fire that never goes out, the lamb offered every day, and the Lord set always before the face.

Theological Significance

I hold that continuance is the shape of faithfulness. I believe the fire that never goes out is the picture: worship is not an event but a constancy, and David's always is the same word as the altar's ever. When the daily sacrifice is taken away, Daniel says, the desolation has come.
