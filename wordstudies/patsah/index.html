pâtsâh

to rend, i.e. open (especially the mouth)

Biblical Usage

Patsah means to open wide, to rend, especially the mouth. The earth opens her mouth to receive Abel's blood in Genesis 4:11, and Jephthah says in Judges 11:35 that he has opened his mouth unto the Lord and cannot go back. God tells Ezekiel in Ezekiel 2:8 to open his mouth and eat what He gives him, and in Lamentations 2:16 the enemies open their mouth wide against Jerusalem.

Theological Significance

I believe the opened mouth is the Bible's test of the heart: Abel's blood cries from the ground, Jephthah's vow cannot be taken back, Ezekiel eats the word, and the enemy's mouth is opened in scorn. My position is that what I open my mouth to say is what I will have to live with.
