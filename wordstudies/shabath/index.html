shâbath

to repose, i.e. desist from exertion; used in many implied relations (causative, figurative or specific)

Biblical Usage

Shabath is ceasing, resting, leaving off, and it works on buildings, priests, and sacrifices alike. Baasha heard the word and left off building Ramah, and let his work cease. Josiah put down the idolatrous priests, and made them cease burning incense to Baal, to the sun, and to the moon. And the covenant of the week says the prince shall cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease in the midst of the week.

Theological Significance

I believe ceasing is the other half of resting: God makes work cease when it is finished, and He makes false worship cease when it is judged. Baasha's building stopped, the priests' incense stopped, and the sacrifice stopped — and the rest that remains is God's. And I hold that the Sabbath is the sign of it: six days of work, and then the ceasing that says God is enough.
