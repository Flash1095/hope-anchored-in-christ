shachaq

a powder (as beaten small); by analogy, a thin vapor; by extension, the firmament

Biblical Usage

Shachaq is the sky itself, the thin expanse stretched above the earth where the clouds move. Elihu asks Job whether he spread out the sky with God, strong as a molten looking glass, and Wisdom was there when God established the clouds above. The psalms set God's mercy in the heavens and His faithfulness reaching to the clouds, and Isaiah calls on the skies to pour down righteousness.

Theological Significance

I believe the sky preaches two things at once, that God is high above us and that His mercy reaches all the way down. When I look up at the clouds I am looking at the same heavens that declare His faithfulness.
