aítēma

from G154; a thing asked or (abstractly) an asking:--petition, request, required.

Biblical Usage

Three times, and each one is a request laid before someone with power to grant it. Pilate grants the crowd's demand for Jesus' death; Paul tells believers to make their requests known to God by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving; and John says that if we know God hears our petitions, we know we have what we asked. One granted to a mob, one offered to God — the word itself does not judge the asking.

Theological Significance

The contrast is the lesson. A request can be wicked or right, and the same word covers both — what matters is who we ask and what we ask for. I believe John's promise is the anchor: prayers laid before God in His will are already answered in His hearing.
