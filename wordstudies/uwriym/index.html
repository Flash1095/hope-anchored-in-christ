ʼÛwrîym

Urim, the oracular brilliancy of the figures in the high-priest's breastplate

Biblical Usage

The Lord told Moses to put the Urim and the Thummim in the breastplate of judgment, and Aaron wore them over his heart whenever he went in before the Lord. Moses placed them there at Aaron's consecration, the priest asked counsel for Joshua after the judgment of the Urim, and Moses blessed Levi by saying, let thy Thummim and thy Urim be with thy holy one.

Theological Significance

I hold that God gave Israel a way to ask Him and be answered: the Urim were not magic stones but a priestly means of seeking the Lord's judgment, worn over the heart because guidance belongs close to the one who seeks it. I believe the pattern still stands — God's people do not guess His will, they ask for it, and He answers.
