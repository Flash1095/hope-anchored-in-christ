maʻădân

a delicacy or (abstractly) pleasure (adverbially, cheerfully)

Biblical Usage

The word names delicacies, the rich food and pleasures that mark a life of ease, and it appears in blessing and in warning alike. Asher's bread shall be fat and he shall yield royal dainties, the daughters of Zion who lived delicately are brought down in Lamentations, and the proverb says a son who is corrected will give delight to his soul. Agag came delicately before Samuel, thinking the bitterness of death was past.

Theological Significance

I believe delicacies are a gift of God to enjoy, but a dangerous master when they become the goal of life. I hold that the delight God gives is meant to point us back to the Giver, and the one who corrects a child in love gives a deeper delight than any feast.
