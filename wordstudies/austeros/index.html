austērós

from a (presumed) derivative of the same as G109 (meaning blown); rough (properly as a gale), i.e. (figuratively) severe:--austere.

Biblical Usage

The wicked servant called his master an austere man, one who takes up what he did not lay down and reaps what he did not sow, and the master answered him out of his own mouth. The word means severe or exacting.

Theological Significance

I hold that the servant's excuse condemned him, because he knew his master's character and still buried the talent.
