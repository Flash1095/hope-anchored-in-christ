oikouménē

feminine participle present passive of G3611 (as noun, by implication, of G1093); land, i.e. the (terrene part of the) globe; specially, the Roman empire:--earth, world.

Biblical Usage

Oikoumene is the inhabited world, the whole earth where people live, and by usage the Roman empire. Caesar's decree taxes all the world in Luke 2:1, and Agabus foretells a dearth throughout all the world in Acts 11:28. Jesus promises the gospel will be preached in all the world in Matthew 24:14, and Paul says the sound of the gospel has gone to the ends of the world in Romans 10:18.

Theological Significance

I believe the inhabited world is the field of the gospel, and the same word that taxes the world in Luke also hears the gospel in Matthew. That is why I hold that the world is not too big for the gospel, it is exactly the size God intended.
