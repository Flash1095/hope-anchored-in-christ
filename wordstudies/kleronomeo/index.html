klēronoméō

from G2818; to be an heir to (literally or figuratively):--be heir, (obtain by) inherit(-ance).

Biblical Usage

Kleronomeo means to be an heir, to inherit. Matthew 5:5 promises the meek shall inherit the earth, Mark 10:17 and Luke 10:25 both ask, what shall I do that I may inherit eternal life, 1 Corinthians 6:9 warns the unrighteous shall not inherit the kingdom of God, Galatians 4:30 says the son of the bondwoman shall not be heir with the son of the freewoman, and Hebrews 1:4 says Christ hath by inheritance obtained a more excellent name than the angels.

Theological Significance

I believe inheritance in the Bible is always by promise, never by wages: the meek inherit the earth because God gives it, and the unrighteous do not inherit because God withholds it. I hold that the question in Mark 10:17 and Luke 10:25 is the question every man must answer, and the answer is the Son who inherited the excellent name.
