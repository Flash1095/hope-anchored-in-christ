shâchâh

to depress, i.e. prostrate (especially reflexive, in homage to royalty or God)

Biblical Usage

Shachah is to bow down until the face touches the ground, and it is the Bible's ordinary word for worship. Abraham told his servants he would go yonder and worship before he climbed the mountain with Isaac, and Moses made haste and bowed his head toward the earth when God's glory passed by. The psalms turn the bow into a call, come let us worship and bow down and kneel before the LORD our maker, and when the fire fell on the temple all Israel bowed with their faces to the ground.

Theological Significance

I believe worship begins in the body, not only in the mind, because bowing says what words cannot. The same bow is offered to kings and to idols, so the question is never whether we bow, but to whom. I hold that the only bow that frees a person is the one bent before the LORD.
