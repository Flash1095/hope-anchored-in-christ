ekkathaírō

from G1537 and G2508; to cleanse thoroughly:--purge (out).

Biblical Usage

This word means to cleanse thoroughly, to purge out, and Paul uses it twice, once for the church and once for the man. Purge out the old leaven, he says, that you may be a new lump, and if a man purge himself from dishonourable vessels, he shall be a vessel unto honour, sanctified and fit for the Master's use. The word is housekeeping for the soul.

Theological Significance

I believe God purges what He loves, because a clean vessel is the only kind the Master can use.
