adēmonéō

from a derivative of ἀδέω (to be sated to loathing); to be in distress (of mind):--be full of heaviness, be very heavy.

Biblical Usage

Ademoneo is distress of mind — heaviness that presses down on the soul. Matthew and Mark use it of Jesus in Gethsemane, sorrowful and very heavy as He faced the cross, and Paul uses it of Epaphroditus, distressed because the Philippians had heard he was ill. The word covers both the Saviour's agony and a missionary's anxious care for his friends.

Theological Significance

This word tells me that heaviness of heart is not unspiritual — Jesus Himself felt it, and Scripture names it plainly. The same Saviour who was very heavy in Gethsemane understands the weight His people carry, and He does not despise them for feeling it.
