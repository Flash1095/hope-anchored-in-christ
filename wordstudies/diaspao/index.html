diaspáō

from G1223 and G4685; to draw apart, i.e. sever or dismember:--pluck asunder, pull in pieces.

Biblical Usage

To tear apart is what a mob does, and the word stands twice in scenes of violence. The crowd in Jerusalem seizes Paul and tears him apart, and the demoniac in the tombs tears his chains asunder, and no man can tame him.

Theological Significance

I hold that tearing is what a crowd does when it has no truth left; the mob tore at Paul and the demon tore at the boy, and in both places Christ stood between the torn and those who tore.
