rhýomai

middle voice of an obsolete verb, akin to G4482 (through the idea of a current; compare G4511); to rush or draw (for oneself), i.e. rescue:--deliver(-er).

Biblical Usage

Deliver us from evil, we pray every day, and Zacharias sang that being delivered out of the hand of our enemies we might serve God without fear. Paul says God delivered us from so great a death and doth deliver, He hath delivered us from the power of darkness and translated us into the kingdom of His dear Son, and the Lord shall deliver me from every evil work and preserve me unto His heavenly kingdom.

Theological Significance

What I take from this is that deliverance is the whole gospel in one word, because we cannot deliver ourselves. God delivers from evil, from death, from darkness and from the evil work, and He does it again and again until the last deliverance brings us home. I hold that the man who prays deliver us from evil is praying the prayer God loves to answer.
