lûwts

properly, to make mouths at, i.e. to scoff; hence (from the effort to pronounce a foreign language) to interpret, or (generally) intercede

Biblical Usage

The word carries two faces: it names the interpreter who stands between speakers, and the scorner who mocks what is true. Joseph's brothers did not know he understood them, because he spoke to them by an interpreter, and the same root names the scoffers Proverbs warns against. Isaiah tells the mockers not to mock, lest their bands be made strong, and Proverbs says the scorner delights in his scorning while the wise man loves rebuke.

Theological Significance

I believe the difference between an interpreter and a scorner is the heart: one stands between people to help them understand, the other stands above them to laugh. I hold that God gives understanding to the humble, but the mocker closes his own ears and calls it wisdom.
