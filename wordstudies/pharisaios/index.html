Pharisaîos

of Hebrew origin (compare H6567); a separatist, i.e. exclusively religious; a Pharisean, i.e. Jewish sectary:--Pharisee.

Biblical Usage

Pharisaios is a Pharisee, a separatist, one of the party that kept the law with a zeal that became a wall. Jesus says they sit in Moses' seat and tells the people to do what they say but not what they do, and He pronounces woe after woe on scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, who shut the kingdom against men. The Pharisee in the temple thanks God he is not like other men, and Paul, who was himself a Pharisee, calls his own zeal loss for Christ.

Theological Significance

I believe the Pharisees are the Bible's warning about religion without mercy: right doctrine, exact practice, and a heart that shuts the door on sinners. Jesus did not condemn their law-keeping; He condemned their love of the seat and the title and the broad phylactery. My position is that I must watch for the Pharisee in myself, because the one who thanks God he is not like other men is the one furthest from the publican's prayer.
