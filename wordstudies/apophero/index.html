apophérō

from G575 and G5342; to bear off (literally or relatively):--bring, carry (away).

Biblical Usage

To bear off, to carry away. The chief priests bound Jesus and carried Him away and delivered Him to Pilate in Mark 15:1, and the beggar Lazarus died and was carried by the angels into Abraham's bosom in Luke 16:22. Handkerchiefs from Paul's body were brought to the sick in Acts 19:12, and the diseases departed from them, and John was carried away in the spirit to a great and high mountain in Revelation 21:10 to see the holy Jerusalem.

Theological Significance

I believe the word carries the whole gospel in miniature: Jesus carried away to Pilate, Lazarus carried away to Abraham's bosom, the sick carried away from their diseases, and John carried away to see the city. Being carried is the Christian life — I do not climb to God; I am borne to Him.
