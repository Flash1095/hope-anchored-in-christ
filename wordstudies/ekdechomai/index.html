ekdéchomai

from G1537 and G1209; to accept from some source, i.e. (by implication) to await:--expect, look (tarry) for, wait (for).

Biblical Usage

To wait for is to expect and receive, and the word covers both waiting and welcoming. Paul tells the Corinthians to send Timothy on his way in peace, that he may come to him, for he looks for him with the brethren, and Luke says Paul's spirit was stirred while he waited at Athens. Abraham looked for a city with foundations, and James says the husbandman waits for the precious fruit of the earth with long patience.

Theological Significance

I believe waiting on God is active, not idle, because Abraham looked for a city while he lived in tents, and the farmer waits while he works. I hold that the God who is worth waiting for is worth waiting on.
