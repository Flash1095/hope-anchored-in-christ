analýō

from G303 and G3089; to break up, i.e. depart (literally or figuratively):--depart, return.

Biblical Usage

The word means to break up, to depart — and to return: be like men that wait for their lord when he will return from the wedding, that when he comes and knocks they may open to him immediately, and Paul says he is in a strait between two, having a desire to depart and to be with Christ, which is far better. The same word is the lord returning and the apostle departing, and both are a breaking up of camp.

Theological Significance

A word for departing carries no doctrine of its own; its weight is the two directions — the lord returning to his servants, and Paul longing to depart and be with Christ.
