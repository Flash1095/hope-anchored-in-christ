nâchal

to inherit (as a (figurative) mode of descent), or (generally) to occupy; causatively, to bequeath, or (generally) distribute, instate

Biblical Usage

Nachal as a verb means to inherit or to take possession, and it is the word for entering the land. In Deuteronomy 12:10 the people are told they will go over Jordan and dwell in the land the Lord gives them to inherit, and in Deuteronomy 1:38 Joshua shall cause Israel to inherit the land. in Isaiah 49:8 the Servant is given to cause the people to inherit the desolate heritages, and in Isaiah 57:13 the one who trusts in the Lord shall inherit His holy mountain.

Theological Significance

I believe inheritance in the Bible is received, not earned, because the land was a gift before it was a possession. Israel inherited Canaan for the same reason the trusting inherit the mountain: God gives it to those who trust Him. I hold that my inheritance is not a place I buy, but a promise I receive.
