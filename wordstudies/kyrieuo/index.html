kyrieúō

from G2962; to rule:--have dominion over, lord, be lord of, exercise lordship over.

Biblical Usage

To have dominion is to rule as a lord, and the word draws the line between God's rule and man's. Paul says in Romans 6:14 that "sin shall not have dominion over you," and in Romans 6:9 that death "hath no more dominion" over the risen Christ; but in Luke 22:25 the kings of the Gentiles "exercise lordship over them," and in 2 Corinthians 1:24 Paul refuses to have dominion over the Corinthians' faith.

Theological Significance

In my reading, the word's contrast is the gospel in one verb: sin and death have lost their dominion, Christ alone is Lord, and no man is to lord it over another's faith. Sin has lost its rule, death has lost its rule, and Christ alone is Lord.
