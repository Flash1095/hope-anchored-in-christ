ekgamískō

from G1537 and G1061; the same as 1547:--give in marriage.

Biblical Usage

This word means to give in marriage, and it stands in the Textus Receptus reading of the days before the flood, when men were marrying and giving in marriage until Noah entered the ark. The Greek text used for this study carries only the plain verb to marry in that verse, so this compound form belongs to the older printed text, not to the manuscripts behind it. The two spellings, with iz and with isk, are the same verb in different dress.

Theological Significance

I believe the days of Noah warn me plainly, because life went on as usual, marrying and giving in marriage, until the flood came and took them all away.
