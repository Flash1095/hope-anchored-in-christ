anaplēróō

from G303 and G4137; to complete; by implication, to occupy, supply; figuratively, to accomplish (by coincidence ot obedience):--fill up, fulfill, occupy, supply.

Biblical Usage

The word means to fill up, to complete: in the hearers of the parables the prophecy of Isaiah is fulfilled, hearing and not understanding; Paul says bear one another's burdens and so fulfil the law of Christ; the unlearned man cannot say Amen because he does not understand, though he occupies the room; and Epaphroditus risked his life to supply the Philippians' lack of service toward Paul. The filling up is a prophecy completed, a law fulfilled, a place occupied, and a lack supplied.

Theological Significance

I believe this word is about completion: the prophecy is fulfilled in the very people who refuse to hear it, and the law of Christ is fulfilled when I carry a burden that is not mine. The filling up is never about adding to what God said — it is about His word coming true, and His people supplying what is lacking in one another's service.
