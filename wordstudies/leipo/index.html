leípō

a primary verb; to leave, i.e. (intransitively or passively) to fail or be absent:--be destitute (wanting), lack.

Biblical Usage

leípō means to lack, to be wanting — plain enough to miss, and too important to pass over. In James 1:5, "If any of you lack wisdom"; and in Luke 18:22, "Yet lackest thou one thing: sell all that thou hast". In James 1:4, "wanting nothing."; and in Titus 1:5, "that thou shouldest set in order the things that are wanting".

Theological Significance

I believe lack is the doorway to prayer — if any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, who gives liberally. I hold that the rich young ruler lacked one thing, and it was the one thing he would not give up. My position is that I would rather lack everything and have Christ than lack Him and have everything.
