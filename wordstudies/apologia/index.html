apología

from the same as G626; a plea ("apology"):--answer (for self), clearing of self, defence.

Biblical Usage

A plea, a defence, an answer for oneself — the word our ‘apology’ comes from, though it means the opposite of saying sorry. Peter commands it in 1 Peter 3:15: be ready always to give an answer to every man that asketh you a reason of the hope that is in you, with meekness and fear. Paul makes his defence before the Jerusalem crowd in Acts 22:1, counts the Philippians partakers with him in the defence and confirmation of the gospel in Philippians 1:7, and remembers in 2 Timothy 4:16 that at his first answer no man stood with him.

Theological Significance

I believe Peter's verse sets the tone for all the rest: ready always, but with meekness and fear. A defence that is loud and proud is no defence at all; the hope itself is the argument, and gentleness is how it is given. Paul stood alone at his first answer and still gave it — and I am to be ready to do the same.
