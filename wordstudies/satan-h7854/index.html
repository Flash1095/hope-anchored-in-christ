sâṭân

an opponent; especially (with the article prefixed) Satan, the arch-enemy of good

Biblical Usage

Satan is an adversary, and in the Old Testament the word is used of men as well as of the devil. The Philistines feared David would become an adversary to them in battle; David called the sons of Zeruiah adversaries for urging death on the day he became king; Solomon's reign had rest on every side, so that there was neither adversary nor evil occurrent. But the word also names the arch-enemy himself: Satan stood up against Israel, and provoked David to number Israel.

Theological Significance

I believe the common word is the warning: an adversary is anyone who stands against God's people, and the great Adversary stands behind them all. David learned it when a numbering that seemed harmless came from Satan's provoking. And I hold that the only rest from adversaries is the rest God gives — Solomon had it until he lost it, and we have it only in the Lord.
