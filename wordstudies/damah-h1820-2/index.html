dâmâh

to be dumb or silent; hence, to fail or perish; trans. to destroy

Biblical Usage

The word means to fall silent, to fail, to be destroyed, and it gathers the silences of Scripture into one. Isaiah cries that he is undone before the holy God, Hosea warns that the people will fall, and Lamentations weeps that the tears have no rest. Silence before God is either judgment or worship, and this word knows both.

Theological Significance

I believe the verse in Isaiah is the hinge: the man who says he is undone is the man God then cleanses and sends. Silence is where the vision begins.
