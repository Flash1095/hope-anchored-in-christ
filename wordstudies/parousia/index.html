parousía

from the present participle of G3918; a being near, i.e. advent (often, return; specially, of Christ to punish Jerusalem, or finally the wicked); (by implication) physically, aspect:--coming, presence.

Biblical Usage

Parousia is presence, a being near, and in the New Testament it is most often the coming of Christ. Paul says in 1 Thessalonians 4:15 that those alive at the coming of the Lord shall not prevent those who are asleep, and in 1 Corinthians 15:23 that every man in his own order: Christ the firstfruits, afterward they that are Christ's at His coming. John says in 1 John 2:28 to abide in Him, that when He shall appear we may have confidence at His coming. The word also covers a human arrival: in 2 Corinthians 7:6 God comforts Paul by the coming of Titus.

Theological Significance

I believe the coming of Christ is the hope that orders everything else: the dead rise, the living are caught up, and no believer needs to be ashamed at His appearing. That is why I hold that abiding is the preparation for the parousia, and the comfort of Titus's arrival is a small picture of the comfort His arrival will be.
