zârach

properly, to irradiate (or shoot forth beams), i.e. to rise (as the sun); specifically, to appear (as a symptom of leprosy)

Biblical Usage

Zarach is the rising of the sun, the light breaking over the horizon after the night. Genesis says the sun rose on Jacob as he limped away from Peniel, and Ecclesiastes notes the same sun rises and sets day after day without fail. Isaiah lifts the word into promise, calling the people to arise and shine because their light has come, and Malachi calls the coming day the Sun of righteousness rising with healing in his wings.

Theological Significance

I believe the sunrise is God's daily sermon that darkness does not get the last word. Every morning the light comes back whether we deserve it or not, and that is grace in its simplest form. My position is that the Sun of righteousness has already risen, and the healing Malachi promised is found in Him.
