ʼărubbâh

a lattice; (by implication) a window, dovecot (because of the pigeon-holes), chimney (with its apertures for smoke), sluice (with openings for water)

Biblical Usage

The window is the lattice in the sky, and Scripture opens it for flood and for blessing alike. In the days of Noah the windows of heaven were opened, a lord of Samaria scoffs that even if the LORD should make windows in heaven such a thing could not be, and Malachi promises that God will open the windows of heaven and pour out a blessing there is not room enough to receive.

Theological Significance

I believe the windows of heaven say the same thing in judgment and in blessing: heaven is not shut. It opens to flood the earth, and it opens to feed the hungry, and I hold that the God who opens the windows is the same God in both, and His opening is always according to His word.
