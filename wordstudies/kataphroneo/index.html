kataphronéō

from G2596 and G5426; to think against, i.e. disesteem:--despise.

Biblical Usage

To think against is to despise, and Jesus says no man can serve two masters, for he will hate the one and despise the other, and warns us not to despise one of these little ones. Paul asks whether we despise the riches of God's goodness, and tells Timothy to let no man despise his youth. The word names the quiet contempt that chooses the wrong master and scorns the small and the young.

Theological Significance

I believe despising is the sin I commit without noticing, because it hides inside a preference for money or a shrug at a child. I hold that God's goodness is not to be despised but to lead me to repentance, and I will not despise the young when God has given them the word.
