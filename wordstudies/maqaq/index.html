mâqaq

to melt; figuratively, to flow, dwindle, vanish

Biblical Usage

mâqaq is to melt away, to pine and dwindle. In Leviticus 26:39 those left in their enemies' lands shall pine away in their iniquity, and in Ezekiel 33:10 the house of Israel asks how they shall live if they pine away in their sins. In Psalms 38:5 the psalmist's wounds stink and are corrupt, and in Zechariah 14:12 the flesh of Jerusalem's enemies shall consume away while they stand. The word is the slow wasting of sin and judgment alike.

Theological Significance

I believe mâqaq tells the truth about sin, that it wastes a person away from the inside. I hold that the only answer to pining away is the repentance Ezekiel's hearers were offered, and I will take it.
