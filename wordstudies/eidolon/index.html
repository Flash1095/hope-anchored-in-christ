eídōlon

from G1491; an image (i.e. for worship); by implication, a heathen god, or (plural) the worship of such:--idol.

Biblical Usage

An idol is an image made for worship, a god shaped by hands, and Paul says plainly that an idol is nothing in the world and that there is no other God but one. He asks what an idol is, and answers that it is nothing, yet the Corinthians still feared the meat offered to it, and John closes his letter with the warning to keep yourselves from idols. The Thessalonians turned from idols to serve the living and true God, and that turning is the whole story.

Theological Significance

I believe an idol is nothing, and that is exactly why it is dangerous, because it takes the worship owed to the living God and gives it to nothing. I hold that turning from idols is not a one-time act but a daily one.
