qetseph

a splinter (as chipped off); figuratively, rage or strife

Biblical Usage

When wrath went out from the LORD, Moses sent Aaron with incense to make atonement, and the plague was stayed. The same word falls on a people rooted out of their land in anger, and lifts when God says, in a little wrath I hid my face from thee for a moment, but with everlasting kindness will I have mercy on thee. Zechariah heard it as a great wrath from the LORD of hosts, and Isaiah heard the promise inside it.

Theological Significance

I believe God's wrath is real and righteous, and the same God who hides his face for a moment keeps everlasting kindness for his people. I hold that his anger is never the last word, because his mercy outlasts it.
