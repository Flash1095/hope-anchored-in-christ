synérchomai

from G4862 and G2064; to convene, depart in company with, associate with, or (specially), cohabit (conjugally):--accompany, assemble (with), come (together), come (company, go) with, resort.

Biblical Usage

When the disciples come together they ask whether the Lord will restore the kingdom to Israel, and Paul says when they come together into one place it is not to eat the Lord's supper, because their divisions have spoiled the meal. Yet when they come together rightly, every one has a psalm, a doctrine, a tongue, a revelation, an interpretation, and all is done to edifying. The same word covers Mary and Joseph before they came together and the Jews who came with Mary weeping to the tomb of Lazarus, so coming together can be a wedding, a funeral, a church, or a kingdom question, and the Lord is in the midst of it all.

Theological Significance

I believe coming together is what the church does, and I hold that the test of any gathering is whether it edifies. I take the warning with the promise: a gathering can shame the Lord's supper or honour it, and I want mine to honour it.
