ʻAmmôwnîyth

an Ammonitess

Biblical Usage

The Ammonites were Lot's descendants through his younger daughter, and they stayed enemies of Israel for centuries. Nahash the Ammonite besieged Jabesh-gilead and demanded the right eye of every man as the price of peace; centuries later, when the exiles came home, the law was read aloud again — no Ammonite was ever to enter the congregation of God, because they had refused Israel bread and water on the road out of Egypt and hired Balaam to curse them.

Theological Significance

I believe the Ammonite story is a warning about how a small refusal hardens into a lasting barrier: they would not give bread and water to tired travellers, and that one unkindness echoed for generations. God remembers how His people are treated, and He also remembers that outsiders like Ruth still found their way in by faith.
