ʻâbar

to cross over; used very widely of any transition (literal or figurative; transitive, intransitive, intensive, causative); specifically, to cover (in copulation)

Biblical Usage

This is the great crossing-word of the Old Testament — to pass over, go through, or cross to the other side. Jacob says, 'with my staff I passed over this Jordan'; the priests stand firm while 'all the Israelites passed over' on dry ground; and all the people 'passed over' the brook Kidron as David flees Jerusalem.

Theological Significance

Crossing over is the shape of the whole Bible story: from one side to the other, from bondage to promise, from danger to safety. I believe every crossing in Scripture points to the same truth — God makes a way where there is no way, and He goes with His people through the water.
