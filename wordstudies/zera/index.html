zeraʻ

seed; figuratively, fruit, plant, sowing-time, posterity

Biblical Usage

Seed is one of the Bible's biggest little words. It begins in creation — every plant yielding seed after its kind — and grows into the promise: Abraham's seed, the seed of the woman who would crush the serpent's head, the seed of David. By Isaiah the word carries the Servant's seed, the offspring who prolong His days after He is cut off. From garden to gospel, seed is how God promises a future.

Theological Significance

I believe the seed of the woman in Genesis 3:15 is the thread that runs through the whole Bible. God promised a descendant, kept the line through Abraham and David, and brought it to Christ — the seed who was bruised and yet bruised the serpent. My position is that every promise of seed is a promise of a person, and that person is Jesus.
