Athēnai

plural of Ἀθήνη (the goddess of wisdom, who was reputed to have founded the city); Athenæ, the capitol of Greece:-- Athens.

Biblical Usage

Athens was the city of philosophers where Paul waited for Silas and Timothy, and his spirit was stirred when he saw it wholly given to idolatry. He reasoned in the synagogue and in the marketplace and spoke on the Areopagus, then went on to Corinth, and later wrote that he had been left at Athens alone.

Theological Significance

I believe Athens shows the gospel meeting the world's wisdom on its own ground, and Paul did not flinch. The city that had an altar to the unknown god heard the name of the God it did not know, and I hold that no city is too clever for the cross.
