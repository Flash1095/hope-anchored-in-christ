hymnéō

from G5215; to hymn, i.e. sing a religious ode; by implication, to celebrate (God) in song:--sing a hymn (praise unto).

Biblical Usage

Scripture uses hymnéō where we would say to sing a hymn, to praise. In Matthew 26:30, the text says "And when they had sung an hymn." In Mark 14:26, the text says "And when they had sung an hymn."

Theological Significance

For me, hymnéō is a reminder that worship is not a performance but a response — we answer what God has done with what we are. That shapes how I read the passages where it appears: not as filler, but as God's own wording for what He is doing.
