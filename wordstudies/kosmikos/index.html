kosmikós

from G2889 (in its secondary sense); terrene ("cosmic"), literally (mundane) or figuratively (corrupt):--worldly.

Biblical Usage

Kosmikos means worldly, of this world, terrene. Titus 2:12 teaches us to deny ungodliness and worldly lusts, and to live soberly, righteously, and godly in this present world, and Hebrews 9:1 says the first covenant had ordinances of divine service, and a worldly sanctuary.

Theological Significance

I believe the worldly sanctuary and the worldly lusts are the two things the new covenant leaves behind: a sanctuary made with hands, and a life ruled by lusts.
