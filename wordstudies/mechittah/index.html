mᵉchittâh

properly, a dissolution; concretely, a ruin, or (abstractly) consternation

Biblical Usage

Destruction is the ruin that follows folly, and Proverbs says the mouth of the foolish is near destruction, and the way of the LORD is destruction to the workers of iniquity. The fool's mouth is his own destruction, the psalm says God cast the king's crown to the ground, and Jeremiah prays that God would not be a terror to him in the day of evil.

Theological Significance

I believe destruction is usually self-inflicted: the fool's mouth destroys him, the worker of iniquity walks into it, and the crown cast down is the end of a covenant broken. Jeremiah's prayer is the way out — make God your hope, not your terror, and the day of evil will not destroy you.
