kâmâr

properly, an ascetic (as if shrunk with self-maceration), i.e. an idolatrous priest (only in plural)

Biblical Usage

Kamar is the idolatrous priest — the Chemarim, the black-robed priests of Baal whom Josiah put down, whose name the prophets use for the priests of the calf at Bethel and the remnant of Baal God promises to cut off. The name itself became a byword for a priesthood God rejected.

Theological Significance

I believe the Chemarim show that false worship always finds its priests. Baal had his, the calf had his, and God cut them off — and that is why I hold that true worship needs no priest but Christ.
