kippur

expiation (only in plural)

Biblical Usage

Kippur is expiation, the atonement, and it is always plural in this form. Exodus 29:36 commands the daily bullock for a sin offering for atonement, Exodus 30:10 has Aaron making atonement on the horns of the altar once a year with the blood of the sin offering of atonements, and Leviticus 23:27 and Leviticus 23:28 name the tenth day of the seventh month a day of atonement, when Israel shall afflict their souls and do no work. Numbers 5:8 provides the ram of the atonement for a trespass with no kinsman, and Leviticus 25:9 sounds the trumpet of the jubile on the day of atonement.

Theological Significance

I believe the day of atonement is the hinge of the whole year: one day when the blood is carried in and the people wait outside. I hold that every kippur pointed forward to the one atonement that does not need repeating, because the blood offered once is enough.
