máchaira

probably feminine of a presumed derivative of G3163; a knife, i.e. dirk; figuratively, war, judicial punishment:--sword.

Biblical Usage

The word names a sword, a knife or dirk, and it is the weapon of the New Testament. Peter drew his sword and cut off the ear of the high priest's servant, and Jesus told him to put up his sword, for all they that take the sword shall perish with the sword. Paul calls the word of God the sword of the Spirit, and Hebrews says it is sharper than any twoedged sword, piercing to the dividing of soul and spirit.

Theological Significance

I believe the sword shows us both kinds of warfare: Peter's sword cut a man's ear and was rebuked, while the sword of the Spirit cuts the heart and saves. I hold that the only sword Christians are told to take up is the word of God, and it is sharper than any blade men forge.
