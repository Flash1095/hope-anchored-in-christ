shâlam

to be safe (in mind, body or estate); figuratively, to be (causatively, make) completed; by implication, to be friendly; by extension, to reciprocate (in various applications)

Biblical Usage

Shalam is the word behind shalom, and it means to be whole, to make whole, and to pay back in full. The owner of a pit shall make it good, and the thief shall make full restitution, for the law measures justice by making things whole again. David asks whether he has rewarded evil to a friend at peace with him, and Solomon offered peace offerings three times a year on the altar he built.

Theological Significance

I believe peace is not the absence of trouble, it is wholeness restored. The law made a thief restore what he stole, and the peace offering made a worshipper whole before God. That is why I hold that Christ is our shalom, because He paid back in full what we could never repay.
