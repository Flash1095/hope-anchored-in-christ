leitourgía

from G3008; public function (as priest ("liturgy") or almsgiver):--ministration(-try), service.

Biblical Usage

Ministry is what leitourgía means — public service done for God and for His people, and the word carries the weight of both. In Hebrews 8:6, "But now hath he obtained a more excellent ministry"; and in 2 Corinthians 9:12, "For the administration of this service not only supplieth the want of the saints". In Philippians 2:17, "and if I be offered upon the sacrifice and service of your faith"; and in Luke 1:23, "as soon as the days of his ministration were accomplished".

Theological Significance

I believe Christ's ministry is more excellent because it rests on a better covenant — the old service was a shadow, and His is the substance. I hold that the gift of the Gentile churches and Paul's poured-out life are the same service: supplying want, and offering faith. My position is that my ministry is measured by what it gives, not by what it gets.
