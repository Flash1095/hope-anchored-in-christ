gínomai

a prolongation and middle voice form of a primary verb; to cause to be ("gen"-erate), i.e. (reflexively) to become (come into being), used with great latitude (literal, figurative, intensive, etc.):--arise, be assembled, be(-come, -fall, -have self), be brought (to pass), (be) come (to pass), continue, be divided, draw, be ended, fall, be finished, follow, be found, be fulfilled, + God forbid, grow, happen, have, be kept, be made, be married, be ordained to be, partake, pass, be performed, be published, require, seem, be showed, X soon as it was, sound, be taken, be turned, use, wax, will, would, be wrought.

Biblical Usage

gínomai — arise, be assembled, be, be brought (to pass), (be) come (to pass), continue, be divided, draw, be ended, fall, be finished, follow, be found, be fulfilled, + God forbid, grow, happen, have, be kept, be made, be married, be ordained to be, partake, pass, be performed, be published, require, seem, be showed, X soon as it was, sound, be taken, be turned, use, wax, will, would, be wrought. That is the whole of it, and it is enough: 625 occurrences, and in Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John the meaning never wavers. I keep coming back to the simple reading because the simple reading is the true one.

Theological Significance

What gínomai tells me is that God speaks plainly, and plain words carry real weight. I hold that the simple reading is the true one, and I will not trade it for a clever one.
