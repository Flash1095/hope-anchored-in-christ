peitharchéō

from a compound of G3982 and G757; to be persuaded by a ruler, i.e. (genitive case) to submit to authority; by analogy, to conform to advice:--hearken, obey (magistrates).

Biblical Usage

Peitharcheo means to obey authority, to submit to a ruler. Peter told the council, “We ought to obey God rather than men,” and the Spirit is given to those who obey Him. Paul told the sailors they should have hearkened to him and not left Crete, and Titus is told to remind believers to obey magistrates and be ready for every good work.

Theological Significance

I am sure that obedience has an order: God first, and every human authority under Him. When rulers command what God forbids, I obey God — and when they command what is good, I obey them gladly, because God put them there. Peter's answer still stands as the rule for every clash between the two, and I will not trade it for a quieter life.
