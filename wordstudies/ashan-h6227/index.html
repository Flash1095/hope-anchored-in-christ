ʻâshân

smoke, literally or figuratively (vapor, dust, anger)

Biblical Usage

Smoke is the sign of God's presence, and it fills the Bible from the covenant to the temple. A smoking furnace and a burning lamp pass between the pieces of Abram's sacrifice, Mount Sinai is altogether on a smoke because the LORD descends on it in fire, the house of the temple is filled with smoke when the seraphim cry Holy, and David says there went up a smoke out of His nostrils.

Theological Significance

I believe smoke is the visible sign of a God who cannot be looked at: fire on the mountain, a furnace in the dark, a temple full of cloud. It hides Him and it shows Him at once, and I hold that the smoke over Sinai and the smoke in the temple are the same sign, that God has come down to meet His people.
