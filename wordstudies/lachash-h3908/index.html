lachash

properly, a whisper, i.e. by implication, (in a good sense) a private prayer, (in a bad one) an incantation; concretely, an amulet

Biblical Usage

The whisper is the word for what is said low, and it covers a charm, an amulet, and a prayer. In Ecclesiastes 10:11 the serpent bites "without enchantment," in Jeremiah 8:17 God sends serpents "which will not be charmed," in Isaiah 3:3 the "eloquent orator" is literally the skilful whisperer, and in Isaiah 26:16 the afflicted "poured out a prayer" — a whispered prayer — when God's chastening was upon them.

Theological Significance

I take it that the word's range is a warning about power: whispered charms cannot stop the serpent, but a whispered prayer in trouble reaches the God who chastens and heals. The charmer's whisper fails, and the sufferer's whisper is heard.
