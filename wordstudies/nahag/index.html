nâhag

to drive forth (a person, an animal or chariot), i.e. lead, carry away; reflexively, to proceed (i.e. impel or guide oneself); also (from the panting induced by effort), to sigh

Biblical Usage

Nahag means to drive or lead forth, to carry away people or cattle or a cart, and it is used for armies and for a woman on an ass. In 1 Chronicles 13:7 Uzza and Ahio drave the cart that carried the ark of God, and in 1 Samuel 23:5 David brought away the cattle of the Philistines and saved Keilah. In 2 Chronicles 25:11 Amaziah led forth his people to the valley of salt and smote the children of Seir, and in 2 Kings 4:24 the Shunammite says to her servant, Drive, and go forward, and slack not.

Theological Significance

I hold that driving and leading are the same word because the work is the same: getting people and things where they need to go. The cart, the cattle, the army, and the ass all move because someone drives them, and the Bible notices who is holding the reins.
