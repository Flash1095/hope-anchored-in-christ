hairéomai

probably akin to G142; to take for oneself, i.e. to prefer:--choose. Some of the forms are borrowed from a cognate ἕλλομαι; which is otherwise obsolete.

Biblical Usage

Scripture uses hairéomai where we would say to choose. In Philippians 1:22, the text says "yet what I shall choose I wot not." The word keeps that same sense wherever it appears.

Theological Significance

I take hairéomai to mean that faith is not a feeling but a holding-on — trusting what God has said when nothing else makes sense. That is why I keep this word in mind when I read those passages — it carries more than its size suggests.
