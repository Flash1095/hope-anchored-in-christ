lâqaṭ

properly, to pick up, i.e. (generally) to gather; specifically, to glean

Biblical Usage

lâqaṭ means to pick up, and the contexts could hardly be more different. In Exodus 16:16, "Gather of it every man according to his eating"; and in Ruth 2:3, "and gleaned in the field after the reapers: and her hap was to light on a part of the field [belonging] unto Boaz". In Leviticus 19:9, "neither shalt thou gather the gleanings of thy harvest."; and in 2 Kings 4:39, "and gathered thereof wild gourds his lap full".

Theological Significance

I believe gleaning is God's welfare system — the field is never stripped bare because the poor still have a right to eat. I hold that the God who rained manna for a day also left grain in the corners for Ruth. My position is that provision is not charity added on afterwards; it is built into the harvest from the start.
