lâchats

properly, to press, i.e. (figuratively) to distress

Biblical Usage

To oppress is the verb of the same weight, and it is laid at the door of Egypt, of Syria, and of every unjust power. In Exodus 3:9 God has seen "the oppression wherewith the Egyptians oppress" Israel, in 1 Samuel 10:18 He reminds Israel He delivered them "out of the hand of all kingdoms, and of them that oppressed you," and in Isaiah 19:20 Egypt's oppressed will cry to the Lord and He will send them a saviour.

Theological Significance

In my reading, the verb is the promise and the warning together: The promise stands for Egypt, for Syria, and for every unjust power since. The promise stands for Egypt, for Syria, and for every unjust power since.
