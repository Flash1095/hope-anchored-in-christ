ʻânâph

a twig (as covering the limbs)

Biblical Usage

The word means a twig, a branch — the limbs of a tree. In Aramaic it describes the great tree of Nebuchadnezzar's dream: the leaves were fair, the fruit was much, the beasts of the field had shadow under it, and the fowls of the heaven dwelt in the boughs thereof; and in the interpretation Daniel repeats it, the branches where the birds had their habitation. In Hebrew the same word is the twig of Ezekiel's cedar and the boughs of the feast of tabernacles.

Theological Significance

A word for a twig carries no doctrine of its own; its weight is the tree — the great tree of Nebuchadnezzar's dream, whose branches sheltered the beasts and the birds until it was cut down.
