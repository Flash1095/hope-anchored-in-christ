Bêyth ha-Yshîy-môwth

Beth-ha-Jeshimoth, a town East of the Jordan

Biblical Usage

Beth-Jeshimoth means house of the desolation, and it sits at both ends of Israel's story east of the Jordan. Israel camped from Beth-jesimoth to Abel-shittim in Numbers 33:49, on the edge of the promised land, and Joshua 12:3 and Joshua 13:20 list it among the conquered towns. Long after, Ezekiel 25:9 names it again among the cities of Moab that God would open to judgment.

Theological Significance

I believe a town called house of desolation, standing where Israel camped before crossing over, is a reminder that God brings his people through desolate places, not around them.
