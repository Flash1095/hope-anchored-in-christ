rhantízō

from a derivative of ῥαίνω (to sprinkle); to render besprinkled, i.e. asperse (ceremonially or figuratively):-- sprinkle.

Biblical Usage

The blood of bulls and of goats and the ashes of an heifer sprinkling the unclean sanctified to the purifying of the flesh, and Moses sprinkled both the book and all the people, and the tabernacle and all the vessels of the ministry. Let us draw near with our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.

Theological Significance

I am convinced that sprinkling is how God applies what He has provided. The blood was shed once, but it must be sprinkled where the sin is, and the heart sprinkled from an evil conscience is the heart made free to draw near. I hold that the old sprinklings were shadows, and the new is the blood of Jesus on the conscience itself.
