ʼăthar

a place; (adverb) after

Biblical Usage

This Aramaic word means a place, and then, by a small step, what comes after. The stone cut out without hands became a mountain and filled the whole earth, a kingdom would arise after Nebuchadnezzar's, Daniel saw a beast like a leopard, and after it another, and Cyrus ordered the house of God rebuilt in its place.

Theological Significance

I believe the word fits Daniel's whole message, because kingdoms rise in their place and fall to make room for the next, until the stone fills the earth. What comes after is God's doing, and I hold that His kingdom is the one that has no after.
