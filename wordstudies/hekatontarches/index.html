hekatontárchēs

or ἑκατόνταρχος from G1540 and G757; the captain of one hundred men:--centurion.

Biblical Usage

A centurion was a captain of a hundred soldiers, and the New Testament gives the rank to some of its finest believers. In Matthew 8:13 the centurion's servant is healed by a word, in Luke 7:2 the same captain's servant lies sick, in Acts 10:1 Cornelius the centurion sends for Peter, and in Acts 27:1 Julius the centurion takes charge of Paul.

Theological Significance

I believe the centurions are there to show that faith wears a uniform too: the man who commands a hundred soldiers says, speak the word only, and he is the one Jesus calls a man of great faith. I hold that their faith shames mine sometimes: they understood authority because they lived under it, and they trusted the word of Jesus the way a soldier trusts an order.
