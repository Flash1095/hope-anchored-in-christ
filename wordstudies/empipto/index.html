empíptō

from G1722 and G4098; to fall on, i.e. (literally) to be entrapped by, or (figuratively) be overwhelmed with:--fall among (into).

Biblical Usage

Empipto is to fall into something — a pit, a ditch, a snare. A novice lifted up with pride may fall into the condemnation of the devil in 1 Timothy 3:6, and the blind leading the blind both fall into the ditch in Luke 6:39. But the heaviest use is Hebrews 10:31: it is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God.

Theological Significance

I believe falling into God's hands is fearful only for those who refuse Him — the same hands that judge are the hands that lift the sheep out of the pit on the Sabbath, as Jesus says in Matthew 12:11. I hold that the fear of God is the beginning of wisdom because it teaches me where the pit is and who can pull me out.
