ʻâmâl

toil, i.e. wearing effort; hence, worry, wheth. of body or mind

Biblical Usage

Toil, wearing effort, worry of body and mind, and the Bible uses it for the weariness that work leaves behind. What profit hath a man of all his labour which he taketh under the sun? The Preacher keeps his heart from no joy, and his heart rejoices in all his labour, and this is his portion. Job curses the day because it shut not up the doors of his mother's womb, nor hid sorrow from his eyes; and the psalm counts the years: our strength is labour and sorrow, for it is soon cut off, and we fly away.

Theological Significance

I believe this word names the tiredness everyone knows and no one can fix by working harder. The labour is the portion and the sorrow is the shadow of it, and the psalm does not hide either — the years are short, the strength is labour and sorrow, and we fly away. I hold that the comfort is not in the toil ending but in the God who counts the years with us, and who gives rest to the labouring that the Preacher could not find under the sun.
