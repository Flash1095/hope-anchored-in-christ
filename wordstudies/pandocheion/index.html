pandocheîon

neuter of a presumed compound of G3956 and a derivative of G1209; all-receptive, i.e. a public lodging-place (caravanserai or khan):--inn.

Biblical Usage

Pandocheion is the inn, the place where all travellers are received. The good Samaritan binds up the wounded man's wounds, sets him on his own beast, and brings him to an inn and takes care of him.

Theological Significance

I hold that the inn is where mercy spends money: the Samaritan does not just feel sorry, he pays for the man's lodging and care.
