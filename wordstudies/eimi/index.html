eimí

the first person singular present indicative; a prolonged form of a primary and defective verb; I exist (used only when emphatic):--am, have been, X it is I, was. See also G1488, G1498, G1511, G1526, G2258, G2071, G2070, G2075, G2076, G2771, G2468, G5600.

Biblical Usage

To be is the commonest verb in the New Testament, and it carries the greatest claim in the Bible. John opens his Gospel by saying the Word was in the beginning and the Word was God, Jesus says before Abraham was, I am, calls Himself the good shepherd, and says, I am the way, the truth, and the life. The verb is ordinary everywhere else, but in His mouth it is the name God gave Moses at the burning bush.

Theological Significance

I believe Jesus is the I am, and that changes everything, because the God who spoke to Moses now speaks to me in a man I can follow. My position is that there is no other name under heaven by which I can be saved.
