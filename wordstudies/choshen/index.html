chôshen

perhaps a pocket (as holding the Urim and Thummim), or rich (as containing gems), used only of the gorget of the highpriest

Biblical Usage

The breastplate is the pouch the high priest wears over his heart, holding the Urim and Thummim and set with twelve stones for the twelve tribes. God gives its pattern on the mountain, and Aaron puts it on when he goes into the holy place. It is not decoration: the names of Israel are carried on the priest's heart whenever he stands before the LORD.

Theological Significance

I believe the breastplate shows that intercession is personal, not distant. The priest does not carry a list; he carries names, close to his heart, into God's presence. That is how I understand Christ's priesthood too — He carries His people, by name, before the Father.
