eiságō

from G1519 and G71; to introduce (literally or figuratively):--bring in(-to), (+ was to) lead into.

Biblical Usage

To bring in is to carry someone or something inside, and the Gospels use it for the temple and the house. Simeon brings the child Jesus into the temple, the servant brings the poor, the maimed, the halt, and the blind into his master's house, and Stephen says the fathers brought the tabernacle in with Joshua. Hebrews says when God brings His firstborn into the world, all the angels are told to worship Him.

Theological Significance

I believe God brings people in, because His house has room for the poor and the broken, and He brought His own Son into the world to open the door. I want to be one who brings others in, not one who keeps them out.
