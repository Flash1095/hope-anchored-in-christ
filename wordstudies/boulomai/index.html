boúlomai

middle voice of a primary verb; to "will," i.e. (reflexively) be willing:--be disposed, minded, intend, list, (be, of own) will (-ing). Compare G2309.

Biblical Usage

To will is the word for being willing, intending, disposed — and it appears thirty-seven times. In James 1:18 of his own will begat he us with the word of truth, that we should be a kind of firstfruits of his creatures. In 2 Peter 3:9 the Lord is not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance. In Hebrews 6:17 God, willing more abundantly to show the heirs of promise the immutability of his counsel, confirmed it by an oath. And in Acts 27:43 the centurion, willing to save Paul, commanded them which could swim to cast themselves first into the sea.

Theological Significance

I believe the willing in James and Peter is the heart of the gospel: God willed to beget us by his word, and he is not willing that any should perish.
