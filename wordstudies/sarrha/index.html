Sárrha

of Hebrew origin (H8283); Sarra (i.e. Sarah), the wife of Abraham:--Sara, Sarah.

Biblical Usage

Sara is Sarah under her Greek spelling, and the New Testament remembers her for faith, not for failure. Abraham considered not the deadness of Sara's womb; the word of promise said, At this time will I come, and Sara shall have a son; and through faith also Sara herself received strength to conceive seed, because she judged him faithful who had promised.

Theological Significance

I believe Sara's story is the proof that God keeps His word past the point where nature says it is over. She was past age, and her womb was dead — and she believed the Promiser, and the son came.
