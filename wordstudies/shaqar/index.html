shâqar

to cheat, i.e. be untrue (usually in words)

Biblical Usage

God will not lie, and neither must His people. The law says ye shall not steal, neither deal falsely, neither lie one to another, and Samuel says the Strength of Israel will not lie nor repent, for He is not a man, that He should repent. Isaiah calls Israel children that will not lie, and the psalm says we have not forgotten thee, neither have we dealt falsely in thy covenant.

Theological Significance

I believe a God who cannot lie is the only ground for a people who must not lie, because our truthfulness rests on His. Samuel's word is the anchor, the Strength of Israel will not lie nor repent, and Isaiah's children that will not lie are the fruit of it. My position is that every lie I tell is a small denial of the God who cannot lie, and every truth I speak is a confession of Him.
