taʻărubâh

suretyship, i.e. (concretely) a pledge

Biblical Usage

When Jehoash of Israel beat Amaziah of Judah, he took all the gold and silver in the temple and the king's house, and hostages, and returned to Samaria. The Chronicler tells the same story the same way. The word is a pledge, a surety, a hostage: men held to guarantee what a defeated king had promised.

Theological Significance

I believe the hostages are the sad arithmetic of war: when kings break faith, it is not only treasure that is taken, but sons, held until the promise is kept.
