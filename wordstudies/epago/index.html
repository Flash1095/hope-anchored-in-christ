epágō

from G1909 and G71; to superinduce, i.e. inflict (an evil), charge (a crime):--bring upon.

Biblical Usage

To bring upon — to inflict. False teachers bring upon themselves swift destruction in 2 Peter 2:1, God brought the flood upon the world of the ungodly in 2 Peter 2:5, and the council accuses the apostles of intending to bring this man's blood upon us in Acts 5:28.

Theological Significance

I believe what we bring upon ourselves is the surest judgment of all — the flood came on a world that would not listen, and destruction comes on teachers who deny the Lord who bought them. I hold that the blood the council feared is the very blood that saves anyone who will own it.
