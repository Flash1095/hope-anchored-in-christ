chûwç

properly, to cover, i.e. (figuratively) to compassionate

Biblical Usage

To pity is to spare what you could destroy, and Joseph tells his brothers not to regard their stuff, because all Egypt is before them. God tells Israel not to pity the idols of the nations, and David's eye spares Saul in the cave. Jonah learns the same lesson last: God pities Nineveh, and the prophet must learn to pity what God pities.

Theological Significance

I believe pity is the test of whether we have understood God's mercy, because we are quick to spare ourselves and slow to spare others. David spared the man who hunted him, and God spared the city Jonah wanted judged. My position is that a heart that cannot pity has not yet learned how much it has been pitied.
