lûwach

probably meaning to glisten; a tablet (as polished), of stone, wood or metal

Biblical Usage

The word names the tablets of stone on which God wrote His covenant, and Moses went up the mountain and stayed there forty days and forty nights to receive them. Scripture calls them the tablets of the covenant, written with the finger of God, and when Moses came down his face shone from speaking with the LORD. The tablets were not man's idea of right and wrong; they were God's own words cut in stone.

Theological Significance

I believe the tablets matter because God wrote them Himself, and no one may edit what He has written. I hold that the same God who carved His law in stone writes it now on the hearts of His people, so His commands are not a burden to escape but a covenant to keep.
