bouleúō

from G1012; to advise, i.e. (reflexively) deliberate, or (by implication) resolve:--consult, take counsel, determine, be minded, purpose.

Biblical Usage

To take counsel is the word for deliberating, resolving — and it appears in the middle voice, as a man counselling with himself. In Luke 14:31 a king going to war sitteth down first and consulteth whether he be able with ten thousand to meet him that cometh against him with twenty thousand. In John 11:53 the chief priests took counsel together to put Jesus to death. And in 2 Corinthians 1:17 Paul asks, when I therefore was thus minded, did I use lightness? The Greek text spells the word βουλεύομαι, the middle form, where the dictionary gives the active form, but it is the same word.

Theological Significance

I believe the three counsels are a warning and a pattern: the king counts the cost, the priests plot a murder, and Paul asks whether his own mind was steady — and only one of the three was right.
