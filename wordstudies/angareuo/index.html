angareúō

of foreign origin (compare H104); properly, to be a courier, i.e. (by implication) to press into public service:--compel (to go).

Biblical Usage

To press into service. A Roman soldier compels Simon of Cyrene to bear the cross, and Jesus says whoever compels you to go a mile, go with him two. The word is the power of a stronger man laying a burden on a weaker one, and Jesus turns it into a command to give more than is demanded.

Theological Significance

I believe Jesus took the ugliest picture of power, a soldier pressing a man into carrying a cross, and made it the picture of His people: go further than you were forced to go. Simon carried the cross once, and I hold that every act of going the second mile still follows His steps.
