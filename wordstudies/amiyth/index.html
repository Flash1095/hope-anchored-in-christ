ʻâmîyth

companionship; hence (concretely) a comrade or kindred man

Biblical Usage

Companionship, a comrade, a kindred man — and the law uses it for the neighbour at your elbow. If a soul sin and lie unto his neighbour in that which was delivered him to keep, or in fellowship, or in a thing taken away by violence, or hath deceived his neighbour. Ye shall not steal, neither deal falsely, neither lie one to another. Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him.

Theological Significance

I believe this word is the whole of the second table in one root: the neighbour is not an abstraction but the comrade, the kindred man, the one whose goods are in your keeping and whose sins are your business to rebuke in love. The law does not ask me to love humanity; it asks me not to lie to the man next door, not to steal from him, not to hate him in my heart. I hold that is where love is proved, not in the grand gesture but in the neighbour, and the neighbour is always nearer than I want him to be.
