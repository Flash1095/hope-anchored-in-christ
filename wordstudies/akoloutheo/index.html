akolouthéō

from G1 (as a particle of union) and κέλευθος (a road); properly, to be in the same way with, i.e. to accompany (specially, as a disciple):--follow, reach.

Biblical Usage

The great discipleship word. The fishermen leave their nets and follow Him; Jesus says, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me; He promises, He that followeth me shall not walk in darkness; and the sheep follow because they know his voice. Following is the whole of the Christian life in one verb: leave, deny, take up, walk.

Theological Significance

I hold that following is not agreeing with Jesus — it is going where He goes. The fishermen left their nets in the middle of the workday, and the cross Jesus names is carried daily, not once. That is the cost, and it is also the promise: the sheep who follow never walk in darkness.
