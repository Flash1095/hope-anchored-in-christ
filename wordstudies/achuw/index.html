ʼâchûw

a bulrush or any marshy grass (particularly that along the Nile)

Biblical Usage

Achu is marsh grass, the reeds along the Nile — the meadow where Pharaoh's fat kine fed in his dream, and the flag Bildad asks about: "Can the rush grow up without mire? can the flag grow without water?" The word appears only three times, always in a picture of life that needs its source.

Theological Significance

Marsh grass is a detail of the Nile bank, and Bildad's question about the rush is what gives the word its meaning — nothing grows without its source.
