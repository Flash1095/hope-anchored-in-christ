aphtharsía

from G862; incorruptibility; genitive, unending existence; (figuratively) genuineness:--immortality, incorruption, sincerity.

Biblical Usage

This is the word for what does not decay — incorruption, unending existence. Paul sets it at the heart of the resurrection in 1 Corinthians 15:53, where this corruptible must put on incorruption, and in 1 Corinthians 15:54, when that putting-on is done, death is swallowed up in victory. Christ has already brought life and immortality to light through the gospel in 2 Timothy 1:10, and Romans 2:7 promises eternal life to those who by patient continuance seek glory, honour, and immortality.

Theological Significance

I believe incorruption is not just living forever; it is living forever without the rot — no decay in the body, no fading in the hope. That is what Christ's appearing has already secured, and what the resurrection will finally put on us. Everything we do now in patient well-doing is aimed at a life that cannot spoil.
