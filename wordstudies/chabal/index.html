châbal

to wind tightly (as a rope), i.e. to bind; specifically, by a pledge; figuratively, to pervert, destroy; also to writhe in pain (especially of parturition)

Biblical Usage

Chabal in Hebrew covers binding, pledging, and ruining — one root for ropes, debts, and wreckage. The law would not let a man take a neighbour's garment or millstone as a pledge for a debt, Nehemiah confessed that Israel had dealt very corruptly, and Job said his breath was corrupt and his days were nearly spent.

Theological Significance

I believe this word warns me twice: do not bind your neighbour in a debt he cannot pay, and do not let your own heart grow corrupt. God hates the pledge that crushes the poor, and He heals the ruin that sin makes.
