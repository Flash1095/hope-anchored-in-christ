zebach

properly, a slaughter, i.e. the flesh of an animal; by implication, a sacrifice (the victim or the act)

Biblical Usage

Zebach is the sacrifice, the slaughtered animal offered on the altar, and it is one of the most common words in the Old Testament. Leviticus sets out the peace offering as a sacrifice made by fire, and Samuel tells Saul that to obey is better than sacrifice, because God wants the heart before the gift. Hosea says the same thing, that God desires mercy and not sacrifice, and the Psalmist agrees, saying the sacrifices of God are a broken spirit.

Theological Significance

I believe sacrifice without obedience is empty, and the prophets never let Israel forget it. God gave the sacrifices Himself, yet He refused the ones offered by a proud heart. My position is that every sacrifice in the Old Testament was a shadow, and the substance is Christ, who offered Himself once and needs no other offering.
