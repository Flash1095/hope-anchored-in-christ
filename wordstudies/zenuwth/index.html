zᵉnûwth

adultery, i.e. (figuratively) infidelity, idolatry

Biblical Usage

This is the word for whoredom, and the prophets use it for far more than the body. Hosea charges Israel with whoredom when she runs after other gods, Jeremiah says the land was polluted by it, and Ezekiel traces the same sickness through Jerusalem's story. The word always means unfaithfulness: a people who belonged to God giving themselves to someone else.

Theological Significance

I believe the prophets chose this word on purpose, because nothing else stings like it. Calling idolatry whoredom says what it is — not a mistake of the mind but a betrayal of the heart. My position is that the charge still stands: every loyalty I give to something other than God is the same unfaithfulness, and His call back is still mercy.
