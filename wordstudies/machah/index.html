mâchâh

properly, to stroke or rub; by implication, to erase; also to smooth (as if with oil), i.e. grease or make fat; also to touch, i.e. reach to

Biblical Usage

The word means to blot out, to erase, and it is used for names, sins and whole nations. God tells Moses to let Him alone that He may blot out the name of the idolaters from under heaven, and Moses begs God to blot him out of His book rather than destroy Israel. The law says to blot out the remembrance of Amalek, and David prays that God would blot out his transgressions according to His tender mercies.

Theological Significance

I believe blotting out is God's terrible mercy: He erases the names of the unrepentant and the sins of the penitent, and the difference is everything. I hold that Moses offering to be blotted out for Israel is the picture of Christ, who was blotted out in our place so our names would stay in the book.
