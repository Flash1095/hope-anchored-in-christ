chârash

to scratch, i.e. (by implication) to engrave, plough; hence (from the use of tools) to fabricate (of any material); figuratively, to devise (in a bad sense); hence (from the idea of secrecy) to be silent, to let alone; hence (by implication) to be deaf (as an accompaniment of dumbness)

Biblical Usage

Charash covers ploughing, engraving, and keeping silent, and the same root does all three. Elijah found Elisha plowing with twelve yoke of oxen, Samuel warns that a king will set men to ear his ground and make his instruments of war, Absalom tells Tamar to hold her peace, and Israel begs Samuel not to cease crying to the LORD, not to keep silent for them.

Theological Significance

I believe the three uses are one picture: the plough cuts a furrow, the engraver cuts a line, and silence cuts off speech. Elisha was ploughing when his life changed, and Tamar was told to be silent when hers broke. I hold that God speaks into all three, whether He calls a ploughman or hears a cry that will not be silent.
