entháde

from a prolonged form of G1722; properly, within, i.e. (of place) here, hither:--(t-)here, hither.

Biblical Usage

Enthade is 'here' — this place, hither. The Samaritan woman asks for water so she need not come hither to draw in John 4:15, the risen Jesus asks the disciples if they have here any meat in Luke 24:41, and Paul cries out in the Philippian prison, we are all here, in Acts 16:28. Peter is asked whether Simon is lodged there — here — in Acts 10:18.

Theological Significance

I believe 'here' is where Jesus meets us — at the well, at the table, in the prison at midnight. I hold that He is not far off in some other place; He is here, in this place, with food and forgiveness and freedom.
