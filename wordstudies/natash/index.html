nâṭash

properly, to pound, i.e. smite; by implication (as if beating out, and thus expanding) to disperse; also, to thrust off, down, out or upon (inclusively, reject, let alone, permit, remit, etc.)

Biblical Usage

Jeshurun waxes fat and forsakes the God who made him, and Solomon prays that the LORD would never leave or forsake His people. Samuel promises that the LORD will not forsake His people for His great name's sake, yet the LORD warns that He will forsake the remnant of His inheritance if they turn away.

Theological Significance

I believe forsaking runs one way with God and the other way with man: I forsake Him and He is faithful still, until the day my forsaking becomes final. His promise not to forsake is what holds me when I have no strength to hold Him.
