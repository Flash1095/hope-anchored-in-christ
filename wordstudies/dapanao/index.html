dapanáō

from G1160; to expend, i.e. (in a good sense) to incur cost, or (in a bad one) to waste:--be at charges, consume, spend.

Biblical Usage

To spend is the story of the prodigal, the widow, and the apostle. The prodigal wasted his substance with riotous living until he had spent all; the woman with the issue of blood spent all she had on physicians and was nothing bettered; and Paul was willing to spend and be spent for the souls of the Corinthians. The same word covers waste and sacrifice, and the difference is what it is spent on.

Theological Significance

I believe the word asks us what our lives are being spent on. Paul's answer is my own: to be spent for others is not waste, it is the offering.
