akroatḗs

from ἀκροάομαι (to listen; apparently an intensive of G191); a hearer (merely):--hearer.

Biblical Usage

The hearer who only hears. Paul says not the hearers of the law are just before God, but the doers of the law shall be justified; James says be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves, and compares the hearer who forgets to a man who looks in a mirror and walks away. The word always carries the warning: hearing without doing is self-deception.

Theological Significance

I believe this is the most practical word in the New Testament. The mirror does not lie — the man who hears and forgets has deceived himself — and the blessed man is the one James names last: the doer, who looks into the perfect law of liberty and continues in it.
