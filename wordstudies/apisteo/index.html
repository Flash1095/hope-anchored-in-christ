apistéō

from G571; to be unbelieving, i.e. (transitively) disbelieve, or (by implication) disobey:--believe not.

Biblical Usage

To disbelieve, to refuse the word. The disciples heard that Jesus was alive and had been seen, and believed not in Mark 16:11, and the women's words seemed to them as idle tales and they believed them not in Luke 24:11. Paul asks the harder question in Romans 3:3 — if some did not believe, shall their unbelief make the faith of God without effect? — and answers it in 2 Timothy 2:13: if we believe not, yet He abideth faithful; He cannot deny Himself.

Theological Significance

I believe this word draws the line between honest doubt and settled refusal. The disciples' unbelief at the tomb was cured by seeing Him; the unbelief Paul warns about is the kind that calls God's faithfulness into question. But the comfort holds either way: my unbelief cannot undo His faithfulness, because He cannot deny Himself.
