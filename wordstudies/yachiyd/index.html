yâchîyd

properly, united, i.e. sole; by implication, beloved; also lonely; (feminine) the life (as not to be replaced)

Biblical Usage

Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest, says God to Abraham, and Jephthah's daughter came out to meet him, his only child. The psalmist cries, deliver my darling from the power of the dog, and Solomon says, I was my father's son, tender and only beloved in the sight of my mother.

Theological Significance

I hold that only is the word that makes the heart catch: the only son on Moriah, the only child at Mizpeh, the darling in the psalm, the only beloved before his mother — and in every case the one who is only is also the one who is loved most. I believe the word was kept for Isaac so that, centuries later, it could be kept for another only Son, and the Bible never uses it lightly.
