çôlᵉlâh

a military mound, i.e. rampart of besiegers

Biblical Usage

A siege mound is the ramp an army builds against a walled city, and Joab's men build one against Abel while a wise woman saves the city. Sennacherib is told he will not build a mound against Jerusalem, and Jeremiah says to build one against the city that will not hear. The word is the slow work of war, and God decides whether it stands or falls.

Theological Significance

I believe the siege mound shows that human armies plan slowly and God answers quickly. Joab's mound is stopped by a wise word, and Sennacherib's is stopped by the angel of the LORD. I hold that no ramp built against God's people can stand when He says it will not.
