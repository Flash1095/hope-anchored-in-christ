sâmach

probably to brighten up, i.e. (figuratively) be (causatively, make) blithe or gleesome

Biblical Usage

The word sâmach means to brighten up, that is (figuratively) be (causatively, make) blithe or gleesome. In 1 Chronicles 16:10, we read, "…holy name: let the heart of them rejoice…"; in 1 Kings 5:7, "…Blessed the LORD this day, which hath given…". You hear it again in 1 Samuel 2:1 — "…My heart rejoiceth in the LORD, mine horn…" — and in 2 Chronicles 6:41, "Now therefore arise, O LORD God, into thy…".

Theological Significance

I believe sâmach is the Lord's own word, and it shows his people what he is like. I hold that sâmach still speaks, because the God who spoke then has not changed.
