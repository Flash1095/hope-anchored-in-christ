drákōn

probably from an alternate form of δέρκομαι (to look); a fabulous kind of serpent (perhaps as supposed to fascinate):--dragon.

Biblical Usage

The dragon, the great red dragon of Revelation, the old serpent called the Devil and Satan, who deceives the whole world. He stood before the woman to devour her child, gave his power to the beast from the sea, and at the end is bound for a thousand years and cast into the lake of fire.

Theological Significance

I believe the dragon is real, ancient, and already defeated, because the child he tried to devour is enthroned and the angel who binds him needs only a chain. The war is won at the cross, and the binding at the end is the sentence being carried out.
