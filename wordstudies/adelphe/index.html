adelphḗ

feminine of G80; a sister (naturally or ecclesiastically):--sister.

Biblical Usage

Adelphe is simply 'sister,' used both of blood sisters — Mary and Martha, sisters of Lazarus — and of sisters in the faith, like Phoebe, whom Paul commends to the church at Rome. Jesus stretches the word further still: whoever does the will of His Father is His sister. In the New Testament the word is never small; it names a real bond, whether of birth or of belief.

Theological Significance

I believe this word matters because it shows how the gospel remakes family. In Christ, 'sister' is not just a courtesy title — it is a true relationship, with the duties of love and care that belong to family.
