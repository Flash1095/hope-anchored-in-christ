aichmalōteúō

from G164; to capture (like G163):--lead captive.

Biblical Usage

This verb occurs once, in Ephesians 4:8, where Paul says Christ led captivity captive when He ascended. The picture is a victorious king marching home with his prisoners behind him — except here the prisoners are the powers that once held humanity captive, and the triumph ends in gifts, not chains, for His people.

Theological Significance

One verb in one verse carries no theology of its own; the weight is in the scene, Christ the ascended King leading captives and handing out gifts.
