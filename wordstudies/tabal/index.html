ṭâbal

to dip, to immerse

Biblical Usage

The priest dips the living bird and the scarlet and the hyssop in the blood of the bird killed over running water. Naaman dips himself seven times in Jordan, and his flesh comes again like a child's. Joseph's brothers dip his coat in the blood of a kid, and Boaz tells Ruth to dip her morsel in the vinegar. The word is dipping, plunging: into blood, into water, into a bowl, and what comes up is changed.

Theological Significance

I hold that dipping is the Bible's picture of cleansing by contact. I believe Naaman shows it best: seven dips, and the leper comes up clean, because the water was Jordan's and the word was God's.
