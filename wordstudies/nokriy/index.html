nokrîy

strange, in a variety of degrees and applications (foreign, non-relative, adulterous, different, wonderful)

Biblical Usage

A stranger is the outsider, and the Bible knows him in four ways. Moses names his son Gershom because he has been a stranger in a strange land, the alien in the gates may eat what dies of itself, the Levite will not lodge in a stranger's city, and Ruth the stranger falls on her face and asks why she has found grace.

Theological Significance

I believe Ruth turns the whole word around: the stranger who expected nothing is the one who finds grace in the field. Moses was a stranger in a strange land, and Ruth was a stranger in the field — both of them found that God keeps strangers.
