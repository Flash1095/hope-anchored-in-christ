kakós

apparently a primary word; worthless (intrinsically, such; whereas G4190 properly refers to effects), i.e. (subjectively) depraved, or (objectively) injurious:--bad, evil, harm, ill, noisome, wicked.

Biblical Usage

Kakos is evil in its plainest form — worthless, bad, harmful. Paul says Israel's story was written so we should not lust after evil things; love thinketh no evil; evil communications corrupt good manners; Peter says not to render evil for evil; and the love of money is the root of all evil.

Theological Significance

I believe evil is not a mystery to be solved but a master to be refused. It corrupts, it returns evil for evil, and it roots itself in love of money — and the only answer is the love that thinketh no evil and the life that renders good for evil.
