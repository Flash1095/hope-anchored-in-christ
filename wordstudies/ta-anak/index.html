Taʻănâk

Taanak or Tanak, a place in Palestine

Biblical Usage

Taanach was an old Canaanite city, taken by Joshua and given to Manasseh, though it lay in Issachar's land, and later to the Levites. The kings of Canaan fought by the waters of Megiddo at Taanach, and took no gain of money. Under Solomon it sat in Baana's district, provisioning the king's table, and the name means sandy — a city on the road where battles were fought.

Theological Significance

I believe Taanach shows how much of the Bible's story runs through ordinary towns: a city given, a battle fought, a table supplied, and God working through all of it.
