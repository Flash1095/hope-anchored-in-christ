lâchak

to lick

Biblical Usage

To lick is the word for the tongue's work, and it is used for fire, for armies, and for humbled enemies. In 1 Kings 18:38 the fire of the Lord "licked up the water that was in the trench," in Numbers 22:4 Moab fears this company will "lick up all that are round about us, as the ox licketh up the grass," and in Isaiah 49:23 kings "shall lick up the dust of thy feet."

Theological Significance

I am convinced the word's picture is total: fire licks the trench dry, an army licks a land bare, and one day kings will lick the dust before the people they once oppressed. What the fire did to the trench, God will do to the proud.
