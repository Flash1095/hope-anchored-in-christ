phthartós

from G5351; decayed, i.e. (by implication) perishable:--corruptible.

Biblical Usage

Phthartos means corruptible, perishable, decayed, and it is the word for everything that falls apart. Paul says this corruptible must put on incorruption, and that we are not redeemed with corruptible things like silver and gold, but with the precious blood of Christ. Peter says we are born again, not of corruptible seed but of incorruptible, by the word of God, and Paul says men changed the glory of the uncorruptible God into an image like corruptible man.

Theological Significance

I believe corruptible is the Bible's diagnosis of everything I can see: bodies decay, silver and gold cannot redeem, and the image of God is traded for an image of man. The gospel's answer is incorruption: a seed that does not die, a blood that does not fail, and a body that will put on what cannot decay. My position is that I live between the two: corruptible now, incorruptible then, and the down payment is already in me.
