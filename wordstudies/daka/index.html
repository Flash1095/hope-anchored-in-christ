dâkâʼ

to crumble; transitively, to bruise (literally or figuratively)

Biblical Usage

To crush is what the world does to the poor and what God does to sin, and the word holds both. The prophets charge the rulers who beat God's people to pieces and grind the faces of the poor, and Job pictures frail men crushed before the moth. But the same word falls on the Servant: He was bruised for our iniquities, and it pleased the Lord to bruise Him. The high and lofty One dwells with the one who is of a contrite and humble spirit, to revive the heart of the contrite.

Theological Significance

I believe the cross is where the two meanings meet. The crushing that should have fallen on us fell on Christ, and the God who bruised His own Son now revives the bruised and broken who come to Him.
