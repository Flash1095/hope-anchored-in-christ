bâʻar

to kindle, i.e. consume (by fire or by eating); to be (-come) brutish

Biblical Usage

Ba'ar means to burn — to kindle, to consume, to set ablaze, and to sweep away like stubble. The bush burned and was not consumed when Moses turned aside to see it, Deuteronomy says the Lord's anger shall burn against the land, Isaiah says the fire shall burn and none shall quench it, and Jeremiah says the word of the Lord shall go forth like a fire in the house of Israel. The same fire warms, judges, and speaks.

Theological Significance

I believe fire is the picture of God himself in this word: the bush that burns without consuming is the God who draws near without destroying. The fire that none can quench is the same God in judgment.
