gâdîysh

a stack of sheaves; by analogy, a tomb

Biblical Usage

Gadish is a shock or stack of sheaves, and by the same picture, a tomb. Samson burned the shocks of the Philistines in Judges 15:5, and the law makes the man who kindles a fire pay for the stacks of corn in Exodus 22:6. But Job uses the same word for the grave: he shall be brought to the grave and remain in the tomb in Job 21:32, and a man shall come to his grave in full age, like a shock of corn in his season, in Job 5:26.

Theological Significance

I believe the shock of corn is a gentle picture of death. A sheaf is gathered in its season, not torn up green, and Job's hope is to come to his grave the same way. I hold that God gathers His people in their season, as a farmer gathers his harvest.
