qâlâh

to be light (as implied in rapid motion), but figuratively, only (be (causatively, hold) in contempt)

Biblical Usage

To hold light is to despise: cursed is he that setteth light by his father or his mother, David tells Saul's servants that he is a poor man, lightly esteemed, and asks whether it seems a light thing to be a king's son in law, Proverbs says he that is despised and hath a servant is better than he that honoureth himself and lacketh bread. The law caps a beating at forty stripes lest thy brother should seem vile unto thee.

Theological Significance

I hold that lightly esteeming is the quiet sin behind the loud ones, because contempt for parents and contempt for a brother grow from the same root. My position is that honour is not flattery, it is weight, and the moment we treat a person as light we have already turned from God.
