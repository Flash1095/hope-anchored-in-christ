râshaʻ

to be (causatively, do or declare) wrong; by implication, to disturb, violate

Biblical Usage

Rasha' is to be wicked, to do or declare wrong. Solomon prayed that if Israel sinned and returned, saying, 'We have sinned, and have done perversely, we have committed wickedness'; Daniel confessed, 'We have sinned, and have committed iniquity, and have done wickedly, and have rebelled'; and Nehemiah says, 'Howbeit thou art just in all that is brought upon us; for thou hast done right, but we have done wickedly.'

Theological Significance

I believe the confession is the same in every age: we have done wickedly, and Thou art just. And I hold that the prayer only works because the confession is honest — Solomon, Daniel, and Nehemiah all say the same words, and God hears them all.
