ʻâtsab

properly, to carve, i.e. fabricate or fashion; hence (in a bad sense) to worry, pain or anger

Biblical Usage

To grieve or to pain is the sense here, and the word runs from prayer to sorrow. Jabez prayed that God would keep him from evil so that it might not grieve him, Jonathan rose from the table in fierce anger and ate no food because he was grieved for David, and the day of victory turned to mourning when the people heard the king was grieved for his son.

Theological Significance

I believe grief is honest in the Bible, and it is never wasted when it is carried to God. Jabez asked to be kept from it, Jonathan felt it for his friend, and David's grief over Absalom shows that even a king's heart can break, and I hold that God meets the grieving where they are.
