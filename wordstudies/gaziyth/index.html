gâzîyth

something cut, i.e. dressed stone

Biblical Usage

Gazith is hewn stone, stone cut and dressed for building. David set masons to hew wrought stones to build the house of God in 1 Chronicles 22:2, and the inner court was built with three rows of hewed stone in 1 Kings 6:36. The houses of hewn stone that the rich built on the poor shall not be dwelt in, says Amos 5:11, and God forbids an altar of hewn stone in Exodus 20:25, because lifting a tool upon it pollutes it.

Theological Significance

I believe the hewn stone shows the difference between God's house and man's. David's masons cut stone for the temple, and the rich cut stone for houses they would never live in. I hold that the altar God wants is uncut stone, because His work needs no tool of ours.
