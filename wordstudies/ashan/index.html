ʻâshan

to smoke, whether literal or figurative

Biblical Usage

To smoke is what God's anger and God's mountain do, and the Bible uses it for both. The whole mount quaked greatly and smoked as a furnace when the LORD descended, Moses warns that the anger of the LORD and His jealousy shall smoke against the man who blesses himself in his heart, and the psalm says He looketh on the earth, and it trembleth, He toucheth the hills, and they smoke.

Theological Significance

I believe the smoking of the mountain and the smoking of anger are the same picture: God is a consuming fire, and what He touches trembles. The man who blesses himself while he walks in his own heart is warned plainly, and I hold that the warning is mercy, that he might turn before the fire falls.
