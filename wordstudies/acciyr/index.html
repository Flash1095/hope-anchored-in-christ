ʼaççîyr

{bound, i.e. a captive}

Biblical Usage

This is the word for a prisoner — one bound, one shut up. Joseph is put 'into the prison, a place where the king's prisoners were bound'; Isaiah promises the Servant will 'bring out the prisoners from the prison'; and Lamentations says God does not delight in crushing 'all the prisoners of the earth' under His feet.

Theological Significance

Joseph the prisoner becomes the keeper of the prisoners, and Isaiah promises a Servant who will open the prison doors. I believe the word runs straight to Christ: the prisoners of the earth are not forgotten by God, and the One who was bound sets the bound free.
