chaírō

a primary verb; to be "cheer"ful, i.e. calmly happy or well-off; impersonally, especially as salutation (on meeting or parting), be well:--farewell, be glad, God speed, greeting, hall, joy(- fully), rejoice.

Biblical Usage

Chairo means to rejoice, to be glad, and the Gospels use it for gladness of every kind. The wise men rejoiced with exceeding great joy when they saw the star, many would rejoice at John's birth, the friend of the bridegroom rejoices greatly at the bridegroom's voice — and the chief priests were glad when Judas offered to betray Jesus.

Theological Significance

I believe joy is proved by its object: the wise men rejoiced at a star that led to a child, the bridegroom's friend rejoiced at a voice, and the priests rejoiced at a betrayal. Gladness is not good by itself — it depends on what you are glad about.
