maççêkâh

properly, a pouring over, i.e. fusion of metal (especially a cast image); by implication, a libation, i.e. league; concretely a coverlet (as if poured out)

Biblical Usage

The word names a molten image, a god poured in metal, and it is the sin of Israel from Sinai to Samaria. While Moses was on the mountain, the people made a molten calf and said these are your gods, and God told Moses they had quickly turned aside and made a molten image. Jeroboam made two calves and Israel worshipped the host of heaven, and the law curses the man who makes any graven or molten image and puts it in a secret place.

Theological Significance

I believe the molten image is the oldest idolatry and the newest, because we still pour our gods out of what we own. I hold that the calf was made while Moses was still on the mountain, and that is how fast a people forgets: the gold in their ears became the god in their hands.
