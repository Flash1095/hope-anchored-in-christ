aítion

neuter of G159; a reason or crime (like G156):--cause, fault.

Biblical Usage

Luke uses it three times in the trial of Jesus — Pilate's repeated verdict, I find no fault in this man, and I have found no cause of death in him. The fourth use is the town clerk at Ephesus warning the riotous crowd that we are in danger to be called in question for this day's uproar, there being no cause whereby we may give an account. In every case the word is a reason that will stand up in court — and in Jesus' trial there was none.

Theological Significance

Pilate said it three times, and the crowd shouted it down three times. I believe Luke records the repetition on purpose: the faultless Man was condemned by men who knew He was faultless, which is exactly why His death can count for the faulty.
