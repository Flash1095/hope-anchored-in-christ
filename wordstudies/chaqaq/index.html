châqaq

properly, to hack, i.e. engrave (Judges 5:14, to be a scribe simply); by implication, to enact (laws being cut in stone or metal tablets in primitive times) or (gen.) prescribe

Biblical Usage

Chaqaq is engraving, cutting a law or a picture into something that lasts. The sceptre shall not depart from Judah, nor a lawgiver from between his feet, until Shiloh come. Isaiah is told to write the vision in a table and note it in a book, that it may be for the time to come for ever. Ezekiel is told to pourtray the city of Jerusalem upon a tile, and Moses says Gad provided the first part for himself, because there, in a portion of the lawgiver, was he seated.

Theological Significance

I believe engraving is the Bible's answer to forgetting: what is cut into stone or written in a book outlasts the men who made it. The lawgiver's portion and the prophet's tablet both say the same thing. I hold that God's word is not spoken into the air but engraved, so that the time to come can still read it.
