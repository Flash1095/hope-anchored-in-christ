syneudokéō

from G4862 and G2106; to think well of in common, i.e. assent to, feel gratified with:--allow, assent, be pleased, have pleasure.

Biblical Usage

Paul says those who know God's judgment not only do the things worthy of death but have pleasure in those who do them, and Saul was consenting to Stephen's death, standing by and keeping the raiment of those who slew him. The Jews tell Pilate they allow the deeds of their fathers, who killed the prophets while they build their tombs. Consent can be as guilty as the deed, and taking pleasure in another's sin is consent with a smile.

Theological Significance

What grips me is that consenting to evil is itself evil, and I hold that Saul's story is the warning and the hope together. I take the hope seriously: the man who consented to Stephen's death became the apostle who preached his forgiveness, so no consenter is beyond grace.
