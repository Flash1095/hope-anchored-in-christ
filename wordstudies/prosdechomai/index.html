prosdéchomai

from G4314 and G1209; to admit (to intercourse, hospitality, credence, or (figuratively) endurance); by implication, to await (with confidence or patience):--accept, allow, look (wait) for, take.

Biblical Usage

Prosdechomai means to receive gladly, to welcome, to wait for with expectation. Simeon waited for the consolation of Israel, Anna spoke of the child to all who looked for redemption in Jerusalem, and Joseph of Arimathaea waited for the kingdom of God. Paul tells Titus to live looking for that blessed hope, the glorious appearing of our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ.

Theological Significance

I believe waiting is not idle: Simeon and Anna spent their lives watching for what God promised, and they were the ones holding the child when He came. I hold that the same hope still shapes how I live today, because the One we wait for is coming again.
