krínō

properly, to distinguish, i.e. decide (mentally or judicially); by implication, to try, condemn, punish:--avenge, conclude, condemn, damn, decree, determine, esteem, judge, go to (sue at the) law, ordain, call in question, sentence to, think.

Biblical Usage

Krino is the great word for judging, and it refuses to be simple. Jesus says "Judge not, that ye be not judged" in Matthew 7:1, then says judge righteous judgment; Paul says in 1 Corinthians 4:5 to judge nothing before the time, until the Lord comes; Peter says God judges every man according to his work in 1 Peter 1:17; and Paul says in 2 Timothy 4:1 that Christ shall judge the quick and the dead.

Theological Significance

What I take from it is that the word holds two truths together that I must not pull apart: I am not the judge of my brother, and there is a Judge before whom we will all stand, and the second truth is what keeps the first one honest. The Judge is coming, and that is what makes my mercy toward others a duty, not a favour.
