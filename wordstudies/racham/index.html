râcham

to fondle; by implication, to love, especially to compassionate

Biblical Usage

Racham is to love with tender compassion, to fondle and cherish — the love a mother has for the child of her womb. God told Moses, 'I will be gracious to whom I will be gracious, and will shew mercy on whom I will shew mercy'; He promised to have compassion on Israel and gather her from all the nations; and the psalm says, 'Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him.'

Theological Significance

I am convinced that God's mercy is not a mood but a promise: He has compassion because He chooses to, and He chooses to because He is the LORD. And I hold that His pity is a father's pity — He knows our frame, He remembers that we are dust, and He does not despise the dust He made.
