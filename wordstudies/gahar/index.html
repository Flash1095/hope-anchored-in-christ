gâhar

to prostrate oneself

Biblical Usage

Gahar means to bow down low, to stretch oneself out flat. Elijah cast himself down upon the earth on Carmel and put his face between his knees in 1 Kings 18:42, waiting for rain. Elisha went up and stretched himself upon the dead child in 2 Kings 4:34, mouth to mouth and hands to hands, until the flesh waxed warm, and in 2 Kings 4:35 he stretched himself upon him again, and the child sneezed seven times and opened his eyes.

Theological Significance

I believe bowing low is the posture of power in God's kingdom. Elijah bowed on Carmel and the rain came; Elisha stretched himself over a dead boy and life returned. I hold that God works through those willing to get low.
