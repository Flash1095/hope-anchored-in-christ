râchats

to lave (the whole or a part of a thing)

Biblical Usage

Rachats is to wash — to lave the whole or a part, hands or feet or body. Aaron and his sons were washed with water at the door of the tabernacle before they were clothed; the priests washed their hands and feet at the laver so they would not die; and on the day of atonement the high priest washed his flesh in the holy place. Isaiah turns the rite into a command: 'Wash you, make you clean; put away the evil of your doings from before mine eyes.'

Theological Significance

What this says to me is that washing is the Bible's picture of cleansing from the outside in: the priests washed because they were coming near to God, and Isaiah says the washing that matters is the putting away of evil. And I believe the water of the laver points forward to the washing that only God can do — the clean heart He gives, not the hands we scrub.
