makʼôb

anguish or (figuratively) affliction

Biblical Usage

Makob is sorrow, grief, pain, the ache that will not leave. Exodus 3:7 has God saying He knows the sorrows of His people in Egypt, and Isaiah 53:3 calls the servant a man of sorrows and acquainted with grief, while Isaiah 53:4 says surely He hath borne our griefs and carried our sorrows. Psalms 32:10 says many sorrows shall be to the wicked, but he that trusteth in the LORD, mercy shall compass him about.

Theological Significance

I believe Isaiah 53:4 is the heart of this word: the sorrows I carry were carried first by Him, and that is why they do not have the last word. My position is that Exodus 3:7 and Psalms 32:10 together say everything, because God knows my sorrows and His mercy still compasses me in them.
