chârêb

parched or ruined

Biblical Usage

Chareb is parched, dry, ruined, and it describes both land and bread. The land that was desolate shall become like the garden of Eden, and the waste cities shall be fenced and inhabited. Haggai asks whether it is time to dwell in cieled houses while God's house lies waste, Jeremiah hears that the desolate streets shall hear joy again, and the law gives the dry meat offering to all the sons of Aaron alike.

Theological Significance

I believe the word holds ruin and restoration in the same breath: the same land called desolate is promised to become Eden. Haggai's rebuke and Ezekiel's promise belong together. I hold that God wastes what His people neglect and restores what they return to.
