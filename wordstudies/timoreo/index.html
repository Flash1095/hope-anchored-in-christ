timōréō

from a compound of G5092 and οὖρος ouros (a guard); properly, to protect one's honor, i.e. to avenge (inflict a penalty):--punish.

Biblical Usage

Before his conversion Paul used this word of his own cruelty. He tells the council he went to Damascus to bring Christians bound to Jerusalem to be punished, and before Agrippa he admits he punished them often in every synagogue and compelled them to blaspheme.

Theological Significance

I believe Paul's own mouth makes it sharpest: the punisher became the punished, and the man who once inflicted penalties now counts them as part of following Christ.
