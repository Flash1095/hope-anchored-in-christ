koimáō

from G2749; to put to sleep, i.e. (passively or reflexively) to slumber; figuratively, to decease:--(be a-, fall a-, fall on) sleep, be dead.

Biblical Usage

Koimao means to fall asleep, and in the New Testament it is the gentle word for dying. John 11:11 has Jesus saying, our friend Lazarus sleepeth, but I go, that I may awake him out of sleep, 1 Thessalonians 4:13 comforts the brethren concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope, and 1 Corinthians 15:20 calls Christ the firstfruits of them that slept.

Theological Significance

I believe sleep is the Christian's word for death because the sleeper wakes: Lazarus woke, the brethren will wake, and Christ is the firstfruits of all who slept.
