artýō

from a presumed derivative of G142; to prepare, i.e. spice (with stimulating condiments):--season.

Biblical Usage

To season is to make food fit to eat, and Jesus and Paul both use salt for the seasoning. Salt is good, says Jesus, but if the salt have lost his saltness, wherewith will ye season it; and Paul tells the Colossians to let their speech be always with grace, seasoned with salt.

Theological Significance

I believe seasoning is the right picture for Christian speech: not bland and not bitter, but salted with grace, so that the words are worth hearing and the hearer is the better for them. And I hold that salt which has lost its saltness is fit for nothing, which is a warning to every disciple who has gone flat.
