ʻâthar

to burn incense in worship, i.e. intercede (reciprocally, listen to prayer)

Biblical Usage

To entreat is to pray for another, and this word is the hinge of the plague story. Pharaoh begged Moses to entreat the LORD for him when the frogs covered Egypt, and Moses agreed and set the time, and Pharaoh promised to let the people go. Isaac entreated the LORD for Rebekah because she was barren, and the LORD was entreated of him.

Theological Significance

I believe intercession is one of the highest works a person can do, because it stands between a need and the God who can meet it. Pharaoh asked for it and then broke his word, Isaac asked for it and received a son, and I hold that God still listens when His people pray for others.
