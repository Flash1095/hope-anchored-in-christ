anakrínō

from G303 and G2919; properly, to scrutinize, i.e. (by implication) investigate, interrogate, determine:--ask, question, discern, examine, judge, search.

Biblical Usage

The Bereans were more noble than those in Thessalonica, Luke says, because they received the word with all readiness of mind and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so. Paul says the spiritual man judges all things, yet he himself is judged of no man, and that an unbeliever who comes into the assembly is convinced of all and judged of all. Pilate uses the same word when he tells the chief priests he has examined Jesus before them and found no fault in Him.

Theological Significance

I believe this word is the heart of how Christians are meant to think: examine everything, including the preaching you hear on a Sunday. The Bereans searched the Scriptures daily, and that is still the test — not whether a teacher sounds convincing, but whether what he says is so. And I hold that the same examination falls on me: the spiritual man judges all things, and I should expect my own life to stand up to the same searching.
