apéchō

from G575 and G2192; (actively) to have out, i.e. receive in full; (intransitively) to keep (oneself) away, i.e. be distant (literally or figuratively):--be, have, receive.

Biblical Usage

The word runs in two directions. On one side it means to have received in full — the hypocrites have their reward already, and Paul says he has received everything the Philippians sent. On the other side it means to be far off, like the prodigal still a great way from home when his father saw him. Full payment and far distance share one word, and both turn on whether something is already in hand.

Theological Significance

I believe the warning and the comfort sit side by side here. A reward taken now is a reward already spent, while a father who sees his son a long way off is already running to meet him, and I hold that God's kind of fullness is the second one.
