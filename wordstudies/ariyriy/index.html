ʻărîyrîy

bare, i.e. destitute (of children)

Biblical Usage

To be childless in Scripture is to be cut off from the future, and the word carries that grief plainly. Abram says to God, I go childless, and the heir of my house is a servant; the law makes childlessness the penalty for taking a brother's wife; and Coniah is written childless, a man who will have no son to sit on David's throne.

Theological Significance

I believe childlessness here is more than sadness, it is a sentence: no heir, no throne, no name carried on. And that is why I hold the promise to Abram matters so much, God answers a childless man with a son, and through that son, a nation.
