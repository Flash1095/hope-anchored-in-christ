akairéomai

from a compound of G1 (as a negative particle) and G2540 (meaning unseasonable); to be inopportune (for oneself), i.e. to fail of a proper occasion:--lack opportunity.

Biblical Usage

Once, in a sentence full of grace: Paul tells the Philippians he rejoiced that their care for him hath flourished again; wherein ye were also careful, but ye lacked opportunity. The word is to have no season, no opening.

Theological Significance

One verse about a missed season carries no doctrine of its own; its weight is Paul's grace toward the Philippians, whose love had not cooled but only lacked a door.
