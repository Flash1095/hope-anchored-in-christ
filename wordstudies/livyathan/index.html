livyâthân

a wreathed animal, i.e. a serpent (especially the crocodile or some other large sea-monster); figuratively, the constellation of the dragon; also as a symbol of Babylon

Biblical Usage

livyâthân means Leviathan, the sea serpent — plain enough to miss, and too important to pass over. In Isaiah 27:1, "even leviathan that crooked serpent"; and in Job 41:1, "Canst thou draw out leviathan with an hook?". In Psalms 74:14, "Thou brakest the heads of leviathan in pieces"; and in Psalms 104:26, "There go the ships: [there is] that leviathan".

Theological Significance

I believe Leviathan is God's answer to every proud power — the piercing serpent punished by His sword, the sea monster broken and given for meat, the great beast made to play in the sea. I hold that no creature is too large for God to handle, and no chaos too deep for Him to rule. My position is that the monster I fear is God's plaything, and the God I worship is its Master.
