chráomai

middle voice of a primary verb (perhaps rather from G5495, to handle); to furnish what is needed; (give an oracle, "graze" (touch slightly), light upon, etc.), i.e. (by implication) to employ or (by extension) to act towards one in a given manner:--entreat, use. Compare G5531; G5534.

Biblical Usage

To use a thing is to take it up for its purpose, and Paul uses the word for the law, for marriage, for freedom, and even for a kind word from a centurion. A slave who can gain freedom should use it, and a man who uses the law must use it lawfully. The word covers the whole range of handling what God has put in our hands.

Theological Significance

I believe use is a moral question, not a neutral one, because everything we handle belongs to God. Paul's rule is plain: use the world, but do not abuse it, and use freedom for the good of others. I hold that the test of any use is whether it serves love.
