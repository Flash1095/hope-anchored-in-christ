nâphal

to fall, in a great variety of applications (intransitive or causative, literal or figurative)

Biblical Usage

Naphal means to fall, and it covers a deep sleep, a righteous man stumbling, and an angel cast from heaven. In Genesis 2:21 the Lord causes a deep sleep to fall upon Adam, and in Psalm 37:24 though he fall, he shall not be utterly cast down, for the Lord upholds him with His hand. In Proverbs 24:16 a just man falleth seven times and riseth up again, but the wicked shall fall into mischief, and in Isaiah 14:12 Lucifer falls from heaven, cut down to the ground.

Theological Significance

I believe falling is not the end of the story for the righteous, because the same word that drops Lucifer from heaven catches the just man by the hand. Adam falls asleep and wakes to a wife, the just man falls seven times and rises seven times, and only the wicked fall and stay down. I hold that the difference is not whether I fall, but whose hand holds me when I do.
