châphêr

to blush; figuratively, to be ashamed, disappointed; causatively, to shame, reproach

Biblical Usage

Chapher is blushing with shame, and it is the colour of judgment and of grace. Fear not, says Isaiah, for thou shalt not be ashamed, and thou shalt forget the shame of thy youth. Jeremiah pictures the mother of seven languishing, ashamed and confounded, and Job says his friends were confounded because they had hoped and came and were ashamed. Isaiah warns Israel they shall be ashamed of the oaks they desired.

Theological Significance

I believe shame in the Bible is a true feeling with a false master. The prophets use it to describe judgment, and then God promises to take it away. I hold that the shame God removes is not denied but forgiven, and the promise to forget the shame of youth is one I cling to.
