kᵉçîyl

properly, fat, i.e. (figuratively) stupid or silly

Biblical Usage

Keciyl means the fool, the stupid fellow, the dullard: Psalms 94:8 says, understand, ye brutish among the people, and ye fools, when will ye be wise? Proverbs 15:14 says the mouth of fools feedeth on foolishness. Ecclesiastes 2:14 says the fool walketh in darkness, and one event happeneth to them all. Ecclesiastes 5:1 warns to be more ready to hear, than to give the sacrifice of fools, for they consider not that they do evil.

Theological Significance

I believe the fool is not the man who lacks wit, but the man who feeds on foolishness and walks in darkness. I hold that the wise man's eyes are in his head, and the fool's sacrifice is refused because he never stopped to hear.
