ʻâtsêl

indolent

Biblical Usage

The sluggard is the Bible's portrait of laziness, and the proverbs paint him from every side. Go to the ant, thou sluggard, consider her ways and be wise, how long wilt thou sleep, the sluggard is as vinegar to the teeth and smoke to the eyes, and he hides his hand in his bosom and will not bring it to his mouth again.

Theological Significance

I believe the sluggard's trouble is not that he cannot work, it is that he will not, and the proverbs hold up the ant to shame him. A little sleep and a little slumber is all it takes to let poverty come like a robber, and I hold that diligence is the quiet virtue that keeps a household standing.
