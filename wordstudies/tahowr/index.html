ṭâhôwr

pure (in a physical, chemical, ceremonial or moral sense)

Biblical Usage

The mercy seat is overlaid with pure gold, and the table and the lampstand are pure gold too. The priests must put a difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean. Noah takes seven pairs of every clean beast into the ark, and the psalmist says the fear of the Lord is clean, enduring for ever. The word is pure, clean: gold without mixture, beasts fit for sacrifice, and a fear that endures.

Theological Significance

I hold that purity is not a feeling but a standard, and the standard is God's. I believe the pure gold of the mercy seat shows what purity is for: to hold the place where God meets His people, and to keep it clean.
