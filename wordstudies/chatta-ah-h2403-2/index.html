chaṭṭâʼâh

an offence (sometimes habitual sinfulness), and its penalty, occasion, sacrifice, or expiation; also (concretely) an offender

Biblical Usage

This word is sin in its full weight: the offence, its guilt, its penalty, and the offering that answers it. Sin lies at Cain's door like a crouching beast, Moses pleads to make atonement for Israel's great sin, Solomon prays for forgiveness when the people turn back, and Isaiah's sin is purged by the coal from the altar.

Theological Significance

I believe sin is not a small mistake but a weight that needs an altar. The coal that touches Isaiah's lips is the answer to every verse here: God provides the purging He demands.
