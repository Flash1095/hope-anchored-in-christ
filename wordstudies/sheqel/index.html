sheqel

probably a weight; used as a commercial standard

Biblical Usage

Abraham weighs out four hundred shekels of silver for Sarah's burial field, and the law fixes the shekel as the measure for everything from the price of a servant gored by an ox to the half shekel every Israelite paid as atonement money. The sanctuary had its own shekel, so no one could cheat the standard.

Theological Significance

The shekel matters because God cares about honest measures. A fixed weight meant the rich and the poor paid the same atonement price, and that is my picture of the gospel: one price, one standard, the same for everyone.
