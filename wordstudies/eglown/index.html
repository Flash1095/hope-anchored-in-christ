ʻEglôwn

Eglon, the name of a place in Palestine and of a Moabitish king

Biblical Usage

Eglon is both a royal city in the lowlands of Judah and the fat king of Moab who oppressed Israel for eighteen years. Joshua's army took the city of Eglon in the conquest, and later the king Eglon ruled from his summer parlour until Ehud the left-handed judge came with a message from God and struck him down. The same name covers a city that fell and a king who fell, both before God's people.

Theological Significance

I believe Eglon shows that oppression has a set time, because eighteen years felt endless to Israel and ended in a single afternoon. God raises up deliverers, and the fattest king is no match for Him.
