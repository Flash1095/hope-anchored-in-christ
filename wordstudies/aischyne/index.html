aischýnē

from G153; shame or disgrace (abstractly or concretely):--dishonesty, shame.

Biblical Usage

The word runs both ways. Jesus warns the guest who takes the highest seat will be put down with shame; Paul says of enemies of the cross that their glory is in their shame; Hebrews says Jesus despised the shame of the cross; Jude calls false teachers wandering stars with shame like foam; and Laodicea is told to buy white raiment so the shame of thy nakedness do not appear. Shame is sometimes the punishment, sometimes the thing Christ bore for us.

Theological Significance

I hold that the gospel turns shame inside out. The world glories in what should shame it, and Christ took the shame that was ours so that we could be clothed instead of exposed. The cross is where shame ends — despised by Him, removed from us.
