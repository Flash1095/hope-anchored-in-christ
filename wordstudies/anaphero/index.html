anaphérō

from G303 and G5342; to take up (literally or figuratively):--bear, bring (carry, lead) up, offer (up).

Biblical Usage

The word means to take up, to carry up, to offer up — and it holds the whole ladder of sacrifice. Abraham offered Isaac his son upon the altar, and James says he was justified by works when he did it; the high priest offers up sacrifice daily, but Christ offered up Himself once; Peter says He bore our sins in His own body on the tree; and Jesus takes Peter, James and John up into a high mountain apart, and is transfigured before them. The offering goes up, and the Lord goes up, and both are the same word.

Theological Significance

I believe this word holds the gospel in both directions: Abraham carried his son up the mountain to offer him, and God carried His Son up the mountain to offer Him — and the difference is everything. Isaac came down alive, and Christ bore our sins in His own body on the tree so that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness. The offering that Abraham could not finish, God finished Himself.
