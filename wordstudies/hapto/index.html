háptō

a primary verb; properly, to fasten to, i.e. (specially) to set on fire:--kindle, light.

Biblical Usage

Hapto means to fasten to, to touch, and in its active voice the New Testament uses it for lighting a fire. In Luke 8:16 and Luke 11:33 no man lights a candle and hides it, in Luke 15:8 the woman lights a candle to search for her lost coin, and in Acts 28:2 the islanders kindled a fire for Paul and his companions in the cold rain.

Theological Significance

I believe the picture is simple and searching: a lit candle is meant to be seen, and a fire is meant to warm strangers. The touching sense of this verb belongs to its middle voice, and I want the light it kindles to be the thing people remember.
