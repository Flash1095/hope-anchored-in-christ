nâtsach

properly, to glitter from afar, i.e. to be eminent (as a superintendent, especially of the Temple services and its music); to be permanent

Biblical Usage

The word names the one who leads the music, setting the singers in order with their harps, and the one set over the work of the temple, directing the labourers. It is the chief singer's mark on the psalm, and the overseer's place over Solomon's builders.

Theological Significance

I believe God puts overseers over both song and stone: the music must be led and the work must be watched, and the same word covers them both because both are His service. The chief singer and the overseer answer to the same Lord, and neither task is beneath His notice.
