ʻervâh

nudity, literally (especially the pudenda) or figuratively (disgrace, blemish)

Biblical Usage

ʻErvâh is nakedness — the shame of being uncovered, the disgrace of exposure. Ham sees his father's nakedness, and the law guards the nakedness of kin; the prophets use it for a nation's disgrace laid bare. It is the word for shame made visible.

Theological Significance

I believe ʻervâh shows why clothing matters in Scripture: nakedness is shame, and covering is mercy. I hold that the coats of skins God made for Adam and Eve are the first picture of covering — and of the covering Christ provides.
