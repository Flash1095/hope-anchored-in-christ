pâshaṭ

to spread out (i.e. deploy in hostile array); by analogy, to strip (i.e. unclothe, plunder, flay, etc.)

Biblical Usage

Pashat means to spread out, to strip off, to raid and plunder. Jonathan strips himself of his robe and gives it to David in 1 Samuel 18:4, and Saul strips off his clothes and prophesies in 1 Samuel 19:24. The Philistines strip Saul in 1 Chronicles 10:8 when they find him slain on Mount Gilboa, and David raids the Geshurites and the Amalekites in 1 Samuel 27:8.

Theological Significance

I believe the stripping in these verses tells the story of two kings: Jonathan strips himself to honour David, Saul is stripped by his enemies, and the robe that is given freely is worth more than the armour that is taken. I hold that the robe given freely honours the giver, and the armour taken by force only proves the taker.
