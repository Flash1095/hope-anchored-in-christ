nâgad

properly, to front, i.e. stand boldly out opposite; by implication (causatively), to manifest; figuratively, to announce (always by word of mouth to one present); specifically, to expose, predict, explain, praise

Biblical Usage

Nagad means to tell or declare, to make a thing known plainly, and it is used for news from the road and for the heavens themselves. In 1 Kings 18:13 Obadiah says, was it not told my lord what I did when Jezebel slew the prophets of the Lord, and in Psalm 19:1 the heavens declare the glory of God. In Psalm 22:31 a people yet to be born shall be told of His righteousness, and in Isaiah 40:21 the prophet asks, hath it not been told you from the beginning.

Theological Significance

I believe declaring is the work of everyone who has heard good news, because the heavens do it without being asked and Moses did it over a meal. What God has done is not a secret to keep but a story to tell, and I hold that the telling is part of the worship.
