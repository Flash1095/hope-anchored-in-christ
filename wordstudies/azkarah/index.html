ʼazkârâh

a reminder; specifically remembrance-offering

Biblical Usage

The azkarah is the memorial portion — the handful of flour and oil and frankincense the priest burns on the altar, so that the whole offering is remembered before God. Leviticus gives it in the meat offering, in the sin offering, and on the shewbread, and Numbers gives it in the jealousy offering. A small part burned, and the whole remembered.

Theological Significance

I hold that God remembers his people the way the memorial offering remembers the sacrifice: a handful burned on the altar stands for the whole. My prayers are remembered the same way.
