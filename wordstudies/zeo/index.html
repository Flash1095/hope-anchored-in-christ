zéō

a primary verb; to be hot (boil, of liquids; or glow, of solids), i.e. (figuratively) be fervid (earnest):--be fervent.

Biblical Usage

To boil, to glow, to burn hot — the word describes Apollos as fervent in spirit as he teaches, and Paul commands the same heat in every believer: ‘fervent in spirit; serving the Lord.’ It is not excitement for its own sake but a steady fire under the work of God.

Theological Significance

I believe a lukewarm faith was never the goal: God wants hearts that burn, not smoulder.
