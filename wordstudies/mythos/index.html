mŷthos

perhaps from the same as G3453 (through the idea of tuition); a tale, i.e. fiction ("myth"):--fable.

Biblical Usage

Mythos is a made-up tale, a fable, and the New Testament warns against trading truth for it. Paul tells Timothy not to give heed to fables and endless genealogies in 1 Timothy 1:4, and to refuse profane and old wives' fables in 1 Timothy 4:7. In 2 Timothy 4:4 people turn away their ears from the truth and are turned to fables, while Peter insists in 2 Peter 1:16 that the apostles did not follow cunningly devised fables when they told of Christ's power and coming.

Theological Significance

I believe the danger of a fable is not that it is boring, but that it is interesting enough to replace the truth. A good story that flatters the ear can crowd out the gospel that saves, and the church has to keep choosing the plain truth over the tale. My position is that eyewitness truth beats a clever story every time.
