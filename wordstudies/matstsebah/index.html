matstsêbâh

something stationed, i.e. a column or (memorial stone); by analogy, an idol

Biblical Usage

A pillar is a standing stone, and the Bible uses it for memorials, for worship, and for idols. Jacob sets up the stone at Bethel and pours oil on it, and again at the place where God spoke to him; Moses builds twelve pillars at the foot of Sinai; and Hezekiah breaks the images and cuts down the groves, because pillars meant for memory had become idols.

Theological Significance

I believe a pillar is a marker of what a man decided to remember, and the Bible shows both sides of it: Jacob's stone marks the place God met him, and the pillars Hezekiah broke mark the places men met their idols. What we set up to remember will shape what we worship.
