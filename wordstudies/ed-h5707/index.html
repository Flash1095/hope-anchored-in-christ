ʻêd

concretely, a witness; abstractly, testimony; specifically, a recorder, i.e. prince

Biblical Usage

A witness is someone who saw and can speak, and the law of God rests on that simple fact. The ninth commandment forbids false witness against a neighbour, Deuteronomy requires two or three witnesses before anyone is put to death, and Samuel calls on the Lord and His anointed as witnesses that he took nothing from Israel. The psalmist even calls the moon God's faithful witness in the sky, because creation itself testifies.

Theological Significance

I believe truth needs witnesses, and God gave His people the job of telling what they have seen Him do. My position is that a faithful witness is worth more than a clever argument, because the truth stands on what really happened.
