châraph

to pull off, i.e. (by implication) to expose (as by stripping); specifically, to betroth (as if a surrender); figuratively, to carp at, i.e. defame; to spend the winter

Biblical Usage

Charaph is reproaching, defying, taunting, and Goliath is its loudest voice. I defy the armies of Israel this day, he said, and David answered by asking who this uncircumcised Philistine is that he should defy the armies of the living God. David came in the name of the LORD of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom thou hast defied. Centuries later Sennacherib wrote letters to rail on the LORD God of Israel the same way.

Theological Significance

I believe defiance is always aimed past the people at God Himself: Goliath defied the armies, but David heard the defiance of the living God. The same is true of Sennacherib's letters. I hold that God answers reproach, and the valley of Elah is the proof.
