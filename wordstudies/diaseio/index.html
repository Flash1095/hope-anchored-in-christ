diaseíō

from G1223 and G4579; to shake thoroughly, i.e. (figuratively) to intimidate:--do violence to.

Biblical Usage

To do violence to a man is to shake him down by force, and the word stands once, where John tells the soldiers to do violence to no man and to be content with their wages. It is the only place the soldiers are told how to behave.

Theological Significance

I hold that John's charge to the soldiers is the gospel applied to power; men with swords are told to use them justly and to be content, which is the beginning of all honest government.
