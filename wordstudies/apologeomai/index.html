apologéomai

middle voice from a compound of G575 and G3056; to give an account (legal plea) of oneself, i.e. exculpate (self):--answer (for self), make defence, excuse (self), speak for self.

Biblical Usage

To give an account of oneself, to answer for oneself before accusers. Paul counts himself happy to answer for himself before Agrippa in Acts 26:2, and Jesus promises His disciples in Luke 12:11 that when they are brought before synagogues and magistrates they need not plan their answer. Paul says in Romans 2:15 that men's thoughts accuse or else excuse one another, and in 2 Corinthians 12:19 he wonders whether the church thinks he is excusing himself to them.

Theological Significance

I hold that the word covers two courts: the one before men and the one inside me. Before men I may have to answer, and the Spirit will give the words; inside, my own thoughts accuse or excuse me daily. Paul's answer to both is the same — he speaks before God in Christ, for their edifying, not for his own defence.
