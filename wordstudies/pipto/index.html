píptō

, a reduplicated and contracted form of πέτω; (which occurs only as an alternate in certain tenses); probably akin to G4072 through the idea of alighting; to fall (literally or figuratively):--fail, fall (down), light on.

Biblical Usage

Pipto means to fall — a seed, a house, a star, a man. The house on the sand fell, and great was the fall of it, in Matthew 7:27, and Paul warns the man who thinks he stands to take heed lest he fall in 1 Corinthians 10:12. But falling is not only ruin: the corn of wheat must fall into the ground and die to bear fruit, in John 12:24, and Jesus saw Satan fall as lightning from heaven in Luke 10:18.

Theological Significance

I believe there are two kinds of falling — the fall that breaks you and the fall that plants you. I hold that the difference is whether I fall on the stone, or fall at His feet.
