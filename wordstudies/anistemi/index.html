anístēmi

from G303 and G2476; to stand up (literal or figurative, transitive or intransitive):--arise, lift up, raise up (again), rise (again), stand up(-right).

Biblical Usage

To rise, to stand up, and by extension to be raised from the dead. Peter stands up with the eleven on the day of Pentecost, God raised up Jesus from the dead, the prodigal rises and goes to his father, and the dead in Christ shall rise first. The word joins the simplest act of standing to the greatest hope of the gospel.

Theological Significance

I believe standing up is the gospel in a single motion: the dead Christ stood up, the prodigal stood up, and the dead in Christ will stand up. I hold that resurrection is not an idea but a rising, and my own rising is already promised in His.
