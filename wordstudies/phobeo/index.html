phobéō

from G5401; to frighten, i.e. (passively) to be alarmed; by analogy, to be in awe of, i.e. revere:--be (+ sore) afraid, fear (exceedingly), reverence.

Biblical Usage

Phobeo means to fear, to be afraid, to stand in awe, and it is the verb behind the Bible's many commands not to fear. Jesus says to fear not them which kill the body but Him which can destroy both soul and body in hell, and He tells His disciples not to fear those who can kill the body and after that have no more that they can do. Peter says not to fear the terror of persecutors but to sanctify the Lord God in your hearts.

Theological Significance

I hold that the fear of God is the cure for the fear of men: fear the One who holds the soul, and the ones who can only touch the body lose their terror.
