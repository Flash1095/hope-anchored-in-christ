xénos

apparently a primary word; foreign (literally, alien, or figuratively, novel); by implication, a guest or (vice-versa) entertainer:--host, strange(-r).

Biblical Usage

I was a stranger, and ye took me in, says the King in Matthew, and Paul found the Athenians given to strange gods. Gaius was Paul's host and the host of the whole church, and Paul tells the Ephesians they were once aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers from the covenants of promise.

Theological Significance

I hold that stranger is one of the gospel's hinge words: Christ counts Himself among them, Paul names the gods of Athens strange, and the church is told it was once alien and is now brought near. I believe hospitality is not kindness added to the gospel — it is the gospel practised on the person at the door.
