erēmóō

from G2048; to lay waste (literally or figuratively):--(bring to, make) desolate(-ion), come to nought.

Biblical Usage

Erēmóō means to lay waste — to leave a place desolate and stripped. Jesus uses it for a kingdom divided against itself, brought to desolation (Matthew 12:25; Luke 11:17), and it returns in Revelation, where the great city is laid waste in a single hour and the merchants weep over her. The word is never gentle; it is the language of judgment falling all at once.

Theological Significance

I believe erēmóō shows what judgment looks like when it arrives: sudden, total, and impossible to argue with. That is why the merchants' weeping matters — the city they trusted is gone in an hour.
