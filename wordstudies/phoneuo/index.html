phoneúō

from G5406; to be a murderer (of):--kill, do murder, slay.

Biblical Usage

Phoneuo means to murder, to kill with intent, and it stands in the commandments and in the apostles' warnings. Jesus quotes, Thou shalt not kill, and sets the commandment against anger itself, the young ruler is told to keep the commandments, and James says that the law which forbids adultery also forbids murder. Paul sums up the law in love, and says, Thou shalt not kill.

Theological Significance

I believe murder is the Bible's line between anger and blood: the commandment forbids the deed, and Jesus forbids the heart that leads to it. The law does not rank its commandments; to break one is to break the Lawgiver. My position is that the sixth commandment guards more than life: it guards the image of God in every man, and to kill is to strike at the image.
