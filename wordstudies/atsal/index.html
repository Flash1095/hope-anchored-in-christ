ʼâtsal

to separate; hence, to select, refuse, contract

Biblical Usage

To withhold or take away is the sense, and the word turns on what is kept back. Esau cried that Jacob had supplanted him twice and taken away his blessing, God took of the Spirit on Moses and put it on the seventy elders, and the Preacher says he withheld not his heart from any joy.

Theological Significance

I believe the word shows both sides of taking: a brother's theft, God's generous sharing, and a king's refusal to deny himself anything. What is taken away in deceit God can give back in grace, and I hold that the Spirit shared with the elders is the pattern for all His gifts.
