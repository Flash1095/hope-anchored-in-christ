sēmeîon

neuter of a presumed derivative of the base of G4591; an indication, especially ceremonially or supernaturally:--miracle, sign, token, wonder.

Biblical Usage

A semeion is a sign, and Scripture is careful about which signs are true. The Jews require a sign, and the Greeks seek after wisdom, but Paul says the signs of an apostle were wrought among the Corinthians in all patience, in signs, and wonders, and mighty deeds. The same word warns the other way: the coming of the man of sin is after the working of Satan with all power and signs and lying wonders. And Paul's own hand is a token in every epistle — so I write.

Theological Significance

I believe signs prove nothing on their own: apostles work true signs in patience, and Satan works lying wonders with power, and the difference is the truth they serve. The Jews required a sign and missed the Sign standing before them. And I hold that the surest token is the plain one — Paul's own hand on the letter, and Christ's own word behind it.
