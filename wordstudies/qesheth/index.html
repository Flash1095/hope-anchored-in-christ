qesheth

a bow, for shooting (hence, figuratively, strength) or the iris

Biblical Usage

God sets his bow in the cloud as a token of the covenant between him and the earth. The same word is the bow God bends and makes ready against the unrepentant, the bow he bares in Habakkuk's vision, and the bow of Israel he breaks in the valley of Jezreel. One word holds the promise and the judgment together.

Theological Significance

I believe the bow in the cloud is God's own signature on the sky, and every rainbow since is his reminder, not ours. I hold that the same God who bends his bow in judgment set it in the cloud as a token of mercy, and he has never taken it back.
