lûwn

to stop (usually over night); by implication, to stay permanently; hence (in a bad sense) to be obstinate (especially in words, to complain)

Biblical Usage

The word means to lodge or spend the night, and it covers both the welcome of a guest and the stubbornness of a complaint. Lot begged the angels to turn in and tarry all night in his house, Ruth promised Naomi that where she lodged, she would lodge, and the psalm promises that whoever dwells in the secret place of the Most High shall abide under His shadow. Israel also lodged in the wilderness on the way to the promised land, and the stones from the Jordan were left in the lodging place.

Theological Significance

I believe lodging is a picture of belonging: we stay where we are welcome, and God makes His presence our lodging place. I hold that the one who abides under His shadow is not a visitor passing through, but a child at home, and that is the rest every weary heart is looking for.
