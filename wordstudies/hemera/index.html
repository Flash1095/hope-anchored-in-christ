hēméra

feminine (with G5610 implied) of a derivative of ἧμαι (to sit; akin to the base of G1476) meaning tame, i.e. gentle; day, i.e. (literally) the time space between dawn and dark, or the whole 24 hours (but several days were usually reckoned by the Jews as inclusive of the parts of both extremes); figuratively, a period (always defined more or less clearly by the context):--age, + alway, (mid-)day (by day, (-ly)), + for ever, judgment, (day) time, while, years.

Biblical Usage

Hemera is a day, and the Bible counts days from the first day to the last. In Matthew 6:34 Jesus says sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof, in John 8:56 Abraham rejoiced to see my day, in 2 Peter 3:8 one day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and in Revelation 1:10 John is in the Spirit on the Lord's day.

Theological Significance

I believe the day is the measure God gives me: enough evil for today and no more, a day Abraham saw from far off, a day that is a thousand years to God and a single morning to me. I live one day at a time because he counts them all.
