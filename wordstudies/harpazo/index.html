harpázō

from a derivative of G138; to seize (in various applications):--catch (away, up), pluck, pull, take (by force).

Biblical Usage

Harpazo means to seize, to snatch away, and it is used for the Spirit's work and the enemy's alike. In 1 Thessalonians 4:17 the living are caught up together to meet the Lord, in Acts 8:39 the Spirit catches Philip away, in John 10:28 no man can pluck the sheep out of Jesus' hand, and in Matthew 11:12 the violent take the kingdom by force.

Theological Significance

I believe the word holds both the danger and the comfort: the enemy snatches, but the Shepherd's grip is stronger, and the catching up at the end is his hand, not mine. No one can pluck me out of it.
