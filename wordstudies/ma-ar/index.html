mâʼar

to be bitter or (causatively) to embitter, i.e. be painful

Biblical Usage

The word means to be bitter or painful, and the law uses it for a plague that eats away at cloth or a house, a fretting leprosy that spreads and cannot be cleansed. Ezekiel uses it for the pricking brier and the grieving thorn that shall trouble Israel no more when God restores her.

Theological Significance

I believe the fretting plague is a picture of sin: it spreads quietly and eats what it touches, and only the priest's cleansing can name it clean. I hold that God's promise to remove the grieving thorn is the promise of the gospel, that what has eaten at us will not eat at us forever.
