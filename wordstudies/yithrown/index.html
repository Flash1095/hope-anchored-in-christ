yithrôwn

preeminence, gain

Biblical Usage

Yithron asks the question Ecclesiastes keeps asking: what profit is there in all our labour? In Ecclesiastes 1:3 the Preacher puts it plainly, "What profit hath a man of all his labour which he taketh under the sun?" He looks at everything his hands have made in Ecclesiastes 2:11 and finds no profit in it, yet in Ecclesiastes 2:13 he still sees that wisdom excels folly as light excels darkness. The word keeps the question in front of us all the way to Ecclesiastes 3:9, where he asks it once more of every worker.

Theological Significance

I believe this word is meant to stop me counting my life the way the world counts it. If profit is only what I can keep, then everything I build ends in vanity, and I hold that the gain that matters is the one God gives, which is wisdom, a heart set right, and work done before him. My position is that a life measured by yithron alone will always come up empty, and that is exactly the Preacher's point.
