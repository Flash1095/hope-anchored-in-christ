akáthartos

from G1 (as a negative particle) and a presumed derivative of G2508 (meaning cleansed); impure (ceremonially, morally (lewd) or specially, (demonic)):--foul, unclean.

Biblical Usage

The great unclean word of the New Testament. Jesus gives His disciples power over unclean spirits; a man with an unclean spirit cries out in the synagogue; Peter refuses the sheet from heaven — I have never eaten any thing that is common or unclean; and Paul quotes Isaiah, touch not the unclean thing. The word covers demons, forbidden foods, and a world the saints must come out of.

Theological Significance

I hold that the arc of this word is the arc of the gospel: what was unclean — spirits, foods, Gentiles — is cleansed by Christ, and the church is told to stay clean not by ritual but by separation from what defiles. The sheet from heaven changed everything Peter thought he knew.
