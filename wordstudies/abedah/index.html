ʼăbêdâh

concrete, something lost; abstract, destruction, i.e. Hades

Biblical Usage

This word names a lost thing — something that has gone astray and must be dealt with honestly. The law covers the man who finds 'that which was lost' and swears falsely about it; the neighbour's lost ox or garment must be restored; and 'all manner of lost thing' is brought before the judges when two men claim it.

Theological Significance

It is a law word, not a theology word, but the principle underneath it is pure gospel: what is lost is not abandoned, and the finder has a duty. I believe God cares about lost things — lost sheep, lost coins, lost sons — and His law was already teaching His people to seek and restore before Jesus told the parables.
