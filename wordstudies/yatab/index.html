yâṭab

to be (causative) make well, literally (sound, beautiful) or figuratively (happy, successful, right)

Biblical Usage

Yâṭab means to do well, to be good and right and to prosper in it. God told Cain that if he did well he would be accepted, and Moses longed for a heart in Israel that would fear God and keep His commandments always, that it might be well with them and their children for ever. Jeremiah warns that a nation that does evil will lose the good God meant to give it, and the psalm says the wicked has left off to be wise and to do good.

Theological Significance

I believe doing well is not earning God's love but answering it, and Cain's story proves the door was open to him. My position is that it is well with the man who fears God and keeps His commandments, not because he is perfect but because God is faithful. And I hold that the good God promises is still good, and I can still choose it today.
