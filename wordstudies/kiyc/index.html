kîyç

a cup; also a bag for money or weights

Biblical Usage

Kis is a cup, and also a bag for money or weights. Deuteronomy 25:13 forbids divers weights in the bag, a great and a small, Proverbs 16:11 says a just weight and balance are the LORD's and all the weights of the bag are his work, and Micah 6:11 asks whether God shall count pure the man with the bag of deceitful weights. Proverbs 1:14 has the sinners saying, let us all have one purse, Isaiah 46:6 shows idol-makers lavishing gold out of the bag to hire a goldsmith, and Proverbs 23:31 warns against the wine when it giveth his colour in the cup.

Theological Significance

I believe the bag is where honesty is weighed: the same bag holds the merchant's weights and the idolater's gold, and God owns the scales. I hold that a just weight is worship, because the LORD's balance does not lie.
