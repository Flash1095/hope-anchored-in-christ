klēros

probably from G2806 (through the idea of using bits of wood, etc., for the purpose; a die (for drawing chances); by implication, a portion (as if so secured); by extension, an acquisition (especially a patrimony, figuratively):--heritage, inheritance, lot, part.

Biblical Usage

Kleros is the lot, the portion secured by casting lots, and so the heritage itself. Matthew 27:35 and Mark 15:24 and Luke 23:34 and John 19:24 all tell how the soldiers parted Jesus' garments, casting lots, that the scripture might be fulfilled, Acts 1:17 says Judas had obtained part of this ministry, and Colossians 1:12 thanks the Father which hath made us meet to be partakers of the inheritance of the saints in light.

Theological Significance

I believe the lots at the cross and in Acts 1:17 are the same word with opposite ends: the soldiers cast lots for His clothes, and the apostles cast lots for His ministry. I hold that the inheritance of Colossians 1:12 is the portion God assigns, not the portion men gamble for.
