epibállō

from G1909 and G906; to throw upon (literal or figurative, transitive or reflexive; usually with more or less force); specially (with G1438 implied) to reflect; impersonally, to belong to:--beat into, cast (up-)on, fall, lay (on), put (unto), stretch forth, think on.

Biblical Usage

Epiballo means to throw upon, to lay on, to cast — hands laid on Jesus in the garden, hands laid on the apostles, a patch laid on an old garment, a thought laid on the heart. It is the word for what is put onto something else, whether violence or repair.

Theological Significance

Hands that seize and hands that heal are not the same, though the word is the same. I believe the gospel is the story of the second kind winning: the hands laid on Jesus in violence became, in His own hands, the laying on of life.
