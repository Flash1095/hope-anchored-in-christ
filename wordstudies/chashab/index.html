châshab

properly, to plait or interpenetrate, i.e. (literally) to weave or (generally) to fabricate; figuratively, to plot or contrive (usually in a malicious sense); hence (from the mental effort) to think, regard, value, compute

Biblical Usage

Chashab is weaving thoughts together, devising, reckoning, accounting, and it is the commonest verb for thinking in the Bible. Joseph tells his brothers they thought evil against him, but God meant it unto good. Eli thought Hannah had been drunken because she prayed in her heart. In Solomon's days silver was nothing accounted of, and the Tyrian craftsman sent for the temple was skilful to grave any manner of graving.

Theological Significance

I believe the word shows that thoughts are weavings: Joseph's brothers wove evil and God wove good from the same thread, Eli wove a wrong conclusion from a silent prayer, and a king's craftsman wove gold into a temple. I hold that God is the master weaver, and my own thoughts are only threads until He sets them in His pattern.
