pâʻam

to tap, i.e. beat regularly; hence (generally) to impel or agitate

Biblical Usage

Pâ'am is the push that stirs a person from inside. Pharaoh's spirit is troubled by his dreams, the Spirit of the LORD begins to move Samson at Mahaneh-dan, Nebuchadnezzar's spirit is troubled by his dream, and the psalmist is so troubled he cannot speak.

Theological Significance

I believe this word shows how God works in the inward parts. He stirs, troubles, and moves, and a troubled spirit is often the first sign that God is speaking.
