aiṓn

from the same as G104; properly, an age; by extension, perpetuity (also past); by implication, the world; specially (Jewish) a Messianic period (present or future):--age, course, eternal, (for) ever(-more), (n-)ever, (beginning of the, while the) world (began, without end). Compare G5550.

Biblical Usage

The word means an age — a stretch of time with a character of its own — and by extension the world shaped by that age. Jesus speaks of sins not forgiven neither in this world, neither in the world to come, of the harvest as the end of the world, and Paul says Christ is exalted above every name not only in this world, but also in that which is to come. Hebrews adds that God made the worlds by His Son.

Theological Significance

I hold that aion is the Bible's way of saying history has a shape and an end. This age is passing, the age to come is Christ's, and the line between them runs through the cross — which is why the same word can mean both the times we live in and the world itself.
