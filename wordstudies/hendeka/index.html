héndeka

from (the neuter of) G1520 and G1176; one and ten, i.e. eleven:--eleven.

Biblical Usage

Hendeka means eleven, and after the resurrection there are eleven apostles, not twelve. In Matthew 28:16 the eleven disciples go to the mountain, in Luke 24:9 they tell the women what they have seen, in Acts 1:26 Matthias is numbered with the eleven, and in Acts 2:14 Peter stands up with the eleven on the day of Pentecost.

Theological Significance

I believe the eleven are the proof that the church is built on men who failed and came back: one betrayed, one denied, and eleven stood up on Pentecost to preach the risen Lord. I hold that the empty twelfth place is itself a sermon: the betrayal was real, the restoration was real, and the eleven were enough for Pentecost.
