hekatón

of uncertain affinity; a hundred:--hundred.

Biblical Usage

Hekaton means a hundred, and Jesus uses the round number for the harvest and the flock alike. In Matthew 13:8 the good ground brings forth a hundredfold, in Matthew 18:12 and Luke 15:4 the shepherd leaves the ninety and nine to find the one lost sheep of a hundred, and in John 21:11 the net holds a hundred and fifty-three great fishes and does not break.

Theological Significance

I believe the hundred is a number of plenty: a hundredfold harvest, a hundred sheep with one that matters enough to chase, and a net so full it should have broken. God does not deal in small numbers.
