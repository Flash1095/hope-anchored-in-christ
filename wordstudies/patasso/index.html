patássō

probably prolongation from G3817; to knock (gently or with a weapon or fatally):--smite, strike. Compare G5180.

Biblical Usage

Patasso means to smite, to strike, to knock with a weapon. Jesus quotes Zechariah in Matthew 26:31: I will smite the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock shall be scattered abroad. A disciple smites the servant of the high priest with the sword in Luke 22:50, and the angel of the Lord smites Herod in Acts 12:23 because he gives not God the glory. Moses smites the Egyptian in Acts 7:24, defending the man who suffers wrong.

Theological Significance

I believe the smiting falls on the Shepherd first, and the scattering that follows is the cost of His being struck in our place. That is why I hold that no other smiting in these verses is like that one: the rest are judgment or defence, but His was substitution.
