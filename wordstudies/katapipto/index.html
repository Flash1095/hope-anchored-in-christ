katapíptō

from G2596 and G4098; to fall down:--fall (down).

Biblical Usage

To fall down is what Paul did on the road to Damascus when the light shone and the voice said it was hard to kick against the pricks, what the people of Malta expected of him when the viper fastened on his hand, and what the seed did on the rock and withered for lack of moisture. The word holds a conversion, a false expectation, and a warning, and all three end on the ground.

Theological Significance

I believe falling down before the Lord is the beginning of standing up for Him, because Paul rose a different man than the one who fell. I hold that the seed on the rock warns me to go deep, and the viper warns me that falling is not always fatal when God is in it.
