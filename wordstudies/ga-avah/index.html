gaʼăvâh

arrogance or majesty; by implication, (concretely) ornament

Biblical Usage

Ga'avah is pride, and it swings both ways in Scripture: God's majesty and man's arrogance. Moses calls God's excellency Israel's help in Deuteronomy 33:26, but Isaiah says God will cause the arrogancy of the proud to cease in Isaiah 13:11. Jeremiah has heard the pride of Moab, exceeding proud, in Jeremiah 48:29, and Proverbs warns that in the mouth of the foolish is a rod of pride in Proverbs 14:3.

Theological Significance

I believe pride is a word with two faces, and I must know which one I am wearing. God's excellency is His rightful majesty; mine is a rod in my own mouth. I hold that the cure for my pride is to look up until His greatness makes mine look small.
