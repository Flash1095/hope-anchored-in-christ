atenízō

from a compound of G1 (as a particle of union) and τείνω (to stretch); to gaze intently:--behold earnestly (stedfastly), fasten (eyes), look (earnestly, stedfastly, up stedfastly), set eyes.

Biblical Usage

To look intently is to fasten the eyes, and this word marks the moments when looking matters. Peter fastened his eyes on the lame man at the temple gate, the eyes of the synagogue were fastened on Jesus as He read Isaiah, Stephen looked up steadfastly into heaven and saw the glory of God, and the Israelites could not look steadfastly at Moses' face for its glory.

Theological Significance

I believe the eyes go where the heart already is, and every one of these gazes landed on something worth seeing. Stephen's last sight was the glory of God, and I hold that a life spent looking at Christ will end the same way.
