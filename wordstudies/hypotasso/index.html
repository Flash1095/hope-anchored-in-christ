hypotássō

from G5259 and G5021; to subordinate; reflexively, to obey:--be under obedience (obedient), put under, subdue unto, (be, make) subject (to, unto), be (put) in subjection (to, under), submit self unto.

Biblical Usage

hypotássō turns up where the Bible says to put under, to be subject, to obey. In Luke 2:51, the text says "and was subject unto them." In Romans 8:7, the text says "for it is not subject to the law of God."

Theological Significance

I take hypotássō to mean that obedience is not drudgery; it is trusting that God's way is the good way. That is why I keep this word in mind when I read those passages — it carries more than its size suggests.
