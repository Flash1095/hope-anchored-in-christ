mâtsâh

to suck out; by implication, to drain, to squeeze out

Biblical Usage

To drain or squeeze out is the word for wringing the last drop from something: Gideon wrings a bowl full of water from the dew-soaked fleece, and the priest wrings the blood of the bird offering out at the side of the altar. The psalm says the wicked will wring out the dregs of the LORD's cup and drink them, and Isaiah says Jerusalem drank the cup of trembling and wrung it out.

Theological Significance

I believe the word is about getting everything out: God's judgment is not a sip but the whole cup, drained to the dregs. Yet Gideon shows the other side — God lets His servant wring out a sign, and the same hands that pour judgment also pour mercy.
