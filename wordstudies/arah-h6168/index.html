ʻârâh

to be (causatively, make) bare; hence, to empty, pour out, demolish

Biblical Usage

The word means to make bare, to empty out or pour out. The Servant pours out His soul unto death, Rebekah hastens and empties her pitcher into the trough for the camels, God lays bare the foundation of the wicked house, and the law speaks of uncovering what should stay covered.

Theological Significance

I believe pouring out is the shape of the Servant's life. He emptied Himself completely, and the emptying is what makes Him able to divide the spoil with the strong.
