ginṓskō

a prolonged form of a primary verb; to "know" (absolutely) in a great variety of applications and with many implications (as follow, with others not thus clearly expressed):--allow, be aware (of), feel, (have) know(-ledge), perceived, be resolved, can speak, be sure, understand.

Biblical Usage

I take ginṓskō to mean allow, be aware (of), feel, (have) know, perceived, be resolved, can speak, be sure, understand, nothing more and nothing less. It occurs 205 times, and Matthew and Mark show it doing ordinary work in ordinary sentences. The Bible uses plain words plainly, and this is one of them.

Theological Significance

I hold that ginṓskō matters because knowing God is not information about Him; it is life with Him. My position is that Scripture does not hide its meaning from honest readers.
