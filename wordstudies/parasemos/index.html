parásēmos

from G3844 and the base of G4591; side-marked, i.e. labelled (with a badge (figure-head) of a ship):--sign.

Biblical Usage

Parasemos means side-marked, labelled with a figure-head. It appears once, in Acts 28:11, where Paul sails from Malta on a ship of Alexandria whose sign was Castor and Pollux, the twin gods carved on her prow.

Theological Significance

I hold that the ship that carried Paul to Rome bore the sign of false gods, and God used it anyway to bring His apostle where He wanted him.
