Ananías

of Hebrew origin (H2608); Ananias, the name of three Israelites:--Ananias.

Biblical Usage

Three men share the name Ananias, and their stories could not be more different: the first sold a possession with his wife Sapphira, kept back part of the price, and fell dead when Peter exposed the lie, while the second was a disciple at Damascus whom the Lord sent in a vision to lay hands on Saul, so that the persecutor could see again. The third was the high priest who commanded that Paul be struck on the mouth, and whom Paul called a whited wall.

Theological Significance

One name, three men: a liar struck dead, a faithful disciple who baptised the apostle to the Gentiles, and a high priest who struck Paul. I believe the name carries no lesson of its own — what it shows is that God judges the heart, not the name, and the same name can belong to a warning and to a faithful servant.
