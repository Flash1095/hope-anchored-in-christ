yâdâh

physically, to throw (a stone, an arrow) at or away; especially to revere or worship (with extended hands); intensively, to bemoan (by wringing the hands)

Biblical Usage

Leah conceived again, and bare a son, and said, now will I praise the Lord, therefore she called his name Judah. The guilty shall confess that he hath sinned, and shall recompense his trespass, and David sings, therefore I will give thanks unto thee, O Lord, among the heathen, and I will sing praises unto thy name.

Theological Significance

I hold that this one word carries praise, confession and thanks together: Leah's praise names Judah, the guilty man's confession makes it right, and David's thanks sings it among the nations. I believe praise and confession are the same motion — the hand thrown up toward God, whether in joy or in guilt — and the Bible keeps them in one word so we never separate them.
