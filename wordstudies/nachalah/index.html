nachălâh

properly, something inherited, i.e. (abstractly) occupancy, or (concretely) an heirloom; generally an estate, patrimony or portion

Biblical Usage

Nachalah is the inheritance itself, the portion, the land, or the people God calls His own. In Numbers 18:20 the Lord tells Aaron He is the priests' part and their inheritance, and in Deuteronomy 4:21 Moses is barred from the good land God gives Israel for an inheritance. In Joshua 13:33 the Lord God of Israel is the inheritance of Levi, and in Psalm 127:3 children are an heritage of the Lord.

Theological Significance

I believe the greatest inheritance in the Bible is not land but the Lord Himself. Levi had no fields because God was their portion, and children are called an heritage because people, not acres, are what God treasures. My position is that if God is my inheritance, I am rich already.
