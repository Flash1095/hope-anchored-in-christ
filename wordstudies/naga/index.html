nâgaʻ

properly, to touch, i.e. lay the hand upon (for any purpose; euphemistically, to lie with a woman); by implication, to reach (figuratively, to arrive, acquire); violently, to strike (punish, defeat, destroy, etc.)

Biblical Usage

Naga means to touch, to lay the hand upon, and it runs from a wrestling match to the cleansing of a prophet's lips. In Genesis 32:25 the man touches the hollow of Jacob's thigh as they wrestle, and in Exodus 19:12 whoever touches the mount shall surely be put to death. In Ruth 2:9 Boaz charges the young men not to touch Ruth, and in Isaiah 6:7 the live coal touches the prophet's lips, and his iniquity is taken away and his sin purged.

Theological Significance

I believe a touch in the Bible is never small, because the same hand can wound and can cleanse. Jacob is touched and walks lame for life, the mountain must not be touched at all, and Isaiah is touched and made clean. I hold that what touches me matters, and who touches me matters more.
