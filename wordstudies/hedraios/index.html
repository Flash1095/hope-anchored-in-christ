hedraîos

from a derivative of ἕζομαι (to sit); sedentary, i.e. (by implication) immovable:--settled, stedfast.

Biblical Usage

Hedraios means settled, steadfast, sitting firm, and Paul uses it for the believer who will not be moved. In 1 Corinthians 7:37 the man who stands steadfast in his heart does well, in 1 Corinthians 15:58 the church is to be steadfast, unmoveable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, and in Colossians 1:23 they are to continue in the faith, grounded and settled.

Theological Significance

I believe steadfast is not stubbornness, it is staying where you were planted. The resurrection is the reason Paul gives for it: because the work is not in vain, the worker can stand still.
