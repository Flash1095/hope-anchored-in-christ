ʼăshîyshâh

something closely pressed together, i.e. a cake of raisins or other comfits

Biblical Usage

The cake of raisins is the festival food, pressed together and sweet, and it is used for feasting and for love. David deals to every one of Israel a cake of bread and a good piece of flesh and a flagon of wine when the ark comes home, the beloved asks to be stayed with flagons and comforted with apples, for she is sick of love, and Hosea is told to love a woman beloved of her friend, yet an adulteress, as the children of Israel love flagons of wine.

Theological Significance

I believe the cake of raisins says that God gives His people sweet things for the feast, and that love asks for them plainly: stayed with flagons, comforted with apples, sick of love. And Hosea's flagons are the warning with it, that the same sweetness Israel loves becomes the love of an adulteress, and I hold that the heart must choose which love it will feed on.
