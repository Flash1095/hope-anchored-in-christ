nâdad

properly, to wave to and fro (rarely to flap up and down); figuratively, to rove, flee, or (causatively) to drive away

Biblical Usage

Nadad means to wander or flee, to wave to and fro like a bird from its nest, and it follows the restless from Cain onward. In Job 15:23 the wicked wanders abroad for bread, and knows the day of darkness is ready at his hand, and in Psalm 55:7 the psalmist says he would wander far off and remain in the wilderness. In Proverbs 27:8 a man that wandereth from his place is like a bird that wandereth from her nest, and in Hosea 7:13 woe is pronounced on those who have fled from the Lord, though He redeemed them.

Theological Significance

I believe wandering is what happens when a person leaves the place God gave them, and the bird out of the nest is the truest picture of it. The wicked wanders abroad for bread, the psalmist longs to wander far off and remain in the wilderness, and Hosea pronounces woe on those who flee from the God who redeemed them. I hold that the cure for wandering is not a map, but a return.
