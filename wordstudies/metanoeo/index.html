metanoéō

from G3326 and G3539; to think differently or afterwards, i.e. reconsider (morally, feel compunction):--repent.

Biblical Usage

John the Baptist and Jesus both open with the same cry, repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand. Luke says heaven rejoices over one sinner who repents, Peter tells the crowd at Pentecost to repent and be baptized, and the risen Lord tells Ephesus to repent and do the first works.

Theological Significance

I believe repentance is not a single act but the doorway of the whole Christian life: we enter by turning from sin to God, and we stay on the road by turning again whenever we wander, because the kingdom is still at hand. The kingdom is still at hand, so the turning is never finished in this life.
