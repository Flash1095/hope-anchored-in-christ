amphóteros

comparative of ἀμφί (around); (in plural) both:--both.

Biblical Usage

The word simply means both, and it shows up wherever two things are held together: new wine in new bottles, and both are preserved; Jew and Gentile reconciled both unto God in one body by the cross. It appears fourteen times, always joining a pair — both the bottles and the wine, both the peoples, both the sides of a story.

Theological Significance

Both is a small word, and it carries no theology on its own; its weight is in the pairs it joins — especially the two peoples made one in Christ.
