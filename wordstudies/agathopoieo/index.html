agathopoiéō

from G17; to be a well-doer (as a favor or a duty):--(when) do good (well).

Biblical Usage

Agathopoieo is to do good — to be a well-doer, as a favour or a duty. Jesus asks whether it is lawful to do good on the Sabbath; He warns that even sinners do good to those who do good to them; John says he that doeth good is of God; and Peter says it is better to suffer for well-doing than for evil-doing. The word covers doing good as a command, as a mark of belonging to God, and as a reason to suffer.

Theological Significance

I hold that this word keeps the Christian life practical. Doing good is not a feeling — it is healing on the Sabbath, it is the mark John gives for knowing God, and it is suffering patiently when the world repays good with evil.
