sebastós

from G4573; venerable (august), i.e. (as noun) a title of the Roman Emperor, or (as adjective) imperial:--Augustus(-').

Biblical Usage

Sebastos is Augustus, the title of the Roman emperor, and it carries Paul's case to Rome. Paul appealed to be reserved unto the hearing of Augustus, and Festus determined to send him; and when it was determined they should sail into Italy, Paul was delivered to Julius, a centurion of Augustus' band. The Greek text behind the King James Version spells the title with a small letter, while the text I have checked spells it with a capital.

Theological Significance

I hold that the emperor's name frames the story without ruling it: Paul appealed to Augustus, but the appeal was answered by God, who sent him to Rome to bear witness there.
