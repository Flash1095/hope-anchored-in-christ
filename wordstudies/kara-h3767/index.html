kârâʻ

the leg (from the knee to the ankle) of men or locusts (only in the dual)

Biblical Usage

Kara is the leg — from the knee to the ankle, of men and of locusts, named in the law of the sacrifices. The Passover lamb was not to be eaten raw or sodden, but roast with fire, his head with his legs; and the legs of the burnt offering were washed with water and burned on the altar.

Theological Significance

I believe the leg matters because the sacrifice was whole: head and legs and inwards, washed and burned together, and nothing left unexamined — and that is how I understand consecration, the whole of me offered, not a part. He asks for the whole of me, and I will not offer Him a part.
