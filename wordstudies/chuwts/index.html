chûwts

properly, separate by a wall, i.e. outside, outdoors

Biblical Usage

Outside is where the unclean is carried, where the Passover lamb may not be taken, and where Noah pitches the ark within and without. The word marks the boundary between what belongs inside and what must go out, and the camp's purity depends on keeping it. Outside is not always punishment: it is also where the work is done and the waste is burned.

Theological Significance

I believe the line between inside and outside matters because holiness is a real boundary, not a feeling. The camp kept its purity by sending out what defiled it, and the ark was sealed inside and out. I hold that the gospel reverses the direction in the best way: Christ went outside the gate so that the unclean could come in.
