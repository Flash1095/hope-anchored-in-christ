châṭâʼ

properly, to miss; hence (figuratively and generally) to sin; by inference, to forfeit, lack, expiate, repent, (causatively) lead astray, condemn

Biblical Usage

To sin is first of all to miss, to miss the mark and the way. Joseph calls adultery sin against God, Moses tells Israel they have sinned a great sin at the calf, David confesses to Nathan that he has sinned against the LORD, and Daniel owns the same sin for the whole people.

Theological Significance

I believe sin is missing the mark God set, not merely breaking a rule, and that is why confession is the way back. David's plain words still show the pattern: name it before God, and He puts it away.
