chôshek

the dark; hence (literally) darkness; figuratively, misery, destruction, death, ignorance, sorrow, wickedness

Biblical Usage

Darkness is there from the very first verses, covering the face of the deep before God says let there be light. In Egypt it falls as a plague so thick it can be felt, and at Sinai it wraps the mountain where God speaks. The word moves easily from night to misery, from ignorance to death, because in Scripture darkness is never just the absence of light.

Theological Significance

I believe darkness is the Bible's picture of life without God, and that is why light matters so much in the story. God does not leave His people in it: He speaks into it, leads through it, and promises a day when night ends. My position is simple — wherever darkness is named, I look for what God is doing about it.
