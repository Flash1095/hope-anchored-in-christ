ʼâgêm

figuratively, sad

Biblical Usage

Agem is an adjective meaning sad — literally 'stagnant,' like still water — and it appears only once, in Isaiah's judgment on Egypt: 'all that make sluices and ponds for fish' — or, as the sense runs, all who work for hire shall be sad of soul. The word pictures a spirit gone flat and still, like water that no longer flows, in a land whose idols and counsellors have failed it.

Theological Significance

Sadness here is the prophet's word for a broken Egypt, and I take it as grief, not as a system — the verse carries the sorrow.
