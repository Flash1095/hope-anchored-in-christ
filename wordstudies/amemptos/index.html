ámemptos

from G1 (as a negative particle) and a derivative of G3201; irreproachable:--blameless, faultless, unblamable.

Biblical Usage

Blameless, without fault, and the New Testament uses it for the life that holds up under inspection. Zacharias and Elisabeth are both righteous before God, walking in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord blameless. Paul tells the Philippians to be blameless and harmless, the sons of God, without rebuke, in the midst of a crooked and perverse nation. And he counts his own record touching the righteousness which is in the law, blameless — before he counts it all loss for Christ.

Theological Significance

I believe blameless does not mean sinless; it means there is nothing to charge, and the charge is the thing that matters. Zacharias and Elisabeth walked blamelessly and still needed a Saviour, and Paul was blameless in the law and still called himself the chief of sinners. I hold that the blameless life is the one that has nothing to hide from men and everything to confess to God, and that is why the same word can describe both the priest and the apostle who threw his record away.
