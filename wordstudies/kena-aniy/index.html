Kᵉnaʻanîy

a Kenaanite or inhabitant of Kenaan; by implication, a pedlar (the Canaanites standing for their neighbors the Ishmaelites, who conducted mercantile caravans)

Biblical Usage

Kena-aniy means a Canaanite, an inhabitant of Canaan, and so a trader: Exodus 3:8 promises a land flowing with milk and honey, unto the place of the Canaanites. Joshua 12:8 lists the Canaanites among the peoples of the land. 1 Chronicles 2:3 says Judah's three sons were born of the daughter of Shua the Canaanitess. Zechariah 14:21 says in that day there shall be no more the Canaanite in the house of the LORD of hosts.

Theological Significance

I believe the Canaanite runs through the story as the neighbour, the wife, the trader, and the one who must go: God promised the land, judged its people, and ends with a house where no Canaanite remains. I hold that the promise is bigger than the peoples who stood in its way.
