analambánō

from G303 and G2983; to take up:--receive up, take (in, unto, up).

Biblical Usage

The word means to take up, and it holds both the taking up of Jesus and the taking up of His people: the angels tell the men of Galilee that this same Jesus, taken up from them into heaven, shall so come in like manner; Mark says He was received up into heaven and sat on the right hand of God; Paul says to take unto you the whole armour of God, that you may withstand in the evil day; and the hymn of 1 Timothy says He was received up into glory. The taking up is both His ascension and our equipment.

Theological Significance

I believe the taking up of Christ is the pattern of the Christian life: He was received up into glory, and I am told to take up the armour He left for me. He went up so that I could stand, and the same word that describes His ascension describes my daily taking up of the shield of faith and the sword of the Spirit.
