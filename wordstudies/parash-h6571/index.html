pârâsh

a steed (as stretched out to a vehicle, not single nor for mounting); also (by implication) a driver (in a chariot), i.e. (collectively) cavalry

Biblical Usage

Parash is the horse, the warhorse, and by extension the horseman. Pharaoh's horses chase Israel to the sea in Exodus 14:9, Solomon keeps forty thousand stalls of horses in 1 Kings 4:26, and Elisha cries out at Elijah's departure in 2 Kings 2:12, the chariot of Israel and the horsemen thereof. Joel 2:4 pictures the coming army as horses, running like horsemen into battle.

Theological Significance

I believe the horse is Scripture's picture of human strength, and the lesson runs the whole way through: horses are for the day of battle, but safety is of the Lord. That is why I hold that no army of horsemen ever decided a battle God had already decided.
