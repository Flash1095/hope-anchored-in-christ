nâʼats

to scorn;

Biblical Usage

Na'ats means to scorn, to despise, to provoke, and it is what Israel does to the Lord again and again. In Numbers 14:11 the Lord asks how long this people will provoke Him, and in Deuteronomy 31:20 He says they will turn to other gods and provoke Him when they are full. Psalm 10:3 says the wicked bless the covetous whom the Lord abhors, and Isaiah 1:4 calls them children that have provoked the Holy One of Israel.

Theological Significance

I believe scorn is the sin underneath the other sins, because it treats God as if He does not matter. Israel did not fall in a day; they provoked the Lord a little at a time until the provoking became their life. I hold that the cure is the fear of God, which is the opposite of scorn.
