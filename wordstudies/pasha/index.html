pâshaʻ

to break away (from just authority), i.e. trespass, apostatize, quarrel

Biblical Usage

Pasha means to break away, to transgress, to rebel against just authority. Israel rebels against the house of David in 1 Kings 12:19, and Moab rebels against Israel after the death of Ahab in 2 Kings 1:1 and 2 Kings 3:5. God says in Isaiah 1:2 that He has nourished children and they have rebelled against Him, and in 1 Kings 8:50 Solomon prays that God would forgive His people their transgressions.

Theological Significance

I believe rebellion is the breaking away that names every other sin, and Isaiah's charge is the plainest in the Bible: children nourished by God who turned on Him. That is why I hold that forgiveness is the only answer to rebellion, and Solomon prayed for it before the kingdom ever split.
