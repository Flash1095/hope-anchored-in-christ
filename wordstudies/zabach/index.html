zâbach

to slaughter an animal (usually in sacrifice)

Biblical Usage

Zabach is to slaughter an animal, usually in sacrifice, and it runs through the whole story of worship. In Genesis 31:54 Jacob "offered sacrifice upon the mount" and shared a meal with his brethren. Exodus 3:18 sends Moses to Pharaoh to ask that Israel may go "sacrifice to the LORD our God," and Leviticus 9:4 sets out peace offerings "to sacrifice before the LORD." Even Balak sacrifices in Numbers 22:40, offering oxen and sheep to win Balaam's help.

Theological Significance

I believe sacrifice is the shape of worship before Christ, and every animal on those altars was pointing forward. I hold that Jacob's meal on the mountain in Genesis 31:54 shows sacrifice was never only about death, it was about fellowship with God, and that the sacrifices Israel offered only made sense because a greater sacrifice was coming. My position is that zabach teaches me worship costs something, and God himself paid the cost.
