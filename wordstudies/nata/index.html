nâṭaʻ

properly, to strike in, i.e. fix; specifically, to plant (literally or figuratively)

Biblical Usage

God plants a garden eastward in Eden and puts the man He formed in it, and the psalm asks whether He who planted the ear shall not hear. Jeremiah is set to root out and to plant, and the LORD promises to plant His people on their land and never pull them up again.

Theological Significance

I believe God is a gardener before He is a judge: He plants, He tends, and what He plants He means to keep. My life is planted ground, and He will not uproot what His own hand set.
