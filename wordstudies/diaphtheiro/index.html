diaphtheírō

from G1225 and G5351; to rot thoroughly, i.e. (by implication) to ruin (passively, decay utterly, figuratively, pervert):--corrupt, destroy, perish.

Biblical Usage

To corrupt is to ruin a thing from the inside, and the word covers bodies, minds, and treasures. Paul warns that men of corrupt minds suppose gain is godliness, the outward man perishes while the inward is renewed, the moth corrupts no treasure laid up in heaven, and the destroyers of the earth will themselves be destroyed.

Theological Significance

I believe corruption is the slow work of sin, and the antidote is the daily renewing of the inward man. The body wastes, the mind is pierced by greed, but God renews what the world cannot touch.
