aleíphō

from G1 (as particle of union) and the base of G3045; to oil (with perfume):--anoint.

Biblical Usage

To anoint with oil, and the New Testament pours it out in four directions. The twelve anoint many sick with oil and heal them. A sinful woman weeps on Jesus' feet, wipes them with her hair, kisses them and anoints them. Mary takes a pound of costly spikenard and anoints His feet, and the house fills with the smell; and James tells the church to anoint the sick with oil in the name of the Lord and pray the prayer of faith.

Theological Significance

I believe anointing is the body taking part in the prayer, not magic and not medicine alone. Oil on the skin says what words cannot, that this person belongs to God and is being given back to Him for healing. I hold that James keeps it for the church because the sick should never be prayed for alone — the elders come, the oil is poured, and the Lord is asked to raise them up.
