zaʻaq

a shriek or outcry

Biblical Usage

Za'aq is a cry, a shriek, an outcry that cannot be ignored. Genesis 18:20 says "the cry of Sodom and Gomorrah is great, and because their sin is very grievous." In Nehemiah 5:6 the governor hears the cry of the poor and is very angry, and in Esther 4:1 Mordecai "cried with a loud and a bitter cry" in the city. Job 16:18 pleads, "O earth, cover not thou my blood, and let my cry have no place."

Theological Significance

I believe God hears cries, and this word is the proof. I hold that the cry of Sodom reached him, the cry of the poor moved Nehemiah, and Mordecai's bitter cry in Esther 4:1 was the beginning of deliverance, so no cry of mine is wasted. My position is that za'aq teaches me to cry out rather than go silent, because the Lord who heard these cries still hears.
