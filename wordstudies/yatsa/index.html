yâtsâʼ

to go (causatively, bring) out, in a great variety of applications, literally and figuratively, direct and proxim.

Biblical Usage

Yâtsâ is the word for going out, for coming forth, and it carries the whole Exodus in a single verb. All the hosts of the LORD went out from the land of Egypt on the selfsame day, and God introduces His law by saying He brought them out of the house of bondage. The word also brings Abraham forth to count the stars, and brings Joab's words out of the tabernacle to the king.

Theological Significance

I believe the God who brought Israel out is the God who brings me out, and the Exodus is my story too. My position is that salvation is a going out, out of bondage and into promise, and I cannot stay in Egypt. And I hold that the God who brought Abraham forth to see the stars will bring me forth to see His promise kept.
