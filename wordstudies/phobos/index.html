phóbos

from a primary φέβομαι (to be put in fear); alarm or fright:--be afraid, + exceedingly, fear, terror.

Biblical Usage

Phobos is fear, alarm, fright, and it covers both the terror that drives men from God and the reverence that draws them to Him. John says perfect love casts out fear, because fear has torment, and Paul says believers have not received the spirit of bondage again to fear but the Spirit of adoption. Yet the churches walk in the fear of the Lord, and Paul tells the Philippians to work out their salvation with fear and trembling.

Theological Significance

I believe phobos holds two fears in one word, and I must know which one I am feeling. The fear that has torment is cast out by perfect love, and the fear that works out salvation is the reverence of a child, not the terror of a slave. My position is that the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom because it is the end of every other fear.
