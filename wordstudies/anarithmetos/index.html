anaríthmētos

from G1 (as a negative particle) and a derivative of G705; unnumbered, i.e. without number:--innumerable.

Biblical Usage

The word appears once: 'Therefore sprang there even of one, and him as good as dead, so many as the stars of the sky in multitude, and as the sand which is by the sea shore innumerable.' It is the number that cannot be numbered — Abraham's descendants, from one man as good as dead, as many as the stars and the sand.

Theological Significance

One word for innumerable carries no doctrine of its own; its weight is the promise — from one man as good as dead, a people like the stars and the sand.
