baptízō

from a derivative of G911; to immerse, submerge; to make whelmed (i.e. fully wet); used only (in the New Testament) of ceremonial ablution, especially (technically) of the ordinance of Christian baptism:--Baptist, baptize, wash.

Biblical Usage

Baptizo means to immerse, to dip under completely. It is the word for John's baptism in Jordan, for the baptism Jesus commands His disciples to give in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost, for the three thousand baptized on the day of Pentecost, and for Paul's burial with Christ in baptism.

Theological Significance

I believe baptism is not a sprinkle of religion on a life but a burial and a rising. Paul says we are baptized into Christ's death so we can walk in newness of life, and I hold that every baptism in Scripture points to that same death and that same rising.
