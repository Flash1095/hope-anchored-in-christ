maçgêr

a fastener, i.e. (of a person) a smith, (of a thing) a prison

Biblical Usage

The word names a fastener, and it is used for the smith who works metal and for the prison that holds men. Nebuchadnezzar carried away the craftsmen and smiths of Jerusalem, and Jeremiah lists the carpenters and smiths among those who went into exile. Isaiah says God will bring the prisoners out of the prison, and the psalm cries to bring my soul out of prison, that I may praise God's name.

Theological Significance

I believe the same word holds the smith and the prisoner, because the hands that forge chains are the hands God looses. I hold that He opens the prison house for those who sit in darkness, and the soul He brings out will praise His name.
