antophthalméō

from a compound of G473 and G3788; to face:--bear up into.

Biblical Usage

Paul's ship is caught in the storm and cannot bear up into the wind, so the sailors give way and let her drive. The word means to face into the wind, to hold the ship's head against the gale, and here the wind is too strong for it.

Theological Significance

One word for facing into the wind carries no doctrine of its own; its drama is the storm, that the ship could not bear up and had to let herself drive.
