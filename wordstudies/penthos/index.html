pénthos

strengthened from the alternate of G3958; grief:--mourning, sorrow.

Biblical Usage

Penthos is mourning, sorrow, grief. James tells sinners to let their laughter be turned to mourning, and their joy to heaviness. Babylon says she shall see no sorrow, but her plagues come in one day — death, and mourning, and famine. And the end of the story is the promise: God shall wipe away all tears, and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither any more pain.

Theological Significance

I take it that sorrow has two ends: for Babylon it is judgment, and for God's people it is wiped away. I hold that the mourning James commands is the mourning God comforts, and the sorrow Babylon denies is the sorrow that falls on her anyway. The last word is not mourning but the wiping of tears — and I live toward that day.
