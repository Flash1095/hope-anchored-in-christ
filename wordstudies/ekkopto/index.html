ekkóptō

from G1537 and G2875; to exscind; figuratively, to frustrate:--cut down (off, out), hew down, hinder.

Biblical Usage

To cut off is to hew down, and John the Baptist and Jesus use it for the tree that bears no fruit. Every tree that does not bring forth good fruit is hewn down and cast into the fire, Jesus says, and Paul warns the Romans to behold the goodness and severity of God, because the branches that do not continue in His goodness will be cut off. The axe is already laid to the root, and the word leaves no room for a fruitless tree.

Theological Significance

I believe fruit is the proof of the root, because a tree that bears nothing will be cut down, and a life that bears nothing is in the same danger. I hold that God's severity is as real as His goodness, and I want to continue in both.
