nâkâh

to strike (lightly or severely, literally or figuratively)

Biblical Usage

Nakah means to strike or smite, and it covers everything from a blow in a fight to the wounding of the Servant. In Exodus 2:11 Moses sees an Egyptian smiting a Hebrew, one of his brethren, and in 1 Samuel 17:50 David smites the Philistine with a sling and a stone and slays him. In Isaiah 53:4 the Servant is esteemed stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted, and in Amos 4:9 the Lord says He has smitten Israel with blasting and mildew, yet they have not returned to Him.

Theological Significance

I believe the smiting that matters most is the one in Isaiah 53:4, because the Servant was smitten in our place. Moses struck an Egyptian, David struck a giant, and God struck His own Son so that the smitten would not have to be struck forever. I hold that the wound of Christ is the end of my wounding.
