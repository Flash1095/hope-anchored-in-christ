mâlach

properly, to rub to pieces or pulverize; intransitively, to disappear as dust; to salt whether internally (to season with salt) or externally (to rub with salt)

Biblical Usage

Malach means to salt, to season, to rub with salt, and it also means to vanish away like dust. Leviticus 2:13 commands every meat offering to be seasoned with salt, the salt of the covenant of God, and Exodus 30:35 has the holy perfume tempered together, pure and holy. Ezekiel 16:4 says Jerusalem at her birth was not salted at all, and Isaiah 51:6 says the heavens shall vanish away like smoke and the earth wax old like a garment.

Theological Significance

I believe this word holds covenant and judgment together: salt preserves what God keeps, and the unsalted thing vanishes. My position is that the salt of the covenant in Leviticus 2:13 is the promise behind it all, because what God salts, He keeps.
