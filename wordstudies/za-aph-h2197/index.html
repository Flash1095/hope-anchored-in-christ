zaʻaph

anger

Biblical Usage

Za'aph is anger that boils over into rage. In 2 Chronicles 16:10 King Asa is so wroth with the seer who rebuked him that he throws him into prison. Proverbs 19:12 compares a king's wrath to "the roaring of a lion," and Isaiah 30:30 shows the Lord's own anger falling with "the flame of a devouring fire." Even the sea rages with this word: in Jonah 1:15 the sea "ceased from her raging" once Jonah is cast in.

Theological Significance

I believe anger is the word that shows me what rules a heart. I hold that Asa's rage in 2 Chronicles 16:10 is a warning, because a king who jails the prophet has let anger become his master, and that the only anger in these verses I can trust is God's, which is always just. My position is that za'aph asks me whose anger I am living under: my own, or the Lord's righteous one.
