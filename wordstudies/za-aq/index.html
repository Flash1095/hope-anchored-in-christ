zâʻaq

to shriek (from anguish or danger); by analogy, (as a herald) to announce or convene publicly

Biblical Usage

Za'aq as a verb is what God's people do when they are pressed: they cry out. In Exodus 2:23 the children of Israel "sighed by reason of the bondage, and they cried, and their cry came up unto God." Judges 3:9 shows the pattern: "when the children of Israel cried unto the LORD, the LORD raised up a deliverer." The same word can also mean a public call, as in Joshua 8:16, where all the people of Ai "were called together" to pursue.

Theological Significance

I believe crying out to the Lord is the first act of faith, not the last resort. I hold that Israel's cry in Exodus 2:23 went up to God and he answered with a deliverer, and the same pattern runs through Judges 3:9. My position is that za'aq is the sound of a people who know they cannot save themselves, and that is exactly the sound God answers.
