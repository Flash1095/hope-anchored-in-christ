rhábdos

from the base of G4474; a stick or wand (as a cudgel, a cane or a baton of royalty):--rod, sceptre, staff.

Biblical Usage

Aaron's rod that budded lay in the ark beside the golden pot of manna, and Jacob worshipped leaning upon the top of his staff. The disciples were sent without a staff for their journey, yet the risen Christ rules the nations with a rod of iron, and out of His mouth goes a sharp sword.

Theological Significance

I am convinced that the rod is both the pilgrim's staff and the king's sceptre, and the same God holds them both. Aaron's rod budded because God chose it, Jacob leaned on his staff because he had learned to lean on God, and the rod of iron is the promise that the nations will be ruled rightly at last. I hold that the staff in the hand is the sign of a man who walks by faith.
