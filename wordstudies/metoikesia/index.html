metoikesía

from a derivative of a compound of G3326 and G3624; a change of abode, i.e. (specially), expatriation:--X brought, carried(-ying) away (in-)to.

Biblical Usage

Matthew's genealogy turns on this word three times: the carrying away into Babylon, the generations until the carrying away, and the generations from the carrying away unto Christ. The exile is the hinge of Israel's history, and Matthew counts his generations by it.

Theological Significance

I believe Matthew counts by the exile because it is the wound that proves the promise: fourteen generations to the carrying away, fourteen more to Christ, and the God who sent them away is the God who brought them home. The God who sent them away is the God who brought them home.
