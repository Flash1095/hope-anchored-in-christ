zēlōtḗs

from G2206; a "zealot":--zealous.

Biblical Usage

Zelotes is a zealous person, someone on fire for a cause, and Paul uses the word for himself twice. He tells the crowd in Jerusalem that he was zealous toward God as they all were, and he tells the Galatians he was more exceedingly zealous for the traditions of his fathers than any of his equals. The same word is turned toward the church: Christ gave Himself to purify a people zealous of good works, and Peter asks who will harm you if you are zealous for what is good.

Theological Significance

I believe Paul's story proves zeal can be rescued, because the same fire that drove him to persecute the church was turned by Christ into a fire that built it. Zeal is not the problem; the object of it is. My position is that a people zealous for good works is exactly what Christ died to make, and I want to be counted among them.
