aphrosýnē

from G878; senselessness, i.e. (euphemistically) egotism; (morally) recklessness:--folly, foolishly(-ness).

Biblical Usage

Paul borrows the word to defend himself in 2 Corinthians 11:1, asking the church to bear with him a little in his folly, and in 2 Corinthians 11:17 he speaks as it were foolishly, boasting only because the false apostles have forced his hand. But Jesus puts the word in its proper place in Mark 7:22, listing foolishness among the evils that come out of the heart — thefts, deceit, pride, blasphemy — not a manner of speaking but a moral condition.

Theological Significance

I believe the difference matters: Paul's ‘folly’ is a reluctant boast he hates making, while the folly Jesus names is the recklessness of a heart turned inward. One is a shepherd defending his flock; the other is the very thing that defiles a man.
