korásion

neuter of a presumed derivative of κόρη (a maiden); a (little) girl:--damsel, maid.

Biblical Usage

Korasian is a little girl, a damsel, a maid. Matthew 9:24 has Jesus saying, the maid is not dead, but sleepeth, and Matthew 9:25 has Him taking her by the hand, and the maid arose, Matthew 14:11 has the damsel carrying John's head in a charger to her mother, Mark 5:41 has Him saying, Talitha cumi, damsel, I say unto thee, arise, Mark 5:42 has the damsel arising and walking, for she was of the age of twelve years, and Mark 6:22 has the daughter of Herodias dancing before Herod.

Theological Significance

I believe the damsel is the Bible's picture of resurrection in miniature: twelve years old, dead, and raised by a hand and a word. I hold that the two damsels are the contrast of the gospel, because one arose at His word and the other carried a prophet's head on a charger.
