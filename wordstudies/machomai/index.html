máchomai

middle voice of an apparently primary verb; to war, i.e. (figuratively) to quarrel, dispute:--fight, strive.

Biblical Usage

Machomai means to fight, to quarrel, to strive, and it covers both fists and words. Acts 7:26 shows Moses stepping between two Hebrews as they strove, and John 6:52 has the Jews striving among themselves over Christ's words. James 4:2 says men fight and war because they lust and have not, and 2 Timothy 2:24 commands the servant of the Lord not to strive but to be gentle unto all men.

Theological Significance

I believe the command in 2 Timothy 2:24 is the harder half of this word: not fighting back is itself a kind of fighting, against my own temper. My position is that Moses in Acts 7:26 shows the better use of the word, stepping between fighters instead of joining them.
