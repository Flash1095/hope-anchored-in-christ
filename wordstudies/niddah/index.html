niddâh

properly, rejection; by implication, impurity, especially personal (menstruation) or moral (idolatry, incest)

Biblical Usage

Uncleanness is separation, and the law traces it through childbirth, the ashes of the red heifer, the filthiness carried out of the temple, and the uncleanness of a whole land. The woman is unclean seven days, the water of separation purifies for sin, and Hezekiah's Levites carry the filthiness out of the holy place.

Theological Significance

I believe the law of uncleanness teaches one thing: what is unclean must be carried out, and only God's water of separation can make it clean again. The ashes of the heifer make the water of separation, and the Levites carry the filthiness out of the temple — both of them are the work of cleansing.
