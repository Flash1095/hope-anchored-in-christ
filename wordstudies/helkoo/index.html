helkóō

from G1668; to cause to ulcerate, i.e. (passively) be ulcerous:--full of sores.

Biblical Usage

Helkoo means to be ulcerated, covered with sores, and it appears once, for the beggar at the rich man's gate. In Luke 16:20 Lazarus is laid at the gate full of sores, and the dogs come and lick them, while the rich man feasts inside.

Theological Significance

I believe the sores are the whole point of the parable: the man nobody would touch is the one God carries to Abraham's bosom, and the rich man who never saw him is the one left outside.
