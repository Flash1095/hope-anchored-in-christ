ʼêbel

lamentation

Biblical Usage

Mourning in the Bible is public and physical, with weeping, torn clothes, and days set aside for grief. Esau waits for the days of mourning for his father before he plans his revenge, and all Israel mourns Moses for thirty days. The preacher says the house of mourning is better than the house of feasting, because grief teaches the heart what pleasure never will.

Theological Significance

I believe God does not rush us past grief, because He gave it a place and a season in His people's life. Mourning is honest before Him, and He meets us in it rather than asking us to pretend.
