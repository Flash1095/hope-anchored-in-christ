châçêr

lacking; hence, without

Biblical Usage

Chacer means to lack, to be without, to fail. Achish asks whether he has need of mad men, David curses Joab's house that it will never fail to have one sick or in need, Pharaoh asks Hadad what he has lacked with him, and Proverbs says the adulterer lacks understanding and destroys his own soul.

Theological Significance

I believe lack is the Bible's plainest teacher: what a man lacks shows what he worships. The house that lacks bread, the king who lacks sense, and the heart that lacks understanding all tell the same story.
