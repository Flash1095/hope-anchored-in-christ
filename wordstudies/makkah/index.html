makkâh

a wound; figuratively, carnage, also pestilence

Biblical Usage

Makkah is the wound, the blow, the plague, and it is the word for what sin does to a people and what God heals. Isaiah 1:6 says from the sole of the foot even to the head there is no soundness, but wounds and bruises and putrifying sores, and Jeremiah 30:17 answers, I will restore health unto thee, and I will heal thee of thy wounds. Isaiah 30:26 says the LORD will heal the stroke of their wound, and Zechariah 13:6 asks, what are these wounds in thine hands, and the answer comes, those with which I was wounded in the house of my friends.

Theological Significance

I believe this word runs straight to the cross: the wounds in Isaiah 1:6 are mine, and the wounds in Zechariah 13:6 are His. My position is that Jeremiah 30:17 is the promise that holds them together, because the God who wounds is the God who heals.
