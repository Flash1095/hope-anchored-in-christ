apospáō

from G575 and G4685; to drag forth, i.e. (literally) unsheathe (a sword), or relatively (with a degree of force implied) retire (personally or factiously):--(with-)draw (away), after we were gotten from.

Biblical Usage

The word means to drag or draw away, and it shows up in three directions at once. Men rise up to draw away disciples after them, Jesus withdraws from His disciples about a stone's cast to pray, and Peter draws his sword in Gethsemane. The same pulling motion can serve ambition, prayer, or violence.

Theological Significance

I believe the question is always what is being drawn, and toward whom. Paul warns that some will draw disciples after themselves; Jesus shows the other way, drawing apart to the Father before the cross.
