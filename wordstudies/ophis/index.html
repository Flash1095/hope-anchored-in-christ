óphis

probably from G3700 (through the idea of sharpness of vision); a snake, figuratively, (as a type of sly cunning) an artful malicious person, especially Satan:--serpent.

Biblical Usage

The serpent winds through the whole Bible. In the wilderness Moses lifted up the serpent of brass so that the bitten might look and live, and Jesus said He must be lifted up the same way; Paul feared the Corinthians would be beguiled as Eve was by the serpent, and he gave the disciples authority to tread on serpents.

Theological Significance

What I hold is that the serpent is the Bible's picture of the enemy's cunning, and the cross is the answer: what was lifted up to save in the wilderness points straight to Christ lifted up for me. The serpent's cunning is real, but so is the power given to tread on serpents and scorpions.
