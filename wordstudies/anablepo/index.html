anablépō

from G303 and G991; to look up; by implication, to recover sight:--look (up), see, receive sight.

Biblical Usage

The word does two things: it means to look up, and it means to get your sight back. Jesus looked up to heaven before He broke the bread for the five thousand; the blind man of Jericho heard, 'Receive thy sight: thy faith hath saved thee'; Bartimaeus received his sight and followed Jesus in the way; and the man born blind told the Pharisees, 'A man that is called Jesus made clay, and anointed mine eyes, and I went and washed, and I received sight.'

Theological Significance

I believe the two meanings belong together: looking up to heaven and receiving sight are the same motion. The blind men looked to Jesus and saw; and I hold that is still how it works — sight comes from looking up to Him, not from looking harder at the dark.
