Abraám

of Hebrew origin (H85); Abraham, the Hebrew patriarch:--Abraham. (In Acts 7:16 the text should probably read Jacob.)

Biblical Usage

Abraham is the name God gives Abram in Genesis 17:5 — ‘Neither shall thy name any more be called Abram, but thy name shall be Abraham; for a father of many nations have I made thee.’ From there it is the name of the covenant — the birth of Isaac, the binding on Moriah — and the name Paul reaches for when he writes that Abraham’s faith ‘was counted unto him for righteousness’.

Theological Significance

God renames a childless old man ‘father of many nations’ before he has the son of promise — the name is a prophecy before it is a fact. I believe that is how God works with me too: He names me by what He will make of me, and the name is His promise that He will finish what He began.
