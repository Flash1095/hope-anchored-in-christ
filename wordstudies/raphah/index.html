râphâh

to slacken (in many applications, literal or figurative)

Biblical Usage

Raphah is to slacken, to let go, to be still — in many applications, literal or figurative. God says, 'Be still, and know that I am God: I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth'; the LORD let Moses go when Zipporah circumcised her son; the Gibeonites cried to Joshua, 'Slacken not thy hand from thy servants'; and Moses said, 'He will not fail thee, nor forsake thee.'

Theological Significance

I hold that 'be still' is the heart of the word: the hands that let go are the hands that trust, and the God who says 'be still' is the God who goes with us and will not forsake us. And I believe slackening is not quitting — it is the strength that stops striving and lets God be exalted.
