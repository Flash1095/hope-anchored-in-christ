ʻâbûwr

passed, i.e. kept over; used only of stored grain

Biblical Usage

This word for stored grain — the old corn of the land — occurs only in Joshua 5:11-12. The day after the Passover, Israel eats 'of the old corn of the land... unleavened cakes, and parched corn in the selfsame day. And the manna ceased on the morrow after they had eaten of the old corn of the land.'

Theological Significance

The manna stops the day Israel eats the old corn — forty years of daily bread ends, and the land feeds them instead — and I believe that is the moment the wilderness ends.
