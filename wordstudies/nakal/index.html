nâkal

to defraud, i.e. act treacherously

Biblical Usage

Nakal means to act treacherously, to defraud or deal subtilly, and it is used for brothers, for priests, and for whole peoples. In Genesis 37:18 Joseph's brothers conspire against him before he comes near to them, and in Numbers 25:18 the Midianites vex Israel with their wiles in the matter of Peor. In Psalm 105:25 God turns the Egyptians' hearts to deal subtilly with His servants, and in Malachi 1:14 cursed is the deceiver who has a male in his flock and vows and sacrifices a corrupt thing.

Theological Significance

I believe treachery is the sin that hides behind a smile, because the brothers waited until Joseph was close, and the deceiver brings a corrupt thing to the altar with a vow on his lips. God sees the wiles, and the curse in Malachi 1:14 falls on the one who thought no one was watching. I hold that plain dealing is the only safe dealing.
