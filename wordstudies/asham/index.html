ʼâsham

to be guilty; by implication to be punished or perish

Biblical Usage

To be guilty is to owe God for a sin, whether you knew it or not, and the law will not let you plead ignorance. If the whole congregation sin through ignorance and the thing be hid, yet are they guilty; though he wist it not, yet is he guilty; Hosea says God will go and return to His place till they acknowledge their offence; and the law says when a man or woman shall commit any sin, and be guilty, they shall confess.

Theological Significance

I believe guilt in the Bible is a debt, not a feeling: you can be guilty and not feel it, and you can feel guilty and not be. The debt is real either way, and the only way out of it is the confession the law commands and the offering the law provides, and I hold that Christ paid the debt the law could only point to.
