níptō

to cleanse (especially the hands or the feet or the face); ceremonially, to perform ablution:--wash. Compare G3068.

Biblical Usage

To wash is to cleanse hands, face, and feet — and the Bible is careful about which washing matters. The Pharisees wash their hands by tradition, Jesus tells His disciples to wash their faces when they fast, the blind man washes in Siloam and comes seeing, and the godly widow has washed the saints' feet.

Theological Significance

I believe washing is the picture of a clean life: the hands that serve, the face that does not advertise its fasting, and the feet of the saints washed by love. The blind man washed and came seeing, and the widow washed the saints' feet — the water serves the lowly and the seeing alike.
