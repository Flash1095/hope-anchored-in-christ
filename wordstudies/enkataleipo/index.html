enkataleípō

from G1722 and G2641; to leave behind in some place, i.e. (in a good sense) let remain over, or (in a bad sense) to desert:--forsake, leave.

Biblical Usage

To leave behind — in a good sense, to let remain; in a bad sense, to desert. Paul says we are persecuted but not forsaken in 2 Corinthians 4:9, and he names Demas, who forsook him having loved this present world, in 2 Timothy 4:10. David's prophecy says God will not leave Christ's soul in hell in Acts 2:27, and Hebrews warns us not to forsake assembling together in Hebrews 10:25.

Theological Significance

I believe forsaking is the one thing God never does to His own — men forsake, Demas forsook Paul, but God did not forsake Christ in the grave and He will not forsake me. I hold that the gathering of believers is the visible proof of that promise, and I will not give it up.
