gâg

a roof; by analogy, the top of an altar

Biblical Usage

Gag is the roof, the flat top of a house where men walked, talked, and looked out. Samuel communed with Saul upon the top of the house in 1 Samuel 9:25, and David arose from his bed and walked upon the roof of the king's house in 2 Samuel 11:2, where he saw Bathsheba. The law commands a battlement for the roof in Deuteronomy 22:8, so no one falls from it, and the grass on the housetops withers in 2 Kings 19:26.

Theological Significance

I believe the roof is where a man's heart is tested. David walked on his roof in the evening and fell, and Samuel spoke with Saul on a roof before he was king. I hold that high places are dangerous when the heart is idle.
