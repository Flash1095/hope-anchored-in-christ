dólos

from an obsolete primary verb, δέλλω (probably meaning to decoy; compare G1185); a trick (bait), i.e. (figuratively) wile:--craft, deceit, guile, subtilty.

Biblical Usage

Guile, deceit, the baited trap, the fishhook hidden in the offer. Jesus saw Nathanael and said here is an Israelite in whom there is no guile, and Peter tells believers to lay aside all guile along with malice and hypocrisy. Paul insists his exhortation was not in deceit, even when his enemies charged him with catching them by craft.

Theological Significance

I believe guile is the opposite of the gospel, because the gospel has nothing to hide. A life without guile is the proof that Christ has made me new, and I want to be known for it.
