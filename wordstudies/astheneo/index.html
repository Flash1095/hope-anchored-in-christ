asthenéō

from G772; to be feeble (in any sense):--be diseased, impotent folk (man), (be) sick, (be, be made) weak.

Biblical Usage

To be weak is to be sick in body or feeble in faith, and the word covers both. Lazarus was sick, James says the sick should call for the elders to pray over them, Paul tells the church to receive the weak in faith without doubtful disputations, and he takes pleasure in weaknesses because when he is weak, then he is strong.

Theological Significance

I believe God never treats weakness as a reason to set a person aside, He treats it as a reason to draw near. The church that prays for its sick and makes room for its weak is doing what Christ does, and I hold that His power rests on the people who admit they need it.
