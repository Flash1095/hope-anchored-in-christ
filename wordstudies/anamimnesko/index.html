anamimnḗskō

from G303 and G3403; to remind; (reflexively) to recollect:--call to mind, (bring to, call to, put in), remember(-brance).

Biblical Usage

The word means to call to mind, to remind: the second time the cock crew, Peter called to mind the word Jesus had said to him — before the cock crow twice, you will deny me three times — and he wept; and Paul tells Timothy to stir up the gift of God in him, putting him in remembrance of the laying on of hands. One remembering brings tears, and the other brings courage, and both are the same word.

Theological Significance

A word for remembering carries no doctrine of its own; its weight is the two rememberings — Peter weeping when he called the word to mind, and Timothy stirred up by being put in remembrance.
