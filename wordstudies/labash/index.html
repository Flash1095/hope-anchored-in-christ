lâbash

properly, wrap around, i.e. (by implication) to put on a garment or clothe (oneself, or another), literally or figuratively

Biblical Usage

To be clothed is to be wrapped in a garment, and the word dresses kings, priests, and the first sinners. In Genesis 3:21 God Himself made coats of skins and clothed Adam and Eve, in 1 Kings 22:10 the kings sat "having put on their robes," in 2 Chronicles 5:12 the singers were "arrayed in white linen," and in 2 Samuel 1:24 David remembers Saul, who "clothed you in scarlet, with other delights."

Theological Significance

What convinces me is that clothing in Scripture is always a covering with a meaning: God clothed the fallen pair, kings clothed themselves for battle and for judgment, and the singers clothed themselves in white for worship. God clothed the fallen pair, and He still covers His people.
