rûwaʻ

to mar (especially by breaking); figuratively, to split the ears (with sound), i.e. shout (for alarm or joy)

Biblical Usage

The Hebrew word rûwaʻ means to mar (especially by breaking); figuratively, to split the ears (with sound). In 1 Samuel 4:5, we read, "…great shout, so that the earth rang again"; in 2 Chronicles 13:12, "…priests with sounding trumpets to cry alarm against…". You hear it again in Ezra 3:11 — "…sang together by course in praising and giving…" — and in Hosea 5:8, "…Gibeah, the trumpet in Ramah: cry aloud Beth-aven…".

Theological Significance

I believe rûwaʻ is the Lord's own word, and it shows his people what he is like. I hold that rûwaʻ still speaks, because the God who spoke then has not changed.
