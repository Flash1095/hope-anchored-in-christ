lypéō

from G3077; to distress; reflexively or passively, to be sad:--cause grief, grieve, be in heaviness, (be) sorrow(-ful), be (make) sorry.

Biblical Usage

The word means to cause grief or to be made sorrowful, and it runs through the letters as the cost of honest love. Paul wrote to the Corinthians so that they would not be made sorry, and then rejoiced that their sorrow led to repentance, and he tells the Thessalonians not to sorrow over the dead as others who have no hope. He also warns us not to grieve the Holy Spirit of God, and Peter was grieved when Jesus asked him the third time whether he loved Him.

Theological Significance

I believe grief is not a sign that faith has failed, because even the apostles grieved and the Spirit Himself can be grieved. I hold that the sorrow of God's people has hope woven through it, and the Spirit who can be grieved is the same Spirit who comforts us in it.
