aischýnomai

from αἶσχος (disfigurement, i.e. disgrace); to feel shame (for oneself):--be ashamed.

Biblical Usage

John says abide in Him, that when He appears we may have confidence, and not be ashamed before Him at His coming; Peter says a Christian who suffers, let him not be ashamed; and Paul says twice over that in nothing shall he be ashamed — neither of his authority, which the Lord gave for building up, nor of Christ being magnified in his body, whether by life, or by death. The verb always faces the same choice: hide Christ, or own Him.

Theological Significance

I believe this is the daily test of the Christian life. Shame is the pressure to keep quiet, and the answer is not boldness for its own sake but a clear sight of who Christ is — a Saviour no one need be ashamed of, and a coming no one who abides in Him need fear.
