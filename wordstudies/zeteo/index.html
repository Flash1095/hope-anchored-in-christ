zētéō

of uncertain affinity; to seek (literally or figuratively); specially, (by Hebraism) to worship (God), or (in a bad sense) to plot (against life):--be (go) about, desire, endeavour, enquire (for), require, (X will) seek (after, for, means). Compare G4441.

Biblical Usage

To seek is the word, and Jesus makes it a command and a promise at once: seek first the kingdom of God, seek and you shall find, ask and it shall be given. The same verb covers the seeking that saves and the seeking that kills — the crowds seeking Jesus, the rulers seeking to destroy Him, the Father seeking true worshippers. What a person seeks shows what a person loves.

Theological Significance

I believe seeking is the direction of the heart, not a single act. The Father is seeking worshippers, and the command is to seek Him first, above everything else I might chase. My position is that a life spent seeking the kingdom is never wasted — the promise attached to it is that seeking finds.
