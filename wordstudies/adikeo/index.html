adikéō

from G94; to be unjust, i.e. (actively) do wrong (morally, socially or physically):--hurt, injure, be an offender, be unjust, (do, suffer, take) wrong.

Biblical Usage

Adikeo is the verb for doing wrong or causing hurt — to wrong someone, to injure them, or to suffer wrong yourself. The householder tells the grumbling labourer, 'I do thee no wrong'; Jesus promises His disciples that nothing shall by any means hurt them; Stephen's speech remembers Moses defending a man who suffered wrong; and the risen Christ promises the overcomer shall not be hurt of the second death. The word covers the whole range, from a labour dispute to eternal safety.

Theological Significance

What I take from this word is the promise tucked inside it: those who belong to Christ may be wronged here, but they will not be hurt in the end. The second death has no claim on them, and that is a stronger word than any injury this life can hand out.
