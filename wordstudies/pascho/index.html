páschō

, including the forms πάθω, and πένθω, used only in certain tenses for it apparently a primary verb; to experience a sensation or impression (usually painful):--feel, passion, suffer, vex.

Biblical Usage

Pascho means to suffer, to endure, to experience. Jesus tells His disciples in Matthew 16:21 that He must go to Jerusalem and suffer many things of the elders and chief priests and scribes, and be killed. Luke 24:26 asks, ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and to enter into His glory? Peter says in 1 Peter 2:21 that Christ also suffered for us, leaving us an example, and in 1 Peter 4:1 that he that hath suffered in the flesh hath ceased from sin.

Theological Significance

I believe suffering is not an accident in the gospel, it is the road Christ walked first and the road He calls us to walk after Him. My position is that the suffering that ceases sin is the suffering that follows Him, because He suffered for us and left us the pattern, not just the pardon.
