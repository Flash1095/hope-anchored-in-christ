apōthéomai

or ἀπώθομαι from G575 and the middle voice of ὠθέω or ὤθω (to shove); to push off, figuratively, to reject:--cast away, put away (from), thrust away (from).

Biblical Usage

To thrust away, to push off and reject, is what Stephen says Israel did to Moses, and what Paul says the Jews at Pisidian Antioch did to the word of life. Paul then asks whether God has cast away His people, and he warns Timothy that some have thrust away a good conscience and shipwrecked their faith.

Theological Significance

I hold that rejection has two directions and only one of them is final. Men thrust God's messengers away, but God has not cast away His people, and the warning stands for anyone tempted to push conscience aside.
