ekleípō

from G1537 and G3007; to omit, i.e. (by implication) cease (die):--fail.

Biblical Usage

To fail is to run out, to cease, and the word covers money, faith, and light. Jesus tells His disciples to make friends with the mammon of unrighteousness so that when it fails they will receive us into everlasting habitations, and He tells Peter that He has prayed for him that his faith fail not. The sun is darkened at the cross, and Hebrews says of the Son that His years shall not fail, because He is the same forever.

Theological Significance

I believe my faith can fail, and that is why Jesus prays for it, because Peter's faith was kept by prayer, not by his own strength. I hold that the Son whose years never fail is the one who keeps me from failing.
