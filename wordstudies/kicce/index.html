kiççêʼ

properly, covered, i.e. a throne (as canopied)

Biblical Usage

Kisse is the throne, the covered seat of a king. Genesis 41:40 has Pharaoh telling Joseph that only in the throne will he be greater than him, 2 Samuel 3:10 promises the throne of David over Israel and Judah, and 1 Kings 10:18 describes Solomon's great throne of ivory overlaid with gold. Isaiah 6:1 lifts the word past every earthly king: in the year king Uzziah died, Isaiah saw the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up. Jeremiah 17:12 calls it a glorious high throne from the beginning, the place of God's sanctuary, and Psalms 103:19 says the LORD hath prepared his throne in the heavens, and his kingdom ruleth over all.

Theological Significance

I believe every throne in the Bible is measured against the one in heaven. I hold that Pharaoh's throne and Solomon's throne and David's throne all pass away, and the throne that stands is the one Isaiah saw, high and lifted up, where the Lord still sits.
