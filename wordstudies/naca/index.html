nâçaʻ

properly, to pull up, especially the tent-pins, i.e. start on a journey

Biblical Usage

Nasa means to pull up the tent-pins and set out on a journey, and it is the great travel word of the Exodus. In Genesis 12:9 Abram journeyed, going on still toward the south, and in Exodus 13:20 Israel took their journey from Succoth and encamped in Etham. In Numbers 10:12 the children of Israel took their journeys out of the wilderness of Sinai, and in Exodus 12:37 the children of Israel journey from Rameses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand on foot.

Theological Significance

I believe the journey is the shape of the life of faith, because God rarely lets His people settle where they start. Abram pulled up his tent, Israel pulled up theirs forty years running, and the cloud told them when to move. I hold that a Christian is a traveller, and the tent-pins are never in for good.
