agriélaios

from G66 and G1636; an oleaster:--olive tree (which is) wild.

Biblical Usage

Agrielaios is the wild olive tree — the uncultivated olive, growing where it was not planted — and it appears twice, both in Paul's picture of Israel and the Gentiles. The Gentiles were a wild olive branch, grafted in among the natural branches, partaking of the root and fatness of the olive tree; and Paul says if God could graft the wild branch in against nature, He can graft the natural branches back in again.

Theological Significance

A wild branch grafted in among the natural ones is Paul's picture of us, and this word only says the tree was wild — the grafting is the gospel.
