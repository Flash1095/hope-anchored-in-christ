epangéllō

from G1909 and the base of G32; to announce upon (reflexively), i.e. (by implication) to engage to do something, to assert something respecting oneself:--profess, (make) promise.

Biblical Usage

To promise — to announce and engage to do it. Abraham was fully persuaded that what God had promised He was able also to perform in Romans 4:21, God who cannot lie promised eternal life before the world began in Titus 1:2, and we hold fast because He who promised is faithful in Hebrews 10:23. The crown of life is promised to those who love Him in James 1:12.

Theological Significance

I believe God's promises are only as good as His power and His truth, and He has both — He cannot lie, and He is able to perform. I hold that Abraham's persuasion is the pattern for my faith: fully persuaded, not half-hoping, that what He promised He will do.
