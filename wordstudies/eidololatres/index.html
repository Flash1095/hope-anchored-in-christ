eidōlolátrēs

from G1497 and the base of G3000; an image- (servant or) worshipper (literally or figuratively):--idolater.

Biblical Usage

An idolater is one who serves images, and Paul puts the word in his hardest lists. He tells the Corinthians not to keep company with a brother who is an idolater, says idolaters will not inherit the kingdom of God, and warns the Ephesians that no covetous man, who is an idolater, has any inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and of God. The word covers both the temple worshipper and the greedy heart, because both bow to something other than God.

Theological Significance

I believe covetousness is idolatry with a respectable name, because it bows the heart to getting instead of to God. My position is that the kingdom has no room for a divided heart, and mine must belong wholly to Him.
