châqâh

to carve; by implication, to delineate; also to entrench

Biblical Usage

Chaqah is carving and delineating, and it is used for beauty and for idolatry alike. Solomon carved cherubims and palm trees and open flowers on the temple doors and overlaid them with gold. Ezekiel saw every form of creeping things and abominable beasts pourtrayed on the wall of the temple, and Aholibah saw images of the Chaldeans pourtrayed with vermilion. Job says God set a print upon the heels of his feet.

Theological Significance

I believe carving shows what a people love: Solomon carved heaven's creatures in gold, and apostate Israel carved creeping things on the wall. The same skill serves worship or idolatry. I hold that my own hands will carve something, and the question is only what image I am tracing.
