katapínō

from G2596 and G4095; to drink down, i.e. gulp entire (literally or figuratively):--devour, drown, swallow (up).

Biblical Usage

To drink down is to swallow whole, and death is swallowed up in victory, and our mortality is swallowed up of life, while the blind guides strain at a gnat and swallow a camel. Peter warns that the devil walks about as a roaring lion seeking whom he may devour. The word holds both the victory and the danger: what swallows what decides everything.

Theological Significance

I believe death has been swallowed by life, and that is the victory I stand in. I hold that the lion is still looking for someone to swallow, so I will stay sober and watchful, and I will not be the one he finds asleep.
