nâbêl

to wilt; generally, to fall away, fail, faint; figuratively, to be foolish or (morally) wicked; causatively, to despise, disgrace

Biblical Usage

Nabel means to wilt or wither, to fall away and fail, and it is the word for grass in the sun and for a leaf in autumn. In Isaiah 40:7 and Isaiah 40:8 the grass withereth and the flower fadeth, but the word of our God shall stand for ever, and in Psalm 1:3 the righteous man's leaf shall not wither. In Jeremiah 8:13 the leaf shall fade and the things God gave shall pass away from the faithless.

Theological Significance

I believe the withering grass is the Bible's gentlest warning and its firmest comfort at once. Everything green fades, and that is exactly why the word that does not fade matters so much. I hold that my life is grass, and His word is the only thing in it that will not wither.
