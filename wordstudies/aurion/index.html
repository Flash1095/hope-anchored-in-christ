aúrion

from a derivative of the same as G109 (meaning a breeze, i.e. the morning air); properly, fresh, i.e. (adverb with ellipsis of G2250) to-morrow:--(to-)morrow, next day.

Biblical Usage

Tomorrow is the word for the day that has not come, and the Bible treats it with both warning and patience. The rulers gathered on the morrow to judge Peter and John, James says to those who plan their business for tomorrow, ye know not what shall be on the morrow, Jesus says take no thought for the morrow, and Paul quotes the saying, let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die.

Theological Significance

I believe tomorrow belongs to God, and the two errors are to presume on it and to despair of it. The rulers' tomorrow brought a bold witness, the merchants' tomorrow was a vapour, and I hold that the only safe plan for tomorrow is the one that says, if the Lord will.
