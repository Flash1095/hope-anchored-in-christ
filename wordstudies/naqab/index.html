nâqab

to puncture, literally (to perforate, with more or less violence) or figuratively (to specify, designate, libel)

Biblical Usage

Naqab means to pierce or to bore, and in its sharpest use it means to blaspheme, to pierce the Name with a curse. In Leviticus 24:11 the Israelitish woman's son blasphemes the name of the Lord and curses, and is brought to Moses, and in Leviticus 24:16 whoever blasphemes the name of the Lord shall surely be put to death. In Numbers 23:8 Balaam asks how he shall curse whom God has not cursed, and in 2 Kings 18:21 the staff of Egypt is a bruised reed that will pierce the hand of any man who leans on it.

Theological Significance

I believe the word pierces in both directions, because a curse pierces the Name and a false trust pierces the hand. The blasphemer is stoned because words against God are not small, and the reed is a warning that leaning on Egypt cuts. I hold that my mouth should bless the Name it once could have cursed.
