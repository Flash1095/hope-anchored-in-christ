kakopoiéō

from G2555; to be a bad-doer, i.e. (objectively) to injure, or (genitive) to sin:--do(ing) evil.

Biblical Usage

Kakopoieo is to do evil — to be a bad-doer. Jesus asks in the synagogue whether it is lawful on the Sabbath to do good or to do evil; and Peter says it is better, if the will of God be so, that ye suffer for well doing than for evil doing; and John warns, he that doeth evil hath not seen God.

Theological Significance

I believe the choice is put to us every day, and the Sabbath question is the daily question: to do good or to do evil. Suffering for doing good is better than suffering for doing evil — and doing evil is the surest proof a man has not seen God.
