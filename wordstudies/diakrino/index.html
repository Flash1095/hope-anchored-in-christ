diakrínō

from G1223 and G2919; to separate thoroughly, i.e. (literally and reflexively) to withdraw from, or (by implication) oppose; figuratively, to discriminate (by implication, decide), or (reflexively) hesitate:--contend, make (to) differ(-ence), discern, doubt, judge, be partial, stagger, waver.

Biblical Usage

To discern is to separate one thing from another, and the word covers both good judgment and doubting faith. James warns the doubter who wavers like a wave, Paul says the man who doubts over his food is condemned, and Jude tells the church to make a difference between the doubting and the hardened.

Theological Significance

I believe discernment is the gift of seeing what is truly there. It separates truth from error and faith from wavering, and it is the gift I need most when my own heart is divided.
