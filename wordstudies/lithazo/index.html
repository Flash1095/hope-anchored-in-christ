litházō

from G3037; to lapidate:--stone.

Biblical Usage

litházō means to stone — and its range, from the ordinary to the holy, is the point. In Acts 14:19, "having stoned Paul"; and in John 10:31, "Then the Jews took up stones again to stone him.". In 2 Corinthians 11:25, "once was I stoned"; and in Hebrews 11:37, "They were stoned".

Theological Significance

I believe stoning is the price of telling the truth — Paul was stoned and left for dead, and the Jews took up stones against Jesus for His good works. I hold that the heroes of faith were stoned, sawn asunder, and slain, and still they did not turn back. My position is that I will speak the truth even if stones are the answer.
