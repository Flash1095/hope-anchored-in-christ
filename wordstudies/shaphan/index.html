shâphân

Shaphan, the name of a scribe; also a species of rockrabbit (probably the hyrax)

Biblical Usage

Shaphan the scribe carried the book of the law to King Josiah and read it before him, after Hilkiah found it in the house of the LORD. Shaphan's son Gemariah had a chamber in the temple where Baruch read the words of Jeremiah, and Shaphan's sons Ahikam and Elasah stood by Jeremiah and carried his letters. The same spelling also names the coney, the rock-dwelling animal the law counts unclean.

Theological Significance

I hold that a scribe who reads the book aloud can change a kingdom, because Josiah tore his clothes when he heard it. Shaphan's house served the word for three generations, and the book they carried is the same book I read today.
