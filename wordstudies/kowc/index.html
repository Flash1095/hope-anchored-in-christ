kôwç

a cup (as a container), often figuratively, a lot (as if a potion); also some unclean bird, probably an owl (perhaps from the cup-like cavity of its eye)

Biblical Usage

The cup is first an ordinary vessel — Pharaoh's cup in the butler's hand in Genesis 40:11, the great sea's brim wrought like the brim of a cup in 1 Kings 7:26 — and then it becomes a picture of what God gives a person to drink, for good or for ill. David says "my cup runneth over" in Psalms 23:5, the psalmist lifts "the cup of salvation" in Psalms 116:13, and Ezekiel 23:31 warns Jerusalem she will drink her sister's cup of judgment.

Theological Significance

In my reading, the cup is one of Scripture's plainest pictures of a lot in life: what God pours for us, whether blessing or judgment, is the portion we must drink, and Christ drank the bitter cup so that mine could run over. That is why I lift the cup of salvation and call on the name of the Lord.
