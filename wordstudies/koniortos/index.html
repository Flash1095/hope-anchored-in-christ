koniortós

from the base of G2867 and ὄρνυμι (to "rouse"); pulverulence (as blown about):--dust.

Biblical Usage

Koniortos is dust, the fine powder blown about. Matthew 10:14 and Luke 9:5 command the disciples to shake off the dust of their feet against the house or city that will not receive them, Luke 10:11 has them wiping off even the dust of the city that cleaveth to them, Acts 13:51 shows Paul and Barnabas shaking off the dust of their feet against Iconium, and Acts 22:23 has the crowd casting off their clothes and throwing dust into the air.

Theological Significance

I believe the shaken dust is a testimony, not a tantrum: the disciples leave the dust of the rejecting city behind, and the city keeps its dust. I hold that the crowd throwing dust in Acts 22:23 is the same dust turned to rage, because men who will not hear the word throw what they have at the man who speaks it.
