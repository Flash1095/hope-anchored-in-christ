nýmphē

from a primary but obsolete verb νύπτω (to veil as a bride; compare Latin " nupto," to marry); a young married woman (as veiled), including a betrothed girl; by implication, a son's wife:--bride, daughter in law.

Biblical Usage

Nymphe is a bride, a young married woman, veiled, and by extension a daughter-in-law. Jesus uses it of family division in Matthew 10:35 and Luke 12:53, where daughter-in-law stands against mother-in-law, and John the Baptist uses it at its height in John 3:29: he that hath the bride is the bridegroom. Revelation 18:23 turns the word into judgment, when the voice of the bridegroom and the bride is heard no more in fallen Babylon.

Theological Significance

I believe the bride is the Bible's picture of the people God loves, and John the Baptist had it right: the friend of the bridegroom rejoices to hear his voice. My position is that the church is the bride, and her joy is not in herself but in the Bridegroom who has her.
