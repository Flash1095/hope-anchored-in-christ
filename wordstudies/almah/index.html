ʻalmâh

a lass (as veiled or private)

Biblical Usage

A young woman, a lass, veiled or private — and the word carries the whole story of the virgin birth in one verse. Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel. But the word is older than Isaiah: Rebekah's servant meets a damsel at the well, Pharaoh's daughter sends a maid to call the child's mother, and the Song counts threescore queens and fourscore concubines and virgins without number. The word means a young unmarried woman, and the question the centuries argued is whether Isaiah meant more than that.

Theological Significance

I believe Isaiah meant more, and Matthew tells me I am right to read it so: the virgin is Mary, and the child is God with us. The word itself is ordinary, a young woman at a well, a maid by the river, a damsel among the queens — and I hold that is exactly how God works, taking the ordinary word and filling it with the one birth that changes everything.
