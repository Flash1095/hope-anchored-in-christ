taʻâb

to loathe, i.e. (morally) detest

Biblical Usage

Moses warns Israel not to bring an abominable thing into the house, lest they become a cursed thing like it. Job says his closest friends abhor him; Micah charges the rulers with abhorring judgment; Ahab, we are told, did very abominably in following idols. The psalmist says he hates every false way, and Amos says the people hate the one who rebukes in the gate. The word covers both sides: God abhors idolatry, and men abhor God, His servants, and His truth.

Theological Significance

I hold that abhorrence is a moral sense, and it can point the wrong way. I believe the psalmist shows the right direction: hate the false way, and love the law. When a people starts hating the rebuke instead, Amos says the end is near.
