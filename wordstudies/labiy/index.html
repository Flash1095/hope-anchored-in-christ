lâbîyʼ

to roar; a lion (properly, a lioness as the fiercer (although not a roarer;))

Biblical Usage

The lion is the king of beasts in Scripture, and this word is the lioness and the old lion together. Judah "is a lion's whelp" in Genesis 49:9, Gad "dwelleth as a lion" in Deuteronomy 33:20, the lioness nourishes her whelps among young lions in Ezekiel 19:2, and the enemy's roaring is "like a lion" in Isaiah 5:29.

Theological Significance

I believe the lion is the picture of strength that can bless or destroy: Judah's lion is the king who saves, and the roaring lion of the enemy is the one who devours, and I need to know which lion I am following. I follow the lion who saves, not the one who devours.
