lâqaq

to lick or lap

Biblical Usage

lâqaq means to lick or lap — an ordinary word that carries real weight in the Bible. In Judges 7:5, "Every one that lappeth of the water with his tongue"; and in Judges 7:7, "By the three hundred men that lapped will I save you". In 1 Kings 21:19, "In the place where dogs licked the blood of Naboth shall dogs lick thy blood"; and in 1 Kings 22:38, "and the dogs licked up his blood".

Theological Significance

I believe God sees what lapping dogs and licking tongues reveal — the small, unguarded habits that show a person's heart. I hold that the same lapping that chose Gideon's three hundred also pronounced judgment on Ahab's house. Nothing about us is too small for God to notice, and nothing He notices is without meaning.
