pâṭar

to cleave or burst through, i.e. (causatively) to emit, whether literal or figurative (gape)

Biblical Usage

Patar means to cleave, to burst through, to let out. David escapes from Saul in 1 Samuel 19:10 when Saul smites at the wall with his javelin and David slips away. The psalmist says in Psalm 22:7 that they shoot out the lip at him, they shake the head, and Proverbs 17:14 says the beginning of strife is as when one letteth out water, so leave off contention before it be meddled with.

Theological Significance

I believe the letting out is the picture in all three verses: David let out of danger, scorn let out of mocking lips, and strife let out like water through a breach. My position is that what is let out cannot be called back, so the wisdom is to keep the breach shut before the water runs.
