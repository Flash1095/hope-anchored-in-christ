laktízō

from adverb λάξ (heelwise); to recalcitrate:--kick.

Biblical Usage

To kick appears once, and it is the voice of Christ to Saul on the Damascus road in Acts 26:14: "Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me? it is hard for thee to kick against the pricks." The pricks are the goad, and the kicking is the ox fighting the hand that drives it.

Theological Significance

For me, the goad is the mercy in the verse: Saul was kicking against Christ and hurting only himself, and the voice that stopped him was the voice that saved him.
