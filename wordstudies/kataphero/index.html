kataphérō

from G2596 and G5342 (including its alternate); to bear down, i.e. (figuratively) overcome (with drowsiness); specially, to cast a vote:--fall, give, sink down.

Biblical Usage

To bear down is to sink under the weight, and Eutychus sank into a deep sleep and fell from the third loft, and the Jews laid many grievous complaints against Paul which they could not prove, and Paul himself had once cast the saints into prison with authority. The word carries the weight of sleep, of accusation, and of persecution, and all three press down hard.

Theological Significance

I believe the weight that bore Eutychus down was lifted by Paul's prayer, and the weight of false charges falls the same way. I hold that no accusation can stand against a man God has cleared, and no sleep is too deep for the Lord to wake.
