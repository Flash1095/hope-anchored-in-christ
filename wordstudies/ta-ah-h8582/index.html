tâʻâh

to vacillate, i.e. reel or stray (literally or figuratively); also causative of both

Biblical Usage

Isaiah says all we like sheep have gone astray, every one to his own way. Joseph's brothers find him wandering in the field at Shechem; Jeremiah says God's people have been lost sheep, led astray by their shepherds; Proverbs says those who devise evil err, and Ezekiel charges the Levites who went astray after idols. The word is wandering with a moral edge: straying from the path, from the shepherd, and from God.

Theological Significance

I hold that going astray is the Bible's plainest picture of sin. I believe Isaiah 53:6 says it best — we have all done it, every one to his own way — and that is exactly why the Lord laid on Him the iniquity of us all.
