skýllō

apparently a primary verb; to flay, i.e. (figuratively) to harass:--trouble(self).

Biblical Usage

Twice the word is spoken to a father whose daughter has just died: trouble not the Master any further. The same word describes the crowds Jesus saw, fainted and scattered abroad as sheep having no shepherd, worn out and harassed with no one to care for them.

Theological Significance

I believe the word shows what trouble really does: it wears people down until they scatter, and the Lord who sees the scattered sheep is the one who gathers them.
