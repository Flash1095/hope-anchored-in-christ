mataióō

from G3152; to render (passively, become) foolish, i.e. (morally) wicked or (specially), idolatrous:--become vain.

Biblical Usage

The word means to become vain, and Paul uses it once, of people who knew God but would not honour Him: their thinking went empty, and their foolish hearts were darkened. It is the picture of a mind that trades the truth of God for its own imaginations.

Theological Significance

I believe this is how idolatry starts, not with a carved image but with a mind that stops thanking God, and once the thinking goes vain the heart follows.
