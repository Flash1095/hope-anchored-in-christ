ʼÂçâph

Asaph, the name of three Israelites, and of the family of the first

Biblical Usage

Asaph is the chief musician of David’s tabernacle — a Levite set over the songs, the cymbals, and the psalms. His name heads twelve psalms, from the great Psalm 73 to the laments of 74 through 83; his sons sing at the return from exile.

Theological Significance

Asaph sings the hardest psalms in the book — 'my feet were almost gone' when he envied the wicked — and he sings his way back to God. I believe that is why his name heads so many psalms: worship is not only for the certain, it is for the doubting too, and the way back is through the sanctuary, not around it.
