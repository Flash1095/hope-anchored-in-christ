allóphylos

from G243 and G5443; foreign, i.e. (specially) Gentile:--one of another nation.

Biblical Usage

Once, when Peter explains himself to Cornelius: Ye know how that it is an unlawful thing for a man that is a Jew to keep company, or come unto one of another nation; but God hath shewed me that I should not call any man common or unclean. The word is the foreigner, the man of another tribe, and the sentence that breaks the wall down.

Theological Significance

One word for a foreigner carries no doctrine of its own; its weight is the vision behind it, that God showed Peter the sheet from heaven so the gospel could cross into a Gentile house.
