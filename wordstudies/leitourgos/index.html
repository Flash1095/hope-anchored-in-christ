leitourgós

from a derivative of G2992 and G2041; a public servant, i.e. a functionary in the Temple or Gospel, or (genitive case) a worshipper (of God) or benefactor (of man):--minister(-ed).

Biblical Usage

leitourgós means minister(-ed) — and its range, from the ordinary to the holy, is the point. In Hebrews 8:2, "A minister of the sanctuary"; and in Romans 15:16, "That I should be the minister of Jesus Christ to the Gentiles". In Philippians 2:25, "and he that ministered to my wants."; and in Romans 13:6, "For for this cause pay ye tribute also: for they are God's ministers".

Theological Significance

I believe a minister is a public servant of God, not a master of men — Christ is the minister of the true tabernacle, and Paul was His minister to the Gentiles. I hold that the same word covers the priest, the apostle, and even the tax collector, because all of them serve God's purpose. My position is that I am a servant first, and any honour I receive is borrowed from the Master.
