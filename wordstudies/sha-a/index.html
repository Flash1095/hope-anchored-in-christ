shâʻaʻ

(in a good acceptation) to look upon (with complacency), i.e. fondle, please or amuse (self); (in a bad one) to look about (in dismay), i.e. stare

Biblical Usage

Sha'a' is delighting, playing, and also staring in a daze — the same word holds both. The psalmist says it plainly: in the multitude of my thoughts within me Thy comforts delight my soul. Isaiah gives the other side of the delight: the sucking child shall play on the hole of the asp, and the weaned child shall put his hand on the cockatrice' den. And the daze is there too: shut their eyes, lest they see, and stay yourselves, and wonder — drunken, but not with wine.

Theological Significance

I believe the word shows what delight does and what daze does: delight opens the soul to God's comforts, and the child plays safe where the serpent lives, because the Lord is there. But the daze shuts the eyes and staggers a whole people. And I hold that the difference is the Lord — delight in Him is safety, and staring past Him is judgment.
