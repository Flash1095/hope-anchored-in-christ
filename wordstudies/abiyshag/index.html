ʼĂbîyshag

Abishag, a concubine of David

Biblical Usage

Abishag the Shunammite is the young woman brought to cherish King David in his old age — 'a very fair damsel' who ministers to him and lies in his bosom, 'but the king knew her not.' After David's death Adonijah asks for her as wife, and Solomon treats the request as a claim on the throne and has him put to death.

Theological Significance

A servant girl caught between two princes, and her body becomes a claim on a kingdom. I believe Abishag's story shows how power uses people — Adonijah wanted her as a step to the throne, and Solomon saw through it — and how a woman with no voice in the matter still ends up at the centre of the kingdom's politics.
