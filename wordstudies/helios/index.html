hḗlios

from ἕλη (a ray; perhaps akin to the alternate of G138); the sun; by implication, light:--+ east, sun.

Biblical Usage

Helios is the sun, and the Bible uses it for God's common grace, his glory, and the end of days. In Matthew 5:45 God makes his sun rise on the evil and on the good, in Matthew 17:2 the face of Jesus shines as the sun on the mountain, in Revelation 21:23 the city has no need of the sun because the glory of God lightens it, and in Acts 2:20 the sun shall be turned into darkness before the great day of the Lord.

Theological Significance

I believe the sun is the most honest picture in the Bible of how God works: it rises on everyone, it shines from the face of Christ, and one day it will not be needed because the light it borrowed will be there in person. I hold that the day the sun goes dark is not the end of light, it is the beginning of the light that needs no sun.
