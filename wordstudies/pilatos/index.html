Pilâtos

of Latin origin; close-pressed, i.e. firm; Pilatus, a Roman:--Pilate.

Biblical Usage

Pilate was the Roman governor who judged Jesus and found no fault in Him, yet handed Him over. He asked, What is truth, in John 18:38, and then answered his own question with a scourging. He washed his hands in Matthew 27:24, saying he was innocent of the blood of this just person, and wrote the title on the cross in John 19:22: What I have written I have written.

Theological Significance

I hold that Pilate is the warning of a man who knew the truth, said it aloud, and still did the wrong thing. His life is told on his People profile.
