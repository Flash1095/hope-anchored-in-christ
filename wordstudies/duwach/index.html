dûwach

to thrust away; figuratively, to cleanse

Biblical Usage

To rinse, to wash, the washing of the sacrifices in the temple, where the lavers stood to rinse the burnt offering, and the chambers where the offering was washed. Isaiah promises the Lord will wash away the filth of the daughters of Zion, and Nebuchadnezzar is charged with devouring Israel like a dragon and rinsing her out.

Theological Significance

I believe washing is God's way of making the unclean clean, and the temple lavers pointed to the washing He would do Himself. The filth of Zion is washed by the spirit of judgment and burning, and I am washed in the same way.
