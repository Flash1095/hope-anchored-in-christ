kârath

to cut (off, down or asunder); by implication, to destroy or consume; specifically, to covenant (i.e. make an alliance or bargain, originally by cutting flesh and passing between the pieces)

Biblical Usage

Karath is to cut — to cut off, to cut down, and so to cut a covenant. God cut a covenant with Abram when the smoking furnace and burning lamp passed between the pieces; Moses sprinkled the blood and said, behold the blood of the covenant; and the psalm says, I have made a covenant with my chosen, I have sworn unto David my servant.

Theological Significance

I believe the cutting is the point: a covenant is not a handshake but a sacrifice, sealed with blood and bound by an oath. God cut His covenant with Abram and with David, and in Christ He cut the new covenant in His own blood — and that is why it cannot be broken.
