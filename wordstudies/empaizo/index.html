empaízō

from G1722 and G3815; to jeer at, i.e. deride:--mock.

Biblical Usage

To mock is to jeer at someone as if they were nothing. Herod felt mocked by the wise men and answered with slaughter in Matthew 2:16, Jesus says He will be mocked and spitefully entreated in Luke 18:32, and Mark records the same prediction in Mark 10:34. The word also warns ordinary people: the builder who cannot finish is mocked by all who see it in Luke 14:29.

Theological Significance

I believe mockery is the weapon of the proud against the lowly, and the Lord took it on Himself before He asked us to bear it. I hold that when I am mocked for following Him, I am standing where He stood — and He rose on the third day.
