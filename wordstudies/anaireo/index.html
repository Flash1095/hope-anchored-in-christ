anairéō

from G303 and (the active of) G138; to take up, i.e. adopt; by implication, to take away (violently), i.e. abolish, murder:--put to death, kill, slay, take away, take up.

Biblical Usage

The word means to take up — and to take away. Pharaoh's daughter took Moses up when he was cast out and nourished him for her own son, and Hebrews says Christ takes away the first covenant that He may establish the second. In Acts the word is mostly used for killing — Herod slew the children, the Jews sought to kill Paul — but the taking up and the taking away are the same motion.

Theological Significance

A word that means both taking up and taking away carries no doctrine of its own; its weight is the two takings — a baby lifted from the river, and an old covenant lifted away so the new one can stand.
