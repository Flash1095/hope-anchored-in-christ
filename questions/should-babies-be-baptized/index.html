Should babies be baptized?

Question
Should babies be baptized?
Short Answer
Christians disagree on this question, and both views are held by faithful, Bible-loving churches. Some baptize infants as children of believing parents under the new covenant; others baptize only those old enough to profess faith themselves. What all agree on is that baptism matters and that salvation is by grace through faith in Christ.
Detailed Answer

Churches that baptize infants point to the covenant pattern of Scripture: in the Old Testament, children received the covenant sign of circumcision (Genesis 17), and in the New Testament whole households were baptized (Acts 16:31-34). They see baptism as God's sign and seal of His covenant promise, applied to children of believers, with the expectation that the child will later own the faith personally. Churches that practice believers' baptism point to the pattern of the New Testament, where baptism follows repentance and faith: 'those who accepted his message were baptized' (Acts 2:41), and the Great Commission joins making disciples with baptizing them (Matthew 28:19-20). They argue that baptism is a believer's public profession and picture of union with Christ (Romans 6:3-4). Because godly Christians read these passages differently, this is a question to work through with your church, your conscience, and your Bible open — with grace toward those who land differently, and with the shared conviction that it is Christ, not the timing of baptism, who saves.
