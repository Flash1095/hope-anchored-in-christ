How is the new covenant different from the old?

How is the new covenant different from the old?

Short Answer

The new covenant fulfills the old: where the old covenant was written on stone and mediated through priests and repeated sacrifices, the new covenant is written on hearts, sealed by Christ's once-for-all sacrifice, and lived by the power of the Holy Spirit.

Detailed Answer

Jeremiah looked forward to a day when God would make a new covenant, 'not like the covenant I made with their ancestors' (Jeremiah 31:31-32): He would put His law in their minds and write it on their hearts, and 'I will forgive their wickedness and will remember their sins no more.' Hebrews explains how Jesus brings this about. The old covenant, good and God-given as it was, could expose sin but could not finally remove it — its sacrifices had to be repeated, and its priests kept dying (Hebrews 7; 10:1-4). The new covenant has a better mediator and a better sacrifice: Jesus, who offered Himself once for all and now intercedes for His people forever (Hebrews 8:6; 9:11-14). That does not mean the old covenant was a mistake — it was God's tutor, preparing His people for Christ (Galatians 3:24). But under the new covenant, forgiveness is complete, access to God is open, and the Spirit empowers the obedience the law commanded. The old said, 'Do this and live'; the new says, 'It is finished — now live in what Christ has done.'
