How should Christians handle conflict?

Question
How should Christians handle conflict?
Short Answer
Christians should handle conflict humbly and promptly: examine your own heart, go directly and gently to the person, seek reconciliation, and involve the church only if needed. The goal is restoration and witness, not winning.
Detailed Answer

Conflict is inevitable wherever sinners, even redeemed ones, live and work together. The question is whether we will handle it Jesus' way. His pattern in Matthew 18 begins with humility and directness: first examine the log in your own eye, then go to your brother or sister privately and gently, with restoration, not victory, as the goal. Most conflicts die a merciful death at this first, smallest step when it is taken early, before bitterness recruits an audience. If private conversation fails, involve one or two wise others, and only then, for serious unrepentant sin, the church's care. Paul adds the atmosphere: be kind, tenderhearted, forgiving, pursue what makes for peace, and as far as it depends on you, live peaceably with all. That last phrase gives honest relief. Reconciliation takes two, and you are responsible for your obedience, not for controlling another's response. Some conflicts need mediators, pastors, or counselors, and seeking help is strength, not failure. Above all, remember the watching world reads our conflicts as a commentary on our gospel. A community that confesses, forgives, and reconciles preaches Christ more loudly than one that never disagrees. Anchor disputes in the cross, where God reconciled His enemies, and let that mercy set your tone.
