What happened at the Fall?

Question
What happened at the Fall?
Short Answer
The Fall is humanity's first rebellion against God: Adam and Eve, tempted by the serpent, disobeyed God's one command and ate from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil (Genesis 3). That single act broke humanity's relationship with God and brought sin and death into the world.
Detailed Answer
Genesis 3 tells the story plainly. God had placed Adam and Eve in a good garden with freedom to enjoy everything except one tree. The serpent twisted God's words — 'Did God really say?' — and promised that disobedience would make them like God. They ate, and immediately everything changed: shame replaced innocence, blame replaced honesty, and they hid from the God who had walked with them. God's judgments followed: pain in childbearing and work, conflict between man and woman, and death itself — 'for dust you are and to dust you will return' (Genesis 3:19). The garden was closed, and humanity was sent out. But even in judgment, God gave hope: He promised that the woman's offspring would one day crush the serpent's head (Genesis 3:15), a promise Christians see fulfilled in Jesus. Paul explains the reach of the Fall: 'sin entered the world through one man, and death through sin' (Romans 5:12). The Fall explains why the world is both beautiful and broken — made good, marred by rebellion, and awaiting the redemption God promised from the very beginning.
