What is the difference between the Old and New Testaments?

Question
What is the difference between the Old and New Testaments?
Short Answer
The Old Testament records God's creation, covenant with Israel, law, history, poetry, and prophecy pointing forward to Christ. The New Testament announces Jesus' life, death, and resurrection and the church's mission, fulfilling the Old Testament's promises.
Detailed Answer

Think of the Bible as one great story in two acts. The Old Testament, thirty-nine books, lays the foundation: God creates a good world, humanity falls, and God chooses Abraham's family, Israel, through whom blessing will come to all nations. It contains the Law of Moses, histories of kings and exiles, wisdom and worship in Psalms and Proverbs, and prophets who confront sin while promising a coming Messiah, a new covenant, and a renewed creation. The New Testament, twenty-seven books, announces that the promises have arrived in Jesus. Four Gospels tell His life, death, and resurrection. Acts follows His Spirit-empowered church. Letters teach believers how to live in light of the gospel, and Revelation closes the story with the hope of a new heaven and earth. The testaments need each other. Without the Old, we would not understand Jesus' cross, titles, or mission. Without the New, the Old's promises would hang unfinished. Jesus Himself said He came not to abolish the Law and Prophets but to fulfill them. Read the whole Bible as one story, and watch how every thread weaves toward Christ, the anchor of both testaments.
