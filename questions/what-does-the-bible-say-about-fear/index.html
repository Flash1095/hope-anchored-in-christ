What does the Bible say about fear?

Question
What does the Bible say about fear?
Short Answer
The Bible talks about two kinds of fear: the healthy fear of the Lord — reverent awe before a holy God — and anxious fear of people, circumstances, and the future, which God repeatedly tells His people to lay down. 'Fear not' is one of the Bible's most repeated commands.
Detailed Answer

Fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom (Proverbs 9:10): it is not terror before a monster, but awe before a holy, powerful, and good God who is worthy of our reverence. Anxious fear is different. When God says 'Do not fear, for I am with you' (Isaiah 41:10), He pairs the command with His presence — fear shrinks when we remember who is with us. David walked through the valley of the shadow of death without fearing evil, because 'you are with me' (Psalm 23:4). Paul says God has not given us a spirit of fear, but of power, love, and a sound mind (2 Timothy 1:7). And John gives the deepest reason fear loses its grip: 'perfect love drives out fear' (1 John 4:18) — as we grasp how deeply God loves us in Christ, the fear of punishment and abandonment fades. The Bible never promises a fear-free life, but it does promise a faithful presence in every fearful place, and a love strong enough to cast fear out.
