What about people who have never heard the gospel?

Question
What about people who have never heard the gospel?
Short Answer
Scripture teaches God judges fairly according to the light each person has received, that He is perfectly just and merciful, and that salvation is through Christ, which fuels the church's mission. We can trust the Judge of all the earth to do right while obeying Christ's call to proclaim Him to all nations.
Detailed Answer

What about people who have never heard of Jesus? Every thoughtful Christian eventually asks this, usually with a specific face in mind. The Bible gives us anchors rather than a complete map. First, salvation is through Christ alone. His death and resurrection are God's one provision for sinners, with no parallel path taught in Scripture. Second, God judges with perfect fairness, according to the light received. Paul describes Gentiles judged by conscience and the law written on hearts, while those under greater revelation face greater accountability. Third, general revelation is real: creation and conscience genuinely testify to God, and Scripture shows He responds to seekers, sending Philip to an Ethiopian official and Peter to Cornelius, a God-fearing man hungry for more light. Beyond these anchors, honest theology admits mystery rather than inventing details God has not given. Abraham's confidence becomes ours: will not the Judge of all the earth do right? What is not mysterious is our assignment. This question historically does not paralyze the church. It propels her, launching missionaries from Paul to the present day. If the destiny of the unreached weighs on you, let it move you to pray, give, and perhaps go. We trust God's justice and mercy completely, and we preach Christ urgently, because He has told us to, and because the gospel is good news worth carrying to the ends of the earth.
