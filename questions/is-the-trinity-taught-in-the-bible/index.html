Is the Trinity taught in the Bible?

Question
Is the Trinity taught in the Bible?
Short Answer
The word 'Trinity' is not in the Bible, but the doctrine is: Scripture reveals one God who exists as three persons — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. The early church coined the word to summarize what the Bible already teaches.
Detailed Answer

The Bible insists there is one God: 'Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one' (Deuteronomy 6:4). Yet it also reveals three who are fully God. The Father is God (Ephesians 4:6). The Son is God: John opens, 'In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God' (John 1:1). The Holy Spirit is God: lying to the Spirit is lying to God (Acts 5:3-4). And the three appear together: at Jesus' baptism, the Son is baptized, the Spirit descends like a dove, and the Father speaks from heaven (Matthew 3:16-17). The Great Commission names all three as the one name into which believers are baptized: 'the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit' (Matthew 28:19). Paul's blessing does the same: 'the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit' (2 Corinthians 13:14). The word 'Trinity' came later, at the Councils of Nicaea and Constantinople, as shorthand for this biblical pattern — not an invention added to the Bible, but a summary of it. One God, three persons: that is the faith the church has confessed from the beginning.
