Why does God allow suffering?

Question
Why does God allow suffering?
Short Answer
The Bible does not give a single simple reason for all suffering, but shows it entered a good world through the Fall, and that God is present, compassionate, and redeeming suffering, supremely in the cross. He promises an end to suffering in the new creation, even when we cannot see His reasons now.
Detailed Answer
Why does God allow suffering? No honest answer fits on a bumper sticker, and this blog will not pretend otherwise. Scripture gives us a framework rather than a formula. Suffering was not in God's very good creation. It entered through humanity's rebellion and now touches everyone in a groaning world: disasters, disease, the cruelty of others, and consequences of our own choices all intertwine in ways even theologians cannot untangle case by case. The book of Job rebukes the idea that suffering always maps neatly onto personal sin. What the Bible does give, gloriously, is a God who does not watch pain from a distance. He hears Israel's groans in Egypt, He weeps at Lazarus' tomb, and in Jesus He suffers with us and for us, bearing wounds He still carries in resurrection. God often redeems suffering without explaining it, growing endurance, compassion, and hope that does not disappoint, and He promises history's final chapter: no more death, mourning, or pain. If you are suffering now, you do not need platitudes. You need presence, lament, community, and the anchor of the cross, proof that God's love and your pain can both be true. Bring Him your questions. He can hold them, and He will hold you.
