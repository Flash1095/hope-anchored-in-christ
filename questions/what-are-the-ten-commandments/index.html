What are the Ten Commandments?

Short Answer

The Ten Commandments are God's foundational moral laws given to Israel through Moses at Mount Sinai, summarizing love for God and love for neighbor. Jesus affirmed them and fulfilled them, and they still guide Christian life today.

Detailed Answer

Written, tradition says, by the finger of God on tablets of stone, the Ten Commandments stand at the heart of Israel's covenant and of biblical ethics. The first four govern our relationship with God: no other gods, no idols, reverence for His name, and rest on the Sabbath. The remaining six govern life with each other: honor parents, do not murder, commit adultery, steal, lie, or covet. Jesus distilled them into two great commands, love God wholly and love your neighbor as yourself, and showed their full depth reaches the heart's anger, lust, and honesty. For Christians, the commandments function in several ways at once. They expose our sin and drive us to Christ, since no one keeps them perfectly. They reveal God's good character and design for human flourishing. And for those redeemed by grace, they become a grateful guide for holy living, empowered by the Spirit. Far from being outdated, the Ten Commandments still anchor morality in a shifting culture. Read them slowly this week, thank God for the Savior who kept them for you, and ask His Spirit to write their spirit on your heart.
