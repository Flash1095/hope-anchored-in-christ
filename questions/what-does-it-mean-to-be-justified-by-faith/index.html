What does it mean to be justified by faith?

Question
What does it mean to be justified by faith?
Short Answer
To be justified by faith means God declares you righteous — not guilty — because you trust in Jesus, not because you earned it. Justification is a gift received by faith alone, based on Christ's finished work alone.
Detailed Answer

The word 'justified' comes from the courtroom: it is the judge's verdict, 'Not guilty.' Paul's great claim is that this verdict comes 'by faith' — 'a person is not justified by the works of the law, but by faith in Jesus Christ' (Galatians 2:16). That matters for two reasons. First, it means justification is not a process of becoming good enough; it is a declaration that happens the moment you trust Christ, because His righteousness is credited to you (Romans 4:3-5). Second, it means no one can boast: 'Therefore, since we have been justified through faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ' (Romans 5:1). Faith is not a good work that earns the verdict; faith is the empty hand that receives it. James adds the necessary balance: genuine faith always produces works (James 2:17) — not as the price of justification, but as its fruit. So being justified by faith means you can stop trying to earn God's acceptance and start living from it: you are already declared righteous in Christ, and that verdict will never be overturned.
