What should I do when I doubt God?

What should I do when I doubt God?

Short Answer

Doubt is not the end of faith; bring your doubts honestly to God, to Scripture, and to trusted believers, as many biblical saints did. God meets honest questioners with patience, evidence, and renewed assurance anchored in Christ.

Detailed Answer

Doubt visits almost every believer, often in life's hardest chapters, and feeling it does not make you a failed Christian. Some of the Bible's most faithful people doubted openly. John the Baptist, from prison, sent messengers to ask Jesus if He was really the one. Thomas would not believe the resurrection without seeing the wounds. The Psalms ask How long, Lord? and Why have you forsaken me? God's response to honest doubters is strikingly patient. Jesus answered John with evidence and gentle affirmation, invited Thomas to touch His scars, and used a forsaken psalm for His own cry on the cross. Distinguish doubt from unbelief: doubt asks questions while still reaching toward God, and He welcomes that reach. Practically, voice your doubts in prayer rather than hiding them, stay in Scripture and worship even when feelings lag, borrow faith from your community for a season, and revisit the historical anchors of the resurrection. Also examine which kind of doubt you carry: intellectual questions deserve good books and patient study, emotional doubts after trauma deserve counsel and time, and volitional doubts may need honest repentance. Whatever its shape, bring your doubt into the light. The Father of the desperate man who cried, I believe; help my unbelief, still answers that prayer today.
