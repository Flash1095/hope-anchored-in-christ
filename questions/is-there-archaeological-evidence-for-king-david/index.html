Is there archaeological evidence for King David?

Question
Is there archaeological evidence for King David?
Short Answer
Yes, the Tel Dan Stele, discovered in 1993, mentions the House of David in an Aramaic victory inscription from the 9th century BC. Together with the Mesha Stele and Jerusalem excavations, it supports David as a real historical king.
Detailed Answer
For years, critics claimed David was a legend like King Arthur, since no inscription outside the Bible named him. That changed in 1993, when excavators at Tel Dan in northern Israel uncovered a broken basalt stele, an Aramaic victory monument from the ninth century BC, boasting of a victory over the king of Israel and the king of the House of David. That phrase, bytdwd, is widely recognized by scholars across the spectrum as a reference to David's dynasty, stunning confirmation that neighboring nations knew David as a real founder of a royal house within about a century and a half of his reign. The Mesha Stele from Moab is also commonly read as referencing the House of David. In Jerusalem, excavations in the City of David have uncovered large stone structures, seals, and fortifications consistent with an early Israelite capital, though dating details continue to be debated, as honest archaeology often is. Evidence like this does not prove every detail of Samuel's narrative, nor does archaeology need to for faith to stand. But it does something valuable: it anchors David in real history, answering the charge of myth. The shepherd-king who sang of hope was no invention. His greater Son is no invention either.
