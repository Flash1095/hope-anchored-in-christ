Why does the book of Ecclesiastes sound so pessimistic?

Question
Why does the book of Ecclesiastes sound so pessimistic?
Short Answer
Ecclesiastes sounds pessimistic because it looks at life 'under the sun' — from a purely earthly viewpoint, where everything fades. But its bleak honesty is the point: it strips away false hopes so that its ending — 'Fear God and keep his commandments' — lands with full weight.
Detailed Answer

Ecclesiastes opens with its famous refrain: 'Meaningless! Meaningless!... Utterly meaningless! Everything is meaningless!' (Ecclesiastes 1:2). The Hebrew word is 'hevel' — vapor, breath, smoke: here, then gone. The Teacher (traditionally Solomon) tests every path to meaning: pleasure, wealth, work, wisdom — and finds each one vapor when pursued 'under the sun,' that is, without God in view. Death levels everyone, fortunes fade, and even wisdom cannot prevent injustice. That is why the book feels dark: it is deliberately refusing easy answers. But Ecclesiastes is not the Bible's last word — it is a wisdom experiment with a conclusion. Along the way it commends simple joys as gifts from God: eat, drink, enjoy your work, love your spouse (Ecclesiastes 2:24; 3:12-13; 9:7-10). And it ends where true wisdom begins: 'Fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the duty of all mankind' (Ecclesiastes 12:13-14). The pessimism is honest diagnosis, not despair: once you admit that nothing under the sun can bear the weight of ultimate meaning, you are ready for the God above the sun, who can.
