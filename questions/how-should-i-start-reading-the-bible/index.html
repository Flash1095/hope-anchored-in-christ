How should I start reading the Bible?

Question
How should I start reading the Bible?
Short Answer
Start with a Gospel like Mark or John to meet Jesus, read a short section daily with prayer, and use a readable translation. Consistency matters more than quantity, and understanding grows with community and good resources.
Detailed Answer

Opening the Bible for the first time can feel like arriving in a foreign country, wonderful but unfamiliar. The best place to start is with Jesus. Begin with the Gospel of Mark for pace or John for depth, and meet the Savior in His words and works before tackling harder sections. Next, try Acts for the church's story and Psalms for prayer. Save Genesis-to-Revelation cover-to-cover reading for a later season with a reading plan, though many thrive doing it with guidance from the start. Practically, choose a readable, faithful translation such as the CSB, ESV, NIV, or NLT, and set a sustainable rhythm: ten focused minutes daily beats an occasional binge. Read with a pen, note what the passage says about God, what it asks of you, and one verse to carry into the day. Pray before and after, asking the Spirit who inspired the Word to illuminate it. Finally, do not read alone forever. A small group, mentor, or trusted commentary can answer questions and keep you going when Leviticus gets tough. Remember, the goal is not finishing a book. It is meeting its Author, day after day, and letting His truth anchor your life.
