What are we redeemed from?

Question
What are we redeemed from?
Short Answer
We are redeemed from sin, its guilt, its slavery, and its curse — and ultimately from death itself. Redemption means being bought out of bondage: Christ paid the price to set us free.
Detailed Answer
The Bible uses 'redemption' the way the ancient world used it for slaves: a price paid to set someone free. Paul says Christ 'redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us' (Galatians 3:13) — the law's condemnation no longer hangs over believers. Peter says we were redeemed 'from the empty way of life handed down to you' (1 Peter 1:18-19) — not with silver or gold, but with the precious blood of Christ. Paul adds that in Christ 'we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins' (Ephesians 1:7). And Romans describes the deepest slavery: we were slaves to sin (Romans 6:17-18), unable to free ourselves, until Christ's death broke sin's power and His resurrection broke death's (Hebrews 2:14-15). So redemption is comprehensive: from guilt (forgiven), from slavery (freed), from the law's curse (released), and one day from death itself (Romans 8:23). Christians are not people who improved themselves; they are people who were bought — and the price was the life of God's Son.
