What was the Exodus from Egypt?

Question
What was the Exodus from Egypt?
Short Answer
The Exodus was God's mighty deliverance of Israel from slavery in Egypt under Moses, climaxing in the Passover and the parting of the Red Sea. It is the Old Testament's defining salvation event, pointing forward to redemption in Christ.
Detailed Answer

The Exodus is the Old Testament's great salvation story, retold by Israel in songs, festivals, and creeds for a thousand years. After four centuries that ended in brutal slavery under Pharaoh, God raised up Moses, confronted Egypt's gods through ten plagues, and on the night of Passover, when lamb's blood marked Israelite doors, He brought His people out with a mighty hand. At the Red Sea, trapped between water and army, Israel watched God make a way where there was none. The Exodus reveals who God is: the Lord who hears the cries of the oppressed, keeps covenant promises to Abraham, defeats tyrants, and dwells with the people He redeemed, giving them His law at Sinai to shape them into a holy nation. It also prefigures the greater Exodus of Jesus, who through His own Passover death leads a worldwide people out of slavery to sin. Whenever Scripture wants to prove God can save, it points back here. If you feel trapped with no way forward, remember the God of the Exodus specializes in seas that must part. He delivered then, and in Christ, He still delivers His people today.
