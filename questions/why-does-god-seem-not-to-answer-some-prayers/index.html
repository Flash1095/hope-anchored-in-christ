Why does God seem not to answer some prayers?

Why does God seem not to answer some prayers?

Short Answer

God hears every prayer of His people, but He does not always answer the way we ask or when we ask. Sometimes the answer is no, sometimes it is wait, and sometimes it is something better than we imagined — because He sees what we cannot.

Detailed Answer

Unanswered prayer is painful, and Scripture does not dismiss that pain — Psalm 13 begins, 'How long, LORD? Will you forget me forever?' But it gives several reasons prayers may seem unanswered. Sometimes we ask wrongly: James says, 'You do not have because you do not ask God. When you ask, you do not receive, because you ask with wrong motives' (James 4:2-3). Sometimes God says no for our good, as He did to Paul, whose thorn remained so that God's grace could shine: 'My grace is sufficient for you' (2 Corinthians 12:7-10). Sometimes the answer is wait: Jesus told a parable precisely so His followers would 'always pray and not give up' (Luke 18:1). And sometimes we misread the answer: God promises to hear prayers 'according to his will' (1 John 5:14-15), and His will is wiser than ours — Isaiah reminds us His thoughts are higher than ours (Isaiah 55:8-9). What unanswered prayer never means is that God is absent or indifferent: Jesus, who prayed 'take this cup from me' and still went to the cross (Luke 22:42), understands unanswered prayer from the inside. Keep praying, keep trusting, and bring your disappointment to Him honestly — He can hold it.
