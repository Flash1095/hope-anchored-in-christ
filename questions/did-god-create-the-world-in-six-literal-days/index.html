Did God create the world in six literal days?

Question
Did God create the world in six literal days?
Short Answer
Christians who all affirm that God created the world disagree about how to read the six days of Genesis 1. Some understand them as six ordinary 24-hour days; others read them as long periods or a literary framework. What unites orthodox Christians is that God is the Creator, and creation is good and purposeful.
Detailed Answer
The six-days question is really a question about what kind of writing Genesis 1 is. Those who read the days as literal 24-hour periods point to the repeated phrase 'and there was evening, and there was morning,' and to Exodus 20:11, which grounds the Sabbath week in God's six days of work. Those who read the days as longer ages or as a literary framework point to the poetic structure of Genesis 1 — three days of forming matched by three days of filling — and to passages like Psalm 90:4, where a thousand years are like a day to God. Both groups agree on the essentials Genesis 1 teaches: one God made everything out of nothing, He made it good, and He made human beings in His image with a purpose. The Bible's main concern in Genesis 1 is not the mechanism or the stopwatch, but the Maker. Christians can discuss the days with conviction and charity, holding firmly to God as Creator while giving grace to brothers and sisters who read the passage differently.
