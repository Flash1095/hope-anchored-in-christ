How can I forgive someone who hurt me deeply?

Question
How can I forgive someone who hurt me deeply?
Short Answer
Forgiving deep hurt is a process, not a single decision: it begins with honestly naming the wrong, choosing before God to release the debt rather than seek revenge, and then, over time, letting God heal the wound. Forgiveness does not excuse what happened, and it does not require forgetting or immediate reconciliation.
Detailed Answer

Jesus ties forgiveness to the forgiveness we have received: 'Be kind and compassionate to one another, forgiving each other, just as in Christ God forgave you' (Ephesians 4:32). That is the foundation — we forgive because we have been forgiven an immeasurable debt (Matthew 18:21-35). But deep hurt rarely resolves in one prayer. Practically, forgiveness often looks like this: first, name what happened honestly, to God and perhaps to a trusted counselor, without minimizing it. Second, make the decision to release the person from your vengeance — 'Do not take revenge... leave room for God's wrath' (Romans 12:19) — even while feelings catch up slowly. Third, pray for the person, even briefly, as Jesus commanded (Matthew 5:44); prayer softens what willpower cannot. Fourth, set wise boundaries: forgiveness and reconciliation are different, and reconciliation requires repentance and safety. If the hurt involves abuse or ongoing danger, protecting yourself and getting help is not unforgiveness — it is wisdom. Finally, expect to forgive repeatedly: Jesus' 'seventy-seven times' (Matthew 18:22) suggests forgiveness is often a direction you keep walking, not a door you walk through once. And if the wound is deep, a Christian counselor can help you carry what is too heavy to carry alone.
