What does the Bible say about raising children?

Question
What does the Bible say about raising children?
Short Answer
The Bible calls children a gift from God and gives parents the central job of teaching them to know and love Him — not perfectly, but daily, patiently, and with grace. The key passages are Deuteronomy 6, Proverbs, and Ephesians 6.
Detailed Answer

Children are 'a heritage from the LORD, offspring a reward from him' (Psalm 127:3) — not projects to perfect, but people to steward. God's core instruction to parents is Deuteronomy 6:6-7: keep His words on your heart, and 'impress them on your children. Talk about them when you sit at home and when you walk along the road, when you lie down and when you get up.' Teaching happens in ordinary life, not just at church. Proverbs adds the long view: 'Start children off on the way they should go, and even when they are old they will not turn from it' (Proverbs 22:6) — a principle, not a guarantee, since children make their own choices. Ephesians balances discipline with gentleness: 'Fathers, do not exasperate your children; instead, bring them up in the training and instruction of the Lord' (Ephesians 6:4) — discipline without harshness, instruction without hypocrisy. And Colossians warns parents not to embitter their children (Colossians 3:21). The Bible's picture is realistic: parents are sinners raising sinners, and the goal is not perfect children but faithful ones — pointed to Christ by parents who repent, apologize, pray, and keep showing grace at home.
