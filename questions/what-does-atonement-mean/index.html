What does atonement mean?

What does atonement mean?

Short Answer

Atonement means the restoring of a broken relationship between God and people through a sacrifice that deals with sin. In the Bible, atonement is how a holy God can forgive guilty people without pretending their sin does not matter.

Detailed Answer

The word pictures a covering and a making-right. Under the old covenant, on the Day of Atonement, the high priest offered sacrifices for the sins of Israel (Leviticus 16): blood was shed because sin brings death, and the life of a substitute stood in place of the people. But those sacrifices had to be repeated year after year. The New Testament says Jesus is the true atoning sacrifice — 'the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world' (John 1:29). Paul writes that God put Christ forward as a sacrifice of atonement (Romans 3:25), and John says Jesus is the atoning sacrifice for our sins, and not only for ours (1 John 2:2). Through His death, our sins are covered, God's justice is satisfied, and we are reconciled to Him (2 Corinthians 5:19). Atonement is not God overlooking sin; it is God dealing with sin Himself, at His own cost, so that we can come home.
