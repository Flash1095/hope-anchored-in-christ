What is the Parable of the Prodigal Son about?

What is the Parable of the Prodigal Son about?

Short Answer

The Prodigal Son tells of a wayward son who squanders his inheritance, returns in shame, and is embraced by his running, forgiving father. Jesus told it to show God's joyful welcome for repentant sinners and to challenge self-righteous hearts.

Detailed Answer

Few stories capture the heart of God like the Prodigal Son. A younger son demands his inheritance early, wastes it in reckless living, and ends up hungry and broken among pigs. He rehearses a speech and heads home expecting, at best, a servant's place. Instead, his father sees him while he is still far off, runs to him, embraces him, and throws a celebration. Jesus told this parable to grumbling religious leaders who resented His welcome of sinners, and also for the older brother in the story, who stood outside angry at grace he thought should be earned. Both sons were lost, one in rebellion, one in pride. The father's love pursues both. This is the gospel in story form. God does not wait for us to become worthy. He welcomes, restores, and rejoices when the lost come home. If you feel far from God today, this parable is for you. The way home begins with turning around. Your Father is watching the road, and His embrace is more certain than your shame.
