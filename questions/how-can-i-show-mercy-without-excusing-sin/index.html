How can I show mercy without excusing sin?

Question
How can I show mercy without excusing sin?
Short Answer
Show mercy the way Jesus did: with truth in one hand and grace in the other. Mercy forgives and restores; it does not pretend the sin did not happen or remove its consequences.
Detailed Answer

Micah's famous trio holds the balance: 'act justly and love mercy and walk humbly' (Micah 6:8) — justice and mercy together, never one without the other. Jesus shows how in John 8: confronted with a woman caught in adultery, He first silences her accusers, then tells her, 'Neither do I condemn you' — and in the same breath, 'Go now and leave your life of sin' (John 8:10-11). Mercy without truth is not mercy; it is permission. Truth without mercy is not justice; it is cruelty. The prodigal son's father models it too: he ran to his son, embraced him, and restored him — but the son had come home confessing, 'Father, I have sinned' (Luke 15:20-21). Practically, showing mercy without excusing sin means: forgive the debt, tell the truth about what happened, allow appropriate consequences to stand, and walk with the person toward restoration — gently, as Paul says: 'restore that person gently' (Galatians 6:1). Mercy opens the door back; it does not pretend the door was never closed.
