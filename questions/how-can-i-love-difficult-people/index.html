How can I love difficult people?

How can I love difficult people?

Short Answer

You love difficult people the way Jesus taught: by praying for them, doing them good, and refusing to return evil for evil — while also setting wise boundaries. Loving someone does not always mean trusting them or spending unlimited time with them.

Detailed Answer

Jesus did not say to love only lovable people: 'Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you' (Matthew 5:44). Paul gives the practice: 'Do not repay anyone evil for evil... If your enemy is hungry, feed him; if he is thirsty, give him something to drink' (Romans 12:17-20). Luke adds the reason: God 'is kind to the ungrateful and wicked' (Luke 6:35), and we are called to reflect His kindness. Practically, loving a difficult person often starts small: pray for them by name, even briefly; speak about them truthfully rather than gossiping; respond gently rather than matching their tone (Proverbs 15:1); and look for one concrete good you can do for them. At the same time, love is not doormat behavior: Jesus set boundaries, confronted sin honestly, and walked away from crowds that wanted to use Him. If someone is abusive or dangerous, loving them may mean praying from a safe distance and involving wise help — not giving them access to hurt you again. Love wants their ultimate good, which is their repentance and flourishing in God; sometimes the most loving thing is a truthful boundary, held with grace.
