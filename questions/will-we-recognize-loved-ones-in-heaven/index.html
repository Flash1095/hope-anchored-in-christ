Will we recognize loved ones in heaven?

Question
Will we recognize loved ones in heaven?
Short Answer
The Bible does not answer this in a single verse, but everything it says points to yes: we will know one another in heaven, because resurrection life continues our personal identity rather than erasing it.
Detailed Answer
Scripture gives us several encouraging clues. Paul says that now we know in part, but then 'we shall know fully, even as we are fully known' (1 Corinthians 13:12) — heaven means knowing more, not less. When Jesus was transfigured, Peter, James, and John recognized Moses and Elijah, who had lived centuries earlier (Matthew 17:1-8). Paul comforts grieving believers by saying we will be caught up together with those who died in Christ (1 Thessalonians 4:13-18) — 'together' implies reunion, not anonymity. And David, grieving his infant son, said, 'I will go to him' (2 Samuel 12:23), expressing hope of being with his child again. The Bible does not describe exactly how recognition works in resurrected bodies, and it wisely does not promise that every earthly relationship continues unchanged — our deepest joy in heaven will be God Himself. But the continuity of who we are, the reunion language of Scripture, and the recognition scenes we do have all point the same way: in heaven, you will know your loved ones, and they will know you — and every relationship there will be healed, whole, and free of the pain that marks them now.
