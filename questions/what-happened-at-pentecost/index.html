What happened at Pentecost?

Short Answer

At Pentecost, fifty days after Jesus' resurrection, the Holy Spirit came upon the disciples with wind and fire, enabling them to proclaim the gospel in many languages. About three thousand people believed that day, and the church was born.

Detailed Answer

Pentecost is the birthday of the church, and it is a story of God keeping promises with power. Before His ascension, Jesus told His followers to wait in Jerusalem for the gift His Father promised. Ten days later, as they prayed together, the Holy Spirit came like a rushing wind, with tongues of fire resting on each of them. They began declaring God's wonders in languages they had never learned, heard by visitors from many nations gathered for the feast. Peter, who weeks earlier had denied Jesus, stood and preached with bold clarity, explaining that the risen Christ had poured out the Spirit just as Joel prophesied. About three thousand people repented and were baptized that day. Pentecost reversed Babel's confusion with redemption's clarity: one gospel, many tongues, one new people. It also means the Spirit's presence is not reserved for the especially spiritual. He has been given to the whole church, including you, for witness, unity, and power to live the Christian life. The same Spirit who launched the church at Pentecost is at work in believers today.
