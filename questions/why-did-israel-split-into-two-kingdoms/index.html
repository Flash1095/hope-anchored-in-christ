Why did Israel split into two kingdoms?

Question
Why did Israel split into two kingdoms?
Short Answer
After Solomon's death, Israel split into the northern kingdom of Israel and southern kingdom of Judah because of heavy burdens under Rehoboam and generations of idolatry and unfaithfulness. Prophets warned both kingdoms, and both eventually fell to exile.
Detailed Answer
The united kingdom of Israel lasted only three generations: Saul, David, and Solomon. Solomon's wisdom built the temple, but his many foreign wives turned his heart toward other gods, and his heavy labor demands sowed resentment. When his son Rehoboam refused the elders' counsel and threatened even harsher burdens, ten northern tribes seceded under Jeroboam, forming Israel, while Judah and Benjamin remained loyal to David's line in the south. The split was both a political failure and a spiritual unraveling God had foretold as judgment on Solomon's idolatry. What followed were centuries of mostly unfaithful kings in the north, with dynasties rising and falling by violence, until Assyria destroyed Samaria in 722 BC and scattered its people. Judah lasted longer, graced with a few reforming kings like Hezekiah and Josiah, but persistent idolatry led to Babylon's destruction of Jerusalem and the temple in 586 BC. Through it all, prophets like Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, and Hosea called God's people back and promised a coming King who would reunite and heal what sin tore apart. The divided kingdom warns us that blessings squandered in disobedience fracture communities, and it makes us long for Jesus, the one Shepherd-King under whom God's people are made one.
