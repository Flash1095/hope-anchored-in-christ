What is the Book of Revelation about?

Question
What is the Book of Revelation about?
Short Answer
Revelation is John's prophetic vision unveiling Jesus Christ as the victorious Lamb and King who defeats evil, judges the world, and brings the new creation. Written to persecuted churches, its message is: endure, worship, and hope, because the Lamb wins.
Detailed Answer

Revelation intimidates many readers with its dragons, seals, trumpets, and beasts, yet its own first words tell us its true subject: the revelation of Jesus Christ. John, exiled on Patmos, received visions for seven struggling churches in Asia Minor, and through them for the church in every age. The book's heart is a throne room scene where a Lamb, looking as if slain, is worshiped as worthy to open history's scroll. From there, cycles of judgments unfold against evil empires, symbolic and vivid, while God's people are sealed, witnessed, martyred, and finally vindicated. The beast and Babylon fall, the great supper of the Lamb is announced, Christ returns as King of kings, Satan is judged, and the story lands where Genesis began, only better: a garden-city, the tree of life, no more curse, God with His people forever. Interpreters differ on how literally to map its symbols onto timelines, and humble readers hold those debates loosely. Its central message thunders clearly in any reading: Jesus is Lord, empires are not, suffering is temporary, evil will lose, and the Lamb wins. Revelation was not given to fuel fear or date charts. It was given to anchor persecuted, ordinary believers in worshipful endurance, a hope as needed in comfortable times as in costly ones.
