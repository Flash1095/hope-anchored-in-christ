Who were the prophets and what did they do?

Question
Who were the prophets and what did they do?
Short Answer
The prophets were God's messengers who called Israel back to covenant faithfulness, confronted injustice and idolatry, comforted the suffering, and foretold the coming Messiah. Major and minor prophets alike spoke God's word with courage, tears, and hope.
Detailed Answer

When Israel's kings failed and priests compromised, God raised up prophets, men and women who spoke His word to their generation with unnerving clarity. Moses was the prototype, but the prophetic ranks include Samuel the kingmaker, Elijah confronting Baal's prophets on Carmel, Isaiah beholding God's holiness and foretelling the suffering Servant, Jeremiah weeping through Jerusalem's fall, Ezekiel preaching to exiles by Babylon's canals, and twelve so-called minor prophets whose messages are anything but minor. Their work had a consistent shape. They indicted God's people for idolatry and injustice, especially cruelty to the poor, widows, and foreigners. They announced judgment when repentance was refused, because a holy God takes sin seriously. And remarkably, they always left a door of hope open: a remnant preserved, a new covenant promised, hearts of stone replaced with hearts of flesh, and a coming King from David's line who would suffer for sins and reign in righteousness. Reading the prophets is bracing and consoling in equal measure. They strip away religious pretense and point us to Jesus, in whom their promises converge. Their voices still call the church to holiness, justice, and unshakable hope in God's unfolding plan.
