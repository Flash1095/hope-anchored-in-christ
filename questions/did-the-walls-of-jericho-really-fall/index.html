Did the walls of Jericho really fall?

Did the walls of Jericho really fall?

Short Answer

Excavations at Jericho found a collapsed mudbrick wall and a city destroyed by fire with full grain stores, consistent with a springtime conquest as Joshua describes. Dating the destruction remains debated among archaeologists, but the collapse itself is well documented.

Detailed Answer

Joshua's Jericho is archaeology's most famous collapsed wall, and one of its liveliest debates. Excavators from John Garstang in the 1930s to Kathleen Kenyon in the 1950s uncovered something striking: a massive mudbrick wall that had toppled outward, forming a ramp against the stone retaining wall, precisely the unusual detail that would let Israelites climb up into the city as Joshua describes. The city had burned with stores of grain still full, suggesting a spring harvest conquest that did not plunder food, matching Joshua's command and the Passover-season timing. The argument is over dates. Kenyon dated the destruction to about 1550 BC, before a traditional Exodus chronology would place Joshua, while Garstang and later scholars like Bryant Wood argued for about 1400 BC, citing pottery, scarabs, and the grain evidence. Radiocarbon and ceramic readings keep the discussion moving, as they should in an honest science. Where does that leave the reader? The physical picture at Jericho, a fallen wall, a burned city, full storehouses, fits the biblical account in remarkable specifics, even while scholars debate its century. Faith anchored in Scripture can welcome both the spade's findings and its open questions, trusting that God's acts in history leave traces worth investigating.
