Are we guilty for Adam's sin?

Question
Are we guilty for Adam's sin?
Short Answer
The Bible teaches that Adam's sin affects all of us, but Christians understand the 'how' in two complementary ways: we inherit a fallen nature from Adam, and we are each guilty for our own sins. What is certain is that no one is condemned by God for something they had no part in — and Christ's work answers both.
Detailed Answer

Romans 5 is the key passage: 'just as sin entered the world through one man, and death through sin... death came to all people, because all sinned' (Romans 5:12). The traditional Reformed reading says Adam was our federal head — his guilt is imputed to us, just as Christ's righteousness is imputed to believers (Romans 5:18-19). Other Christians emphasize inherited corruption: we are born with a bent toward sin, and that bent produces our own guilty acts, for which God justly holds us accountable (Ezekiel 18:20; Romans 3:23). Both views agree on the essentials: every person is born into a fallen world with a fallen nature, every person sins personally, and no one is saved by claiming innocence. Paul's parallel is the comfort: 'For as in Adam all die, so in Christ all will be made alive' (1 Corinthians 15:22). Whatever the precise mechanics of Adam's guilt, the gospel's answer is complete: in Christ, both inherited brokenness and personal sin are forgiven, and believers receive a new nature and a new head. The question that matters most is not 'Am I guilty for Adam?' but 'Have I trusted the second Adam?'
