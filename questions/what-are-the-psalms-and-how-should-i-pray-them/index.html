What are the Psalms and how should I pray them?

Question
What are the Psalms and how should I pray them?
Short Answer
The Psalms are Israel's inspired songbook of 150 prayers and hymns expressing praise, lament, thanksgiving, and trust. Praying them gives us God's own words for every emotion and season of the soul.
Detailed Answer

The Psalms may be the most pastorally generous book in the Bible. In its 150 songs you will find exuberant praise, raw lament, confession of sin, cries for justice, quiet trust, and even anger poured out honestly before God. Kings, priests, and ordinary sufferers composed them over centuries, and Jesus Himself prayed them, including from the cross. This means no emotion is off-limits in prayer. Whatever you feel, there is almost certainly a Psalm that has felt it first and can give your heart words when your own run out. Praying the Psalms is simple. Read one slowly, out loud if you can. Make its words your own, pausing where a line matches your situation, and turn its promises into personal prayer. Many believers pray a Psalm each morning or turn to specific ones by need: Psalm 23 for fear, Psalm 51 for confession, Psalm 46 for chaos, Psalm 103 for gratitude. Over time, the Psalms tune your heart like an instrument, teaching lament without despair and praise without denial. Let Israel's songbook become your prayer book, and let it anchor your hope in the God who hears every honest cry.
