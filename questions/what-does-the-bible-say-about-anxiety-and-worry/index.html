What does the Bible say about anxiety and worry?

Question
What does the Bible say about anxiety and worry?
Short Answer
The Bible invites anxious hearts to bring every concern to God in prayer with thanksgiving, promising His peace that guards hearts and minds. Scripture meets worry not with shame but with the Father's care, daily bread, and unshakable kingdom.
Detailed Answer

If anxiety is your frequent companion, the Bible speaks to you with unusual tenderness. Jesus, in the Sermon on the Mount, does not scold worriers. He reasons with them as a Father: look at the birds your Father feeds, consider the lilies He clothes, and remember you are worth far more. Worry, He shows us, cannot add a single hour to life, but seeking God's kingdom first reorders our fears beneath a greater security. Paul, writing from prison, gives the practical pathway: do not be anxious about anything, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, present your requests to God, and His peace, which transcends understanding, will guard your hearts and minds. Peter adds that we can cast all our anxieties on Him because He cares for us. None of this shames those with persistent anxiety or clinical struggles, and seeking medical or counseling help is wise stewardship, not weak faith. Scripture's practices help daily: name specific fears in prayer, thank God for specific past mercies, take the next faithful step, and let community carry you. Your Father knows what you need. His peace is not the absence of trouble but the presence of an Anchor within it.
