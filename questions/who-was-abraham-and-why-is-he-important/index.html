Who was Abraham and why is he important?

Who was Abraham and why is he important?

Short Answer

Abraham is the father of the nation of Israel and of all who believe, chosen by God to receive promises of land, descendants, and blessing for all nations. His faith, credited as righteousness, points forward to salvation in Christ.

Detailed Answer

Abraham's story is where God's rescue plan takes a name and an address. Called from Ur to leave country, kindred, and gods behind, this childless older man received staggering promises: a great nation, a land, countless descendants, and blessing that would one day reach all families of the earth. Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness, a line Paul seizes upon to show that justification by faith is as old as Abraham himself. His journey was not flawless faith. He faltered in Egypt, laughed at the promise in his old age, and tried to help God through Hagar, with painful consequences. Yet he kept returning to trust, culminating on Mount Moriah, where he offered Isaac and learned that God Himself would provide the lamb, a foreshadowing of Christ. Through Abraham came Israel, the Scriptures, and ultimately Jesus, the offspring through whom the worldwide blessing flows. That is why Abraham is called the father of all who believe. If God seems slow with His promises in your life, Abraham's decades of waiting are for you. The God who called him is faithful across a lifetime, and He still counts simple trust as precious in His sight.
