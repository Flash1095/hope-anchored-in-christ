What is a covenant in the Bible?

Question
What is a covenant in the Bible?
Short Answer
A covenant is a binding promise-relationship between God and His people. Unlike a simple contract, a biblical covenant is personal: God commits Himself to His people, gives them promises and commands, and seals the relationship with a sign.
Detailed Answer
Covenants shape the whole Bible's story. God made a covenant with Noah, promising never again to flood the earth, with the rainbow as its sign (Genesis 9). He made a covenant with Abraham, promising him offspring, land, and blessing for all nations (Genesis 15; 17). He made a covenant with Israel at Sinai, giving the law as the terms of life together (Exodus 19-20). He made a covenant with David, promising a king from his line forever (2 Samuel 7). Each covenant has the same shape: God speaks first, God makes promises, and God's people respond in trust and obedience. The climax is the new covenant, promised by Jeremiah — God's law written on hearts, sins forgiven — and sealed by Jesus at the Last Supper: 'This cup is the new covenant in my blood' (Luke 22:20). When you read the Bible, watching for covenants is like following the spine of the story: they show a God who binds Himself to His people and keeps His promises, even when His people fail.
