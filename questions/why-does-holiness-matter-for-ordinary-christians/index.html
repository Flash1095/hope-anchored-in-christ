Why does holiness matter for ordinary Christians?

Question
Why does holiness matter for ordinary Christians?
Short Answer
Holiness matters because God made us to share His character, not just to be forgiven. Peter writes, 'Be holy in all you do' (1 Peter 1:15) — holiness is how ordinary life becomes a witness to who God is.
Detailed Answer

It is tempting to think holiness is for monks and missionaries, while ordinary Christians just need to believe. But Peter addresses 'ordinary' believers — scattered, suffering, working people — and tells them to be holy in all they do, 'because it is written: Be holy, because I am holy' (1 Peter 1:15-16). Hebrews adds that without holiness 'no one will see the Lord' (Hebrews 12:14) — not because holiness earns salvation, but because a life untouched by God suggests a heart untouched by Him. Holiness also matters practically: it protects your joy (sin promises freedom and delivers slavery), it strengthens your witness (Jesus said people would know His disciples by their love, and a holy life makes the gospel believable), and it prepares you for eternity (the new creation is a holy place for a holy people). Paul frames it positively: offer your body as a living sacrifice, and be transformed by the renewing of your mind (Romans 12:1-2). Holiness is not grim rule-keeping; it is becoming the person God made you to be — in your kitchen, your workplace, and your relationships, not just on Sundays.
