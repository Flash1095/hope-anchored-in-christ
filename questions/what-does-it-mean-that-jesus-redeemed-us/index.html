What does it mean that Jesus redeemed us?

Question
What does it mean that Jesus redeemed us?
Short Answer
To say Jesus redeemed us means He paid the price to set us free from sin and death. Like a slave bought out of bondage, we now belong to Him — not as property to be used, but as beloved people brought home.
Detailed Answer

In the ancient world, redemption was a marketplace word: paying a ransom to free a slave or a prisoner of war. Jesus used it for Himself: 'the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many' (Mark 10:45). Paul echoes it: Christ 'gave himself as a ransom for all people' (1 Timothy 2:6), and Titus says He 'gave himself for us to redeem us from all wickedness and to purify for himself a people that are his very own' (Titus 2:14). The price was His own life, poured out on the cross; the freedom is comprehensive — from sin's guilt, its power, and one day its presence. And redemption changes ownership in the best way: 'You are not your own; you were bought at a price' (1 Corinthians 6:19-20). That is not grim servitude; it is belonging. A redeemed person is someone who was headed for judgment and is now headed home, someone who was a slave and is now a son or daughter, someone whose life is no longer defined by what enslaved them but by the One who paid to set them free.
