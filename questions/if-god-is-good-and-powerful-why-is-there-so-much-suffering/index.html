If God is good and powerful, why is there so much suffering?

If God is good and powerful, why is there so much suffering?

Short Answer

This is the hardest question in faith, and the Bible does not offer a quick answer — but it does offer a framework: suffering entered through a fallen world, God permits it for purposes we do not always see, and in Jesus, God entered suffering Himself to defeat it.

Detailed Answer

The Bible never says suffering is good, and it never blames victims glibly. It gives several threads. First, the world is fallen: creation itself groans under sin's curse (Romans 8:20-22), so disease, disaster, and death are not God's original design. Second, human freedom has real consequences: much suffering comes from human choices — wars, abuse, greed — and a world without freedom to choose evil would be a world without freedom to choose love. Third, God can use suffering redemptively: Joseph told his brothers, 'You intended to harm me, but God intended it for good' (Genesis 50:20), and Paul says God works all things for the good of those who love Him (Romans 8:28) — not that all things are good, but that God works good through them. Fourth, and most importantly, God did not stay distant from suffering: in Jesus, He entered it, wept at gravesides (John 11:35), and died on a cross. The cross means God knows suffering from the inside — and the resurrection means suffering does not win. The Bible's final answer is not an explanation but a promise: a day when God 'will wipe every tear from their eyes' and 'there will be no more death or mourning or crying or pain' (Revelation 21:4). Until then, we grieve honestly, help practically, and trust the God who suffers with us.
