What is faith according to the Bible?

Question
What is faith according to the Bible?
Short Answer
Biblical faith is confident trust in God based on who He is and what He has promised. Hebrews defines it as 'confidence in what we hope for and assurance about what we do not see' (Hebrews 11:1) — not wishful thinking, but reliance on a trustworthy God.
Detailed Answer

Faith in the Bible is more than agreeing that God exists; even demons believe that much (James 2:19). It has three sides that belong together: knowing the truth about God, trusting that truth personally, and acting on it. Abraham is the great example: he believed God's promise of a son when it seemed impossible, 'and it was credited to him as righteousness' (Romans 4:3). Faith comes by hearing God's Word (Romans 10:17) — it is a response to what God has said and done, supremely in Jesus. And genuine faith shows itself: James insists that faith without works is dead (James 2:17), not because works earn salvation, but because living trust always bears fruit. Faith is also a gift: Paul says it is by grace we are saved, through faith, 'and this is not from yourselves, it is the gift of God' (Ephesians 2:8). So faith is not a feeling you manufacture or a leap into the dark; it is trusting a God who has proven Himself faithful, and walking in that trust day by day.
