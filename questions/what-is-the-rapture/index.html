What is the rapture?

Question
What is the rapture?
Short Answer
The rapture describes Christ's catching up of believers to meet Him in the air at His return, as Paul teaches in 1 Thessalonians 4. Christians differ on its timing relative to tribulation, but unite that Jesus will gather His people to Himself.
Detailed Answer

Few end-times topics spark more discussion than the rapture. The word itself comes from the Latin translation of Paul's phrase caught up in 1 Thessalonians 4, where he comforts grieving believers: the dead in Christ will rise first, then the living will be caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air, and so we will always be with the Lord. Paul describes the same moment in 1 Corinthians as the twinkling of an eye, when the trumpet sounds, the dead are raised imperishable, and mortal bodies put on immortality. Believers debate the timing: whether this gathering happens before, during, or after a tribulation period, and how it relates to Christ's visible return to earth, and faithful Christians hold each view. Those debates matter less than the passage's purpose. Paul did not write to give us a chart. He wrote to give grieving people comfort: therefore encourage one another with these words. The dead in Christ are not lost, separation is temporary, reunion is certain, and the Lord Himself will descend to gather His own. Whatever your position on timing, hold the hope tightly: Jesus keeps His people, death has an expiration date, and a trumpet morning is coming when faith becomes sight forever.
