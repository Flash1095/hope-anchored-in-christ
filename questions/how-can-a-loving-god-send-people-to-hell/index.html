How can a loving God send people to hell?

Question
How can a loving God send people to hell?
Short Answer
This question flows from God's love and justice held together: God respects human choices, judges evil righteously, and in love provided rescue at the cost of His own Son. Hell is less about God rejecting people than about people finally receiving the separation they chose, which is why the gospel invitation is so urgent and wide.
Detailed Answer

How can a loving God send people to hell? It is one of the most heartfelt objections to Christianity, and it deserves more than a shrug. Begin here: the same Bible that teaches hell also says God is love, that He takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked, and that He desires all people to be saved. His love is not in question. But love in Scripture is holy love, never indulgence that calls evil good. A God who shrugged at murder, oppression, and cruelty forever would not be loving at all, least of all to victims. Judgment is love's integrity. Add human freedom: God made persons, not puppets, and He honors our choices with sobering permanence. C.S. Lewis observed that in the end there are two kinds of people: those who say to God, Thy will be done, and those to whom God says, Thy will be done. Hell is the terrible dignity of a finally granted refusal. Most importantly, God has not left us to judgment. At the cross, He took hell's separation into Himself, crying forsaken, so that none who trust Christ need face it. The question finally turns around: given such costly rescue, urgently offered to all, the wonder is not that judgment exists but that grace is so wide. Come to Jesus, and urge others to come, while the invitation stands open.
