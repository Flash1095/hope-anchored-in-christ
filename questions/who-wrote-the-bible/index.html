Who wrote the Bible?

Question
Who wrote the Bible?
Short Answer
The Bible was written by about forty human authors over roughly 1,500 years, including prophets, kings, fishermen, and a doctor, as the Holy Spirit inspired them. Christians call this dual authorship inspiration: fully God's Word through fully human writers.
Detailed Answer

One of the Bible's wonders is its unity amid astonishing diversity. About forty authors wrote its sixty-six books over roughly fifteen centuries: Moses the lawgiver, David the shepherd-king, Isaiah the prophet, Amos the farmer, Luke the physician, Paul the scholar-missionary, and John the fisherman among them. They wrote law, history, poetry, prophecy, letters, and apocalyptic visions in Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek, on three continents. Yet together their writings tell one unfolding story culminating in Jesus. Christians explain this through inspiration. Peter says prophecy never had its origin in the human will, but prophets spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit. Paul calls all Scripture God-breathed. This does not mean the authors were robots. Their personalities, research, vocabularies, and circumstances shine through, Luke even describes his careful investigation. It means God so superintended the process that the result is exactly His Word for His people. Knowing the human authors makes the Bible richer, not weaker. You are reading real voices from real history, through whom the living God still speaks today with clarity, authority, and hope.
