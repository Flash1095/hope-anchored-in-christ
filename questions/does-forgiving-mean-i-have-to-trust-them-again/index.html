Does forgiving mean I have to trust them again?

Question
Does forgiving mean I have to trust them again?
Short Answer
No. Forgiveness and trust are different things. Forgiveness releases the debt and gives up revenge; trust is rebuilt slowly, over time, through changed behavior — and in some cases, wisdom says trust should not be restored.
Detailed Answer

The Bible commands forgiveness — 'Forgive as the Lord forgave you' (Colossians 3:13) — but it never commands instant trust. Trust is earned, and Proverbs is full of warnings about trusting the untrustworthy (Proverbs 25:19). Jesus Himself did not entrust Himself to everyone (John 2:24-25). Forgiveness looks backward: it cancels the debt and refuses revenge. Trust looks forward: it asks whether this person is safe and reliable now. Reconciliation, which is different from both, requires repentance: 'If your brother or sister sins against you, rebuke them; and if they repent, forgive them' (Luke 17:3). Paul's advice captures the balance: 'If it is possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with everyone' (Romans 12:18) — 'as far as it depends on you' leaves room for the other person's choices and for safety. If someone abused, betrayed, or endangered you, forgiving them does not mean giving them access to hurt you again. Boundaries are not bitterness; they are stewardship of the life God gave you. You can forgive fully and still say, 'Trust will take time — and in this case, distance is wise.'
