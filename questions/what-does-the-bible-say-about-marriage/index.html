What does the Bible say about marriage?

Question
What does the Bible say about marriage?
Short Answer
The Bible presents marriage as a lifelong covenant between a man and a woman, created by God, meant to picture Christ's love for His church. Jesus rooted it in creation: 'the two will become one flesh' (Matthew 19:5-6).
Detailed Answer
Marriage begins in Genesis: God made man and woman for each other, and 'a man leaves his father and mother and is united to his wife, and they become one flesh' (Genesis 2:24). Jesus quoted that passage when asked about divorce and added, 'So they are no longer two, but one flesh. Therefore what God has joined together, let no one separate' (Matthew 19:6). Malachi calls marriage a covenant (Malachi 2:14) — not a contract to be broken when feelings fade, but a promise kept through seasons. Paul gives marriage its deepest meaning: husbands are to love their wives 'just as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her' (Ephesians 5:25), and wives are to respect their husbands — a mutual picture of Christ and the church. The Bible is also honest about marriage's difficulties: Paul notes that married people face 'many troubles in this life' (1 Corinthians 7:28), and it gives careful guidance for hardship, including the painful subjects of divorce and remarriage. At its best, marriage is a living parable: two sinners, forgiven by grace, learning to love each other the way Christ loves them — and showing the world what God's faithful love looks like.
