Why are there so many Bible translations?

Question
Why are there so many Bible translations?
Short Answer
Many translations exist because translators balance word-for-word accuracy and thought-for-thought readability differently, and languages change over time. Faithful translations agree on all core doctrines, and comparing them often deepens understanding.
Detailed Answer

New Bible readers are often surprised, and sometimes unsettled, by the shelf of translations: KJV, ESV, NIV, NLT, CSB, and more. The reason is actually reassuring. Translators all work from the same Hebrew and Greek texts, but they make different choices on a spectrum. Formal translations like the ESV and NASB stay close to the original wording and structure. Dynamic translations like the NLT prioritize how the meaning would sound naturally in today's English. Medial translations like the NIV and CSB aim for a balance. Languages also change, so new translations serve new generations, just as the beloved KJV served its own. Crucially, no major doctrine depends on a translation difference, because translators across the spectrum labor with rigorous scholarship and mutual checking. Far from being a problem, having several good translations is a tool: comparing two or three often illuminates a passage like light from different windows. Choose one accurate, readable translation as your main Bible, use others for study, and be wary of anyone claiming only one English version is God's Word. The abundance of translations in thousands of languages is really a story of God's Word reaching people in the language of their hearts.
