What are the Dead Sea Scrolls?

Question
What are the Dead Sea Scrolls?
Short Answer
The Dead Sea Scrolls are ancient Jewish manuscripts, dating from about 250 BC to AD 68, discovered in caves near Qumran from 1947 onward. They include the oldest known copies of Old Testament books and confirm the Bible's careful preservation.
Detailed Answer

Few discoveries electrified biblical studies like the Dead Sea Scrolls. In 1947, Bedouin shepherds exploring caves near Qumran by the Dead Sea found clay jars containing ancient scrolls, the first of eventually hundreds of manuscripts from eleven caves. Dating from roughly 250 BC to AD 68, the collection includes fragments of every Old Testament book except Esther, sectarian writings, and the magnificent Great Isaiah Scroll, a complete copy of Isaiah over seven meters long. Before this find, our oldest complete Hebrew manuscripts were from about AD 1000. The scrolls pushed the evidence back a full millennium, and the comparison was remarkable: the Isaiah scroll matched the later text with such fidelity that it demonstrated how carefully Jewish scribes had copied Scripture across centuries. The scrolls also illuminate the Jewish world of Jesus' day, its hopes for a Messiah, its purity concerns, its earnest study of prophecy. For readers of this blog, the takeaway is confidence and gratitude. The book in your hands has been carried through deserts, empires, and millennia by meticulous hands and a faithful God. The Word that anchored ancient hope in caves above the Dead Sea can anchor yours today.
