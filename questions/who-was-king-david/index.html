Who was King David?

Short Answer

David was Israel's shepherd-king, a man after God's own heart who united the kingdom, wrote many Psalms, and received God's promise of an everlasting dynasty. Jesus, David's greater Son, fulfills that promise as eternal King.

Detailed Answer

David's life reads like an epic: shepherd boy, giant-slayer, fugitive, king, psalmist, sinner, and brokenhearted father. Anointed while still tending sheep, he defeated Goliath by faith, endured Saul's jealousy without seizing the throne by violence, and eventually united Israel, establishing Jerusalem as its capital and bringing the ark of God home with dancing joy. God made him an extraordinary promise: his house and kingdom would endure forever, a covenant that kept Israel hoping through exile and finds its fulfillment in Jesus, the Son of David whose kingdom never ends. David also gave the church its prayer book. His Psalms voice every human emotion before God, from exultant praise to the agony of Psalm 51, his confession after his grievous sins with Bathsheba and Uriah. That failure matters to the story. Scripture hides nothing about its heroes, and David's repentance shows why he is called a man after God's heart, not because he never fell, but because he always returned. His life encourages us that God uses imperfect people, disciplines those He loves, and keeps promises across a thousand years. In David's greater Son, our hope has a King who will never fail.
