How can a Christian marriage survive hard seasons?

Question
How can a Christian marriage survive hard seasons?
Short Answer
Christian marriages survive hard seasons the way any covenant survives: by keeping promises when feelings fade, forgiving quickly, praying together, and letting God's people help. Hard seasons do not mean a marriage has failed; often they are where it deepens.
Detailed Answer

Every marriage faces hard seasons — illness, money pressure, parenting stress, grief, or simply the slow drift of two busy people. Scripture's answer is covenant before feelings: Malachi calls marriage a covenant (Malachi 2:14), and Ecclesiastes says a cord of three strands is not quickly broken (Ecclesiastes 4:12) — with God woven in, the marriage holds. Practically, surviving looks like: forgiving as Christ forgave you (Ephesians 4:32), even for the same offense repeatedly; speaking truth kindly instead of keeping score; protecting small daily kindnesses when grand romance is impossible; praying together, even briefly, because shared prayer re-centers the marriage on God; and inviting help early — a trusted mentor couple, a pastor, or a Christian counselor — before distance hardens into resentment. Paul's love chapter is a marriage manual in miniature: love is patient, kind, not easily angered, keeps no record of wrongs, and always perseveres (1 Corinthians 13:4-7). And Song of Songs reminds couples that delight matters too: even in hard seasons, make room for tenderness, laughter, and affection. A marriage that survives is rarely one that never struggled; it is one where two forgiven sinners kept choosing each other, and kept turning to Christ, again and again.
