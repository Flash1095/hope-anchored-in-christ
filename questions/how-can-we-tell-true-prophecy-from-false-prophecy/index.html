How can we tell true prophecy from false prophecy?

Question
How can we tell true prophecy from false prophecy?
Short Answer
True prophecy is tested by Scripture: it comes true, it agrees with God's Word, and it bears good fruit. Deuteronomy gives the test — if a prophet's prediction fails, or if they lead you away from God, do not listen to them.
Detailed Answer

The Old Testament gives two clear tests. First, accuracy: 'If what a prophet proclaims in the name of the LORD does not take place or come true, that is a message the LORD has not spoken' (Deuteronomy 18:21-22). Second, loyalty: even if a sign comes true, if the prophet says, 'Let us follow other gods,' reject them (Deuteronomy 13:1-5) — accuracy alone is not enough. Jeremiah's showdown with Hananiah illustrates both: Hananiah prophesied peace within two years, Jeremiah said the exile would last seventy, and events proved Jeremiah right (Jeremiah 28). The New Testament adds more tests: does the message confess Jesus Christ come in the flesh (1 John 4:1-3)? Does the prophet's life bear good fruit or bad (Matthew 7:15-20)? And the community's role: 'Do not treat prophecies with contempt but test them all; hold on to what is good' (1 Thessalonians 5:20-21). No modern claim to prophecy stands above Scripture — the Bible is the final court of appeal. If a prophecy contradicts God's Word, promotes the prophet, predicts dates that fail, or produces fear and control rather than repentance and hope, it fails the test.
