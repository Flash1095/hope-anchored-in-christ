Is original sin unjust?

Question
Is original sin unjust?
Short Answer
Original sin can feel unjust — why should Adam's sin affect us? The Bible answers on two levels: we are connected to Adam as our representative head, and we also personally sin. God judges no one for a sin they did not share in, and His grace in Christ more than answers Adam's fall.
Detailed Answer

The charge is fair to raise: how can it be just that one man's disobedience affects everyone? Paul's answer in Romans 5 is that Adam acted as humanity's representative head, the way a nation's leader can bind its people: 'sin entered the world through one man, and death through sin, and in this way death came to all people, because all sinned' (Romans 5:12). But Scripture also insists on personal responsibility: 'The one who sins is the one who will die... The child will not share the guilt of the parent' (Ezekiel 18:20), and Moses' law forbade punishing children for parents' sins (Deuteronomy 24:16). Both are true: we inherit a fallen nature from Adam, and we each confirm it by our own sins (Romans 3:23; Psalm 51:5). No one stands before God guilty only of Adam's sin; everyone has their own. And the symmetry of Romans 5 is the good news: 'just as one trespass resulted in condemnation for all people, so also one righteous act resulted in justification and life for all people' (Romans 5:18). If it seems unfair that Adam's fall reaches us, it is gloriously unfair that Christ's obedience reaches us too — and grace, Paul says, abounds all the more.
