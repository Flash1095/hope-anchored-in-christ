How does the Exodus point to Jesus?

Question
How does the Exodus point to Jesus?
Short Answer
The Exodus points to Jesus as the greater deliverance: where Moses led Israel out of slavery in Egypt through the blood of the Passover lamb, Jesus leads His people out of slavery to sin through His own blood on the cross.
Detailed Answer

The New Testament reads the Exodus as a deliberate preview. John the Baptist introduces Jesus as 'the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world' (John 1:29) — the Passover lamb whose blood shielded Israel's firstborn (Exodus 12) now shields all who trust in Him, and Paul calls Christ 'our Passover lamb' outright (1 Corinthians 5:7). Moses, the mediator who stood between God and Israel, points to Jesus, the greater mediator (Hebrews 3:1-6). The Red Sea crossing becomes a picture of baptism (1 Corinthians 10:1-4), and even Luke's account of the Transfiguration says Moses and Elijah spoke with Jesus about His 'departure' — the Greek word is literally 'exodus' (Luke 9:31). Israel's journey from slavery to the promised land becomes the pattern of the Christian life: delivered by grace, led through the wilderness, and heading home. When you read Exodus, you are reading the first act of a story Jesus completes.
