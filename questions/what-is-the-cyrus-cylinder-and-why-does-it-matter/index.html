What is the Cyrus Cylinder and why does it matter?

Question
What is the Cyrus Cylinder and why does it matter?
Short Answer
The Cyrus Cylinder is a Persian clay inscription from about 539 BC recording Cyrus the Great's policy of restoring exiled peoples and their temples. It corroborates the biblical account of Cyrus decreeing the Jews' return from Babylonian exile.
Detailed Answer

Imagine finding the press release of the very decree your Bible describes. The Cyrus Cylinder, a barrel-shaped clay inscription in Babylonian cuneiform from about 539 BC, now in the British Museum, records Cyrus the Great's conquest of Babylon and his policy of returning displaced peoples to their homelands and restoring their sanctuaries. Ezra opens with precisely this event: the Lord moved the heart of Cyrus, who issued a decree allowing the Jews to return to Jerusalem and rebuild the temple, even returning its stolen vessels. The cylinder does not name the Jews specifically, but it confirms the exact policy, era, and imperial style Scripture records, a striking fit between artifact and text. Isaiah adds wonder to the history: more than a century before Cyrus was born, the prophet named him as God's shepherd who would decree Jerusalem's rebuilding. Skeptics debate how to read that prophecy, but the cylinder itself is uncontested history. For our hope, the lesson is heartening. God works not only through miracles at seas and empty tombs but also through emperors, edicts, and clay. The same Lord who stirred Cyrus still directs the turns of history toward His promises, and He can open doors for His people in any generation.
