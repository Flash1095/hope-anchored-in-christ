What does the Bible say about hell?

Question
What does the Bible say about hell?
Short Answer
The Bible describes hell as the solemn reality of final judgment and separation from God's presence for those who reject Him, pictured as outer darkness and unquenchable fire. This teaching, spoken often by Jesus Himself, underscores both God's holiness and the urgency of the gospel.
Detailed Answer

Few doctrines are harder, or more important to handle honestly and tenderly, than hell. Jesus spoke of it more than anyone in Scripture, using images of Gehenna, outer darkness, weeping, and a fire that does not go out, to warn that rejecting God has eternal consequence. The New Testament describes final judgment where every person gives account, and those whose names are not in the Lamb's book of life are separated from God's life and presence, which Paul calls everlasting destruction away from the glory of His presence. Christians have discussed the precise nature of hell's duration and imagery across history, but the mainstream evangelical confession is clear that it is real, righteous, and to be avoided by fleeing to Christ. Hell ultimately reveals two truths at once: God is holy and will not call evil good or force rebels into a heaven they would hate, and sin is far more serious than our casual age assumes. Yet the beating heart of the Bible is that God takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked and has provided rescue at infinite cost. No one need go there. The cross stands open, the invitation stands wide, and today is the day of salvation for all who will come to Jesus.
