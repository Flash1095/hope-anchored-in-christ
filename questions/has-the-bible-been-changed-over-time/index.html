Has the Bible been changed over time?

Question
Has the Bible been changed over time?
Short Answer
No, the Bible has not been meaningfully changed; thousands of early manuscripts and discoveries like the Dead Sea Scrolls show the text was copied with remarkable accuracy. Minor copyist variants exist but affect no major doctrine.
Detailed Answer
Has the Bible been changed over time? It is a fair question for a book copied by hand for centuries. The answer from manuscript evidence is genuinely encouraging. The New Testament is supported by more than five thousand Greek manuscripts, some dating within decades of the originals, an embarrassment of riches compared to other ancient literature. With that volume, scholars can compare copies line by line and reconstruct the original text with very high confidence. Yes, small variants exist: spelling differences, a word order shift, an occasional scribal slip, which is exactly what honest hand-copying produces. Textual critics catalog them openly, and notably, no core Christian doctrine depends on any disputed variant. For the Old Testament, the Dead Sea Scrolls were the great test. A copy of Isaiah a thousand years older than previous manuscripts proved nearly identical, confirming the Jewish scribes' legendary care. Translations do update language, and councils recognized, rather than invented, the canon of books the churches already treasured. In short, the Bible you read today faithfully conveys what the prophets and apostles wrote. That is one more reason your hope can rest securely on its promises.
