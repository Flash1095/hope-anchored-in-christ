What is the difference between mercy and grace?

Question
What is the difference between mercy and grace?
Short Answer
Mercy is God not giving us the punishment we deserve; grace is God giving us the blessing we do not deserve. Mercy withholds judgment; grace pours out favor — and at the cross, we receive both.
Detailed Answer

The Good Samaritan is the Bible's great picture of mercy: a wounded stranger, beaten and left for dead, receives compassion, bandaging, and paid care from someone who owed him nothing (Luke 10:30-37). Mercy sees misery and moves toward it. Grace goes one step further: Paul says God, 'being rich in mercy,' made us alive with Christ 'even when we were dead in transgressions — it is by grace you have been saved' (Ephesians 2:4-5). Mercy kept us from the death we deserved; grace gave us life we could never earn. Psalm 103 holds them together: God 'does not treat us as our sins deserve' (mercy) and 'crowns you with love and compassion' (grace). If mercy is the judge dismissing your case, grace is the judge adopting you as His child and throwing a feast. Christians need both truths daily: when guilt accuses you, remember mercy — your punishment fell on Christ. When you feel unworthy of any blessing, remember grace — God delights to give good gifts to His children.
