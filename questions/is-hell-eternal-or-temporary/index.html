Is hell eternal or temporary?

Question
Is hell eternal or temporary?
Short Answer
The Bible describes hell as a real, terrible, and enduring judgment, and the mainstream Christian view has always been that it is eternal. Some faithful Christians hold a minority view called annihilationism, but all agree hell is serious — and that Christ is the way to be saved from it.
Detailed Answer

Jesus spoke about hell more than anyone else in the Bible, and His words are sobering: a place of 'eternal punishment' contrasted with 'eternal life' (Matthew 25:46), where 'their worm does not die, and the fire is not quenched' (Mark 9:48), and Revelation pictures the lake of fire enduring forever (Revelation 20:10). That is why the church has traditionally understood hell as eternal, conscious judgment — not because Christians enjoy the doctrine, but because Jesus' words point that way. A minority of Christians, called annihilationists, argue that passages like 'fear him who can destroy both soul and body in hell' (Matthew 10:28) and 'eternal destruction' (2 Thessalonians 1:9) mean the wicked are ultimately destroyed rather than tormented forever. Both sides agree on what matters most: hell is real, it is the just consequence of rejecting God, and it is avoidable — God 'wants all people to be saved' (1 Timothy 2:4), and Jesus bore judgment on the cross so that whoever trusts in Him will not face it (John 3:16; Romans 10:9-13). If this doctrine troubles you, that is a healthy response; let it drive you to Christ, and to sharing Him with others.
