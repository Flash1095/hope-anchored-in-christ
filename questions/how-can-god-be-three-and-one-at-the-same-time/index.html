How can God be three and one at the same time?

Question
How can God be three and one at the same time?
Short Answer
God is three and one because 'one' describes what God is (one divine essence) and 'three' describes who God is (three persons). It is a mystery, not a contradiction: God is not one person and three persons, but one God in three persons.
Detailed Answer
The apparent contradiction dissolves when you see that 'one' and 'three' answer different questions. God is one in essence — one divine nature, one will, one power. And God is three in person — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, each fully God, each distinct from the others, relating to one another in love. Jesus said, 'I and the Father are one' (John 10:30) — one in essence — yet He prayed to the Father (John 17), showing distinction of persons. The Spirit is sent by the Father and the Son (John 14:16-17, 26), yet is fully God. Common analogies all break down: water as ice, liquid, and steam suggests one person changing forms (the ancient error of modalism); an egg's shell, white, and yolk suggests parts of God (but each person is fully God, not a third of Him); a shamrock has three leaves but they are not persons. Analogies fail because nothing in creation is exactly like God — and that is the point. The Trinity is not a math puzzle to solve but a mystery to worship: a God who is, in Himself, a communion of love — which is why love is not something God merely does, but something He eternally is (1 John 4:8).
