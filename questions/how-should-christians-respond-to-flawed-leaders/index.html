How should Christians respond to flawed leaders?

Question
How should Christians respond to flawed leaders?
Short Answer
Christians should honor leaders, pray for them, and obey them where possible — but never follow them into sin, and never treat any leader as above truth. Scripture holds respect for authority together with loyalty to God above all.
Detailed Answer

Paul tells believers to be subject to governing authorities, since authority comes from God (Romans 13:1-7), and to pray for kings and all in authority (1 Timothy 2:1-2). But Scripture never makes obedience absolute: when the authorities ordered the apostles to stop preaching Jesus, Peter answered, 'We must obey God rather than human beings!' (Acts 5:29). David models the balance beautifully: he refused to kill Saul, God's anointed, even when Saul was hunting him — 'I will not lay my hand on my lord, because he is the LORD's anointed' (1 Samuel 24:10) — yet he also refused to follow Saul's evil or pretend it was good. For Christians today, that means: pray for leaders even when you disagree with them, speak truthfully rather than flattering or slandering, refuse any order that contradicts God's commands, and resist the idolatry of putting ultimate hope in any politician, pastor, or party. Leaders are God's servants, not God's replacements — and when they fail, as all flawed leaders do, our hope rests not in them but in the one perfect King, Jesus.
