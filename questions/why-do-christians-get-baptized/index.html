Why do Christians get baptized?

Question
Why do Christians get baptized?
Short Answer
Christians are baptized because Jesus commanded it, and because baptism pictures what God has done for us: buried with Christ and raised to new life. It is a public sign of belonging to Him and to His people.
Detailed Answer

Baptism begins with obedience. Jesus' final command to His followers was to make disciples of all nations, 'baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit' (Matthew 28:19). But baptism is far more than a ritual. Paul explains that in baptism we are buried with Christ in His death and raised to walk in newness of life (Romans 6:3-4) — going under the water pictures dying to the old life, and coming up pictures resurrection. Peter links baptism with repentance and the gift of the Holy Spirit (Acts 2:38), and Paul says that in baptism we 'clothe' ourselves with Christ (Galatians 3:27). Baptism does not earn salvation; it proclaims it. It is the visible, public way a believer says, 'I belong to Jesus,' and the church says, 'We receive you as one of us.' That is why baptism is normally done in the presence of God's people, and why it marks the beginning of the Christian life rather than its end.
