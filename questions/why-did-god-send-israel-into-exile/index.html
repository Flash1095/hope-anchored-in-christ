Why did God send Israel into exile?

Short Answer

God sent Israel into exile because, after centuries of warnings, the nation persisted in idolatry, injustice, and breaking the covenant He had made with them. Exile was God's severe discipline — but also His mercy, preserving a remnant and pointing them back to Him.

Detailed Answer

The books of Kings trace a long decline: Israel and Judah worshiped other gods, oppressed the poor, and ignored the prophets God sent again and again (2 Kings 17:7-23). Jeremiah announced the sentence: because they would not listen, Judah would serve Babylon for seventy years (Jeremiah 25:8-11). That sounds harsh until you see the patience behind it — God had warned, pleaded, and waited for generations. Exile was also covenantal: at Sinai, God had promised blessing for obedience and exile for persistent rebellion (Deuteronomy 28), and the prophets presented the exile as God keeping His word, not breaking it. Even in judgment, His purpose was redemptive: the exile stripped away false gods, preserved a faithful remnant, and prepared the way for return and, ultimately, for the Messiah. Lamentations grieves the pain honestly while confessing that God's compassions never fail (Lamentations 3:21-24). Exile shows that God takes sin seriously — and that even His discipline serves His promise to restore.
