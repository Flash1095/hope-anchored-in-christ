What can Christians learn from the exile?

Question
What can Christians learn from the exile?
Short Answer
The exile teaches God's people how to stay faithful far from home: God is present even in discipline, He works through ordinary life in a strange land, and His promises outlast our worst seasons.
Detailed Answer

When Babylon carried Judah away, everything the exiles thought was permanent — temple, king, land — was gone. Yet Jeremiah's letter to them is surprising: 'Build houses and settle down; plant gardens... seek the peace and prosperity of the city to which I have carried you into exile' (Jeremiah 29:5-7). God had not abandoned them; He was with them in Babylon, and He gave them a 70-year horizon of hope: 'I know the plans I have for you... plans to give you hope and a future' (Jeremiah 29:11). Daniel shows what that faithfulness looks like: serving a pagan king with excellence, praying openly when it was illegal, and trusting God in a lions' den (Daniel 6). The exile also teaches humility: Israel's suffering was the consequence of persistent idolatry and injustice, and God's discipline was real. But discipline is not the end of the story — the exile ends with return, rebuilding, and a deeper hope for a Messiah. For Christians living in a culture that often feels foreign, the exile is a pattern: be faithful where you are, seek the good of your neighbors, and trust that God's promises are bigger than your circumstances.
