Why does the resurrection matter if Jesus already died for sins?

Question
Why does the resurrection matter if Jesus already died for sins?
Short Answer
The resurrection matters because without it, the cross would be just another death. Paul says it plainly: 'If Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile; you are still in your sins' (1 Corinthians 15:17).
Detailed Answer

It is tempting to think the cross did all the work and the resurrection was a bonus — but the New Testament treats them as one saving event. First, the resurrection vindicates Jesus: it proves His claims were true and His sacrifice was accepted. Paul says Jesus 'was delivered over to death for our sins and was raised to life for our justification' (Romans 4:25) — the resurrection is God's 'Amen' to the cross. Second, it defeats death itself: Jesus did not just die for us, He rose ahead of us, as 'the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep' (1 Corinthians 15:20), guaranteeing our own resurrection. Third, it gives us new life now: 'just as Christ was raised from the dead... we too may live a new life' (Romans 6:4). A dead savior cannot intercede, cannot reign, and cannot return — but a risen Savior does all three: He lives to pray for us (Hebrews 7:25), He rules as King, and He will come again. That is why Peter calls the resurrection the source of 'a living hope' (1 Peter 1:3): because Jesus is alive, forgiveness is real, death is not the end, and the Christian life is powered by a living Lord, not a beautiful memory.
