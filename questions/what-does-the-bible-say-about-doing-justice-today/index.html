What does the Bible say about doing justice today?

Question
What does the Bible say about doing justice today?
Short Answer
The Bible commands God's people to do justice as part of loving Him: to defend the vulnerable, correct oppression, and live fairly. Micah sums it up: 'act justly and love mercy and walk humbly with your God' (Micah 6:8).
Detailed Answer
Justice in the Bible is not an abstract political idea; it is concrete care for real people. The prophets thundered against it when Israel's courts favored the rich, its markets cheated the poor, and its worship ignored both (Amos 5:24; Isaiah 1:17). James defines pure religion as looking after orphans and widows in their distress (James 1:27). Jesus made justice personal in the parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25-37): the question is not 'Who deserves my help?' but 'Am I being a neighbor?' And He warned that at the final judgment, how we treated the hungry, the stranger, and the prisoner will matter (Matthew 25:35-40). Doing justice today looks like: paying workers fairly, telling the truth in business, defending the unborn and the abused, welcoming the stranger, caring for the poor in your own community, and refusing to use power to exploit others. It is always paired with mercy and humility (Micah 6:8) — justice without mercy becomes cruelty, and mercy without justice becomes sentimentality. Christians do justice not to earn God's favor, but because a just God has shown them mercy, and His people should look like Him.
