How can a loving God also be just?

Question
How can a loving God also be just?
Short Answer
God's love and justice meet at the cross. Justice means God takes evil seriously and will not let it stand; love means He took the penalty for that evil upon Himself in Jesus, so sinners can be forgiven without justice being ignored.
Detailed Answer

If God were only loving in the sentimental sense, He would shrug at cruelty, abuse, and murder — and a God who shrugs at evil is not good. If He were only just, every one of us would stand condemned, because 'all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God' (Romans 3:23). The Bible refuses to choose. Psalm 85 says 'Love and faithfulness meet together; righteousness and peace kiss each other' (Psalm 85:10) — and that meeting happens at the cross. Paul explains: God put Christ forward as a sacrifice 'so as to be just and the one who justifies those who have faith in Jesus' (Romans 3:25-26). At the cross, God's justice was fully satisfied — sin was punished — and God's love was fully displayed — the punishment fell on His own Son, in our place. And justice is not finished yet: Scripture promises a final judgment where every wrong will be set right (Revelation 20:11-15). A loving God is just because He will not leave evil unaddressed; a just God is loving because He addressed it Himself, at His own cost, so that we could be free.
