Why does God seem silent in my hardest seasons?

Question
Why does God seem silent in my hardest seasons?
Short Answer
God's silence in Scripture is never His absence: He was silent before the Exodus, before Easter, and with Job, yet working His purposes throughout. In silent seasons, hold to His promises, lament honestly, and trust that the God who seemed silent on Saturday raised Jesus on Sunday.
Detailed Answer

There is a particular ache in crying out to heaven and hearing nothing back. If God feels silent in your hardest season, your experience has deep biblical precedent, and precedent with a hopeful pattern. Israel groaned under slavery for years before the Exodus, yet God later says He heard, remembered, and knew all along. Job endured long chapters of divine quiet before God spoke. David's psalms oscillate between Why do you hide your face? and renewed trust, sometimes within verses. Above all, Holy Saturday stands between cross and resurrection as history's silent day, when every appearance said God had finished, while He was in fact accomplishing everything. Silence in Scripture is consistently the prelude, not the conclusion. God may be growing roots where you expected fruit, teaching a trust that does not depend on feelings, or simply inviting the honesty of lament, which is itself an act of faith. Practically, stay tethered in silence: keep a simple rhythm of Scripture and prayer, borrow your church's faith in worship, tell trusted believers the truth, and resist interpreting silence as rejection. The cross forbids that interpretation forever. Your Father has not moved. Hold on through Saturday. This story, and yours in Christ, ends with a Sunday morning.
