Why did God allow Adam and Eve to sin?

Why did God allow Adam and Eve to sin?

Short Answer

God allowed Adam and Eve to sin because He made them with real freedom, and real love requires real choice. He did not cause their sin, but He permitted it — and from the beginning, He had a plan to redeem it through Christ.

Detailed Answer

This is one of the hardest questions in the Bible, and Scripture does not give us a complete explanation — it gives us truths we hold together. God made Adam and Eve good and gave them one command (Genesis 2:16-17), which means obedience was meaningful precisely because disobedience was possible. Love that cannot choose is not love; it is programming. God warned them plainly, so their choice was informed and free. At the same time, God was not caught off guard: before the foundation of the world, He had chosen a people in Christ (Ephesians 1:4-5), and Revelation speaks of 'the Lamb who was slain from the creation of the world' (Revelation 13:8). God permitted the Fall as part of a larger story in which His grace, mercy, and justice would be displayed more fully than they ever could be in an unfallen world — supremely at the cross, where He took the consequences of human sin upon Himself. Paul ends his own wrestling with God's purposes in wonder: 'Oh, the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable his judgments' (Romans 11:33). We may not understand fully, but we can trust the God who brought redemption out of rebellion.
