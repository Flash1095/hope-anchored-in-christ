What was the Babylonian exile?

Question
What was the Babylonian exile?
Short Answer
The Babylonian exile was God's judgment in which Judah was conquered by Babylon, Jerusalem and its temple were destroyed in 586 BC, and many Judeans were deported to Babylon. After seventy years, God brought a remnant back under Persia to rebuild.
Detailed Answer

The Babylonian exile is the Old Testament's darkest valley, and one of its most hope-producing. After generations of prophetic warnings, Nebuchadnezzar's Babylon destroyed Jerusalem in 586 BC, burned Solomon's temple, and carried Judah's leaders, artisans, and families into exile by the rivers of Babylon, where Psalm 137 says they wept and hung up their harps. Exile raised agonizing questions: Had God abandoned His promises to Abraham and David? Jeremiah's answer reordered everything. The exile was God's discipline, not His desertion, and even in Babylon His people were to seek the city's welfare, build homes, and trust His astonishing promise: plans to prosper you and not to harm you, plans to give you hope and a future. After seventy years, just as Jeremiah foretold, God moved Cyrus of Persia to decree the return. Under Zerubbabel, Ezra, and Nehemiah, a remnant rebuilt the temple and walls, frail beginnings of restoration. The exile teaches that God's discipline is real but never His final word, that hope survives in foreign lands, and that the God who judges also gathers His scattered people home. In Christ, that homecoming reaches the nations.
