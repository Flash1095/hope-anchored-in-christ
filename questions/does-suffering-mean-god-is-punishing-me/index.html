Does suffering mean God is punishing me?

Question
Does suffering mean God is punishing me?
Short Answer
Not necessarily — and often, no. The Bible shows suffering coming from many causes: a fallen world, other people's sins, spiritual opposition, and sometimes God's discipline — but personal suffering is not reliable evidence that God is punishing you for a specific sin.
Detailed Answer

Job is the great counterexample: Job was blameless, yet suffered catastrophically, and his friends' insistence that he must have sinned was rebuked by God (Job 42:7). Jesus made the same point when asked about a man born blind: 'Neither this man nor his parents sinned' (John 9:3) — his suffering had another purpose entirely. That said, Scripture does recognize two narrower cases. Sometimes suffering is the natural consequence of our choices (Galatians 6:7-8) — and naming that honestly can be freeing. And sometimes God disciplines His children the way a father disciplines a son — not to punish in anger, but to train in holiness (Hebrews 12:5-11). The difference matters: punishment looks backward and condemns; discipline looks forward and restores. How do you tell? Ask honestly: Is there a sin I need to confess? If yes, confess it and receive grace (1 John 1:9). If no, do not invent one — bring your suffering to God like the psalmists did, lean on His people, and trust that Romans 8:1 stands: 'There is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.' Suffering may refine you, but it does not mean God has turned against you.
