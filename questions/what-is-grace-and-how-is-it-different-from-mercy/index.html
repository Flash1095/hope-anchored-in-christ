What is grace and how is it different from mercy?

Short Answer

Grace is God giving us good we do not deserve; mercy is God withholding the punishment we do deserve. They are two sides of His kindness: mercy spares us, and grace blesses us beyond sparing.

Detailed Answer

A classic way to remember it: mercy is not getting what you deserve; grace is getting what you do not deserve. When God showed mercy to Israel, He did not destroy them for their rebellion (Lamentations 3:22-23). When He showed grace, He went further — He adopted rebels as His children and gave them eternal life (Ephesians 2:4-9). Paul packs both into one sentence: 'it is by grace you have been saved, through faith' (Ephesians 2:8) — salvation is grace because we could never earn it, and mercy because we deserved judgment instead. Titus says God saved us 'not because of righteous things we had done, but because of his mercy' (Titus 3:5), and then calls that salvation an outpouring of grace. At the cross, mercy and grace meet: God withholds the punishment our sins deserved and gives us, in Christ, righteousness, adoption, and hope we could never merit. Mercy says, 'You will not be punished.' Grace says, 'You will be welcomed home as a beloved child.'
