What evidence is there for the resurrection?

Question
What evidence is there for the resurrection?
Short Answer
The evidence for the resurrection includes the empty tomb, the eyewitness appearances, the transformation of the disciples, and an early creed dating to within a few years of the event. Together they make the resurrection the best explanation of the facts, not a blind leap.
Detailed Answer

Start with the earliest evidence: Paul quotes a creed in 1 Corinthians 15:3-7 — 'Christ died for our sins... was buried... was raised on the third day... appeared to Cephas, then to the Twelve' — that scholars date to within a few years of the crucifixion, too early for legend to grow. Then the empty tomb: all four Gospels agree the tomb was found empty by women, whose testimony counted for little in that culture — an awkward detail no invented story would include (Mark 16:1-8). Then the appearances: to individuals, to groups, to skeptics like Thomas (John 20:24-29) and James, Jesus' own brother, who had not believed during His ministry (John 7:5) but became a leader of the Jerusalem church. Then the transformation: frightened disciples who fled at Jesus' arrest were, weeks later, publicly proclaiming Him risen in the very city where He was executed (Acts 2:32) — and most would die for that claim. No single piece proves the resurrection alone, but together they point one way: the tomb was empty because Jesus rose. As Paul put it, if Christ has not been raised, Christian faith is useless (1 Corinthians 15:14) — which is exactly why the evidence matters.
