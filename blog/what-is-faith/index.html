What Is Faith?

2026-10-27 · Foundations of Faith · Hebrews 11:1

Series: Foundations of Faith · Part 1

Everyone talks about faith. "Have faith," people say, usually when there is nothing else left to say. But what is faith, really? Is it a feeling? A leap in the dark? Positive thinking with a Bible verse attached?

The Bible gives us a definition, not a slogan:

"Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen." — Hebrews 11:1 (KJV)

Two words in that verse do most of the work: substance and evidence. Faith is not the absence of evidence. It is the settled conviction that what God has promised is as solid as what you can already see — the ground under your feet before the house is built.

Faith Is Trust in a Person

Faith always has an object. You do not simply "have faith" in the air; you trust someone. A child trusts a parent's hand on a dark staircase not because the child has mapped every step, but because the child knows whose hand it is.

Christian faith is like that. Its object is not an idea, not a system, and not our own sincerity. Its object is Jesus Christ — who He is, what He has done, and what He has promised. That is why this blog is called Hope Anchored in Christ: an anchor only holds if it is fastened to something stronger than the storm. Hebrews 6:19 calls our hope "an anchor of the soul, both sure and stedfast." Faith is the rope. Christ is the rock.

What Faith Is Not

It helps to clear away what faith is not, because the counterfeits are everywhere.

Faith is not a blind leap. A leap in the dark asks you to stop thinking. Biblical faith asks you to open your eyes. God has given reasons to trust Him: a creation that declares its Maker, a resurrection witnessed by hundreds, promises kept across centuries. Faith leans on evidence; it does not run from it.

Faith is not wishful thinking. Wishing something were true does not make it true, and naming a desire does not oblige God to grant it. Faith does not bend God's will to ours. It rests in His will, even when the answer is "wait" or "no."

Faith is not merely agreeing with facts. The Bible says the devils believe that God exists — and tremble (James 2:19). Agreement in the head, with no trust in the heart and no obedience in the life, is not faith. It is information.

Faith is not earning. "For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: not of works, lest any man should boast" (Ephesians 2:8–9). Faith is the empty hand that receives. It is not the wage that earns.

Faith in Action

Hebrews 11 is sometimes called the Hall of Faith, and it reads less like a museum and more like a march. Abel offered. Noah built. Abraham went out, "not knowing whither he went" (Hebrews 11:8). Moses chose suffering with God's people over the treasures of Egypt.

Notice the pattern: in every case, faith did something. It built an ark before a single drop of rain. It left home without a map. Faith is not proven by intensity of feeling but by obedience in the next step — the step you can see, taken because you trust the One who sees the whole road.

That is also how faith grows. "Faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God" (Romans 10:17). You do not grow faith by staring at your faith. You grow it by looking at Christ, in His Word, again and again, until trust becomes the settled habit of your heart. And when trials come — and they will — they do not destroy faith. Peter says they refine it, "much more precious than of gold that perisheth" (1 Peter 1:7).

The Anchor Holds

So what is faith? It is confident trust in Jesus Christ — grounded in evidence, expressed in obedience, received as a gift, and strong enough to hold when everything else gives way.

If your faith feels small today, that is not the end of the story. A small anchor in a strong rock holds better than a great anchor in sand. The question is never how much faith you can work up. The question is whether the One you trust is worthy of it.

He is.
