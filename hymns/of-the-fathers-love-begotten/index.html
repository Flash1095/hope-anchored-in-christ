Of the Father's Love Begotten

The Latin poet Aurelius Prudentius wrote this hymn of Christ's eternal sonship in the late fourth or early fifth century, as part of a longer poem for the hours of the day. It confesses that the Son, begotten before the world began, was born of Mary yet remains God. John Mason Neale's translation and the medieval chant tune Divinum Mysterium carry it in English hymnals; it is also known by the alternate title Of the Father's Heart Begotten.
