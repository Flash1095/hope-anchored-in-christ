Judah

Civilization
Judah
Time Period
c. 1400 BC – first century AD
Region
Southern hill country, from Jerusalem to Beersheba
History
Judah was Jacob's fourth son by Leah, whose name means praise. Jacob's blessing gave Judah the sceptre and the ruler's staff (Genesis 49:10), and the tribe produced the house of David. Judah's allotment covered the southern hill country including Hebron and, later, Jerusalem. After Solomon's death Judah became the southern kingdom, with Jerusalem and the temple, and it outlasted the northern kingdom by over a century before Babylon destroyed Jerusalem in 586 BC. The returning exiles were largely Judahites, and from 'Judah' comes the name 'Jew'. Jesus was born of Judah, of David's line, in Bethlehem.
Culture
Judah's culture was centred on Jerusalem, the temple, and the Davidic court. Its towns included Hebron, Bethlehem, and Lachish, and its people were farmers, shepherds, and traders of the southern hills.
Religion
Judah's religion was temple religion: sacrifice, priesthood, and the festivals in Jerusalem, guarded by prophets who called kings and people back to the covenant. After the exile, Judah's community rebuilt temple worship and the law became central to Jewish life.
Bible Connection
Judah carries the Bible's central thread: the promise of a ruler from Judah runs from Genesis 49 through David to Jesus, called the Lion of the tribe of Judah in Revelation 5.
