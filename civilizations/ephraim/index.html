Ephraim

Civilization
Ephraim
Time Period
c. 1400–722 BC
Region
Central hill country, Shechem and Shiloh
History
Ephraim was Joseph's younger son, adopted by Jacob as a full tribal ancestor (Genesis 48). Jacob deliberately crossed his hands to bless Ephraim above his elder brother Manasseh (Genesis 48:17-20). Ephraim's allotment covered the central hill country, including Shechem and Shiloh, where the tabernacle stood. Joshua was an Ephraimite, and the tribe's pride and power made it the leading tribe of the north: after the kingdom divided, 'Ephraim' became shorthand for the whole northern kingdom, and the prophets use the name that way (Hosea 7:8). Samaria, the northern capital, stood in Ephraim's hills. The kingdom fell to Assyria in 722 BC.
Culture
Ephraim's culture was that of the central highlands: hill farming, the sanctuary at Shiloh, and the political weight of the largest and most influential northern tribe.
Religion
Ephraim shared Israel's worship of the LORD, centred first on the tabernacle at Shiloh, but after the division its kings set up rival sanctuaries at Bethel and Dan, which the prophets condemned.
Bible Connection
Ephraim embodies the younger son chosen over the elder — and the northern kingdom's rise and fall: from Joshua's tribe to Hosea's wayward namesake, still addressed by God as 'my dear son' (Jeremiah 31:20).
Key Scriptures
