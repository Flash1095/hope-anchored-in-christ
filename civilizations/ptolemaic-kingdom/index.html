Ptolemaic Kingdom

Civilization
Ptolemaic Kingdom
Time Period
c. 305-30 BC
Region
Egypt, capital Alexandria, with Cyprus, Cyrene, and at times the Levant
History
Ptolemy I, another of Alexander's generals, seized Egypt and founded a Greek dynasty that ruled from Alexandria for nearly three centuries. The Ptolemies fought the Seleucids for the Levant in the Syrian Wars, and Alexandria's library and lighthouse became wonders of the world. The last ruler, Cleopatra VII, allied with Rome's losers and died as Octavian took Egypt in 30 BC.
Culture
Ptolemaic Alexandria was the ancient world's center of learning — its library, its scholars, and its Greek translation of the Hebrew Scriptures, the Septuagint, which carried the Old Testament to the Greek-speaking world and shaped the New Testament's quotations.
Religion
Ptolemies blended Greek and Egyptian worship, presenting themselves as pharaohs and gods, while Alexandria's large Jewish community worshipped at its synagogues and produced the Septuagint.
Bible Connection
Daniel 11's kings of the South are the Ptolemies, matched against the Seleucid kings of the North, and the Septuagint made in their capital is the Bible most New Testament writers quote.
Key Scriptures
Daniel 11:5-20
Related Places
