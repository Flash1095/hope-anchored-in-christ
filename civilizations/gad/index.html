Gad

Civilization
Gad
Time Period
c. 1400–732 BC
Region
East of the Jordan, Gilead from the Arnon to the Jabbok
History
Gad was Jacob's seventh son, by Leah's servant Zilpah. Jacob blessed him as a troop that would be raided and would raid in return (Genesis 49:19). Like Reuben, Gad asked Moses for Transjordan pasture land (Numbers 32) and settled Gilead. Gadites were famed as warriors: eleven Gadite captains joined David in the wilderness (1 Chronicles 12:8-15), and Gad's territory bore the brunt of Syrian and Ammonite attacks. The Mesha Stele, Moab's own record, says Gad had long dwelt in the land of Ataroth. Gad was deported with the other Transjordan tribes by Tiglath-Pileser (1 Chronicles 5:26).
Culture
Gad's culture was that of a warrior-pastoral frontier tribe, raising flocks in Gilead and manning the eastern border against Moab, Ammon, and Syria.
Religion
Gad shared Israel's worship of the LORD, with its religious life centred on the sanctuary across the Jordan; its territory included towns with altars and, later, the worship centres of the east.
Bible Connection
Gad embodies the Transjordan bargain: land east of the Jordan in exchange for fighting alongside the other tribes for Canaan, a bargain its warriors honoured from Moses to David.
Key Scriptures
Genesis 30:11; Genesis 49:19; Numbers 32:29; Joshua 13:24-28; 1 Chronicles 12:8-15

Gad; Jacob; Moses; Joshua
