Israel

Civilization
Israel
Time Period
c. 1900 BC – first century AD
Region
Canaan and the land of Israel, eastern Mediterranean coast
History
Israel begins with Jacob, renamed Israel after wrestling with God (Genesis 32). His twelve sons became the twelve tribes. After slavery in Egypt, the Exodus under Moses and the conquest under Joshua gave them the land of Canaan. Under David and Solomon Israel was one kingdom; after Solomon it split into Israel in the north and Judah in the south. Assyria destroyed the northern kingdom in 722 BC and Babylon destroyed Judah in 586 BC, but a remnant returned under Cyrus and rebuilt Jerusalem and the temple. In the New Testament the name Israel still denotes God's covenant people, and Paul wrestles with Israel's place in God's purposes in Romans 9–11.
Culture
Israelite culture was built around the family, the tribe, and the covenant law given at Sinai. Agriculture, shepherding, and later town life shaped daily existence; festivals at the tabernacle and then the temple marked the year, and the law governed worship, justice, and neighbourly life.
Religion
Israel worshipped the LORD alone, in contrast to the many gods of its neighbours. The covenant at Sinai, the sacrificial system, the priesthood, and the prophets' call to justice and exclusive loyalty to God define its religion. Idolatry and the worship of Baal and other gods are presented throughout the history as the recurring breach of that covenant.
Bible Connection
Israel is the central people of the whole Bible: the Old Testament is largely their story, and the New Testament presents Jesus as Israel's Messiah, born of Judah, and the church as grafted into Israel's covenant promises.
