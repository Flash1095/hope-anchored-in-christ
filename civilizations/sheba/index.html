Sheba

Civilization
Sheba
Time Period
c. 1000 BC-AD 275
Region
Southwestern Arabia (modern Yemen)
History
Sheba, the Sabaean kingdom of southwestern Arabia, grew wealthy on the frankincense and myrrh trade. Its queen visited Solomon to test his wisdom and left laden with gifts (1 Kings 10). Sabeans raided Job's livestock (Job 1:15), Sheba's merchants traded with Tyre (Ezekiel 27:22), and the prophets picture Sheba's gold and incense coming to Jerusalem (Psalms 72:10; Isaiah 60:6).
Culture
Sabaeans built an irrigation civilization around the Marib dam, carved inscriptions in their own South Arabian script, and ran caravans carrying incense, spices, gold, and precious stones across Arabia.
Religion
Sabaeans worshipped astral deities, chief among them the moon god Almaqah and a sun goddess, with temples and offerings tied to their irrigation state.
Bible Connection
Sheba's queen is the Bible's great example of a foreign ruler seeking Israel's wisdom, and Jesus pointed to her — the queen of the south — as a witness against his own generation (Matthew 12:42).
Key Scriptures
Genesis 10:28; 1 Kings 10:1; Job 1:15; Psalms 72:10; Isaiah 60:6; Ezekiel 27:22; Matthew 12:42
Related Places
Jerusalem; Tyre
