Simeon

Civilization
Simeon
Time Period
c. 1400–586 BC
Region
The Negev within Judah's allotment, around Beersheba
History
Simeon was Jacob's second son by Leah. With his brother Levi he avenged Dinah by killing the men of Shechem (Genesis 34), and Jacob's blessing scattered both brothers in Israel (Genesis 49:5-7). Simeon's allotment lay inside Judah's territory in the south (Joshua 19:1-9), and the tribe was gradually absorbed into Judah; Simeonites are still named among those who joined Hezekiah's and Josiah's reforms (2 Chronicles 15:9; 34:6). By the second census the tribe had already shrunk sharply (Numbers 26:14).
Culture
Simeon shared Judah's southern, semi-pastoral culture, living in Negev towns such as Beersheba among Judah's settlements rather than as a separate territorial power.
Religion
Simeon shared Israel's worship of the LORD, with no separate cult or priesthood of its own; its towns fell under Judah's religious life centred on Jerusalem.
Bible Connection
Simeon's history illustrates Jacob's blessing fulfilled literally: a tribe scattered within Israel rather than holding its own land, yet still counted among the twelve and named in the sealing of Revelation 7.
Key Scriptures
Genesis 29:33; Genesis 34:25-31; Genesis 49:5-7; Numbers 26:14; Joshua 19:1-9
Related People
Simeon; Jacob; Moses; Joshua
Related Places
Beersheba
