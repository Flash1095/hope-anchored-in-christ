Midianites

Civilization
Midianites
Time Period
Patriarchal era through the judges, c. 2000-1100 BC
Region
East of the Gulf of Aqaba, the Sinai wilderness, and Transjordan (northwest Arabia)
History
The Midianites descended from Midian, son of Abraham and Keturah (Genesis 25:2). Moses fled to Midian after killing the Egyptian, married Zipporah, daughter of Jethro the priest of Midian, and met God at the burning bush there (Exodus 2-3). Midian joined Moab in hiring Balaam (Numbers 22) and in the Baal-peor affair (Numbers 25), for which Israel made war on Midian (Numbers 31). In Gideon's day the Midianites, with Amalek and the children of the east, oppressed Israel for seven years until Gideon's three hundred routed them (Judges 6-8).
Culture
Nomadic herders and caravan traders of the desert fringe, living in tents and moving with their flocks. Their caravans linked Arabia, Egypt, and Canaan, and their kings are named in the Gideon campaign (Oreb, Zeeb, Zebah, Zalmunna).
Religion
Midianite religion included Baal-peor, worshipped with Moab in Numbers 25. Yet Jethro, priest of Midian, is shown acknowledging the LORD and offering sacrifice with Moses and Aaron (Exodus 18).
Bible Connection
Midian is where Moses found refuge, a wife, and his calling; and where Gideon won his great victory. The Midianite caravan also carried Joseph toward Egypt (Genesis 37:28, where the traders are called both Midianites and Ishmaelites).
Key Scriptures
Genesis 25:2; Exodus 2:15-21; Exodus 18:1-12; Numbers 25:16-18; Numbers 31:1-12; Judges 6:1-6; Judges 7:19-25
Related People
Moses; Jethro; Hobab; Gideon; Midian; Keturah; Zipporah; Reuel
Related Places
Midian; Mount Sinai
Related Events
Gideon's Victory over Midian; Joseph and His Brothers; The Burning Bush
