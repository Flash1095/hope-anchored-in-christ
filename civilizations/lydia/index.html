Lydia

Civilization
Lydia
Time Period
c. 1200-546 BC
Region
Western Asia Minor (modern Turkey), around Sardis
History
Lud, son of Shem (Genesis 10:22), is the ancestor of the Lydians, whose kingdom in western Asia Minor grew rich on trade and is traditionally credited with the first coined money. Under kings such as Alyattes and Croesus the kingdom flourished until Cyrus of Persia conquered it in 546 BC. In the prophets, Lud's bowmen serve Tyre and fall with Egypt's allies.
Culture
Lydians were traders and craftsmen, famed for textiles, dyes, and gold from the Pactolus river, and for an early coinage that spread across the ancient world.
Religion
Lydians worshipped Anatolian deities, including the mother goddess Cybele, alongside Greek and Near Eastern gods of the region.
Bible Connection
Lydia appears as Lud among the nations in Ezekiel's lament over Tyre (Ezekiel 27:10), among the peoples falling with Egypt (Ezekiel 30:5), and among the nations to which escapees are sent (Isaiah 66:19). The Lydia of Acts 16 is a different matter: she is an individual woman from Thyatira, not the nation, and is not cited here.
Key Scriptures
Genesis 10:22; Isaiah 66:19; Ezekiel 27:10; Ezekiel 30:5
Related Places
Tyre
