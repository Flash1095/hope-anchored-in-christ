Akkadian Empire

Civilization
Akkadian Empire
Time Period
c. 2334-2154 BC
Region
Mesopotamia, centered on Akkad
History
Sargon of Akkad, a court official who seized power, conquered the Sumerian city-states and built the first empire in history, stretching from Elam to the Mediterranean. His dynasty held Mesopotamia for about 150 years before the empire fell to Gutian pressure and internal revolt.
Culture
Akkadians spoke a Semitic language that became Mesopotamia's lingua franca. Their scribes adapted Sumerian cuneiform, and their art — victory stelae and cylinder seals — set the imperial style later empires copied.
Religion
Akkadians worshipped the Mesopotamian pantheon, chief among them Anu, Enlil, and Ishtar. Naram-Sin went further, declaring himself a living god, a claim later kings echoed and Scripture answers.
Bible Connection
Genesis 10:10 names Accad (Akkad) among the first cities of Nimrod's kingdom in Shinar, placing the Akkadian heartland on the Bible's map of the earliest post-Flood powers. The Babel story is set in the same land of Shinar where Akkad rose.
Key Scriptures
Genesis 10:8-12; Genesis 11:1-9
Related People
Nimrod
