Manasseh

Civilization
Manasseh
Time Period
c. 1400–722 BC
Region
Both sides of the Jordan: central hill country west, and Bashan and Gilead east
History
Manasseh was Joseph's elder son, adopted by Jacob as a tribal ancestor alongside Ephraim (Genesis 48). The tribe was the only one besides Reuben and Gad to hold land on both sides of the Jordan: half settled the central hills west of the river, and half took Bashan and northern Gilead east of it (Joshua 17; Numbers 32:33-42). Gideon, who delivered Israel from Midian, was a Manassite of Abiezer (Judges 6:15), and the judge Jephthah was a Gileadite. Manasseh's eastern half was deported early by Assyria, and the western half fell with Samaria in 722 BC.
Culture
Manasseh's culture was split by the Jordan: hill farming in the west, and pastoral, frontier life in Bashan and Gilead in the east, where its warriors guarded Israel's northern border.
Religion
Manasseh shared Israel's worship of the LORD, with its religious life centred on the sanctuary at Shiloh and later the temple, though its eastern towns lived far from both.
Bible Connection
Manasseh's double territory made it a bridge tribe: its western half gave the north its heartland, its eastern half its frontier, and its Gideon gave Israel one of its greatest deliverers.
Key Scriptures
Genesis 41:51; Genesis 48:13-20; Numbers 26:28-34; Joshua 17:1-6; Numbers 32:33-42; Judges 6:15
