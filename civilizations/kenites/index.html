Kenites

Civilization
Kenites
Time Period
Patriarchal era through the monarchy
Region
The Negev and the southern wilderness, associated with Midian and with Judah's southern border
History
The Kenites are named in the land promise of Genesis 15:19 and in Balaam's oracle, which pictures their nest set in the rock (Numbers 24:21). The children of the Kenite, Moses' father-in-law, went up with Judah into the wilderness of Judah (Judges 1:16), and Heber the Kenite had separated from the Kenites when his wife Jael killed Sisera (Judges 4:11, 17-22). Saul warned the Kenites to leave the Amalekites before his campaign, because they had shown kindness to Israel coming out of Egypt (1 Samuel 15:6). David shared spoil with the Kenites (1 Samuel 30:29), and 1 Chronicles 2:55 counts scribal families among the Kenites, including the house of Rechab, whose abstinent rule Jeremiah 35 praises.
Culture
The name is traditionally connected with metalworking (Hebrew qayin, 'smith'), and the Kenites lived as tent-dwellers on the desert fringe, at home among both Midian and Judah. The Rechabites kept Jonadab's rule: no wine, no houses, no sown fields.
Religion
Jethro's family acknowledged the LORD (Exodus 18), and the Rechabites' obedience to their ancestor's command is held up in Jeremiah 35 as a rebuke to Judah's disobedience.
Bible Connection
Kenites are Israel's desert friends: kin by marriage to Moses, allies who showed kindness at the exodus, and the people whose tent Jael's peg ended Sisera's army.
Key Scriptures
Genesis 15:19; Numbers 24:21; Judges 1:16; Judges 4:11; Judges 4:17-22; 1 Samuel 15:6; 1 Chronicles 2:55; Jeremiah 35:6-10
Related People
Moses; Jethro; Hobab; Deborah; Barak
Related Places
Canaan; Midian
Related Events
Deborah and Barak Defeat Sisera
