Cush

Cush, son of Ham (Genesis 10:6) and father of Nimrod, is the biblical name for the Nubian kingdom the Greeks called Ethiopia. Kushite kings ruled Egypt as its Twenty-fifth Dynasty, and Cush appears throughout the prophets as a distant southern nation. The Ethiopian eunuch baptized by Philip (Acts 8) shows Cushites still traveling to Jerusalem to worship in the New Testament period. In English Bibles the people of Cush are also called the Ethiopians.

Culture

Kushites built pyramids at Meroe, worked iron, and traded gold, ivory, ebony, and exotic goods up the Nile to Egypt and beyond.

Bible Connection

From Nimrod's kingdom to the psalmist's promise that Ethiopia shall stretch out her hands to God (Psalms 68:31), Cush stands for the far south brought within God's reach — a theme completed when Philip baptizes the Ethiopian court official.

Details

Civilization: Cush
Time Period: c. 2000 BC-AD 350
Region: Upper Nile, south of Egypt (modern Sudan)
Religion: Kushites worshipped Amun and other gods of the Nile valley, with great temples at Napata and Meroe blending Egyptian and Nubian religion.
Key Scriptures: Genesis 10:6; Psalms 68:31; Isaiah 18:1; Isaiah 43:3; Ezekiel 29:10; Acts 8:27
Related People: Nimrod; Philip
Related Places: Egypt
