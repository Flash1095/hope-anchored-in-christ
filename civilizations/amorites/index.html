Amorites

Civilization
Amorites
Time Period
Bronze Age, c. 2000-1200 BC
Region
The hill country of Canaan and Transjordan, including the kingdoms of Sihon at Heshbon and Og at Bashan
History
The Amorites were one of the major peoples of Canaan. In Genesis 15:16 the LORD tells Abram that his descendants will return to the land when 'the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full,' naming the Amorites for the land's peoples as a whole. In Numbers 21, Sihon king of the Amorites refused Israel passage and was defeated, and Og king of Bashan was defeated likewise, giving Israel its first Transjordan territory. Amos 2:9-10 recalls them as a people tall as cedars and strong as oaks, destroyed before Israel. Amorite names also appear widely in Mesopotamian records of the period.
Culture
A Semitic people who moved from a pastoral life into cities and kingdoms across the Levant and Mesopotamia. In Canaan they held fortified hill-country towns and, east of the Jordan, organized kingdoms with kings, armies, and walled cities such as Heshbon.
Religion
They shared the religious world of Bronze Age Canaan, worshipping the gods of the Canaanite pantheon. The Bible presents their worship as part of the idolatry Israel was commanded to reject.
Bible Connection
The Amorites are the standing example of the peoples whose land Israel inherited: named in the promise to Abram, defeated in the wilderness, listed among the seven nations to be driven out, and remembered by the prophets as proof of God's judgment and gift.
Key Scriptures
Genesis 15:16; Genesis 15:21; Numbers 21:21-35; Deuteronomy 1:27; Deuteronomy 7:1; Amos 2:9-10
Related People
Abraham; Moses; Sihon; Og; Joshua
Related Places
Canaan; Jordan River; Hebron
