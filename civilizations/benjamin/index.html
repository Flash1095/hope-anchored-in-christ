Benjamin

Civilization
Benjamin
Time Period
c. 1400 BC – first century AD
Region
The central hill country between Judah and Ephraim, including Jerusalem's northern approach
History
Benjamin was Jacob's youngest son, by Rachel, who died bearing him; Jacob called him son of his right hand (Genesis 35:18), and Moses' blessing pictured him dwelling safely beside the LORD (Deuteronomy 33:12). Benjamin's small allotment sat between Judah and Ephraim and included Jerusalem's northern approaches, Gibeah, Mizpah, and Bethel. The tribe nearly destroyed itself in the civil war over Gibeah (Judges 19–21). Saul, Israel's first king, was a Benjamite, and after the kingdom divided Benjamin stayed with Judah. Paul the apostle was of the tribe of Benjamin (Philippians 3:5).
Culture
Benjamin's culture was that of a small, fierce border tribe: its men were famed as left-handed slingers and archers, and its towns guarded the passes between north and south.
Religion
Benjamin shared Israel's worship of the LORD, and its territory held some of Israel's oldest sanctuaries, including Bethel and, later, the Jerusalem temple on its border with Judah.
Bible Connection
Benjamin's story runs from near-annihilation to the apostle to the Gentiles: the smallest tribe gave Israel its first king and, centuries later, Paul.
Key Scriptures
Genesis 35:18; Genesis 49:27; Deuteronomy 33:12; Joshua 18:11-28; Judges 20:1-3; 1 Samuel 9:1-2; Philippians 3:5
