Amalekites

Civilization
Amalekites
Time Period
c. 2000-700 BC, from Abraham's day to Hezekiah's reign
Region
The Negev, the Sinai wilderness, and the southern borders of Canaan, ranging at times into the hill country
History
The Amalekites appear as early as Genesis 14:7, in the days of Abraham, and Genesis 36:12 traces Amalek to Eliphaz son of Esau. They attacked Israel at Rephidim after the exodus (Exodus 17:8-16), where Moses' raised hands decided the battle; Balaam's oracle called Amalek 'the first of the nations' (Numbers 24:20); and Deuteronomy 25:17-19 commanded Israel to remember how they struck the stragglers and to blot out their remembrance. Saul's failure to destroy them cost him the kingdom (1 Samuel 15), David destroyed the raiders who burned Ziklag (1 Samuel 30), and a remnant was wiped out in Hezekiah's days (1 Chronicles 4:43).
Culture
Nomadic raiders of the southern desert, striking the weak and the rear of a marching people, then vanishing into the wilderness. They lived by plunder rather than by cities.
Religion
The text describes no Amalekite religion in detail; their defining mark in the Bible is hostility to Israel, from Rephidim to Haman's later line (Esther 3:1 traces Haman to Agag).
Bible Connection
Amalek is Israel's standing enemy: the first nation to make war on Israel after the exodus, the test of Saul's obedience, and the target of David's vengeance at Ziklag.
Key Scriptures
Genesis 14:7; Exodus 17:8-16; Numbers 24:20; Deuteronomy 25:17-19; 1 Samuel 15:1-9; 1 Samuel 30:1-18
Related People
Moses; Joshua; King Saul; King David; Amalek; Esau
Related Places
Canaan; Kadesh Barnea
