Geshurites

Civilization
Geshurites
Time Period
Conquest era through David's reign, c. 1400-1000 BC
Region
Geshur, east of the Sea of Galilee, bordering Bashan and Aram
History
Geshur was a small Aramean kingdom on Israel's northeastern border. Deuteronomy 3:14 notes that Geshur and Maacath were not driven out, and Joshua 12:5 and 13:13 record them as peoples left among Israel. Geshur became royal kin to David: Maacah, daughter of Talmai king of Geshur, was the mother of Absalom (2 Samuel 3:3), and after killing Amnon, Absalom fled to Geshur and stayed three years (2 Samuel 13:37-38). Geshur and Aram later took Havoth-jair from Israel (1 Chronicles 2:23). A separate southern group of the same name appears among David's raid targets in 1 Samuel 27:8; whether it is the same people is unclear.
Culture
A small border kingdom, strong enough to shelter a royal fugitive and to intermarry with David's house, but never a great power. Its kings are named individually (Talmai) rather than as a dynasty.
Religion
No distinct Geshurite religion is described; as an Aramean kingdom it shared the religious world of Aram.
Bible Connection
Geshur gave David a wife and Absalom a refuge, tying Israel's royal house to the Aramean borderlands and shaping the rebellion that followed.
Key Scriptures
Deuteronomy 3:14; Joshua 12:5; Joshua 13:13; 2 Samuel 3:3; 2 Samuel 13:37-38; 1 Chronicles 2:23
Related People
King David; Og; Joshua
Related Places
