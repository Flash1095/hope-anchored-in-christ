Seleucid Empire

Civilization
Seleucid Empire
Time Period
c. 312-63 BC
Region
Syria and Mesopotamia to Persia, capital Antioch
History
Seleucus I, one of Alexander's generals, took the eastern part of Alexander's empire and founded a dynasty ruling from Antioch. His successors fought the Ptolemies for a century over the Levant. Antiochus IV Epiphanes tried to stamp out Jewish worship, sparking the Maccabean revolt, and Rome ended the empire when Pompey annexed Syria in 63 BC.
Culture
Seleucids spread Greek cities, language, and coinage across the East. Antioch became one of the ancient world's great cities and later the launching point of Paul's missionary journeys.
Religion
Seleucid kings promoted Greek gods and ruler cult. Antiochus IV set up a pagan altar in the Jerusalem temple and banned circumcision and Sabbath observance — the crisis behind the Maccabean revolt and the feast of Hanukkah.
Bible Connection
Daniel 11's kings of the North are the Seleucids, whose wars with the Ptolemaic kings of the South fill the chapter's long prophecy. Antiochus IV is the chapter's vile person (Daniel 11:21) and a foreshadowing of later persecutors. Antioch, the Seleucid capital, is where believers were first called Christians (Acts 11:26).
Key Scriptures
Daniel 11:5-35; Acts 11:19-26
