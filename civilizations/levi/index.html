Levi

Civilization
Levi
Time Period
c. 1400 BC – first century AD
Region
No single territory; 48 cities scattered throughout Israel
History
Levi was Jacob's third son by Leah, and with Simeon he shared in the Shechem violence (Genesis 34). The tribe was set apart after the golden calf, when the Levites rallied to Moses (Exodus 32:26-29). Levi received no tribal territory; instead the Levites were given 48 cities scattered among the other tribes (Joshua 21) and lived from the tithes, serving the tabernacle and later the temple. Aaron's descendants were the priests; the other Levites assisted them. Moses, Aaron, and Miriam were all Levites. Korah's rebellion (Numbers 16) was a Levite challenge to Aaron's priesthood.
Culture
Levite culture was defined by service rather than land: teaching the law, assisting at sacrifices, singing and guarding the sanctuary, and living dispersed among the other tribes as their religious instructors.
Religion
The Levites embodied Israel's priestly religion: Aaron's sons offered the sacrifices, and the tribe as a whole maintained the sanctuary, taught the law, and modelled the claim that the LORD himself was their inheritance (Numbers 18:20).
Bible Connection
Levi's blessing in Deuteronomy 33 praises the tribe that taught God's law and kept his covenant. In the New Testament the Levites appear in the parable of the good Samaritan and in the temple service that frames Jesus' ministry.
Key Scriptures
Genesis 29:34; Genesis 49:5-7; Exodus 32:26-29; Numbers 3:5-13; Numbers 18:20-24; Joshua 21:1-3; Deuteronomy 33:8-11
Related People
Levi; Jacob; Moses; Aaron; Miriam; Eleazar; Phinehas
