Civilization
Media
Time Period
c. 678-550 BC
Region
Northwestern Iran, south of the Caspian Sea
History
The Medes were an Iranian people whose kingdom, under kings such as Cyaxares, joined Babylon in destroying Nineveh in 612 BC. Cyrus of Persia conquered Media around 550 BC, and the two peoples formed the Medo-Persian Empire, whose law was famously unchangeable (Daniel 6:8). Darius the Mede took Babylon after its fall and ruled it while Daniel served at court.
Culture
Medes were famed as horsemen and warriors, and their court dress and customs deeply influenced the Persian Empire that absorbed them.
Religion
The Medes practiced an early form of Iranian religion; later tradition connects their priestly class, the magi, with Zoroastrian teaching, though the text itself does not describe their worship.
Bible Connection
The Medes are God's named instrument against Babylon (Isaiah 13:17; Jeremiah 51:11, 28), and Daniel's career spans their rule: the lions' den is dated to Darius the Mede, and Daniel's vision of the ram names the two horns as the kings of Media and Persia (Daniel 8:20).
