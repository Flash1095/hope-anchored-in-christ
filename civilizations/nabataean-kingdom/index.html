Nabataean Kingdom

Civilization
Nabataean Kingdom
Time Period
c. 312 BC-AD 106
Region
Northern Arabia and the Negev, capital Petra, with Damascus at times
History
Arab traders who built Petra, carving tombs and temples into rose-red cliffs and growing rich on the incense route between Arabia and the Mediterranean. Their kings, named Aretas or Obodas in turn, held Damascus in the first century AD, and Rome annexed the kingdom as Arabia Petraea in AD 106.
Culture
Nabataeans engineered desert water — cisterns, channels, and dams — that let Petra flourish in arid land, and their carved facades still stand as one of the ancient world's marvels.
Religion
Nabataeans worshipped Arabian deities, chief among them Dushara, god of the mountains, alongside Allat and al-Uzza.
Bible Connection
Aretas, king of the Nabataeans, held Damascus when Paul escaped the city in a basket through a window in the wall (2 Corinthians 11:32-33) — the kingdom is the backdrop of Paul's first escape.
Key Scriptures
2 Corinthians 11:32-33; Acts 9:23-25
Related Places
Petra
