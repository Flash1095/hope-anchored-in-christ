Paleo-Hebrew Leviticus Scroll (11QpaleoLev a)

Category

Cave

Date Approx

Description
A Hebrew Leviticus scroll in palaeo-Hebrew script, discovered in Cave 11 in 1956 and first unrolled fourteen years later at the Israel Museum in Jerusalem. It survives as seven extant columns — the lowermost portion (about one-fifth of the original height) of the final six columns of the original manuscript, on two sewn sheets of parchment (sheep/goat skin, about 100 cm long as preserved) — plus eighteen small fragments from earlier chapters. The main sheet covers Leviticus 22-27; the fragments extend coverage to Leviticus 4, 10, 11, 13, 14, 16 and 18-22. A single experienced scribe wrote it, with dots as word-dividers and letters suspended from ruled lines.

Significance
The most substantial Leviticus manuscript from Qumran and a showcase for the ancient palaeo-Hebrew script, which scholars associate especially with Torah books thought to come directly from Moses. Its text corresponds in varying degrees to the later Hebrew, Greek and Samaritan versions of Leviticus, making it a central witness for Leviticus textual history and for the laws of sacrifice, atonement, festivals and holiness in Leviticus 22-27.
