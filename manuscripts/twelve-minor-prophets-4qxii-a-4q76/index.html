Description
Best-preserved Qumran copy for Malachi only. A Hebrew Twelve Prophets manuscript from Qumran Cave 4, dated c. 150-125 BC (semicursive hand, c. 150-100 BC in one palaeographic discussion; described as pre-Qumranic, c. 150 BC, in a 2015 study). It preserves a substantial Malachi section (Malachi 2:10 to the end of the book), a single Zechariah verse, and Jonah fragments as published in DJD XV. Note: Jonah fragments once attributed to this scroll in DJD XV are disputed by a later study, which argues the composition following Malachi was in all likelihood not Jonah; they are not counted here as a Jonah copy. The Zechariah attribution itself rests on a few letters on a single small fragment and has been questioned (the letters could also come from Jonah 4:10-11).

Significance
Best-preserved Qumran biblical copy of Malachi by a wide margin: Malachi 2:10-4:6 (English numbering) survives here, including the closing Elijah passage (Malachi 4:5-6, Hebrew 3:23-24), against only a questionable Malachi 3:6-7 fragment in 4Q78. It is also important for the history of the Twelve as a collection: a 2015 study argues it probably contained only three or four of the shortest books, not all twelve, and that Malachi was followed by another composition that was in all likelihood NOT Jonah — so Malachi was not always the last book in a Twelve scroll, and the Twelve could be copied in sets as well as as a single collection.
