Description
Fragments of a Hebrew Psalms copy from Qumran Cave 4, in Hasmonean script, and the oldest extant Psalms manuscript (c. 150 BC). Its psalm order differs from the Masoretic Psalter at points, notably Psalm 31 followed directly by Psalm 33, and Psalm 38 followed by Psalm 71. The DJD edition lists further small fragments (including parts of Psalms 25 and 34-35) not itemised in the summary list used for the passage ranges above. Preserved-passage ranges for this manuscript are given in English (KJV) numbering; the manuscript itself carries no modern psalm numbers. Publish-prep note: every PreservedPassages range in this entry was re-versified to English (KJV) numbering against DJD XVI (Skehan/Ulrich/Flint), Qumran Cave 4.XI, via Fitzmyer (2008), with the SBL Handbook of Style Appendix B English/Hebrew versification table. First-pass conversions (over-length ranges only, from the earlier cleanup): Psalms 5:9-13 to 5:8-12; 31:24-25 to 31:23-24; 38:16-23 to 38:15-22; 53:4-7 to 53:3-6; 62:13 to 62:12. Final-pass conversions: Psalms 6:1-4 to 6:1-3; Psalms 36:1-9 to 36:1-8; Psalms 38:2-12 to 38:1-11; Psalms 47:2 to 47:1; Psalms 54:1-6 to 54:1-4; Psalms 56:4 to 56:3; Psalms 63:2-4 to 63:1-3; Psalms 67:1-7 to 67:1-6; Psalms 69:1-19 to 69:1-18. All other ranges were verified as already correct English numbering (untitled psalms and first-clause-title psalms do not shift). The superscriptions of Psalms 6, 36, 54, 67 and 69 are also preserved; the passage ranges give body verses only.
Significance
An early Hebrew witness to the Psalms, predating the medieval Masoretic manuscripts by about a thousand years. Its chief value is textual: it shows which psalms and which readings were already in circulation in the Second Temple period, and, where its psalm order differs from the Masoretic Psalter, that the order of the Psalter was not yet fully fixed.
