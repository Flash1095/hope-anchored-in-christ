Description
Fragments of a Hebrew Psalms copy from Qumran Cave 4, in a formal ornate rectilinear script dated to the 1st century AD. The Psalm 88:15-17 fragment, formerly catalogued separately as 4Q98c (4QPst), was identified in 2022 by Longacre and Strawn, on material and palaeographic grounds, as part of this same manuscript, so it is counted here once, under 4Q85, not as a separate entry; if that identification is correct, 4Q85 once ranged across both halves of the Psalter. Preserved-passage ranges for this manuscript are given in English (KJV) numbering; the manuscript itself carries no modern psalm numbers. Publish-prep note: every PreservedPassages range in this entry was re-versified to English (KJV) numbering against DJD XVI (Skehan/Ulrich/Flint), Qumran Cave 4.XI (DJD 16:49-61), with the SBL Handbook of Style Appendix B English/Hebrew versification table. First-pass conversions (over-length ranges only, from the earlier cleanup): Psalms 52:6-11 to 52:4-9. Final-pass conversions: Psalms 18:3-14 to 18:2-13; Psalms 18:16-18 to 18:15-17; Psalms 18:33-41 to 18:32-40; Psalms 45:8-11 to 45:7-10; Psalms 49:1-17 to 49:1-16; Psalms 51:1-5 to 51:1-3; Psalms 88:15-17 to 88:14-16. All other ranges were verified as already correct English numbering (untitled psalms and first-clause-title psalms do not shift). The superscriptions of Psalms 49 and 51 are also preserved; the passage ranges give body verses only. The Psalm 53 item attests only the superscription, with no body verse itemised, so it does not appear in the passage ranges.
Significance
An early Hebrew witness to the Psalms, predating the medieval Masoretic manuscripts by about a thousand years. Its chief value is textual: it shows which psalms and which readings were already in circulation in the Second Temple period, and, where its psalm order differs from the Masoretic Psalter, that the order of the Psalter was not yet fully fixed.
