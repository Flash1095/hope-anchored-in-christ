Description
A substantial fragmentary Hebrew copy of Jeremiah from Qumran Cave 4, published in DJD XV (Tov) as 4Q70. It is the oldest Jeremiah manuscript from Qumran, dated by Cross to about 225-175 BC, and is proto-Masoretic in text type. Its editor singled it out for its exceptionally high level of secondary scribal activity, including a long insertion by a second scribe in an intercolumn and the bottom margin whose status (correction of a scribal omission versus a genuine textual variant) is debated. Several identifications within it remain uncertain, marked with '?' above, including whether a fragment belongs at Jeremiah 13:22 or 22:3. Publish-prep note: the compact list '28-9:2, 7-15' has been expanded to '28-9:2, 9:7-15' so the site's passage linker resolves the continuation to chapter 9, the end chapter of the range. Same verses claimed, chapter made explicit.

Significance
The earliest surviving Jeremiah copy by more than a century. It shows the proto-Masoretic Jeremiah text already circulating, with heavy scribal correction, in the early second century BC.
