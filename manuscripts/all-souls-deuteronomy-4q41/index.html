All Souls Deuteronomy (4QDeut n)

Category
Biblical
Cave

Completeness
Substantial fragment
Date Approx
c. 30-1 BC
Description
A small but exceptionally well-preserved parchment manuscript from Qumran Cave 4, published by Sidnie White Crawford (JBL 109/2, 1990) as the All Souls Deuteronomy. It consists of two sheets and six columns — four complete columns and two partially damaged — with columns 2-6 on one continuous sheet of parchment. In the manuscript the passages appear in the order Deuteronomy 8:5-10, then Deuteronomy 5:1-6:1, an ordering that, with the blank space at the bottom of column 1, is the main reason scholars argue it is an excerpted/liturgical collection rather than a regular Deuteronomy scroll (an alternative explanation is incorrect repair of the scroll). Its script is unusually tiny, with waw and yod almost indistinguishable, and its orthography is much fuller than that of either the Masoretic Text or the Samaritan Pentateuch.

Significance
Crawford calls it the best-preserved of all the Deuteronomy manuscripts from Cave 4, and it preserves the Decalogue (Deuteronomy 5) in full. Its Decalogue generally follows Deuteronomy but is harmonised toward the Exodus parallel, including one variant unique to this manuscript: the addition of the Exodus reason for the Sabbath commandment. Press reports have called it the oldest complete copy of the Ten Commandments; that title is disputed — the Nash Papyrus (c. 150-100 BC) is older, and 4Q41 itself may be a liturgical excerpt rather than a Torah scroll — so the entry does not repeat the press claim as fact.
