Ezekiel Scroll (4QEzeka)

Category
Biblical
Cave
Qumran Cave 4
Completeness
Substantial fragment
Date Approx
c. 50-25 BC
Description
The broadest surviving Qumran copy of Ezekiel, published in DJD XV (J. E. Sanderson, 'Ezekiel', in Qumran Cave 4, X: The Prophets, 1997, pp. 209-220; 4Q73 at pp. 209-214, against 4Q74 at pp. 215-218 and 4Q75 at pp. 219-220). It survives as fragments from four separated sections of the book: the temple vision (chapters 10-11), the parable of Oholibah (chapter 23), and the temple measurements (chapter 41). Its hand is described in the Cave 4 list as Herodian; other scholarship describes it as late Hasmonaean (see flags).

Related Books
Ezekiel
Significance
A proto-Masoretic witness that agrees substantially with the Masoretic Text of Ezekiel, including in the temple-vision and temple-measurement passages. Its value is as an early, pre-Christian Hebrew witness to the text of Ezekiel: it shows the book circulating in essentially the form later preserved in the Masoretic tradition, well before the medieval codices.
