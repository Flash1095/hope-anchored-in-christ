Genesis Scroll (4QGen b)

A fragmentary Hebrew copy of Genesis traditionally assigned to Qumran Cave 4, published as 4QGenb in DJD XII (Davila). Its editor suspected it may instead derive from Wadi Murabba'at: its leather is coarse and poorly prepared, unusual for Qumran, its script is typologically late, and no fragment of it was identified among photographs from the controlled Cave 4 excavations. Its best-preserved portion is a relatively intact column covering the opening of Genesis 1 (Genesis 1:1-25a in the Lyon summary), with further scattered remains from Genesis 2, 4 and 5. The surviving text is essentially identical to the Masoretic Text; one exhibit summary notes a single minor spelling difference in Genesis 1:15.

Significance

An early witness to the stability of the Genesis text: a copy made around the turn of the era already agrees with the Masoretic Text that underlies modern Hebrew Bibles, including at the creation account in Genesis 1. Its value is as a textual witness, not as a complete Genesis scroll — only scattered verses survive, so it proves nothing about chapters it does not preserve.
