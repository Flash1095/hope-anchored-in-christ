Song of Solomon Scroll (4QCantb)

Category
Biblical
Cave
Qumran Cave 4

Fragment
Date Approx
c. 30 BC-AD 30 (early Herodian)
Description
Three fragments (four columns) of a Hebrew copy of the Song of Solomon from Qumran Cave 4, in early Herodian script. The copy is selective: it omits Song of Solomon 3:6-8 and 4:4-7 and ends at 5:1, so it is an excerpted or anthologised copy rather than a complete book scroll. Scholars have debated its use: Emmanuel Tov at one point argued it was a liturgical text, later revised that to a text for private use, and then abstained from identifying its use, a position other scholars have also reached.

Significance
One of only four Qumran copies of the Song (4Q106, 4Q107, 4Q108, 6Q6), together preserving about 47 of the book's 117 verses. It confirms the Song was already being copied in Hebrew around the turn of the era, while its omissions and its ending at 5:1 show that at least one Qumran copy circulated the Song in a selective form. Small differences from the Masoretic Text, including word order, are preserved in its fragments.
