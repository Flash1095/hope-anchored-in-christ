The Twelve Minor Prophets (4QXIIg)

Category
Biblical
Cave
Qumran Cave 4
Completeness
Fragment
Date Approx
c. 30-1 BC
Description
Best-preserved Qumran copy for Hosea, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Nahum (and, questionably, Habakkuk). The most extensive Qumran manuscript of the Twelve Minor Prophets: a single Hebrew scroll, originally containing the whole Twelve collection, now surviving in many fragments. Only about 916 words can still be attributed to biblical text. The scribe copied carefully in an early Herodian book hand from the last third of the 1st century BC and corrected himself in nine cases. It does NOT contain Haggai or Malachi in its surviving portions. Note: a possible Habakkuk 2:4 fragment in this scroll is identified only with a question mark in the scholarly summary and is not counted here as a Habakkuk copy.

Significance
Textually important against the Masoretic Text: not counting reconstructed variants, 69 cases of variation between the Masoretic Text, the Septuagint and 4QXIIg are preserved; 4QXIIg agrees with the Masoretic Text 15 times and against it 54 times (44 clear readings, a 4.80% deviation among 916 identifiable words), and also attests 39 non-aligned readings. Its orthography is slightly more plene than the Masoretic Text and uses some Qumran spellings. For Hosea, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah and Nahum it is the best-preserved Qumran biblical copy; for Habakkuk it is only a questionable single-verse fragment (see Description).
