Category

Date Approx

Description
The most extensive Samuel scroll from Qumran Cave 4, published as 4QSama (DJD 17, pp. 1-216, 217). In antiquity Samuel was one work, so this single scroll counts once and covers both 1 and 2 Samuel. It survives in 11 partially preserved columns and some 165 fragments with identified contents plus unidentified fragments, at 47 lines per column and about 32.7 cm high including the bottom margin. Only 8 scribal corrections are reported; the scroll was worn in antiquity and reinforced with papyrus pieces glued on the back plus 2 leather patches. The Leon Levy record lists Material Parchment, Script Square, Period Hellenistic-Roman, and 726 images.

Significance
The most important Qumran witness to Samuel. Many of its readings differ from the Masoretic Text but closely resemble the Septuagint / Old Greek. Its most famous contribution is the restored Nahash passage in 1 Samuel 11 (3 lines, cf. Josephus, Antiquities 6.68-71), absent from the Masoretic Text. It is a Hebrew original, not a translation. Note: different secondary lists differ slightly in verse details (e.g. the 1 Samuel 1:28 / 2:1 boundary and 2 Samuel 3 details); the DJD 17 list should be treated as authoritative at merge time.
