Daniel Scroll (4QDana)

Category
Biblical
Cave
Qumran Cave 4
Completeness
Substantial fragment
Date Approx
c. 75-25 BC
Description
Described in the BASOR preliminary edition as 'the most extensively preserved scroll' of Daniel from Qumran (Ulrich, Part 1; 4QDanb and 4QDanc, the other two substantially preserved Cave 4 copies, followed as Part 2). It survives as fragments spanning Daniel 1 to 11. The preserved text follows the book's own language pattern: Hebrew in chapter 1 and from chapter 8 onward, Aramaic in the middle section (the preserved Aramaic portion runs from Daniel 2:9 through 7:28 in this copy). Portions within the ranges listed are incomplete; this is a fragmentary scroll, not a continuous text.

Related Books
Daniel
Significance
The widest single Qumran witness to Daniel, and the clearest single-copy proof that Daniel's Hebrew-Aramaic-Hebrew structure already existed in a first-century BC Hebrew/Aramaic copy. It agrees in substance with the Masoretic Text. It is NOT the oldest Daniel copy (that distinction belongs to 4QDanc, 4Q114, late 2nd century BC, preserving Daniel 10-11); its claim is extent, not age. Across all eight Qumran Daniel manuscripts every chapter except Daniel 12 is represented; Daniel 12:10 is quoted as Scripture in the Florilegium (4Q174), so the absence of chapter 12 from the surviving Daniel fragments is not evidence the book lacked it at Qumran.
