Description
The longest surviving copy of Jeremiah from Qumran, published in DJD XV (E. Tov, 'Jeremiah', in Qumran Cave 4, X: The Prophets, 1997, pp. 145-207, covering 4Q70, 4Q71, 4Q72, 4Q72a, 4Q72b). It survives only as fragments, but they span chapters 4 to 33, including the cup-of-wrath passage in chapter 25 and the restoration chapters 30-31. It is a proto-Masoretic copy: it follows the longer Masoretic order and text.

Significance
Together with 4QJera it proves that the longer Masoretic form of Jeremiah already existed in Hebrew in the Second Temple period. The shorter form behind the Greek Septuagint (about one-seventh shorter, with the oracles against the nations in a different place) is separately attested in Hebrew by 4QJerb and 4QJerd. Both editions therefore circulated in Hebrew side by side at Qumran; the difference is a real textual history, not a later Greek trimming of the Hebrew.
