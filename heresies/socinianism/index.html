Socinianism

Heresy
Socinianism
Founder
Fausto Sozzini (1539-1604)
Summary
Socinianism developed in Poland and Transylvania in the 16th century under Fausto Sozzini, known as Socinus. It rejected the Trinity and the deity of Christ and emphasized reason in interpreting Scripture.
Beliefs
God is one person, not a Trinity; Jesus was a man, not pre-existent God; Christ's death was an example and moral influence, not a substitutionary atonement; Original sin is denied
Controversy
Debated across Reformation Europe; Socinians were expelled from Poland in 1658 and their views were opposed by Reformed and Catholic theologians
Christian Response
Historic churches affirmed the Trinity and Christ's atoning death, citing John 1, Matthew 28:19, and Isaiah 53.
Related People
Fausto Sozzini; Laelio Sozzini
Related Doctrines
Trinity; Deity of Christ; Atonement
