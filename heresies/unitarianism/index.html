Unitarianism

Unitarianism, in its historic form, teaches that God is one person and denies the Trinity and the pre-existence and full deity of Christ. It developed from 16th-century anti-Trinitarian movements into organized churches in Poland, Transylvania, England, and America.

Beliefs

God is one person; Jesus was a great teacher and moral example, not God incarnate; The Holy Spirit is not a distinct divine person; Reason and conscience guide interpretation of Scripture

Controversy

Debated throughout the Reformation and Enlightenment; historic confessions such as Westminster and the Nicene Creed were written in part to affirm Trinitarian faith against it

Christian Response

Historic churches affirm one God in three persons and salvation through Christ's death and resurrection, citing Matthew 28:19, John 20:28, and 1 Corinthians 15.

Details

Heresy: Unitarianism
Founder: Michael Servetus (1511-1553) and later Unitarian churches
Related People: Michael Servetus; Fausto Sozzini; Joseph Priestley
Related Doctrines: Trinity; Deity of Christ; Atonement
