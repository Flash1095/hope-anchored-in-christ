Mormonism (Latter-day Saints)

The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints was founded by Joseph Smith in the United States in 1830. It is included here as a modern comparison entry because its teachings on God, Christ, and Scripture differ from historic Nicene Christianity, while its members describe themselves as Christians.

Beliefs

God the Father has a physical body and was once mortal in Latter-day Saint teaching; Additional Scriptures include the Book of Mormon; Continuing revelation comes through a living prophet; Humans can progress toward exaltation

Controversy

Discussed in comparative theology since the 19th century; historic churches note differences from the Nicene Creed on God and Christ

Christian Response

Historic churches respond by affirming the Trinity as defined at Nicaea and the sufficiency of the biblical canon, citing Deuteronomy and Revelation on adding to Scripture, while encouraging respectful dialogue.
