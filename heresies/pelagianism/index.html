Pelagianism

Heresy
Pelagianism
Founder
Pelagius (c. AD 354-418)
Summary
Pelagianism taught in the late 4th and early 5th centuries that humans are born morally neutral and can choose good without special divine grace. It denied that Adam's sin was transmitted to his descendants as a corrupted nature.
Beliefs
Humans are born without original sin; Human will alone can achieve righteousness; Grace helps but is not necessary for salvation; Adam's sin affected only Adam
Controversy
Condemned at the Council of Carthage AD 418 and the Council of Ephesus AD 431 in the debate with Augustine
Christian Response
The church affirmed that all people need God's grace because of a fallen nature, citing passages such as Romans 5 and Ephesians 2 on sin and grace.
Related People
Pelagius; Augustine of Hippo
Related Doctrines
Original Sin; Grace
Related Places
Ephesus
Related Events
