Albigensianism (Catharism)

Heresy
Albigensianism (Catharism)
Founder
Cathar teachers in southern France (12th-13th centuries)
Summary
Albigensianism, or Catharism, flourished in southern France in the 12th and 13th centuries. It was a dualistic movement that viewed the material world as evil and taught an elite class of the perfected.
Beliefs
Matter is evil and spirit is good; The God of the Old Testament is different from the God of the New; The perfecti followed strict ascetic practices; Salvation comes through a ritual called the consolamentum
Controversy
Condemned by the Fourth Lateran Council AD 1215; followed by preaching campaigns and the Albigensian Crusade (1209-1229)
Christian Response
The church affirmed one good Creator and the goodness of creation and marriage, citing Genesis 1 and 1 Timothy 4, while later historians note the tragedy of violence in the response.
Related People
Pope Innocent III; Dominic
Related Doctrines
Creation; Nature of God; Salvation
