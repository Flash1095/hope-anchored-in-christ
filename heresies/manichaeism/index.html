Manichaeism

Heresy
Manichaeism
Founder
Mani (AD 216-274)
Summary
Manichaeism was founded by Mani in Persia in the 3rd century as a dualistic religion. It taught an eternal struggle between light and darkness and spread across the Roman and Persian worlds.
Beliefs
Good and evil are two eternal opposing powers; Matter is associated with darkness and evil; Salvation comes through knowledge that frees light trapped in matter; Mani was a final prophet
Controversy
Opposed by Christian, Zoroastrian, and later Muslim writers; Augustine, a former hearer, refuted it in the late 4th and early 5th centuries
Christian Response
The church affirmed one sovereign Creator and that creation was made good, citing Genesis 1 and Colossians 1:16-17.
Related People
Mani; Augustine of Hippo
Related Doctrines
Creation; Nature of God; Evil and Suffering
