Deism

Heresy
Deism
Founder
Thinkers including Thomas Paine (1737-1809) and earlier English deists
Summary
Deism grew in the 17th and 18th centuries during the Enlightenment. It taught that God created the world but does not intervene in it through miracles, revelation, or providence.
Beliefs
God created the world and then does not intervene; Miracles and special revelation are rejected; Religion should rest on reason and nature alone; Prayer does not change God's actions
Controversy
Debated by Christian apologists in the 18th century and contrasted with biblical theism in sermons and treatises of the period
Christian Response
The church affirmed a God who acts in history, answers prayer, and revealed himself in Scripture and in Christ, citing Exodus, the Psalms, and Hebrews 1.
Related People
Thomas Paine; John Locke; Joseph Butler
Related Doctrines
Nature of God; Revelation; Miracles
