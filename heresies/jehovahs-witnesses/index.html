Jehovah's Witnesses

Heresy
Jehovah's Witnesses
Founder
Charles Taze Russell (1852-1916)
Summary
Jehovah's Witnesses originated in the United States in the 1870s under Charles Taze Russell and were later led by Joseph Rutherford. It is included here as a modern comparison entry because its teachings on the Trinity, Christ, and the Holy Spirit differ from historic Nicene Christianity.
Beliefs
God is one person, Jehovah; Jesus is Michael the archangel and a created being; The Holy Spirit is God's active force, not a person; Only 144000 are anointed to reign in heaven in their teaching
Controversy
Discussed in comparative theology since the early 20th century; historic churches contrast its Christology with Arianism and the Nicene Creed
Christian Response
Historic churches affirm Jesus as fully God and the Spirit as a divine person, citing John 1, Acts 5, and Matthew 28:19, while encouraging respectful and factual conversation.
Related People
Charles Taze Russell; Joseph Rutherford
Related Doctrines
Trinity; Deity of Christ; Holy Spirit
