Monophysitism (Eutychianism)

Monophysitism taught in the 5th century that Christ has only one nature, in which his humanity was absorbed into his divinity. Eutyches was a leading figure in the controversy.

Beliefs

Christ has one nature only; Christ's humanity was absorbed by his divinity; Christ is not fully human in the same way as other humans

Controversy

Condemned at the Council of Chalcedon AD 451, which defined Christ in two natures without confusion or separation

Details

Heresy: Monophysitism (Eutychianism)
Founder: Eutyches (c. AD 380-456)
Christian Response: The church affirmed that Jesus is fully God and fully man in one person, citing Hebrews 4:15 and Colossians 2:9.
Related People: Eutyches; Leo I; Flavian
Related Doctrines: Incarnation; Hypostatic Union; Humanity of Christ
