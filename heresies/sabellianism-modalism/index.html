Sabellianism (Modalism)

Sabellianism, also called Modalism or Modalistic Monarchianism, taught in the 3rd century that God is one person who reveals himself in three successive modes. In this view the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are not distinct persons.

Beliefs

God is one person in three modes; Father, Son, and Spirit are not distinct persons; God the Father suffered on the cross in the mode of the Son

Controversy

Condemned in Rome in the early 3rd century and opposed by Tertullian and Hippolytus; later rejected in Trinitarian creeds

Details

Heresy: Sabellianism (Modalism)
Founder: Sabellius (active early 3rd century)
Christian Response: The church affirmed one God in three distinct persons who coexist eternally, citing Matthew 28:19 and the baptism of Jesus in Matthew 3.
Related People: Sabellius; Tertullian; Hippolytus
Related Doctrines: Trinity; Nature of God
Related Places: Rome
Related Events: The Crucifixion
