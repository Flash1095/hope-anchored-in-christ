Jansenism

Heresy
Jansenism
Founder
Cornelius Jansen (1585-1638)
Summary
Jansenism was a movement within Roman Catholicism in the 17th and 18th centuries, based on Jansen's work on Augustine. It emphasized human corruption, predestination, and strict moral practice, and was opposed by Jesuit theologians and several popes.
Beliefs
Human nature is deeply corrupted without grace; Grace is irresistible for the elect; Christ died for the elect in a particular sense; Strict moral and sacramental discipline is required
Controversy
Condemned in part by Pope Innocent X in Cum Occasione AD 1653 and by Pope Clement XI in Unigenitus AD 1713
Christian Response
Responses varied; critics argued it understated God's universal saving will, citing 1 Timothy 2:4, while others valued its emphasis on grace and holiness.
Related People
Cornelius Jansen; Blaise Pascal; Antoine Arnauld
Related Doctrines
Grace; Predestination; Free Will
