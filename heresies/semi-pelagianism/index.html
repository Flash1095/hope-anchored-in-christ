Semi-Pelagianism

Heresy
Semi-Pelagianism
Founder
John Cassian (c. AD 360-435)
Summary
Semi-Pelagianism arose in southern Gaul in the 5th century as a middle position. It taught that the first step toward faith can come from human will, with God's grace then completing what the person began.
Beliefs
Humans can initiate faith on their own; God's grace cooperates with human will after the first step; Original sin weakens but does not fully corrupt the will
Controversy
Addressed at the Second Council of Orange AD 529, which affirmed that even the beginning of faith is a gift of grace
Christian Response
The church taught that faith itself begins by grace, pointing to Philippians 2:13 and John 6:44, while still affirming human responsibility to respond.
Related People
John Cassian; Augustine of Hippo; Prosper of Aquitaine
Related Doctrines
Grace; Free Will; Original Sin
