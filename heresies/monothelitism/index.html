Monothelitism

Heresy
Monothelitism
Founder
Patriarch Sergius I of Constantinople (died 638)
Summary
Monothelitism taught in the 7th century that Christ has two natures but only one will, the divine will. It was promoted as a compromise between Chalcedonian and Monophysite positions.
Beliefs
Christ has one will only; Christ's human will did not truly operate; Unity in Christ requires a single will
Controversy
Condemned at the Third Council of Constantinople AD 680-681, which affirmed two wills in Christ
Christian Response
The church affirmed that Jesus has a true human will as well as a divine will, citing Luke 22:42 where Jesus prays not my will but yours be done.
Related People
Sergius I; Maximus the Confessor; Pope Martin I
Related Doctrines
Incarnation; Humanity of Christ; Hypostatic Union
