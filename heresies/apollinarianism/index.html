Apollinarianism

Heresy
Apollinarianism
Founder
Apollinaris of Laodicea (c. AD 310-390)
Summary
Apollinarianism taught in the 4th century that Christ had a human body and soul but that the divine Logos replaced his human mind. It sought to protect Christ's sinlessness and unity.
Beliefs
Christ had no human rational mind; The Logos took the place of Christ's human mind; Christ was not fully human in mind
Controversy
Condemned at the Council of Constantinople AD 381; Gregory of Nazianzus argued what is not assumed is not healed
Christian Response
The church affirmed that Jesus assumed full humanity, including a human mind, to redeem the whole person, citing Hebrews 2:17 and Luke 2:52.
Related People
Apollinaris; Gregory of Nazianzus
Related Doctrines
Incarnation; Humanity of Christ; Atonement
