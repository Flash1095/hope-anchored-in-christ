The Afflicted Man

Type
The Afflicted Man
Category
Person
Old Testament Passage
Lamentations 1:12; Lamentations 3:1; Lamentations 3:19-24
Fulfillment
The voice that cries, 'Is it nothing to you, all ye that pass by? behold, and see if there be any sorrow like unto my sorrow,' has long been heard at the cross, where passers-by reviled a suffering Saviour; and the same voice that remembers affliction still hopes in mercies new every morning. Jesus is the truly afflicted man, acquainted with grief, who yet trusted the Father through it.
Fulfillment Passage
Matthew 27:39-40; Hebrews 4:15
Explanation
BORDERLINE: TRADITIONAL reading — Christian hymnody and devotion have long applied Lamentations 1:12 to Christ on the cross, and Lamentations 3's movement from affliction to hope in mercy echoes His trust in suffering. The doubt: the speaker is not a named individual but a poetic voice — Jeremiah, or Jerusalem personified — so this is a voice applied to Christ, not a person whose life prefigures Him. Held as borderline for that reason.
