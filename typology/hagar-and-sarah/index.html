Hagar and Sarah

Paul reads the two women of Abraham's household as two covenants: Hagar the bondwoman, whose son was born after the flesh, answers to Mount Sinai and bondage, while Sarah the freewoman, whose son was born by promise, answers to the Jerusalem above, which is free and is the mother of us all. Believers are children of promise, like Isaac, free in Christ.

Explanation

EXPLICIT warrant: Galatians 4:24 states it — 'which things are an allegory: for these are the two covenants.' The parallel is covenantal rather than personal: neither woman is herself Christ, but Sarah's promised son and the freedom of her household point to the new covenant in Christ, set against the bondage of law-keeping. This entry is distinct from the live Seed of Abraham entry (the promised seed as Christ): this is the two-women allegory of the two covenants.
