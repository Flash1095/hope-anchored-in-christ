Genesis Apocryphon (1Q20)

Find Name
Genesis Apocryphon (1Q20)
Description
An Aramaic retelling of the Genesis patriarchal stories, covering Lamech, Noah and Abraham, and one of the original seven Dead Sea Scrolls found in Qumran Cave 1 in 1946–1947. It survives on four sheets of leather and is the least well-preserved of the original seven, partly because its metallic ink has eaten into the parchment. Suggested dates for its composition range from the 3rd century BC to the 1st century AD; it is a pseudepigraphal retelling, not a canonical book.
Historical Period
Hellenistic–Roman
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The Apocryphon shows how Second Temple Jews retold and expanded Genesis, filling narrative gaps (for example Lamech's doubts about Noah's birth and Abraham's meeting with Melchizedek) in the first person. It confirms that Genesis traditions circulated in expanded Aramaic forms before the New Testament period, but it is not Scripture and its expansions must not be treated as historical fact beyond Genesis itself.

Biblical Significance
It illuminates how Genesis was read, retold and treasured in the Second Temple period, and it preserves early Jewish traditions about Noah and Abraham found nowhere else.
