Etemenanki (Tower of Babel)

Find Name
Etemenanki (Tower of Babel)
Historical Period
Neo-Babylonian Period (626-539 BC)
Artifact Date
Rebuilt by Nebuchadnezzar II, 6th century BC (earlier stages older)
Date Discovered
Early 20th century (excavated by Koldewey's expedition)
Discovery Location
Babylon, Iraq
Current Location
Foundations in situ, Babylon
Timeline Era
Exile
Description
The great ziggurat of Babylon, rebuilt by Nebuchadnezzar as a staged tower whose name meant 'temple of the foundation of heaven and earth.' Its excavated foundations show a massive square staged structure at the heart of Babylon. It is traditionally identified with the Tower of Babel of Genesis 11, but that identification is traditional, not proven, and the entry claims no more than the tradition and the archaeology.
Biblical Significance
Babel's builders wanted a name and a tower reaching heaven; Nebuchadnezzar's tower, whatever its link to Genesis, stands for the same ambition in brick. Genesis answers that ambition with confusion and scattering — and with a God who builds His own city, not by human hands.
Evidence Class
Disputed
Interpretations
Scholars agree Etemenanki was Babylon's great ziggurat; its identification with the Genesis 11 tower is traditional rather than demonstrated, since the Genesis account names no excavated structure.
Scripture Illuminated
Genesis 11:1-9
