Karatepe Bilingual Inscription

Find Name
Karatepe Bilingual Inscription
Description
A bilingual inscription on stone slabs (KAI 26) in Phoenician and Hieroglyphic Luwian, set up at the gates of the fortress of Azatiwataya by its ruler Azatiwada, installed by Awariku, king of Adana. Discovered by Helmuth Theodor Bossert and Halet Çambel in 1946, it records Azatiwada's building of the city, his extension of Adana's frontiers, and the prosperity and peace he claims to have secured. Because the same text appears in both scripts, it served as a Rosetta Stone for deciphering Anatolian hieroglyphs.
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Scholars agree on the bilingual character and its decipherment value; the exact date of Azatiwada's rule (early eighth to early seventh century BC) and whether the Phoenician or Luwian version was composed first are debated.

Biblical Significance
Karatepe opens a window onto the Phoenician-speaking world that surrounded Israel in the divided-monarchy period: the same Phoenician language, gods, and seafaring trade that appear in the stories of Hiram of Tyre and the merchants of Ezekiel 27. Its decipherment also unlocked an entire family of ancient inscriptions, letting historians read the neighbours of Israel in their own words.
