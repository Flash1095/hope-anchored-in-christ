Herod Archelaus Coins

Find Name
Herod Archelaus Coins
Description
Bronze prutahs of Herod Archelaus, ethnarch of Judaea, Samaria, and Idumaea (4 BC-AD 6), struck at the Jerusalem mint. Types include a double cornucopia with grapes paired with a war galley showing aphlaston, oars, and ram; grapes paired with a crested helmet; and an anchor paired with a wreath inscribed EΘN (Ethnarch). Inscriptions in Greek read 'of Herod' and 'of the Ethnarch'; there are no portraits, in deference to Jewish sensitivities. The galley type recalls his voyage to Rome to appeal for his father's kingdom after Herod's will was changed.
Historical Period
Herodian Period
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The coins name Herod the Ethnarch, the ruler Matthew 2:22 says Joseph feared on returning from Egypt. The galley's allusion to the Rome voyage is a scholarly reading of the imagery, not an inscriptional statement.
Related Places

Biblical Significance
When Joseph heard that Archelaus reigned in Judaea in place of his father Herod, 'he was afraid to go thither' (Matthew 2:22) and turned aside into Galilee; these coins name the ethnarch whose reign prompted that fear.
