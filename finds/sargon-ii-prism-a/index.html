Sargon II Prism A

Find Name
Sargon II Prism A
Description
A clay prism inscription of Sargon II (reigned 722-705 BC), dated to about 717 BC, carrying the king's annals including his campaigns in the west. In the Samaria passage the king boasts that he besieged and captured Samaria, carried off 27,290 of its inhabitants as booty, formed 50 chariots from them for his royal corps, rebuilt the city, settled it with people from conquered lands, and set an Assyrian governor over it. The account survives in several parallel versions - the Khorsabad annals, the Nimrud Prism, and Louvre prisms - with the deportee figure sometimes read as 27,280.
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The prism is a first-hand Assyrian account of the fall of Samaria that matches 2 Kings 17 in outline - siege, capture, deportation, resettlement - while differing in emphasis: the Bible stresses Israel's unfaithfulness as the cause, the prism stresses Sargon's power. The 27,290/27,280 figure varies between copies and should be quoted as approximate. Sargon's claim to have captured Samaria in his first year sits alongside the biblical attribution of the final siege to Shalmaneser; scholars explain this by Sargon's succession during the siege.

Biblical Significance
It independently confirms the fall of Samaria, the deportation of Israelites, and the resettlement policy described in 2 Kings 17:24, grounding the end of the northern kingdom in Assyria's own record.
