Elephantine Passover Letter

Find Name
Elephantine Passover Letter
Description
An Aramaic letter from Hananiah to Yedaniah and the Jewish garrison at Elephantine, Egypt, dated to year 5 of King Darius II (419 BC) and catalogued as Cowley 21 (Sachau 6). It instructs the garrison to count fourteen days of Nisan, observe Passover on the 14th at twilight, and keep the Festival of Unleavened Bread from the 15th to the 21st of Nisan, with no leaven seen or eaten from the 14th at sunset to the 21st, no work on the 15th and 21st, and ritual purity; it was sent by order of King Darius via Arsames, satrap of Egypt. The surviving fragment measures about 11.4 x 23 cm and was found in 1907 during German excavations at Elephantine; it is now in the Egyptian Museum of Berlin.
Historical Period
Persian Period
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The earliest extra-biblical evidence for Passover observance: it shows a diaspora Jewish military community in Egypt keeping Passover and Unleavened Bread under a Persian royal order relayed through the satrap, and keeping the two festivals as distinct observances at that date. Cautions: the papyrus is fragmentary, and the word 'Pesach' itself is reconstructed in a lacuna (translations bracket it) — the identification rests on the unleavened-bread instructions. The suggestion that Hananiah was the brother of Nehemiah (Nehemiah 1:2; 7:2) is speculation, not established. The letter illuminates Passover observance; it does not prove the Exodus event itself.

Biblical Significance
The letter's rules mirror the Passover and Unleavened Bread instructions of Exodus 12, showing those festivals observed by a Jewish community in Egypt centuries after the Exodus account's setting.
