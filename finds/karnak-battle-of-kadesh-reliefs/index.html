Karnak Battle of Kadesh Reliefs

Find Name
Karnak Battle of Kadesh Reliefs
Description
Monumental wall reliefs at Karnak (with companion versions at Abu Simbel, Luxor, Abydos, and the Ramesseum) commemorating the Battle of Kadesh, fought around 1274 BC in the fifth year of Ramesses II against the Hittite king Muwatalli II at the fortified city of Kadesh on the Orontes River in modern Syria. The reliefs show Ramesses charging almost alone in his chariot against the Hittite army, accompanied by the 'Poem' and 'Bulletin' texts, the best-documented description of a battle in all of ancient history.
Historical Period
New Kingdom, Nineteenth Dynasty
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The reliefs are state propaganda, not a battle report: the battle itself was a near-disaster turned stalemate, in which Hittite chariots smashed a whole Egyptian division and surrounded the camp before reinforcements arrived, and Egypt never took Kadesh. Their value is precisely in showing how ancient kings manufactured victory in stone, a caution for reading all royal inscriptions, including those that touch biblical history. They also confirm the Hittites as a great power rivaling Egypt, the empire whose peoples appear throughout the Old Testament.
Alternative Names
Battle of Kadesh Reliefs; Kadesh Inscriptions
Related Places
Egypt
Biblical Significance
They attest the Hittite empire at its height and the scale of Egypt's northern wars, the world in which Hittites appear in Genesis, Joshua, and the story of Uriah.
Artifact Date
After c. 1274 BC, reign of Ramesses II
Current Location
In situ, Karnak Temple, Luxor, Egypt
Discovery Location
Karnak Temple, Luxor, Egypt
