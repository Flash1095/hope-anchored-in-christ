Habakkuk Commentary (1QpHab)

Find Name
Habakkuk Commentary (1QpHab)
Description
The Habakkuk Commentary, 1QpHab or Pesher Habakkuk, is a verse-by-verse commentary on Habakkuk 1-2 from Qumran Cave 1, found in 1947 as one of the original seven scrolls. Written in Herodian script in the second half of the 1st century BC, it runs to 13 columns of parchment about 1.48 meters long. Each quoted verse is followed by an interpretation introduced by the word 'pishro' ('its meaning'), applying the prophecy to the commentator's own time.
Historical Period
Second Temple period
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The commentary is the best window into how the Qumran community read prophecy: as a coded message about their own days, with the Teacher of Righteousness receiving the true meaning, the Wicked Priest and the Man of Lies as opponents, and the Kittim (understood as the Romans) as the invading Chaldeans of Habakkuk's day. The identities of the Teacher and Wicked Priest are never given by name, so they remain unidentified. Its quoted text of Habakkuk sometimes differs from the Masoretic Text, making it a textual witness for Habakkuk 1-2 as well as a commentary; note that the divine name is written in paleo-Hebrew script, unlike the rest of the text.
Alternative Names
Pesher Habakkuk; Pesher on Habakkuk
Related Books
Habakkuk
Scripture Illuminated
Habakkuk 1:1-2:20
Biblical Significance
It shows how Jews before Christ read Habakkuk as prophecy about their own generation, and its quotations give an independent witness to the text of Habakkuk 1-2.
