Ophel Pithos Inscription

Find Name
Ophel Pithos Inscription
Description
A fragment of a large neckless ceramic storage jar (pithos) bearing an incised inscription, found in 2012 at the Ophel excavation site near Jerusalem's Temple Mount by Eilat Mazar and announced in summer 2013. Published as Mazar, Ahituv and Ben-Shlomo, 'An Inscribed Pithos From the Ophel,' Israel Exploration Journal 63/1 (2013). Dated to the 10th century BCE (Early Iron IIA), it is the earliest alphabetical inscription found in Jerusalem, predating by about 250 years the earliest known Hebrew inscription from the city (Hezekiah period, late 8th century BCE). It was found with six other large storage jars used as fill to reinforce earth under a building's second floor.
Historical Period
Iron Age IIA
Evidence Class
Disputed
Interpretations
The artifact itself is genuine, but its reading is significantly contested, which is why it is classed Disputed here. The surviving letters yield no comprehensible meaning in any known West Semitic language: Ahituv transliterated M, Q, P, H, N, (possibly) L, N (left to right), while Rollston read M, Q, L, H, N, (possibly) R, N and would date it to the 11th century. Scholars also dispute whether the script is Proto-Canaanite or early Canaanite versus Phoenician, and proposed readings range from sensationalist claims to minimalist scepticism. The excavators surmised it might record a Jebusite name or the jar's contents or owner, but no reading is agreed.
Related Places
Jerusalem
Biblical Significance
Whatever it says, its presence proves alphabetic writing was in use in Jerusalem in the 10th century BCE, the era of David and Solomon, even though the text itself cannot yet be read.
