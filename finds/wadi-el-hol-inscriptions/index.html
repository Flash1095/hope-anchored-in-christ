Wadi el-Hol Inscriptions

Find Name
Wadi el-Hol Inscriptions
Description
Two single-line rock inscriptions in limestone cliffs in the Wadi el-Hol valley on the Farshut Road in the Qena Bend, west bank of the Nile between Thebes and Hiw. Found in 1993 by Deborah and John Darnell of the Theban Desert Road Survey and published in 1999, they date to c. 1900-1800 BC (late Middle Kingdom) and are among the oldest examples of phonetic alphabetic writing, closely resembling the Proto-Sinaitic script of Serabit el-Khadim. They were written by Canaanite workers or soldiers in Egyptian service.
Historical Period
Middle Kingdom
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The inscriptions show Semitic speakers in Egypt already using an early alphabet nearly 4,000 years ago, which is the background to how the biblical text itself could later be written in an alphabet rather than hieroglyphs or cuneiform. Exact readings are debated, including claims of Hebrew vocabulary by Douglas Petrovich, which should be treated as debated, not settled. They do not mention Israel, Moses, or any biblical event.
Related Places
Egypt
Biblical Significance
They illuminate the origins of the alphabet in which the Old Testament would eventually be written, showing Semitic speakers adapting Egyptian signs into letters centuries before Israel's monarchy.
