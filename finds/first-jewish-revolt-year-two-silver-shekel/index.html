First Jewish Revolt Year Two Silver Shekel

Find Name
First Jewish Revolt Year Two Silver Shekel
Description
A silver shekel of the First Jewish Revolt, about 13.7-14 g, struck at Jerusalem and dated Year 2 (April AD 67 to March AD 68). The obverse shows a ritual chalice (omer cup) with a pearled rim and the Paleo-Hebrew legend 'Shekel of Israel' with 'Year Two' above; the reverse shows a staff with three pomegranate buds and 'Jerusalem the Holy'. These were the first coins struck by a sovereign Jewish government in silver, probably minted within the temple complex from temple stocks of Tyrian shekels. A Year 2 example found in Jerusalem's City of David was announced by the Israel Antiquities Authority in 2021.
Historical Period
Roman Period (63 BC-AD 324)
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The Year 2 shekel is a declaration of independence in silver: a Jewish government replacing the Tyrian shekel for the temple tax with its own coinage, dated by the years of the revolt rather than by any emperor. Year 2 shekels are relatively common (the Menorah Coin Project counts 360 or more known), which shows the revolt's mint working at scale in its second year.

Biblical Significance
Exodus 30:13 institutes the half-shekel temple tax; the revolt shekels were struck so that tax could still be paid in Jewish silver after the break with Rome, showing temple worship continuing even in wartime.
