Nabonidus Verse Account Tablet

Find Name
Nabonidus Verse Account Tablet
Description
A poetic Babylonian tablet (British Museum BM 38299) composed in Babylonia in the late 6th century BC, after Cyrus the Great had begun to rule. Written in verse, it attacks Nabonidus for neglecting the cult of Marduk and favouring the moon-god Sin, and it praises Cyrus as the king chosen by Marduk to take Babylon. It is a piece of pro-Cyrus propaganda rather than an impartial chronicle.
Historical Period
Neo-Babylonian to Early Achaemenid Period (late 6th century BC)
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The Verse Account must not be read as neutral confirmation of Daniel: it was written by the winning side after the conquest and deliberately blackens Nabonidus. Its value is that it shows how Cyrus's takeover was presented to Babylonians - as Marduk's own choice - which helps explain the smooth transfer of power described in Daniel 5:30-31 and Ezra 1.

Biblical Significance
The tablet describes the same transfer of power the Bible records - Babylon falling to Cyrus - but from the Babylonian priesthood's viewpoint, showing why the conquest was welcomed rather than resisted.
