Mattathias Antigonus Menorah Coin

Find Name
Mattathias Antigonus Menorah Coin
Description
A small bronze prutah of Mattathias Antigonus, the last Hasmonean king, struck at Jerusalem between 40 and 37 BCE. One side shows the seven-branched menorah, the other the showbread table of the temple; the legends read 'Mattatayah the High Priest' in Paleo-Hebrew and 'of King Antigonus' in Greek. The coins are crudely struck. Antigonus chose the temple imagery to rally Jerusalem and the temple against Herod, who was backed by Rome, and a specimen was returned to Israel after seizure in a joint IAA, Manhattan District Attorney, and Homeland Security investigation.
Historical Period
Hasmonean Period (140-37 BC)
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
This is the only ancient Jewish coin known to depict the seven-branched menorah, and one of the earliest artistic images of the temple lampstand made while the temple still stood. Its propaganda purpose is clear: a king fighting for legitimacy against a Roman-backed rival put the temple itself on his small change. It is classified as a national treasure under Israeli law and cannot be exported.

Biblical Significance
Exodus 25:31-40 gives the pattern for the seven-branched lampstand; Antigonus's coin is the only Jewish coin to show that lampstand, struck as a symbol of the temple he claimed to defend.
