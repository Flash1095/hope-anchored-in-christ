Khirbet Qeiyafa Shrine Models

Find Name
Khirbet Qeiyafa Shrine Models
Description
Two portable shrine models found at Khirbet Qeiyafa in the Elah Valley, about 30 km southwest of Jerusalem, excavated by Yosef Garfinkel and Saar Ganor and announced in 2012. One is clay, with an elaborate facade of two guardian lions, two pillars, a main door, roof beams, a folded textile and three birds on the roof; the other is a single limestone block about 35 cm high with a triglyph frieze and traces of red paint. They were found with five standing stones, two basalt altars and two libation vessels, and with no human or animal figurines. The models are dated to the early 10th century BC.
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The pillars and folded textile parallel features of Solomon's Temple as described in 1 Kings (the pillars Jachin and Boaz, and the veil), and the excavators read the models as evidence for a Judahite cult site in David's time, but this reading is debated: other scholars associate the clay shrine with a goddess cult, and the site's Judahite affiliation itself is contested. The models therefore illuminate temple architecture of the period; they are not proof of Solomon's Temple, and the absence of figurines is consistent with, but does not prove, an aniconic Judahite cult.

Biblical Significance
The models give a small-scale, contemporary glimpse of the kind of pillared shrine architecture that 1 Kings describes for Solomon's Temple, from the very century the Bible assigns to David and Solomon.
