Daiva Inscription of Xerxes

Find Name
Daiva Inscription of Xerxes
Description
The trilingual royal inscription of Xerxes I (XPh), known from stone slabs at Persepolis and Pasargadae in Old Persian, Elamite, and Babylonian. After a list of the lands Xerxes ruled 'by the will of Ahura Mazda', it tells how a rebellious land was defeated and how, in another land, the worship of the daivas (demons or rival gods) was suppressed: 'The daivas shall not be worshipped any longer.' The slabs were found reused as bench facing in the Garrison Quarters, not on display.
Historical Period
Persian
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Scholars agree on the text and its royal ideology; the rebel land of section 4 cannot be identified with certainty (Iran, Egypt, and Babylon have all been proposed), and whether the inscription records genuine religious reform or royal propaganda is debated. The identification of Xerxes with Esther's Ahasuerus is widely held but not unanimous.

Biblical Significance
Xerxes is commonly identified with Ahasuerus, the Persian king of Esther, and his inscription shows the religious politics of that court: a king who believed his throne and his victories came from Ahura Mazda and who enforced that god's worship. It is the Persian side of the world in which Esther's Jews lived under a king whose decrees could mean life or death.
