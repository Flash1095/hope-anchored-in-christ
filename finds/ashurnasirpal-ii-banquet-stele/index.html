Ashurnasirpal II Banquet Stele

Find Name
Ashurnasirpal II Banquet Stele
Description
A sandstone stele carrying a 154-line inscription of Ashurnasirpal II (883-859 BC), found in 1951 in a recess at the eastern end of the throne-room facade of the Northwest Palace at Nimrud (Kalhu). Its text recounts the king's campaigns, his rebuilding of Kalhu with palace and temples, its gardens and canals, and closes with the ten-day inauguration feast of 879 BC for 69,574 guests - 47,074 people of the land, 5,000 envoys, 16,000 residents of Kalhu, and 1,500 palace officials - with detailed lists of food and drink, including 10,000 jars of beer and 10,000 skins of wine. The stele was in the Mosul Cultural Museum and was destroyed in 2015; the text survives in publications, squeezes, and photographs.
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The stele shows the scale and political purpose of Assyrian royal hospitality: a feast that displayed the king's wealth and bound guests - including foreign envoys - to his regime. The guest total cannot be independently verified and may be royal exaggeration; it is a guest list, not a census, and its careful enumeration is itself the message. It illuminates the royal-banquet world behind Esther 1, but it is background, not a parallel to any biblical feast.

Biblical Significance
It gives the fullest surviving picture of an ancient Near Eastern royal banquet, the court world in which the book of Esther opens.
