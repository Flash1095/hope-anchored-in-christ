James Ossuary

Find Name
James Ossuary
Date Discovered
2002
Artifact Date
c. AD 63
Discovery Location
Unknown (surfaced via the antiquities market, said to be from Jerusalem)
Current Location
Private collection, Tel Aviv, Israel
Timeline Era
Early Church
Description
A limestone ossuary bearing the Aramaic inscription 'James, son of Joseph, brother of Jesus,' which surfaced in a private collection and was announced to the world in 2002. The box itself is a genuine first-century ossuary; the final phrase 'brother of Jesus' is the disputed part.
Biblical Significance
We include this find with open eyes: Israel's Antiquities Authority declared the inscription a forgery, its owner was acquitted of forgery charges in 2012 without the court ruling the inscription authentic, and most scholars remain sceptical. Our hope does not rest on this box, and presenting the debate honestly models faith that welcomes scrutiny rather than fearing it.
