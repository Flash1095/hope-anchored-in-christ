Nebuchadnezzar II East India House Inscription

Find Name
Nebuchadnezzar II East India House Inscription
Description
A limestone foundation tablet of Nebuchadnezzar II (604-562 BC), 56.5 by 50.2 cm, now in the British Museum (1938,0520.1). Found at Babylon before 1803 by Sir Harford Jones Brydges, it was given to the museum of East India House in London and later transferred to the British Museum. Its Akkadian text records the king's building works: the Processional Way, the Ishtar Gate, the temples Esagila and Ezida, the city walls, and a treasure house.
Historical Period
Neo-Babylonian Period (626-539 BC)
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The tablet is the builder-king of Daniel 4 in his own words, boasting of the Babylon he rebuilt, including a treasure house for his god - the same complex where Daniel 1 says the Jerusalem temple vessels were stored. It confirms the scale of Nebuchadnezzar's Babylon but is royal propaganda; its claims about the gods' favour should not be taken as history.

Biblical Significance
Nebuchadnezzar's boast in Daniel 4:30, 'Is not this great Babylon, that I have built,' matches the building programme this tablet records in detail, and its treasure house matches where Daniel 1 says the temple vessels were kept.
