Dead Sea Samuel Scroll (4Q51)

Find Name
Dead Sea Samuel Scroll (4Q51)
Description
4Q51, also called 4QSama, is the most extensive Samuel manuscript from Qumran, written in Hebrew in the Herodian period (about 50-25 BC). Hundreds of fragments survive from what was originally a scroll of some 55-60 columns, spanning 1 Samuel 1 through 2 Samuel 24. It was found in Qumran Cave 4 in 1952. Its readings often agree with the ancient Greek Septuagint against the later Masoretic Text.
Historical Period
Second Temple period
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The scroll's most famous contribution is a longer passage before 1 Samuel 11:1 describing Nahash's oppression of the Gadites and Reubenites, gouging out their right eyes, with 7,000 escaping to Jabesh-Gilead; this explains the sudden opening of chapter 11 and was adopted by modern translations such as the NRSV. Whether that passage is original or a secondary expansion is debated: Frank Moore Cross and Emanuel Tov accept it as original, while Dominique Barthelemy and others view it as an expansion, a stalemate noted in recent scholarship. Either way, the scroll shows that more than one Hebrew form of Samuel circulated in the Second Temple period.
Alternative Names

Scripture Illuminated
1 Samuel 1:1-2:10; 1 Samuel 10:27; 1 Samuel 11:1-15; 2 Samuel 24:16-17
Biblical Significance
It supplies the missing background to 1 Samuel 11 and proves that the Hebrew text of Samuel existed in more than one ancient form, sometimes agreeing with the Septuagint against the Masoretic Text.
