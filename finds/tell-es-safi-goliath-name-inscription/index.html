Tell es-Safi Goliath-Name Inscription

A small potsherd found in 2005 at Tell es-Safi, identified with biblical Gath, beneath the destruction layer at the beginning of Iron Age IIA (about 950 BC). Written in Proto-Canaanite script, it bears two words, 'LWT and WLT, read as personal names etymologically related to Goliath. It is the earliest known alphabetic inscription from an Iron Age Philistine site in a well-defined archaeological context.

Interpretations

The names are similar to, and possibly of the same linguistic stock as, the Hebrew name Goliath (GLYT), with Indo-European parallels such as Lydian Alyattes; excavation director Aren Maeir noted that names 'quite similar, and possibly identical, to Goliath were in use' in 10th-9th-century Philistine Gath. Stated exactly: the inscription bears names resembling Goliath's family, NOT Goliath himself. It does not name the biblical giant, does not prove any connection to him, and does not prove the sherd belonged to him; it shows only that Goliath-like names were current in Philistine Gath near the traditional time of David.

Scripture Illuminated

1 Samuel 17:4

Biblical Significance

It shows that names like Goliath's were genuinely in use at Philistine Gath within roughly a century of the traditional date of David, lending cultural plausibility to the biblical account without proving the individual.
