Thanksgiving Hymns Scroll (1QHa)

A collection of psalms and hymns of thanksgiving ('I thank you, O Lord') for deliverance, salvation, knowledge and mercy, and one of the first scrolls found by Bedouin in Qumran Cave 1 in 1947, acquired by Eleazar Sukenik. In the Stegemann/Puech reconstruction it contains about 24 compositions; many give thanks in anticipation of eschatological deliverance rather than only retrospectively. Its core 'Teacher Hymns' (columns 10–17) speak in an intensely personal first-person voice of persecution, suffering and divine revelation, and further copies were found in Cave 4 (4Q427–432).

Interpretations

The Hodayot show the sect's devotional heart: thanksgiving rooted in God's mercy and in confidence about the end, not only in past rescue. Attribution of the Teacher Hymns to the Teacher of Righteousness is widely held but not proven, and some scholars see suffering-servant motifs from Isaiah 52:13–53:12 in them; neither point should be stated as certain, and the hymns are the sect's prayers, not Scripture.

Scripture Illuminated

Isaiah 52:13-53:12

Biblical Significance

These hymns let readers hear Second Temple prayer in its own voice — thanksgiving, suffering and hope side by side — and they form the background for the worship language of the early church.
