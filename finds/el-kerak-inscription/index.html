El-Kerak Inscription

Find Name
El-Kerak Inscription
Description
A small basalt fragment (12.5 cm high x 14 cm wide) inscribed in Moabite, dated about 840 BC (KAI 306), acquired by the Jordan Archaeological Museum in 1958 after reportedly being found at Kerak. Three incomplete lines survive, naming 'K]moshyat, king of Moab, the D[ibonite,' in the temple of Chemosh, on what was originally part of a statue or relief (belt, pleated skirt, and navel visible). It is distinct from the Mesha Stele and must not be merged with it.
Historical Period
Iron Age II; 9th century BC
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The inscription is read by some scholars as commissioned by King Chemoshyat, father of Mesha, or by Mesha himself, a proposed reading of the broken first line as 'I am Mesha, son of Kemoshyat,' placing it in the world of Mesha's rebellion against Israel in 2 Kings 3. Cautions: the text is fragmentary, accounts of the finder differ, and the Mesha reading is a proposal, not established; a distinctive four-stroke He letter (versus the usual three strokes) is a noted paleographic feature.
Alternative Names
Kerak Inscription; Kemoshyat Inscription
Related Books
2 Kings
Scripture Illuminated
2 Kings 3:4-27
Biblical Significance
It adds a second Moabite royal inscription from the age of Mesha's rebellion, confirming Moabite kingship and the Chemosh cult in the same period as 2 Kings 3.
