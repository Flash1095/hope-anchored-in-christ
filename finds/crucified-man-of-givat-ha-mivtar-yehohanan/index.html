Description
An ossuary inscribed for Yehohanan containing the skeleton of a crucified man, with an iron nail still bent through his heel bone. It remains the only clear archaeological evidence of Roman crucifixion from antiquity, because nails were rarely left in victims' bodies.
Biblical Significance
Yehohanan's bones confirm details the Gospels describe: crucifixion victims could receive proper Jewish burial in a family tomb, and nails were driven through the feet. They make the horror Jesus endured physically concrete, and deepen our gratitude that His tomb, unlike this one, was found empty.
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Scholars agree Yehohanan’s remains show Roman crucifixion practice — a nail through the heel bone — matching the Gospel accounts; he is not a biblical figure, and the manner of his death (not his identity) is the point.
