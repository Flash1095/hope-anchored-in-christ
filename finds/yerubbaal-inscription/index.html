Yerubbaal Inscription

Find Name
Yerubbaal Inscription
Description
Three fragments of a small jug (about one litre capacity) bearing an ink inscription in Proto-Canaanite alphabetic script, found in the 2019 season at Khirbet al-Ra'i, about 4 km west of Tel Lachish, in a stone-lined storage pit. Deciphered by Christopher Rollston, the legible letters read yod (broken at the top), resh, bet, ayin, lamed: the name Yrb'l, or Jerubbaal. Dated by pottery typology and radiocarbon to the late 12th to early 11th century BCE (about 1100 BCE), it is the first time the name Jerubbaal has appeared outside the Bible, in a stratum from the Judges period.
Historical Period
Iron Age I
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Two cautions must be stated exactly. First, the excavators Garfinkel and Ganor explicitly say they cannot tell whether the jug belonged to the biblical Gideon: Gideon's battles were in the Jezreel Valley by the Harod Spring, about 120 km north of Khirbet al-Ra'i, so it may be another man named Jerubbaal. Second, epigrapher Haggai Misgav notes the inscription is partial and the reading is not the only possible one: the first letter could be a zayin rather than a yodh, and an ayin may have preceded it, which would give 'Azruba'al' instead. The significance stands even if the owner was a different Jerubbaal, because the name was evidently in common use in the Judges period and appears in the Bible only in that period. Judges 6:32 says Gideon was called Jerubbaal after demolishing Baal's altar.

Biblical Significance
It is the first inscription from the time of the biblical Judges, and it shows the name Jerubbaal in ordinary use in exactly the period Judges assigns to Gideon, supporting the preservation of genuine period names in the biblical tradition.
