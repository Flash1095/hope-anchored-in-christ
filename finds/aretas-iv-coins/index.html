Aretas IV Coins

Find Name
Aretas IV Coins
Description
Bronze coins (and a silver drachm struck with his wife Huldu in 2 BC) of Aretas IV Philopatris ('lover of his people'), king of the Nabataeans from 9 BC to AD 40, minted at Petra. Types show the laureate head of Aretas, or jugate busts of Aretas with his queens Huldu (to AD 16) and then Shaqilath, with two crossed cornucopias and Nabataean letters; his full title 'Aretas, King of the Nabataeans, Lover of his People' first appears in Nabataean Aramaic on coins issued in 1 BC.
Historical Period
Early Roman Period (63 BC-AD 40)
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Aretas's coins confirm the reign of the king Paul names in 2 Corinthians 11:32-33, whose ethnarch guarded Damascus in order to arrest him, and they date the window in which Paul escaped in a basket. Josephus also records Aretas defeating Herod Antipas's army after Antipas divorced Aretas's daughter. The coins do not depict Paul's escape; they anchor the king and his dates.
Alternative Names

Biblical Significance
Paul says that in Damascus 'the governor under King Aretas' guarded the city to seize him (2 Corinthians 11:32-33); Aretas IV's coins, struck at Petra across the years of Paul's early ministry, confirm that this named king was ruling exactly then.
