Wenamun Report

A hieratic papyrus of two pages recounting the journey of Wenamun, a priest of Amun at Karnak, sent around 1075 BC at the end of the Twentieth Dynasty to buy cedar timber at Byblos for the sacred barque of Amun. Robbed at Dor, kept waiting at Byblos for months while its ruler demanded payment instead of giving timber as in former times, and blown to Cyprus on the return voyage, Wenamun's misadventures show an Egypt no longer feared abroad. The single incomplete copy was found in 1890 at al-Hibah in a jar with other texts and bought in Cairo in 1891 by Vladimir Golenishchev; it is now Papyrus Pushkin 120 in the Pushkin Museum, Moscow.

Interpretations

Scholars debate whether the report is a genuine administrative document or literary fiction, but agree it accurately reflects the collapse of Egyptian power in the Levant after the Bronze Age collapse: Byblos, once a vassal supplier of cedar, now bargains as an equal. It is the mirror image of Sinuhe's confident Middle Kingdom world. The cedar trade it describes is the same trade through which Solomon later obtained timber from the Phoenician coast.

Scripture Illuminated

1 Kings 5:1-12

Biblical Significance

It documents the Phoenician cedar trade and the new balance of power on the Levantine coast in the century before Solomon, the background to Hiram's timber dealings with Israel.
