Esarhaddon's Succession Treaty

A loyalty oath imposed by Esarhaddon in 672 BC on vassals across the empire - including, scholars argue, Judah under King Manasseh - to secure the succession of his son Ashurbanipal after the murder of Sennacherib by his own sons. The text, several hundred lines on a tablet about 40 by 28 centimeters, binds each oath-taker to protect Ashurbanipal, report any conspiracy, and accept elaborate curses for disloyalty, sworn before the gods as witnesses. The best-known copies were found in 1955 in the throne room of the Nabu temple at Nimrud (Kalhu); a further copy found at Tell Tayinat in 2009 showed the oath was administered in provincial ceremonies across the empire, not only at court.

Interpretations

The treaty is the closest ancient parallel to Deuteronomy's covenant form - preamble, divine witnesses, stipulations, curses, public reading, and a no-alteration clause - and many scholars think it shaped how Deuteronomy was written or heard in Manasseh's Judah. That influence is debated: it is a form parallel, not proof of copying, and the treaty must not be presented as the source of Deuteronomy. Judah's inclusion is inferred from Manasseh's vassal status and the empire-wide administration of the oath, not from a surviving Judah copy.

Scripture Illuminated

Deuteronomy 28; 2 Kings 21:1-18

Biblical Significance

It illuminates the covenant world of Deuteronomy and the political pressure on Manasseh's Judah, where loyalty to Assyria and loyalty to the LORD pulled in opposite directions.
