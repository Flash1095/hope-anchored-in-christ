Herod Antipas Coins

Find Name
Herod Antipas Coins
Description
Bronze coins of Herod Antipas, tetrarch of Galilee and Peraea (4 BC-AD 39), struck at the Tiberias mint in four denominations (eight units to a denarius). The first series (year 24 = AD 20/21) names the city 'Tiberias' in Greek within a wreath on one side with a reed on the other — the first Jewish coinage to name a city. Middle series (years 33, 34, 37 = AD 29/30, 30/31, 33/34) pair 'Tiberias' in a wreath with a palm branch inscribed 'Herod the Tetrarch'; the final series is year 43 (AD 39). No series carries portraits or figural images.
Historical Period
Herodian Period
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The coins name Herod the Tetrarch and his capital Tiberias, and their avoidance of graven images — unusual for a ruler of his time — shows deference to the Jewish ban on images, matching the picture of a ruler balancing Jewish and Roman worlds. Antipas issued only five series in 43 years, and none before his 24th regnal year, suggesting a light footprint in Galilee.

Biblical Significance
Mark 6 names 'king Herod' hearing of Jesus and recalls his execution of John the Baptist; these are the coins of that tetrarch, struck at Tiberias in the very years of John's death and Jesus' ministry.
