Khirbet el-Qom Tomb Inscription

Find Name
Khirbet el-Qom Tomb Inscription
Description
An inscription from Tomb 2, one of two Iron Age bench tombs at Khirbet el-Qom in the Judean foothills near Hebron, investigated in 1967 by William G. Dever (Hebrew Union College) after discovery by tomb robbers. Dated to the second half of the 8th century BCE, slightly after the Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions. The text, beside a 'magic hand' symbol, reads: 'Uriyahu the honourable has written this. Blessed is/be Uriyahu by Yahweh, and [because?] from his oppressors by his asherah he has saved him. [Written] by Oniyahu.' A jug inscribed 'to/for Yahmol' and a bowl inscribed 'El' were also found. Some scholars identify Khirbet el-Qom with biblical Makkedah (Joshua 10; 12:16; 15:41), presented here as a scholarly identification, not a certainty.
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Line 3's translation is debated, and the central debate is what 'his Asherah' means: the goddess Asherah as Yahweh's consort (the reading that, alongside Kuntillet Ajrud's 'Yahweh of Samaria/Teman and his Asherah,' reshaped understanding of Israelite religion) versus a cult object or sacred pole (the asherah of Judges 6:25–26, 1 Kings 16:33 and 2 Kings 21:7). Unlike Kuntillet Ajrud, no place-name is attached to Yahweh here, read by some as reflecting the period after Samaria's fall, when Yahweh was god of one state only. Note a minor source discrepancy: one academic summary gives the discovery as 1968 rather than the 1967 excavation; 1967 is used here.

Biblical Significance
It is one of only two inscriptions pairing Yahweh with 'his Asherah,' forcing Bible readers to face how widespread Asherah devotion was in Judah even as the prophets condemned it.
