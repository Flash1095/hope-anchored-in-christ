Nebuchadnezzar II Wadi Brisa Inscriptions

Find Name
Nebuchadnezzar II Wadi Brisa Inscriptions
Description
Twin rock inscriptions cut beside a relief of Nebuchadnezzar II at Wadi Brisa (Wadi esh-Sharbin) near Hermel in northern Lebanon. The two faces carry the same Akkadian text, one in Old Babylonian script (about 3.6 by 5.5 m) and one in Neo-Babylonian script (about 2.8 by 5.3 m). The text describes cutting mighty cedars in Lebanon with the king's own hands for the joists of Etemenanki, the ziggurat of Babylon, and making Lebanon safe. The inscriptions were published by Weissbach in 1906 and Langdon in 1912 and date to the king's late reign.
Historical Period
Neo-Babylonian Period (626-539 BC)
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The inscriptions show Nebuchadnezzar projecting his power into the Lebanon mountains to secure the same cedar supply that Solomon had used for the Jerusalem temple centuries earlier. They are royal self-display carved on a strategic mountain route, not a record of a biblical event.
Alternative Names
Twin Inscriptions of Nebuchadnezzar at Brisa;Wadi esh-Sharbin Inscriptions

Biblical Significance
Ezra 3:7 records the returned exiles buying cedar from Lebanon for the rebuilt temple; the Brisa inscriptions show the same Lebanon cedar trade operating under Nebuchadnezzar a century earlier, as the region's kings drew on the same forests.
Artifact Date
