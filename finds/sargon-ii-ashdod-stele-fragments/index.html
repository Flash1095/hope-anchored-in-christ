Sargon II Ashdod Stele Fragments

Find Name
Sargon II Ashdod Stele Fragments
Description
Three fragments of a black basalt victory stele of Sargon II, found during the second season of Israeli excavations on the acropolis at Ashdod in 1963, in two separate locations and secondary contexts. The stele, erected around 711 BC when Ashdod was made an Assyrian province, duplicated the type of Sargon's victory stele at Dur-Sharrukin; it was smashed around 705 BC when Ashdod broke free after Sargon's death. The fragments are stored in the Israel Museum, Jerusalem (IAA numbers 63-1053, 63-931).
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The fragments are direct, on-the-ground confirmation of Sargon's conquest of Ashdod, the event Isaiah 20:1 dates to 'the year that Tartan came unto Ashdod.' Caution: the surviving fragments do not themselves preserve the names Ashdod or its ruler — the identification rests on the stele's type, script and find context, reconstructed by comparison with Sargon's other victory steles. A later report that the fragments moved to the museum in Ashdod is unconfirmed; the scholarly edition (RINAP 2) places them in the Israel Museum.

Biblical Significance
Found at Ashdod itself, the fragments corroborate Isaiah 20:1's report that Assyria 'fought against Ashdod, and took it,' anchoring one of Isaiah's dated prophecies in excavated evidence.
