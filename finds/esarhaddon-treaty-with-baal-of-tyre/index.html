Esarhaddon's Treaty with Ba'al of Tyre

Find Name
Esarhaddon's Treaty with Ba'al of Tyre
Description
A vassal treaty imposed by the Assyrian king Esarhaddon (680-669 BC) on Ba'al I, king of Tyre, dated by its eponym year to the mid-670s BC. The text binds Ba'al to loyalty to the crown prince Ashurbanipal after Esarhaddon's death, requires him to report conspiracies against the crown, and seals the oath with curses on anyone who breaks it. It is one of a series of loyalty oaths Esarhaddon imposed on vassals across the empire, and it survives in a badly preserved text.
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The treaty's chief value for Bible study is its form: preamble, stipulations, witnesses, curses, and public reading closely parallel the covenant structure of Deuteronomy, illuminating what a biblical covenant would have sounded like to its first hearers. That is a form parallel, not evidence that Deuteronomy copies this treaty. The treaty also confirms Tyre as an Assyrian vassal in the 7th century BC, the political background of the Tyre oracles in Isaiah 23 and Ezekiel 26-28. Note the text is badly preserved, so detailed stipulations should be quoted with caution.
Related Places
Tyre
Related Books

Scripture Illuminated

Biblical Significance
It shows the treaty-covenant world in which Deuteronomy's blessings and curses were written, and it confirms Tyre's vassal status under Assyria in the generation before its judgment oracles.
