Yehawmilk Stele

Find Name
Yehawmilk Stele
Description
A Phoenician inscription (KAI 10) from Byblos, set up by Yehawmilk, king of Byblos, son of Yeharbaal and grandson of Urimilk, who says the goddess Baalat Gebal made him king and heard his voice. He records building her a bronze altar, a portico with columns and capitals, and a golden winged disk, and curses anyone who damages the work or removes its name. The main part is in the Louvre (AO 22368); a corner found in the 1930s is in Beirut.
Historical Period
Persian
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Scholars agree on the reading of the royal genealogy and the dedication to Baalat Gebal; the stele was found at the end of Renan's Mission de Phénicie, not in a fully recorded primary context.
Alternative Names
Byblos Stele; de Clercq Stele; KAI 10
Related Places
Gebal; Tyre
Related Books
Ezekiel; Joshua; Psalms
Scripture Illuminated
Ezekiel 27:9; Joshua 13:5; Psalm 83:7
Biblical Significance
Byblos is biblical Gebal, whose shipwrights helped build Tyre's ships in Ezekiel 27 and whose land is named among Israel's unconquered borders in Joshua 13. The stele shows the living religion of that Phoenician port in the Persian period: a king who credits his throne to his city's goddess and repays her with temple building, the exact world of idolatry the prophets confronted.
