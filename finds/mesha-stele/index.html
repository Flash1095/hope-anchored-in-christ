Mesha Stele (Moabite Stone)

Find Name
Mesha Stele (Moabite Stone)
Date Discovered
1868
Artifact Date
c. 840 BC
Discovery Location
Dhiban, Jordan (ancient Dibon, Moab)
Current Location
Louvre, Paris
Timeline Era
Divided Monarchy
Description
A black basalt stele inscribed by Mesha, king of Moab, describing his revolt against Israel and his building projects. It is the longest Iron Age inscription found in the region and is written in the Moabite language, very close to biblical Hebrew.
Biblical Significance
The stele tells the same war described in 2 Kings 3 from the other side, naming Omri of Israel and referring to vessels of Yahweh taken from Nebo. It confirms the biblical picture of Moab, Israel, and Chemosh-era politics, and a proposed reading may also mention the House of David, though that line is damaged and debated.
