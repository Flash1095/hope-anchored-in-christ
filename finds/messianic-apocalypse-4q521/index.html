Messianic Apocalypse (4Q521)

A fragmentary poetic text from Qumran Cave 4, dated to about 100–80 BC, rare in the Qumran corpus for its poetic character and its reference to resurrection of the dead. Its best-known lines read, '(the hea)vens and the earth will listen to His Messiah', followed by a catalogue drawn from Psalm 146 and Isaiah 61 — freeing prisoners, opening the eyes of the blind — with one added line not in the biblical text: 'and revive the dead'. It also says God 'will heal the wounded... and bring good news to the poor', closely paralleling Jesus' answer to John the Baptist in Matthew 11:2–6 and Luke 7:18–23.

Interpretations

The messianic readings of 4Q521 are debated, and the dispute must be stated plainly. First, the key phrase may read 'his Messiah' (singular) or 'his anointed ones' (plural, in defective spelling), which would parallel the 'holy ones' of the second half of the line and point to anointed priests or the Messiahs of Aaron and Israel rather than one figure. Second, the figure is commonly read as an Elijah-type herald rather than a Davidic warrior messiah, since bringing good news to the poor is a prophet's task and another fragment echoes Malachi's Elijah promise that fathers will return to sons. The text therefore illuminates messianic expectation without proving a single Christian reading of it.

Scripture Illuminated

Isaiah 61:1-2; Psalms 146:7-9; Malachi 4:5-6

Biblical Significance

It proves that healing, raising the dead and good news to the poor were already joined as messianic signs before Jesus, which is why his reply to John was understood as a messianic answer.
