Nabonidus Cylinder

Clay cylinders of Nabonidus, last king of Babylon, recording his restoration of temples at Ur and naming his eldest son Belshazzar, to whom he entrusted kingship in Babylon while he sojourned at Tayma. Copies were excavated at Ur in 1854, with related cylinders found at Sippar.

Biblical Significance

Daniel 5 calls Belshazzar king and offers the kingdom's third-highest rank to whoever read the writing on the wall, a detail long questioned because Belshazzar was once thought legendary. The cylinders confirm Belshazzar was real and effectively ruled Babylon for his absent father, explaining both his title and why third place was the highest honour he could give.

Interpretations

Scholars agree the cylinder names Belshazzar as the son and coregent of Nabonidus, explaining Daniel 5’s ‘third ruler’ offer — Belshazzar could only promise third place because his father held first.

Scripture Illuminated

Daniel 5:1-30; Daniel 7:1; Daniel 8:1
