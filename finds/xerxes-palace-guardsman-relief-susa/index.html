Xerxes Palace Guardsman Relief from Susa

Find Name
Xerxes Palace Guardsman Relief from Susa
Description
Glazed-brick relief panels of royal guardsmen from the Achaemenid palace complex at Susa, in the tradition of the famous archers' frieze. The guardsmen march in profile wearing patterned robes, carrying a spear with a pomegranate counterweight, a bow over the shoulder, and a quiver of arrows; they are traditionally identified with the elite royal guard known as the Immortals. The best-known panels, in the Louvre (AOD 487 and AOD 488), come from the Apadana of Darius I (c. 510 BC), excavated by Marcel Dieulafoy in 1885-1886; the same guardsman type decorated the palace buildings of Darius's successor Xerxes I at Susa and Persepolis.
Historical Period
Achaemenid Persian Period (550-330 BC)
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
ATTRIBUTION CAUTION: the famous Louvre guardsmen panels are from Darius I's palace at Susa, not from a palace built by Xerxes himself; this entry covers the Susa palace guardsmen frieze tradition of the Darius I to Xerxes I era, and no panel should be described as a Xerxes palace object without that qualification. The frieze is still valuable for Bible study because it shows the court world of Esther, set in the citadel of Susa, and the royal guard that stood behind the Persian kings of Daniel and Esther.
