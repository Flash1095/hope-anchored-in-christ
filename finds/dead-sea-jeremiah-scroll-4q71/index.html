Dead Sea Jeremiah Scroll (4Q71)

4Q71, also called 4QJer-b, is a Hebrew manuscript of Jeremiah from Qumran Cave 4, found in 1952 and dated to the early 2nd century BC (about 200 BC). Only about 10 percent survives, with fragments of Jeremiah 9:22-10:21, including just 31 legible words in the key fragment. It is one of the oldest surviving copies of Jeremiah.

Interpretations

The scroll agrees with the shorter Greek Septuagint edition of Jeremiah against the longer Masoretic Text in verse order and brevity, supporting the view that the Septuagint translated an earlier, shorter Hebrew edition of Jeremiah rather than abbreviating a longer one. This matters for Bible study because Jeremiah exists in two genuinely different ancient editions, and neither can simply be assumed to be the 'original' throughout. Its fragmentary state means it illuminates only the verses it preserves.

Scripture Illuminated

Jeremiah 9:22-10:21

Biblical Significance

It confirms that the shorter Septuagint form of Jeremiah rests on a real, earlier Hebrew edition, not on a translator's cuts.
