Heshbon Ostraca

Find Name
Heshbon Ostraca
Description
Ink-inscribed potsherds from Tell Hesban (biblical Heshbon), excavated by Siegfried Horn's Heshbon Expedition (1968-1970s seasons) from reservoir fill. Written in Ammonite language and script of the 7th-6th centuries BC, the group documents Ammonite administration at Heshbon; Ostracon IV, an 11-line economic text studied by Frank Cross, lists grain, cattle, flour, and wine delivered as tax, including 35 jars of grain plus 8 sheep or goats to the king.
Historical Period
Iron Age II to Persian transition; 7th-6th century BC
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The ostraca show Heshbon under Ammonite administration after passing from Moabite to Ammonite control, and attest a functioning tax system in the southern Levant on the eve of the exile. Cautions: the ostraca are 7th-6th-century Ammonite documents, NOT from the time of Sihon, king of the Amorites, whose capital Heshbon was (Numbers 21), so Sihon is deliberately not linked as a person; the site's large plastered pool is sometimes linked to the 'pools of Heshbon' of Song of Solomon 7:4, but that link is a possibility, not a proof, and the archaeology is explicitly silent on a Sihon-era battle at Heshbon.
Alternative Names
Tell Hesban Ostraca; Hesban Ostraca
Related Books
Numbers; Song of Solomon
Scripture Illuminated
Numbers 21:25-30; Song of Solomon 7:4
Biblical Significance
They document Ammonite administration at biblical Heshbon centuries after Sihon, showing the city's long later history, while the Sihon-era and pools connections remain site associations, not proofs.
