Tel Hazor Cuneiform Letter

Find Name
Tel Hazor Cuneiform Letter
Description
Cuneiform tablets from Tel Hazor in the Upper Galilee, the largest assemblage of cuneiform documents found at any site in Israel: 19 tablets unearthed since Yigael Yadin's first season in 1956, mostly from the palace area. They include a bilingual Sumero-Akkadian dictionary fragment suggesting a scribal school, commodity shipment records, a multiplication-table school exercise, and letters, among them a Late Bronze Age letter (Hazor 12) ordering textiles, luxury goods, and metal objects to be sent to the writer at Mari, with mention of going on to Ekallatum, and a letter fragment to Ibni-Addu, king of Hazor. A later tablet in Hammurabi-law style contains the words 'lord,' 'slave,' and 'tooth.'
Historical Period
Middle to Late Bronze Age
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The tablets prove Hazor was a major, literate Canaanite city-state with scribal schools, a judicial system, and trade and diplomatic networks reaching Mesopotamia, exactly as Joshua 11:10 describes it as 'the head of all those kingdoms.' The long-hoped-for royal archive itself has not been found; archaeologists believe one may still lie buried in the palace area. The tablets confirm Hazor's importance, not the details of Joshua's conquest, and the last Canaanite palace's thick ash layer is consistent with, but does not prove, the biblical destruction account.

They confirm from Hazor's own documents that it was the greatest Canaanite city-state of the Bronze Age, the city Joshua 11 calls the head of all those kingdoms.
