Nahum Commentary (4Q169)

Find Name
Nahum Commentary (4Q169)
Description
A continuous pesher — a running commentary — on the Book of Nahum from Qumran Cave 4, discovered in August 1952, interpreting successive verses of Nahum as the history of the 1st century BC. Its clearest historical passage reads Nahum 2:11 of Demetrius, king of Greece — identified by many scholars as Demetrius III Eucaerus (95–88 BC), who defeated Alexander Jannaeus but withdrew to Syria — who sought to enter Jerusalem through the counsel of the 'Seekers after Smooth Things', and of the Kittim, here certainly the Romans. The manuscript is generally dated to the early years after Pompey's conquest of Judea in 63 BC.
Historical Period
Hellenistic–Roman
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
Pesher Nahum is a dated witness to how the sect read prophecy as its own recent history: Nineveh's fall becomes the fate of its opponents, the Pharisees ('Seekers after Smooth Things') and the Romans ('Kittim'). The identifications are the commentator's sectarian reading, not the prophet's original meaning — Nahum himself prophesied Nineveh's fall to Babylon — and the commentary's value is historical: it dates sectarian conflicts and shows pesher method at work.

Biblical Significance
It demonstrates the pesher method — prophecy fulfilled in the commentator's own day — that shaped how the first Christians, including the New Testament writers, also read the prophets.
