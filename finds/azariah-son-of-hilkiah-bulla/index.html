Azariah Son of Hilkiah Bulla

Find Name
Azariah Son of Hilkiah Bulla
Description
A clay bulla barely a centimetre across, found in 1982 by Yigal Shiloh in the City of David, one of a hoard of 51 bullae from the 'house of bullae', reading 'Belonging to Azariah, son of Hilkiah'. Hilkiah was the high priest under King Josiah who found the Book of the Law in the Temple in 2 Kings 22:8, and the high-priestly genealogy names his son and successor Azariah (1 Chronicles 6:13-14; Ezra 7:1, where Azariah stands in Ezra's ancestry). The bulla dates to the late 7th century BC, roughly the time of Nebuchadnezzar's first siege of Jerusalem in 605 BC. Note: the bulla itself does not state Azariah's office; the identification with the high-priestly family rests on the two-generation name match, the date, and the stratified Jerusalem context.
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
This is a two-generation match to the Bible's high-priestly family, from a carefully controlled excavation, and it was found in the same hoard as the Gemariah son of Shaphan bulla, whose family served alongside Hilkiah's in Josiah's court. Together the two bullae corroborate the pairing of priest and scribe in 2 Kings 22. The identification is strong but not absolute, since the bulla does not give Azariah's title.

Biblical Significance
The seal of the high priest's son survives from the generation of Josiah's reform, corroborating the family behind the rediscovery of the Book of the Law.
