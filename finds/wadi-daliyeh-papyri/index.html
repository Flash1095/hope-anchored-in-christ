Wadi Daliyeh Papyri

Some two hundred Aramaic legal papyri and seal impressions hidden in a cave north of Jericho by Samarian refugees fleeing Alexander the Great. The documents record marriages, debts, and slave sales in the Samaria of the late Persian period, and seal impressions name governors Sanballat — descendants of the Sanballat who opposed Nehemiah a century earlier.

Biblical Significance

Nehemiah's opponent was not a one-off villain but the head of a dynasty. These papyri show Samaria's governorship continuing in his family line into the age of Alexander, and they open a window onto the ordinary legal life — weddings, loans, sales — of the neighbours Judah lived beside and struggled with.

Interpretations

Scholars agree the papyri are 4th-century Samarian legal documents; the Sanballat seals belong to later governors of that name, descendants of Nehemiah's contemporary, not to the man himself.

Scripture Illuminated

Nehemiah 2:10; Nehemiah 4:1
