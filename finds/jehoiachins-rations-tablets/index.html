Jehoiachin's Rations Tablets

Cuneiform tablets from Nebuchadnezzar's palace storerooms at Babylon, excavated by Robert Koldewey's German expedition and published by Ernst Weidner in 1939. They record monthly oil rations issued to 'Yaukin, king of Judah' and his five sons, prisoners at the Babylonian court.

Biblical Significance

2 Kings ends with a quiet mercy: after thirty-seven years in prison, Jehoiachin is released, given a seat above the other captive kings, and eats at the king's table for the rest of his life. The ration lists are the Babylonian paperwork behind that ending — proof that the last king of Judah was kept alive, fed, and named, far from home.

Interpretations

Scholars agree the tablets record rations for ‘Yaukin, king of Judah’ at Babylon, matching 2 Kings 25:27–30; the tablets’ exact findspot within the palace complex is not fully documented.

Scripture Illuminated

2 Kings 25:27-30
