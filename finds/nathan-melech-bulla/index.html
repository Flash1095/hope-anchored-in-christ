Nathan-Melech Bulla

Find Name
Nathan-Melech Bulla
Description
A clay bulla found in March 2019 in the Givati Parking Lot excavation in the City of David, in a large First Temple-period public building destroyed in the 6th century BC, most likely in the Babylonian destruction of 586 BC. It reads '(belonging) to Nathan-Melech, servant of the king', was deciphered by Anat Mendel-Geberovich, and is dated to the mid-7th to early-6th century BC. Nathan-Melech is the official of King Josiah named in 2 Kings 23:11, whose chamber by the Temple entrance housed the horses dedicated to the sun, which Josiah removed and whose chariots he burned.
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The name, the title 'servant of the king', the date and the Jerusalem find-spot all fit the Nathan-Melech of 2 Kings 23:11, making this a direct attestation of a minor biblical official. Mendel-Geberovich herself cautioned that it is 'not possible to determine with complete certainty' that the bulla's owner is the biblical man, and that caution should be kept with the claim; the bulla is still a genuine seal of a royal official of exactly that name and period.

Biblical Significance
The seal of a royal official named in a single verse of Josiah's reform survives from the Jerusalem building where such officials worked, putting a name from 2 Kings 23:11 into the archaeological record.
