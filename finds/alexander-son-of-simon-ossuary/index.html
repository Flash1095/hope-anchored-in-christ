Alexander Son of Simon Ossuary

Find Name
Alexander Son of Simon Ossuary
Description
A limestone ossuary from a rock-cut family tomb in the Kidron Valley, excavated in 1941 by Eleazar Sukenik and Nahman Avigad, inscribed in Greek 'Alexander (son) of Simon' and on the lid in Greek 'of Alexander' with the Hebrew letters QRNYT, read as 'the Cyrenian.' Mark 15:21 names Simon of Cyrene as 'the father of Alexander and Rufus,' as though his readers would know the sons. DOUBT STATED: Simon and Alexander were both very common names, the tomb held no ossuary for Rufus, and the identification cannot be proven. What is certain is a Cyrenian Jewish family buried in Jerusalem before AD 70; whether its Alexander is Mark's Alexander is proposed, not established.
Historical Period
Roman
Evidence Class
Disputed
Interpretations
Scholars divide: some (Lemaire, and recently Johnston) call the identification serious or likely, noting that this is the only known 'Alexander son of Simon' ossuary and that the Cyrene tag adds specificity; others stress the commonness of both names and the missing Rufus, and treat it as suggestive only. The ossuary itself is authentic; only the link to Mark 15:21 is disputed, and this entry is placed in BORDERLINE for that reason.

Biblical Significance
If the identification were right, this would be the bone box of a man whose father carried Jesus's cross — and whose family, by Mark's way of naming him, was known to the first readers of the Gospel. Even without the identification, the ossuary confirms the world Mark describes: Cyrenian Jews living and buried in Jerusalem, exactly as Acts 6:9 records a synagogue of Cyrenians.
