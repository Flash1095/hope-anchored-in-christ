Porcius Festus Prutah

Find Name
Porcius Festus Prutah
Description
A bronze prutah of Porcius Festus, procurator of Judaea from AD 59 to 62, struck at Jerusalem. It is his only known type, dated Year 5 of Nero (AD 58/59): NERONOS in three lines within a wreath on the obverse and KAISAROS with a palm branch on the reverse. Festus was the last Judaean procurator to issue coins; he is the procurator of Acts 25-26 before whom Paul appealed to Caesar.
Historical Period
Roman Period (63 BC-AD 324)
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Festus's single type is the last procuratorial coinage before the First Jewish Revolt, after which the rebel government struck its own coins and Rome answered with the Judaea Capta series. The coin's aniconic design - wreath and palm branch, no portrait - follows the same accommodation to Jewish sensibilities as the other procurators' bronze. Acts 25:12 records Festus's answer to Paul's appeal: 'Unto Caesar shalt thou go.'
