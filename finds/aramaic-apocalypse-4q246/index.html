Aramaic Apocalypse (4Q246)

Find Name
Aramaic Apocalypse (4Q246)
Description
A short Aramaic fragment from Qumran Cave 4, first acquired in 1958, notable for an early messianic mention of a 'son of God'. It is a single page whose first column is damaged and needs interpretative restoration; the second column begins, 'He will be called the Son of God, and they will call him the Son of the Most High.' It is written in fine early formal Herodian script, dated by Józef Milik (with Émile Puech agreeing) to the latter third of the 1st century BC, and it speaks of distress on the land, the disastrous reign of enemies, and a kingdom that endures.
Historical Period
Hellenistic–Roman
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
4Q246 proves that 'Son of God' and 'Son of the Most High' language for an expected king circulated in Jewish Aramaic writing before the Gospels, closely paralleling Luke 1:32–33, 35 and Daniel 7:13–14. The major debate is the identity of the figure: a messianic king, a historical ruler such as Alexander Balas, or a negative figure (an oppressor) whom God then overthrows. The fragment does not name Jesus and must not be quoted as if it did.

Biblical Significance
It roots the Gospel's Son of God language in pre-Christian Jewish expectation, showing that Luke's announcement to Mary spoke a title its first hearers already knew.
