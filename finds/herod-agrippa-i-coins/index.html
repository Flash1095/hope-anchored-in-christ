Herod Agrippa I Coins

Find Name
Herod Agrippa I Coins
Description
Bronze prutahs of Herod Agrippa I, king of Judaea (ruled AD 37-44; king of Judaea from AD 41), struck at the Jerusalem mint. The year 6 issue (AD 41/42, under Claudius) shows a fringed umbrella-like canopy with the Greek inscription 'of King Agrippa' and, on the reverse, three ears of barley with the date L Ϛ flanking (Hendin 1244; Meshorer TJC 120; RPC I 4981; British Museum example 1908,0110.376, 17 mm, 2.34 g). For non-Jewish areas he also struck portrait and pagan types, including issues with Caligula.
Historical Period
Roman Period
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The coins name King Agrippa — the 'Herod' of Acts 12 who had James put to death and Peter imprisoned. The canopy is read as a symbol of royal power and the barley as the land's fertility; those readings are conventional, not inscribed. The aniconic Jerusalem type contrasts with his portrait issues for Gentile areas, showing the same dual policy as his grandfather's coinage.

Biblical Significance
Acts 12:1-4 opens with 'Herod the king' arresting church members, killing James and seizing Peter; these coins name that king, struck at Jerusalem in AD 41/42, just before those events.
