Persepolis Treasury Tablets

A smaller archive of 746 clay tablets and fragments found in 1936-1938 by Erich Schmidt's Oriental Institute expedition in the Treasury building on the southeast terrace at Persepolis. Written mostly in Elamite, with one Akkadian text, they record payments of silver wages to workers and craftsmen from Darius I's 30th year to Artaxerxes I's 7th year, 492-457 BC, with the largest concentration in Xerxes' 19th and 20th years. They mark the shift from payment in food rations to payment in silver.

Interpretations

The Treasury Tablets cover the reigns of the kings who authorised and funded the rebuilding of Jerusalem's temple and walls, and they show an empire moving to a money economy in exactly the period of Ezra and Nehemiah. COUNT NOTE: some summaries (Livius) cite 139 tablets for the Treasury set; the excavators reported 746 tablets and fragments in total (198 tablets and large fragments plus 548 smaller fragments), and this entry uses the excavators' figure.

Scripture Illuminated

Ezra 7:21; Nehemiah 2:8

Biblical Significance

Artaxerxes' decrees funding Ezra's mission and supplying Nehemiah with timber (Ezra 7:21; Nehemiah 2:8) were issued by the same treasury system these tablets document, showing how Persian royal support for Jerusalem was administered.
