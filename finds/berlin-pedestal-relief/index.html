Berlin Pedestal Relief

Find Name
Berlin Pedestal Relief
Description
A granite pedestal-base fragment about 40 by 46 cm, from the Eighteenth or Nineteenth Dynasty, now in the Egyptian Museum of Berlin (AM 21687). It shows three bound prisoners with name rings reading Ashkelon, Canaan, and a broken third ring read by Manfred Gorg (2001) as 'Israel,' based on a vulture hieroglyph; later imaging and 3D work supported the vulture reading (van der Veen, Theis, Gorg 2010). The piece was purchased in 1913 from antiquities trader M. Nachman by Ludwig Borchardt and is unprovenanced.
Historical Period
New Kingdom, Eighteenth or Nineteenth Dynasty
Evidence Class
Disputed
Interpretations
If the third ring really reads 'Israel,' this would be an earlier extra-biblical mention of Israel than the Merneptah Stele (c. 1208 BC). But the reading is rejected by scholars such as James Hoffmeier on linguistic grounds, the spelling would be unique, and the piece has no excavation context because it was bought on the antiquities market. It must be presented as a contested reading, not as the earliest mention of Israel.

Biblical Significance
It bears on the question of how early the name Israel appears outside the Bible, in the context of Egypt's campaigns among the peoples of Canaan; the reading itself remains disputed.
