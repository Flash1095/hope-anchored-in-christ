Description
A stone inscription found near Paphos in 1877 naming Sergius Paulus as proconsul of Cyprus, with a second inscription at Soloi (c. AD 54) naming a proconsul Paulus and a boundary stone from Rome (AD 47) naming Sergius Paulus as a curator of the Tiber's banks. Together they trace the career of the Roman governor Paul and Barnabas met on Cyprus.
Biblical Significance
Luke calls Sergius Paulus by exactly the right title — proconsul — for a senatorial province in exactly the right decade, and calls the island's capital Paphos. Acts reads like an eyewitness account because, in its names, titles, and geography, it keeps proving to be one.
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
Scholars agree the inscriptions name Sergius Paulus as proconsul of Cyprus in the Claudian era, matching Acts 13:7; the Paphos stone is fragmentary, so the case rests on several inscriptions together.
