Greek Minor Prophets Scroll (Nahal Hever)

Find Name
Greek Minor Prophets Scroll (Nahal Hever)
Description
The Greek Minor Prophets Scroll from Nahal Hever (8HevXIIgr, Rahlfs 943) is a Greek revision of the Old Greek translation of the Twelve Prophets, dated to about 50 BC-AD 50. Fragments first reached scholars through Bedouin in 1952, and nine more fragments were excavated in 1960-1961 from Cave 8, the 'Cave of Horror,' at Nahal Hever in the Judean Desert. Surviving portions cover Jonah, Micah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, and Zechariah, with the Haggai portion lost. The scroll writes the divine name in paleo-Hebrew script within the Greek text.
Historical Period
Second Temple period
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The scroll belongs to the kaige recension (also called Proto-Theodotion), a Greek revision that brings the Old Greek closer to the Hebrew text, and it stands closer to the Masoretic Text than the later Christian copies of the Septuagint. It is therefore key evidence that Greek-speaking Jews were already revising their Bible toward the Hebrew in the first century, before Christian scribes standardized the Septuagint text. Its preservation of the Tetragrammaton in paleo-Hebrew letters, where Christian codices later wrote 'Lord,' shows how the divine name was handled before that change.

Biblical Significance
It is the clearest evidence that the Greek Bible was revised toward the Hebrew before Christianity, and that the earliest Greek copies kept God's name in Hebrew letters.
