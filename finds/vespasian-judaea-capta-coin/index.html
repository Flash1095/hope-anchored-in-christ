Vespasian Judaea Capta Coin

Find Name
Vespasian Judaea Capta Coin
Description
A bronze sestertius (the series also ran in gold, silver, and bronze) of the emperor Vespasian, struck at Rome in AD 71. The obverse shows the laureate head of Vespasian; the reverse reads IVDAEA CAPTA ('Judaea conquered') and shows a palm tree with a mourning Jewess seated on a cuirass beneath it, with the victorious emperor standing with his foot on a helmet (or a bound male captive) and SC in the exergue. The series celebrating the defeat of Judaea, the capture of Jerusalem, and the destruction of the Second Temple by Titus in AD 70 ran for about 25 years under Vespasian, Titus, and Domitian, in at least 48 types.
Historical Period
Roman Period (63 BC-AD 324)
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The Judaea Capta coins are Rome's own monument to the war that destroyed the temple: the mourning woman under the palm became the empire's image of a conquered people, and the image is often linked to Isaiah 3:8 and 3:25-26 ('Jerusalem is ruined, and Judah is fallen... her gates shall lament and mourn'). They are propaganda for the victors and should be read as such, but they independently confirm the date and scale of the AD 70 catastrophe.

Matthew 24:2 and Luke 21:24 record Jesus's predictions of the temple's destruction and Jerusalem being trampled by the Gentiles; the Judaea Capta coins are the Roman celebration of exactly that event in AD 70.
