Code of Lipit-Ishtar

Find Name
Code of Lipit-Ishtar
Description
A Sumerian law collection of Lipit-Ishtar, fifth king of the Dynasty of Isin, the second oldest surviving law code after Ur-Nammu and about a century older than Hammurabi's. Its diorite stele, erected at Nippur, survives only in fragments; most of the text comes from scribal-school tablets from Nippur, and about half the code is preserved. It has the classic shape of prologue, laws introduced by 'if', and epilogue with blessings for a king who honours the stele and curses for one who defaces it, covering property, inheritance, marriage, debt, slaves, and hired oxen.
Historical Period
Bronze Age
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Scholars agree on the code's date, structure, and position between Ur-Nammu and Hammurabi; reconstruction is incomplete, with about half the text lost and the placement of some fragments uncertain.
Alternative Names
Laws of Lipit-Ishtar
Related Books
Exodus; Deuteronomy; Leviticus
Scripture Illuminated
Exodus 21:1-11; Deuteronomy 24:10-22; Leviticus 25:35-43
Biblical Significance
Lipit-Ishtar shows that written, case-by-case law ('if a man...') was already ancient in Mesopotamia long before Moses, and it frames the question the Bible itself answers differently: here law comes from a king appointed by the gods, while in Scripture law comes directly from the Lord. Its parallels in family, debt, and injury law sharpen what is distinctive about Exodus and Deuteronomy.
