Halakhic Letter (4QMMT)

Find Name
Halakhic Letter (4QMMT)
Description
A letter-like sectarian work reconstructed as a composite from six fragmentary Cave 4 manuscripts (4Q394–399), found between 1953 and 1959 and now in the Rockefeller Museum, Jerusalem. It is one of the earliest Qumran sect works, with the original dated by some reconstructions to about 150 BC. It has four parts — a lost opening, a 364-day calendar, more than 20 rulings (halakhot) on Temple ritual and purity, and an epilogue urging separation from 'the multitude' and persuading the addressee — and it argues that disagreement on these rulings caused the sect's separation.
Historical Period
Hellenistic–Roman
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
4QMMT is the earliest evidence of the sect's legal disputes with the Jerusalem establishment, and its phrase 'works of the Torah' matches Paul's 'works of the law' (Galatians 2:16; 3:10; Romans 3:20, 28). The letter says righteousness will be 'reckoned' to those who do what is right, which illuminates but does not settle Paul's argument: the letter's author and Paul use similar language to opposite ends, and the parallel must not be flattened into agreement.
Alternative Names
Miqsat Ma'ase ha-Torah;Some of the Works of the Torah;Some Precepts of the Torah;MMT
Related Books
Galatians; Romans
Scripture Illuminated
Galatians 2:16; Galatians 3:10; Romans 3:20; Romans 3:28
Biblical Significance
It is the closest Second Temple parallel to Paul's 'works of the law' language, and it anchors the New Testament's debates about law and righteousness in a real, datable Jewish dispute.
