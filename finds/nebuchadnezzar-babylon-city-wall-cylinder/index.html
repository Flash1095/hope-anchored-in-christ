Nebuchadnezzar II Babylon City Wall Cylinder

Clay barrel-shaped foundation cylinders of Nebuchadnezzar II commemorating the construction of Babylon's outer city wall. The Akkadian text has the king declare, 'I built a strong wall that cannot be shaken, with bitumen and baked bricks.' Examples survive in the Metropolitan Museum of Art and the Museum of the Bible, dated to about 600 BC, and describe the wall on the east bank of the Euphrates.

Interpretations

The cylinders show the defensive might of the city that besieged and destroyed Jerusalem, and why its fall to Cyrus was so startling. They are foundation deposits - texts buried in the wall itself - so they celebrate the king's achievement rather than recording an event.

Scripture Illuminated

Daniel 4:30

Biblical Significance

The wall cylinders give physical substance to the 'great Babylon' of Daniel 4:30 and to the fortified city whose conquest Daniel 5 describes as happening in a single night.
