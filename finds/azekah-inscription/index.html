Azekah Inscription

Find Name
Azekah Inscription
Description
A fragmentary clay tablet from the reign of Sennacherib (705-681 BC), found in the mid-19th century in the Library of Ashurbanipal at Nineveh and identified as a single tablet by Nadav Na'aman in 1974. Now in the British Museum (K.6205 + BM 82-3-23,131), it preserves part of Sennacherib's account of his 701 BC campaign against Hezekiah of Judah, describing Azekah as a stronghold on a mountain ridge, its walls rivaling the highest mountains, taken with siege ramps and battering rams, then spoiled, destroyed, and burned.
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The inscription is independent Assyrian testimony to Sennacherib's 701 BC invasion of Judah, matching 2 Kings 18-19 and Isaiah 36-37, and it shows the campaign's devastation reaching Azekah, one of Judah's fortified cities. It is fragmentary: the surviving text does not mention Jerusalem or its deliverance, so it neither confirms nor contradicts the biblical account of Jerusalem's survival. It is distinct from the Taylor Prism (already on site as the Sennacherib Prism), which carries the fuller campaign narrative.
Related Books
2 Kings; Isaiah; 2 Chronicles
Related Events
Hezekiah and Sennacherib's Invasion
Scripture Illuminated
2 Kings 18:13; Isaiah 36:1
Biblical Significance
It corroborates Sennacherib's invasion of Judah and the fall of its fortified cities in 701 BC, the historical setting of Hezekiah's prayer and Jerusalem's deliverance.
