Brooklyn Papyrus 35.1446

Find Name
Brooklyn Papyrus 35.1446
Description
A papyrus from the Thirteenth Dynasty (early second millennium BC) listing 95 household servants, many specified as Asiatic or western Asian, now in the Brooklyn Museum. About 30 to 40 bear Semitic names, the servants of a noblewoman named Senebtisi, including forms resembling Shiphrah (the midwife of Exodus 1:15), Jacob, Job, and Issachar, alongside names such as Menahema and Ashera. The papyrus shows a household in Egypt staffed substantially by people from Canaan and Syria.
Historical Period
Middle Kingdom, Thirteenth Dynasty
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The papyrus is real evidence that Semitic-speaking servants from the Levant lived and worked in Egypt in the early second millennium BC, which fits the general setting of Israel's sojourn. But critics rightly warn against calling these names 'specifically Hebrew': they are Northwest Semitic names common across Semitic peoples, no distinct Hebrew language is identifiable this early, and one often-cited name (Hy'b'rw) is more likely 'Hay'abi-ilu' ('Where is my father, o El?'), not 'Hebrew.' It must never be presented as proof of Hebrew slaves in Egypt.

Biblical Significance
It illuminates the world behind Exodus 1, in which people from Canaan lived as servants in Egyptian households, while stopping well short of proving the biblical account.
