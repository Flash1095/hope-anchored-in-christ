Eshbaal Son of Beda Inscription

Find Name
Eshbaal Son of Beda Inscription
Description
An inscription incised before firing on the shoulder of a large ceramic storage jar, reading 'Eshba'al son of Beda' (also written Ishba'al) in Canaanite script. Sherds of the jar were excavated at Khirbet Qeiyafa in the Valley of Elah in 2012 and restored by the excavation team, and the inscription was deciphered by Yosef Garfinkel, Saar Ganor, Mitka Golub and Haggai Misgav. The jar dates to the late 11th to early 10th century BCE, and it is the first time the name Eshbaal has appeared in an ancient inscription.
Historical Period
Iron Age IIA
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The reading itself is large, clear and written by a skilled hand, so the inscription's text is not in doubt. What is debated is how much weight it carries: this Eshbaal is NOT Saul's son, because his father is Beda, a name unique to this inscription and absent from the Bible. Scholars note the chronological pattern instead: the name Eshbaal appears in the Bible and now in archaeology only during David's reign, in the first half of the 10th century BCE, and was not used later in the First Temple period. The Bible itself preserves the name two ways: 1 Chronicles 8:33 keeps Eshbaal, while 2 Samuel 2:10 renders the same Saulide as Ish-bosheth ('man of shame'), reflecting reluctance over the Baal theophoric element.

Biblical Significance
It shows a Baal-theophoric Israelite name in everyday use in David's generation, exactly the period in which the Bible places the name Eshbaal, and it illustrates why the biblical writers sometimes replaced such names.
