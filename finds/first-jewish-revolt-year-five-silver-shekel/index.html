First Jewish Revolt Year Five Silver Shekel

Find Name
First Jewish Revolt Year Five Silver Shekel
Description
A silver shekel of the First Jewish Revolt, about 13.25-13.9 g, dated Year 5 (April to August AD 70), with the same chalice and 'Shekel of Israel' obverse and pomegranates and 'Jerusalem the Holy' reverse as the earlier years. Year 5 shekels are the rarest of all First Revolt shekels - about 12 to 13 known from the classic die sets according to the Menorah Coin Project - struck in only about four months before Titus captured Jerusalem and destroyed the temple in the autumn of AD 70. Several Year 5 shekels were excavated at Masada, showing that some fighters escaped Jerusalem before it fell.
Historical Period
Roman Period (63 BC-AD 324)
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Meshorer noted the striking fact that silver shekels kept being minted in the last four months of the war even after bronze coinage stopped entirely, because paying the half-shekel temple tax remained all-important to the defenders. After the war the revolt coins were banned and many were melted for their silver, which helps explain their rarity. The Year 5 shekel is therefore evidence of worship continuing under siege, not of a functioning economy.

Biblical Significance
Exodus 30:13 is the tax these last shekels were struck to pay, and Matthew 24:2 records Jesus's prediction that not one stone of the temple would be left on another - the destruction that ended their minting within months.
