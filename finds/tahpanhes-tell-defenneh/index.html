Tahpanhes (Tell Defenneh)

Find Name
Tahpanhes (Tell Defenneh)
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Artifact Date
7th–6th centuries BC (fortress town)
Date Discovered
1886 (excavated)
Discovery Location
Tell Defenneh, Nile Delta, Egypt
Current Location
In situ, Nile Delta, Egypt
Timeline Era
Exile
Description
The excavated Egyptian fortress town where, after Jerusalem's fall, the remnant of Judah fled with Jeremiah against his warning, and where Jeremiah hid stones in the clay at the entrance to Pharaoh's palace as a sign that Nebuchadnezzar would set his throne there. Petrie's excavations uncovered the fortress the biblical account calls Pharaoh's house, locally known as the Palace of the Jew's Daughter.
Biblical Significance
Tahpanhes is disobedience given an address. Jeremiah told the remnant not to run to Egypt, and they ran anyway — to this very fortress, where his buried stones promised that Babylon would follow them even there. It is a sobering monument to the God who is present even in the refuge we were told not to seek.
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
Scholars broadly accept Tell Defenneh as biblical Tahpanhes; the 'Palace of the Jew's Daughter' name is traditional, and the stones Jeremiah buried have not been identified among the excavated remains.
