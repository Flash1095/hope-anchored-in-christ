Nuzi Tablets

Find Name
Nuzi Tablets
Description
The Nuzi tablets are several thousand cuneiform tablets from Yorghan Tepe (ancient Nuzi, earlier Gasur), about 10 miles southwest of Kirkuk in Iraq, excavated by American expeditions between 1925 and 1931 under Edward Chiera. They are mostly private legal and business records from the 15th-14th centuries BC, when Nuzi was a Hurrian town in the kingdom of Arrapha under Mitanni rule. The tablets record adoptions, marriages, inheritances, and household law in unusual detail.
Historical Period
c. 15th-14th centuries BC
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Nuzi customs famously parallel Genesis: a childless man adopting an heir (compare Genesis 15:2-3, where Abram names Eliezer of Damascus), a wife giving her handmaid to her husband (Genesis 16:1-4; 30:1-13), household gods (teraphim) as tokens of inheritance (Genesis 31:19, 31:34-35), and a man working years for his father-in-law (Genesis 29:15-30). These are parallels, not proof: later study has refined or set aside some proposed parallels, Nuzi is later than the patriarchal setting, and the tablets show a shared Hurrian cultural zone rather than events from Genesis itself.

Biblical Significance
Nuzi makes patriarchal customs in Genesis - adoption for inheritance, handmaids, and teraphim - understandable as real ancient Near Eastern law rather than strange inventions.
