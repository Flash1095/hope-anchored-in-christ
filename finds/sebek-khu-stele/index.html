Sebek-khu Stele

A limestone stele of the Middle Kingdom official Khu-sobek (Sebek-khu), dated c. 1880-1840 BC. Found in 1901 outside Khu-sobek's tomb at Abydos by John Garstang, it is now in the Manchester Museum (ID 3306). Its inscription records the earliest known Egyptian military campaign in Canaan (Retjenu), including the line 'Then Sekmem fell, together with the wretched Retenu.'

Interpretations

The stele is genuine and is the earliest Egyptian record of a campaign into Canaan, but the identification of Sekmem with the biblical city of Shechem is a scholarly proposal ('thought to be'), not a certainty, and the stele does not mention Israel or any biblical figure. It should be presented as evidence of Egyptian activity in Canaan in the patriarchal era, not as confirmation of any Genesis event.

Biblical Significance

It confirms that Canaanite cities such as Shechem were already significant and contested in the Middle Kingdom period, the general era in which the patriarchal stories are set.
