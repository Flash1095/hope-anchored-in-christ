Izbet Sartah Ostracon

Find Name
Izbet Sartah Ostracon
Description
A clay potsherd found in 1976 in storage silo 605 of the Stratum II four-room house at Izbet Sartah, excavated under Moshe Kochavi. Dated to the 12th or early 11th century BC, it carries five lines in early Proto-Canaanite script: four lines of practice exercises by an unskilled or student scribe, and a fifth line forming a complete 22-letter abecedary. It is one of the oldest known non-cuneiform abecedaries.
Historical Period
Iron Age I
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The abecedary, with pe-before-ayin order also seen elsewhere, shows alphabetic literacy already practiced in a small hill-country village in the early Iron Age, the world of the judges. Cautions: the site is frequently identified, tentatively, as biblical Ebenezer, but that identification is not settled, so the 1 Samuel 4 link is flagged as tentative; and a proposed reading of line 1 ('Oreph son of Nahum') is a proposal, not an established reading.
Alternative Names

Biblical Significance
It illustrates the spread of alphabetic writing in early Iron Age Israel, the cultural setting in which the books of Samuel would later be written down, while the Ebenezer identification behind the passage link remains tentative.
