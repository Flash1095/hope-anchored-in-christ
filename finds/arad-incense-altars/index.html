Arad Incense Altars

Find Name
Arad Incense Altars
Description
Two small limestone incense altars standing at the entrance to the Holy of Holies (cella, about 2 m by 2 m) of the 8th-century BCE Judahite shrine at Tel Arad in the Negev, in use about 760–715 BCE. The shrine was excavated by Yohanan Aharoni in 1963–1965, and the Holy of Holies, with its original stairs, floor and furniture, was transferred to the Israel Museum, Jerusalem, where it has been on display since the museum opened in May 1965. Residue analysis published in 2020 (Arie and others, Tel Aviv journal, vol. 47, issue 1) found frankincense mixed with animal fat on the larger altar (just under 1 m tall) and cannabis resin (THC, CBD, CBN plus terpenes) mixed with animal dung on the smaller altar (40 cm tall). This is a specific item within the Arad temple site, distinct from the site's existing 'Arad Temple' and 'Arad Ostraca' entries.
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The residue identifications come from two independent laboratories and are solid, but the interpretation needs care: the researchers concluded cannabis was deliberately used as a psychoactive in cultic ceremonies, the first such evidence in the cult of Judah and the first identification of cannabis in the ancient Near East, and that it must have been imported, implying state-level backing. Separately, the popular 'kaneh-bosm = cannabis' etymology for Exodus 30:23 (Sula Benet) is a linguistic hypothesis, NOT proven by the Arad find, and must not be presented as its conclusion.
