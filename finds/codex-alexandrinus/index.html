Codex Alexandrinus

Find Name
Codex Alexandrinus
Description
A Greek manuscript of the whole Bible on parchment, designated A or 02, one of the four great uncial codices alongside Sinaiticus, Vaticanus, and Ephraemi Rescriptus. About 773 leaves survive, written in two columns of uncial script, containing most of the Septuagint and the New Testament plus 1 Clement and part of 2 Clement. Patriarch Cyril Lucar presented it to King Charles I of England in 1627; it is catalogued as Royal MS 1 D V-VIII.
Historical Period
Byzantine
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
Scholars agree on the fifth-century date and on its standing among the great uncials; its text-type is mixed, Byzantine-leaning in the Gospels and Alexandrian elsewhere, and its exact place of origin (probably Egypt) is debated.
Alternative Names
Codex A; Uncial 02
Related Places
Alexandria
Related Books
Revelation; John; Luke
Scripture Illuminated
Revelation 22; John 1; Luke 24
Biblical Significance
With Sinaiticus and Vaticanus, Alexandrinus is one of the earliest nearly complete Bibles, showing that by the fifth century Christians were already copying the whole Greek Bible into single volumes. Its text is especially valued for Revelation, and it anchors confidence that the New Testament we read rests on very early, carefully made copies.
