Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsa-a)

Find Name
Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsa-a)
Description
The Great Isaiah Scroll is the only complete biblical book found among the Dead Sea Scrolls, containing all 66 chapters of Isaiah in Hebrew on 17 sheets of parchment sewn together into a single scroll about 24 feet long. It was found in Qumran Cave 1 in 1947 by Bedouin shepherds and was among the first seven scrolls recovered. Paleography dates the copy to about 125 BC, roughly a thousand years older than the medieval Masoretic codices. Its text agrees closely with the later Masoretic Text, with over 2,600 mostly minor variants of spelling and grammar.
Historical Period
Second Temple period
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The scroll is the strongest single witness to the stability of the Hebrew text of Isaiah across a thousand years: the book readers know today is essentially the book copied before the time of Christ. Its variants are mostly orthographic, but a few are theologically interesting, such as Isaiah 53:11, where the scroll adds the word 'light' ('he shall see light'). Because it predates Jesus, it also confirms that Isaiah 53 and the other messianic passages were written before the events Christians see them fulfilled in, not composed afterwards.

Biblical Significance
It proves the book of Isaiah was copied faithfully for over a thousand years, and that its prophecies, including Isaiah 53, existed in full more than a century before Jesus.
