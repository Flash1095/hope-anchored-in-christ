Antonius Felix Prutah

Find Name
Antonius Felix Prutah
Description
A bronze prutah of Antonius Felix, procurator of Judaea from AD 52 to 59, struck at Jerusalem. Two types exist, both dated Year 14 of Claudius (AD 54): one with TI KLAUDIOS KAISAR GERMANIKOS and two crossed palm branches on the obverse and IOULIA AGRIPPINA in four lines within a wreath on the reverse (Claudius and his wife Agrippina the Younger); the other with NERON KLAUDIOS KAISAR around two crossed shields and spears and BRITANNIKOS KAISAR with a palm tree (the imperial heirs Nero and Britannicus). Felix is the procurator of Acts 23-24 who kept Paul imprisoned at Caesarea.
Historical Period
Roman Period (63 BC-AD 324)
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Felix's coins name the imperial family, not Felix himself, so they are Contextual rather than Direct evidence: they show the Roman administration under which Paul was held, with its careful avoidance of the emperor's portrait on Judaean bronze. Acts 24:24-26 describes Felix meeting Paul and hoping for a bribe; the coins are the small change of that administration.

Biblical Significance
Acts 24:27 says Felix left Paul in prison for two years to do the Jews a favour; his prutahs, dated AD 54, are the coinage of the procurator who held the apostle at Caesarea.
