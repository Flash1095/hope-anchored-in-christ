Jerusalem Beka Weight

Find Name
Jerusalem Beka Weight
Description
A tiny First Temple-period stone weight inscribed with the ancient Hebrew word 'beka' in reverse (mirror) script, the only known mirror-written example, attributed to a seal-craftsman confusing the direction of the script. It was found in 2018 by a volunteer in the City of David wet-sifting project at Emek Tzurim, in soil from 2013 excavations under Robinson's Arch at the Western Wall foundations, excavated under Eli Shukron of the Israel Antiquities Authority. A beka is a half-shekel, about 5.7-6 g against a shekel of about 11.33 g, and was the Temple tax weight of Exodus 38:26; in the First Temple period there were no coins, so silver was weighed against such stones.
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The weight makes the half-shekel Temple tax of Exodus 38:26 tangible: every Israelite man gave a beka, and this is the physical standard such a payment would have been weighed against. Its mirror writing is a human slip that reminds readers these standards were hand-cut by craftsmen, not machine-made.

Biblical Significance
This small stone is the actual unit of the half-shekel Temple tax, turning the census offering of Exodus 38:26 from an abstract amount into something that can be held in the hand.
