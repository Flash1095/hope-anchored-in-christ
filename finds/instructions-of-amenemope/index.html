Instructions of Amenemope

Find Name
Instructions of Amenemope
Description
An Egyptian wisdom text of thirty short chapters, a father's advice to his son, preserved on Papyrus British Museum EA 10474, bought by the British Museum in 1888 and said to come from el-Hiba. Most likely composed between about 1300 and 1000 BC, it was published in 1923, and in 1924 Adolf Erman showed that Proverbs 22:17-24:22 runs closely alongside it: both open with 'incline your ear' and 'apply your heart,' both warn against moving the ancient boundary stone, and both picture ill-gotten wealth sprouting wings and flying away.
Historical Period
New Kingdom, Ramesside period
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The parallels are real and close, and most scholars think the direction ran from Egypt to Israel through scribal training, with the Hebrew writer adapting rather than copying: Proverbs grounds the same maxims in the fear of Yahweh ('for the LORD will plead their case,' Proverbs 22:23) rather than in Egyptian ma'at. The thirty chapters of Amenemope also help explain the 'thirty sayings' of Proverbs 22:20. This is a parallel that illuminates how biblical wisdom literature was composed, not a challenge to inspiration: Scripture itself presents Proverbs as gathered wisdom.

Biblical Significance
It is the closest known parallel to a whole section of Proverbs, showing that Israel's sages worked within a wider ancient Near Eastern wisdom tradition while reshaping it around the fear of the LORD.
