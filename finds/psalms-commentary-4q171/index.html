Psalms Commentary (4Q171)

Find Name
Psalms Commentary (4Q171)
Description
A pesher on the Psalms from Qumran Cave 4, principally on Psalm 37, and one of the three pesharim on Psalms. It reads Psalm 37:25–26 ('I have been young, and now I am old, but I have not seen a righteous man abandoned...') as referring to the Teacher of Righteousness, whom God chose to found the congregation of his chosen ones, and it reports that the Wicked Priest sought to kill the Teacher for sending him a law — a law some scholars suggest was 4QMMT. Radiocarbon dating at Arizona gave a one-standard-deviation range of 22–78 AD for 4QpPsa.
Historical Period
Hellenistic–Roman
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The Psalms Commentary shows the sect applying the Psalms directly to its founder's life and enemies, the same move as the Habakkuk pesher, with which it shares language and themes suggesting common authorship or method. The Teacher's identity remains unknown, and the Wicked Priest is most commonly argued to be Jonathan Apphus or Alexander Jannaeus, but the identifications are scholarly reconstructions from nicknames, not names in the text, and must be presented as such.

Biblical Significance
It shows Second Temple Jews reading the Psalms as prophecy about their own community and leader — the interpretive world in which the New Testament also applies the Psalms to Christ.
