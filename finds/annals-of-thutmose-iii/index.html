Annals of Thutmose III

Find Name
Annals of Thutmose III
Description
Hieroglyphic military records of the campaigns of Pharaoh Thutmose III in Syria-Palestine, inscribed on the walls of the chamber housing the holy of holies at Karnak, from a daily journal kept by the royal scribe Tjaneni. The fullest account is the first campaign, the Battle of Megiddo in year 23, when Thutmose surprised a Canaanite coalition led by the king of Kadesh by marching through the narrow Aruna pass; later campaigns are recorded more briefly, with emphasis on booty and tribute.
Historical Period
Bronze Age
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Scholars agree the Annals derive from Tjaneni's campaign journal and are the fullest Egyptian military record; as royal propaganda they must be read with caution, and the absolute year of the battle (commonly 1457 BC, sometimes 1479 BC) depends on the Egyptian chronology adopted.
Alternative Names
Annals of Thutmosis III; Karnak Annals

Biblical Significance
Megiddo is the Armageddon of Revelation 16 and a battleground already named in Joshua's conquest lists and Deborah's song, and the Annals are the most detailed battle account to survive from the ancient world. They fix Megiddo's strategic importance on the Egypt-to-Euphrates road and show the Canaanite city-state world Israel would later enter.
