Damascus Document

A rule text of the Qumran-associated community, first published by Solomon Schechter from two medieval manuscripts (10th and 12th centuries AD) found in 1896–1897 in the Cairo Geniza of the Ben Ezra Synagogue. Ten further copies were later found at Qumran (4Q266–273, 5Q12, 6Q15); the oldest Qumran copies date to the late 2nd–early 1st centuries BC, and the work was likely composed or redacted in the mid-2nd century BC. It has two parts: an exhortation recalling the community's origins and a body of statutes on marriage, purity, oaths, Sabbath, food and communal organization, and it introduces the Teacher of Righteousness and the Wicked Priest.

Interpretations

The Document is the fullest picture of the sect's self-understanding: a covenant community that saw itself as the faithful remnant after separating from a corrupt establishment. Whether its 'Damascus' is literal Syria, symbolic exile, or Qumran itself is debated (it quotes Amos 5:27 on exile 'beyond Damascus'), so the place name must not be read as a simple travel record. Its identification of the group with the Essenes is common but debated.

Scripture Illuminated

Amos 5:27

Biblical Significance

It shows a Second Temple Jewish community organizing its whole life around covenant obedience and purity, the world from which both Rabbinic Judaism and the early church emerged.
