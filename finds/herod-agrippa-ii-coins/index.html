Herod Agrippa II Coins

Find Name
Herod Agrippa II Coins
Description
Bronze coins of Herod Agrippa II, the last Herodian ruler (reigned AD 50-c. 100), the most extensive and most problematic of the Herodian coinages because dating by his regnal eras is complex (the common view uses an era starting AD 60/61). Flavian-era denominations pair imperial portraits with fixed reverses: the largest shows Vespasian with the Tyche of Caesarea Philippi, the middle Titus with Nike advancing holding wreath and palm, and the smallest Domitian with Nike writing on a shield (years 14, 15, 18). Earlier issues include a palm-branch coin struck at Tiberias in AD 53 and portrait issues under Nero.
Historical Period
Roman Period
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The coinage is Agrippa's — the king before whom Paul defended himself in Acts 25-26 — though many issues name only the emperor, with Agrippa's name on the reverse. The Nike iconography echoes the Roman Iudea Capta series, but Meshorer argues Agrippa did not intend to celebrate his own people's defeat; the victory celebrated is the Flavian victory in the civil war. Regnal-era dating remains debated, so year attributions carry that caution.

Biblical Significance
Acts 25:13 records Agrippa's arrival at Caesarea to greet Festus, leading to Paul's defense before him in Acts 26; these are the coins of that king, struck across the decades of the early church's growth.
