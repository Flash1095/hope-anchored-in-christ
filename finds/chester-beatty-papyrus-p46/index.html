Chester Beatty Papyrus P46

Find Name
Chester Beatty Papyrus P46
Description
Chester Beatty Papyrus P46 (P. Chester Beatty II) is a Greek codex of Paul's letters, dated to about AD 175-225 and most often placed around AD 200. Found in Egypt, most likely in the Faiyum, it was purchased in 1930-31 and published in the 1930s. Of about 104 leaves, 86 survive: 56 in Dublin and 30 at the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor. It contains Romans (from 5:17), Hebrews, 1-2 Corinthians, Ephesians, Galatians, Philippians, Colossians, and 1 Thessalonians, in that order.
Historical Period
Early Church period
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
P46 is the earliest extensive witness to a collected Paul, and its order is itself evidence: Hebrews placed directly after Romans shows that Egyptian Christians by about AD 200 already counted Hebrews among Paul's letters. Its Alexandrian text, closest to Codex Vaticanus, anchors many readings of the Pauline epistles a century before the great parchment codices. The Romans doxology (16:25-27) appears after chapter 15, illustrating that even the arrangement of Romans was not yet fixed. A late-1st-century date proposed in 1988 has gained little acceptance, and some scholars prefer a 3rd-century date, so its dating should be stated as a range.

Biblical Significance
It is the earliest large collection of Paul's letters, proving the Pauline corpus circulated as a set by about AD 200, with Hebrews already included.
