Great Psalms Scroll (11Q5)

Find Name
Great Psalms Scroll (11Q5)
Description
The Great Psalms Scroll, 11Q5 or 11QPsa, was found in Qumran Cave 11 in 1956 and is dated to about AD 30-50. It contains roughly 50 compositions, including about 40 canonical psalms arranged in a strikingly different order from the traditional Psalter, mostly from Psalms 101-150, plus eight non-Masoretic compositions. Among its extras are Psalm 151 in Hebrew, previously known only from the Greek Septuagint, and a prose list called 'David's Compositions' crediting David with thousands of songs.
Historical Period
Second Temple period
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The scroll challenges the idea that the Psalter's order and contents were fixed early: a first-century community used a Psalms collection ordered differently and containing pieces outside the traditional 150. Its Hebrew Psalm 151 confirms that the Greek psalm had a Hebrew original, and its 'David's Compositions' shows how David's reputation as psalmist and sage was celebrated at Qumran. The scroll's order is not evidence that the traditional Psalter is wrong; it is evidence that more than one arrangement circulated, and the traditional arrangement is the one that endured in Jewish and Christian Bibles.
