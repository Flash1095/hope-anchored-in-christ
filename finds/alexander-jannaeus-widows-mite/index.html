Alexander Jannaeus Widow's Mite

A small bronze prutah or lepton of Alexander Jannaeus (Yehonatan), king of Judaea from 103 to 76 BC, struck at Jerusalem around 95-76 BC. One side shows an eight-rayed star within a diadem with the Paleo-Hebrew legend 'Yehonatan the King' between the rays; the other shows an inverted anchor within the Greek legend 'of King Alexander' (BASILEOS ALEXANDROU). Jannaeus was the first Jewish king to use the title 'King' on his coins and to mint bilingual coins, and his coinage avoided human and animal images. His coins are the most typical Jewish coins found in Jerusalem.

Interpretations

ATTRIBUTION CAUTION: the identification of Jannaeus's small bronzes with the widow's two mites (Mark 12:41-44; Luke 21:1-4) is TRADITIONAL, not certain. The Gospels name no ruler, and any small circulating prutah or lepton of the period could fit; Jannaeus pieces are candidates because they were struck in vast numbers and were still circulating in the first century AD. The coin itself is securely Jannaeus's; the widow's-mite link is a traditional association, not a proven identification.

Scripture Illuminated

Mark 12:41; Luke 21:1

Biblical Significance

Mark 12:41-44 and Luke 21:1-4 describe a poor widow giving two small coins at the temple; Jannaeus's tiny bronzes, still in circulation in Jesus's day, are the traditional candidates for those coins, and they show what such an offering physically looked like.
