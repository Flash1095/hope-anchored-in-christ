Eliakim Seal Impressions from Lachish

Two identical seal impressions (bullae) unearthed in 2014 in renewed excavations at Tel Lachish led by Yosef Garfinkel, Michael Hasel and Martin Klingbeil, reported in the Bulletin of the American Schools of Oriental Research in May 2019. The first was found in situ inside a small juglet in the destruction layer left by Sennacherib's assault on Lachish (Lachish level III, 701 BC); the second was identified months later after the surrounding earth was wet-sifted in Jerusalem. The inscription reads 'Belonging to Eliakim (son of) Yehozarah'. Scholars propose the impressions may belong to Eliakim son of Hilkiah, chief steward of King Hezekiah, who held the key of the house of David (Isaiah 22:20-22) and negotiated with Sennacherib's officials (2 Kings 18:18). These are not the 'Eliakim, steward of Yaukin' jar-handle impressions from Tell Beit Mirsim and Beth Shemesh, which were once linked to King Jehoiachin but are now dated to the late 8th century BC.

Interpretations

The impressions come from exactly the right context for Hezekiah's steward, the 701 BC destruction of Lachish, but the patronymic on the seal is Yehozarah, not Hilkiah, so the identification with the biblical Eliakim is proposed, not certain, and the entry is classed Contextual for that reason. The case is still suggestive: a senior official's seal, sealed in the destruction debris of the Assyrian campaign the Bible describes.

Scripture Illuminated

Isaiah 22:20

Biblical Significance

Seals from the ashes of Sennacherib's destruction of Lachish may bear the name of Hezekiah's chief steward, the man Isaiah says held the key of David's house.
