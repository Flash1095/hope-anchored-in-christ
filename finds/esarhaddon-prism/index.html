Esarhaddon Prism

Find Name
Esarhaddon Prism
Description
A six-sided clay prism, 33 cm tall, inscribed with 493 lines of cuneiform, dated to 673–672 BCE. It was found in the 1927–28 excavation season at Nineveh by Reginald Campbell Thompson and records Esarhaddon's reign and building projects. Among the kings supplying materials for his palace at Nineveh it names 'Menasii, king of Judah' — Manasseh. The prism was published by Thompson in 1931 by order of the trustees of the British Museum, where it is held.
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The prism names Manasseh of Judah as a tributary king supplying building materials, confirming Manasseh as a historical vassal of Assyria during the period 2 Kings 21 and 2 Chronicles 33 describe. It does not mention his reported capture and repentance (2 Chronicles 33:11-13), which comes from the biblical account alone. Caution: several Esarhaddon prisms exist with parallel texts — this entry is specifically the prism from Thompson's 1927–28 Nineveh excavations; parallel texts are a naming caution, not an authenticity dispute.

Biblical Significance
By naming 'Manasseh, king of Judah' among Assyria's tributaries, the prism confirms the historical existence and vassal status of the king whose long reign 2 Kings 21 and 2 Chronicles 33 describe.
