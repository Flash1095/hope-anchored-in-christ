Philip the Tetrarch Coins

Find Name
Philip the Tetrarch Coins
Description
Bronze coins of Philip the Tetrarch (4 BC-AD 34), who ruled the predominantly non-Jewish territories of Ituraea, Trachonitis, Gaulanitis, and the Paneas region, minted at Caesarea Philippi (Paneas). Philip was the first Jewish ruler to place portraits on coins: every issue carries a portrait of Augustus or Tiberius, and sometimes his own — the year 5 (AD 1/2) issue pairing Augustus and Philip is described as the first portrait of a Jewish king. Reverses show the Augusteum, the temple Herod built at Paneas dedicated to Augustus (for example year 37 = AD 33/34, Tiberius portrait with the Augusteum and the year between the columns).
Historical Period
Herodian Period
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The coins name Philip the Tetrarch (Luke 3:1) and show why portrait coinage was possible for him but not for his brothers: few Jews lived in his territory, so the ban on graven images was less binding there. The temple reverse is identified as the Augusteum at Paneas; an earlier reading took it for the Jerusalem Temple, but the Paneas identification is standard.

Biblical Significance
Luke 3:1 dates John the Baptist's ministry by naming 'Philip tetrarch of Ituraea and of the region of Trachonitis'; these coins name that tetrarch and were struck in the years of John's and Jesus' ministries.
