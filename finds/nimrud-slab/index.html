Nimrud Slab of Adad-nirari III

The surviving upper half of a gypsum slab inscribed with a summary inscription of the Assyrian king Adad-nirari III (reigned 811-783 BC). Found at Nimrud (ancient Calah) in 1854 by William Loftus, the slab was then lost and only relocated in 1993 in the Upper Chambers; for over a century scholars knew it only from a squeeze made by Edwin Norris. Its text lists lands subdued and made tributary, including Tyre, Sidon, Edom, Palastu (Philistia), and Humri - the Assyrian name for the land of Omri, that is, Israel.

Interpretations

The slab names Israel (as Humri, 'land of Omri') among the western lands brought under Assyrian dominance in the early 8th century BC, matching the biblical picture of a weakened Israel under Aramean pressure. Scholars widely identify Adad-nirari III as the 'deliverer' of 2 Kings 13:5, though the Bible does not name him; that identification is strong but inferential, not stated in the text.

Scripture Illuminated

2 Kings 13:5

Biblical Significance

It places Israel on the Assyrian record a generation before the fall of Samaria, and gives a historical face to the unnamed 'deliverer' God raised up for Israel in 2 Kings 13:5.
