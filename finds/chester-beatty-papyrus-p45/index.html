Chester Beatty Papyrus P45

Chester Beatty Papyrus P45 (P. Chester Beatty I) is a Greek codex containing portions of the four Gospels and Acts, dated to about AD 175-225. Found in Egypt, possibly in the Faiyum or at Aphroditopolis, it was purchased by Alfred Chester Beatty in 1931 and announced by Frederic Kenyon in 1933. Only 30 leaves survive of an original codex of about 220 pages. It is the earliest known example of the four Gospels gathered in one volume together with Acts.

Interpretations

P45 shows that by around AD 200, Christians were already collecting the four Gospels as a set, not circulating them only separately. Its text is eclectic with an Alexandrian leaning, classed Category I, and its shorter readings in places illustrate how later scribes sometimes expanded the text. One leaf (Matthew 25:41-26:39) is in Vienna rather than Dublin, a reminder that ancient codices were often split by the antiquities trade.

Scripture Illuminated

Matthew 20-21; Matthew 25-26; Mark 4-9; Mark 11-12; Luke 6-7; Luke 9-14; John 4-5; John 10-11; Acts 4-17

Biblical Significance

It is the earliest codex gathering all four Gospels with Acts, showing the fourfold Gospel collection already existed around AD 200.
