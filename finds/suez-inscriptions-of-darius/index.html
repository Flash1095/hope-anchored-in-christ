Suez Inscriptions of Darius the Great

Find Name
Suez Inscriptions of Darius the Great
Description
Five monuments set up along Darius the Great's canal through Wadi Tumilat, joining the Nile to the Red Sea, inscribed in Old Persian, Elamite, Babylonian, and Egyptian. The best preserved, the pink granite Chalouf (Shaluf) Stele found in 1866, records in Darius's own voice: 'I am a Persian; setting out from Persia I conquered Egypt. I ordered to dig this canal from the river that is called Nile... to the sea that begins in Persia.'
Historical Period
Persian
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Scholars agree the stelae commemorate Darius's canal; classical authors disagree on whether Darius actually completed the canal or whether it was finished under Ptolemy II, and Darius's claim to have 'conquered Egypt' overstates the record, since Cambyses II had conquered Egypt before him.

Biblical Significance
Darius is the Persian king of the world in which the exiles returned and the temple was rebuilt, and these stelae are his own voice claiming Egypt and the canal as his achievement. They set the stage for Ezra and Nehemiah: a Persian empire that could move armies, officials, and temple vessels across continents, and whose kings' decrees shaped Judah's restoration.
