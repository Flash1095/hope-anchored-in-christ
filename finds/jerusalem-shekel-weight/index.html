Jerusalem Shekel Weight

Find Name
Jerusalem Shekel Weight
Description
A polished limestone dome weight of the full shekel standard, about 11.3-11.4 grams, of the kind found by the hundred in Jerusalem and Judah. Many carry their denomination engraved in ancient Hebrew script. Before coins reached Judah (about 600 BC), silver was weighed against such stones on balance scales: a shekel was a weight before it was a coin, and the full shekel is the unit of which the beka — already in this collection — is the half.
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Shekel weights are well attested and their standard is agreed at about 11.3-11.4 grams, though individual examples vary slightly and the 'shekel of the sanctuary' may have differed marginally from the ordinary commercial shekel. This entry describes the type, not one single provenanced stone, and is deliberately distinct from the inscribed beka weight and pim weight already in the collection.

Biblical Significance
The shekel is the Bible's standard of value from Genesis to the Temple tax: Abraham weighs four hundred shekels for Machpelah, Jeremiah weighs seventeen shekels for the field at Anathoth, and Exodus 30:13 fixes the sanctuary tax at half a shekel 'after the shekel of the sanctuary.' Holding the full weight makes the half-shekel of the beka, and every weighed payment in Scripture, a physical thing rather than an abstraction.
