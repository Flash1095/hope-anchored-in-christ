Rylands Papyrus 458

Rylands Papyrus 458 (P. Ryl. Gr. 3 458; Rahlfs 957) consists of eight small fragments from four columns of a Greek roll of Deuteronomy, dated to the mid-2nd century BC. Written in Egypt and recovered from mummy cartonnage at Arsinoites in the Faiyum by James Rendel Harris, it was acquired by the John Rylands Library in 1917. It is the oldest known manuscript of the Greek Bible (Septuagint). The surviving text covers Deuteronomy 23:24-24:3; 25:1-3; 26:12; 26:17-19; and 28:31-33.

Interpretations

As the earliest Septuagint manuscript, it proves a Greek Deuteronomy circulated in Egypt more than a century before Christ. Its text agrees more with Codex Alexandrinus than with Codex Vaticanus, a reminder that the Septuagint itself has a textual history with competing readings. Blank spaces in the fragments have fueled debate: C. H. Roberts and Paul Kahle believed the Tetragrammaton stood in those gaps, which would mean the earliest Greek Bible kept the divine name in Hebrew rather than substituting 'Lord.'

Scripture Illuminated

Deuteronomy 23:24-24:3; Deuteronomy 25:1-3; Deuteronomy 26:12; Deuteronomy 26:17-19; Deuteronomy 28:31-33

Biblical Significance

It is the oldest surviving manuscript of the Greek Bible, proving the Septuagint's Deuteronomy existed in the 2nd century BC.
