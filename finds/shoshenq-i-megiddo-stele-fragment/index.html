Shoshenq I Megiddo Stele Fragment

Find Name
Shoshenq I Megiddo Stele Fragment
Description
A large limestone fragment bearing the cartouche of Pharaoh Shoshenq I (Hedjkheperre-setepenre), found in 1925 in a fill/dump at Megiddo during the Schumacher/Fisher excavations. It has long been interpreted as part of a victory stela erected at Megiddo during Shoshenq I's Levantine campaign of about 925 BC. It is now in the Rockefeller Museum, Jerusalem. This is a DIFFERENT artifact from the Bubastite Portal of Shoshenq I at Karnak (researched separately in Batch A) and must not be merged with it.
Historical Period
Iron Age IIA; 22nd Dynasty Egypt
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The inscription names Shoshenq I at Megiddo, matching the Karnak/Bubastite Portal topographical list's inclusion of Megiddo among the towns taken in his campaign, and the biblical account of Shishak's invasion in the fifth year of Rehoboam. Cautions: (1) A 2023 re-examination (Ben-Dor Evian and Finkelstein, BASOR vol. 390) argues the fragment is unusually thick (over 50 cm) with no smoothed edges and may have been an architectural element rather than a stela; the inscription itself is not disputed, only the object's original form. (2) Sources disagree on Megiddo's number in the Karnak list (No. 27 vs No. 87), so no number is given here. (3) Shoshenq I as the biblical Shishak is the widely accepted scholarly identification, not a proven fact.
Alternative Names
Megiddo Stele Fragment; Shishak Megiddo Fragment
Related Books
1 Kings; 2 Chronicles
Related Events
The Kingdom Divides

Scripture Illuminated
1 Kings 14:25-28; 2 Chronicles 12:9
Biblical Significance
It is the only Egyptian royal inscription naming Shoshenq I found in the Levant, giving material support to the biblical account of Pharaoh Shishak's invasion of Judah in Rehoboam's reign.
