Karnak Geographic List of Seti I

The campaign reliefs and topographical lists of Seti I (c. 1290 BC) carved on the walls of the Temple of Amun at Karnak, recording his first-year campaign against the Shasu nomads from the fortress of Tharu to Pe-Kanan (Gaza/Canaan), with captives brought to the temple of Amun. Related lists of conquered peoples by Seti I at Amara West and by Amenhotep III at Soleb in Nubia (c. 1400 BC) include the phrase 'the land of the Shasu of Yahweh' (t3 shasw yhw), the earliest known occurrence of the name Yahweh outside the Bible.

Interpretations

The 'Shasu of Yahweh' phrase is widely accepted as containing the tetragrammaton, the name of Israel's God, and Donald Redford called the group 'nascent Israel'; but whether this means Edomite tribes worshipped Yahweh or the tribal name merely coincides with the divine name was long debated, and the lists do not mention Israel itself. The lists show nomadic Shasu groups in the southern Levant at the end of the Bronze Age, the region and period in which Israel's origins are debated, without settling the question.

Scripture Illuminated

Exodus 3:1-15

Biblical Significance

They contain the earliest extra-biblical occurrence of the name Yahweh, among Shasu nomads in the southern Levant, background to the revelation of the divine name to Moses in Exodus 3.
