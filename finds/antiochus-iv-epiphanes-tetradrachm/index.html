Antiochus IV Epiphanes Tetradrachm

Find Name
Antiochus IV Epiphanes Tetradrachm
Description
A silver tetradrachm of the Seleucid king Antiochus IV Epiphanes (175-164 BC), about 30-32 mm and 16.4-17.1 g. The obverse shows the diademed head of Antiochus; the reverse shows Zeus Nikephoros enthroned, with the legend 'of King Antiochus, God Manifest, Victory-Bringer' (BASILEOS ANTIOCHOU THEOU EPIPHANOUS NIKEPHOROU). An Apollo-on-omphalos type also exists. Mints include Antioch, Ake-Ptolemais (c. 167-164 BC), and Seleucia on the Tigris.
Historical Period
Hellenistic Period (332-63 BC)
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The coin's claim that Antiochus is 'God Manifest' (Epiphanes) is the self-image behind the persecution of the Jews: his subjects punned it as 'Epimanes' (madman), and his plundering of the Jerusalem temple and forced Hellenisation led to the Maccabean revolt and Hanukkah. The coin does not depict the desecration itself; it shows the royal ideology that produced it.

Biblical Significance
Daniel 11:31 speaks of forces that desecrate the sanctuary and abolish the daily sacrifice, a passage commonly identified with Antiochus IV's desecration of the Jerusalem temple; his coins carry the divine title that made that claim of godhood visible across his kingdom.
