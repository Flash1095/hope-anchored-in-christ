Nimrud Tablet K.3751

Find Name
Nimrud Tablet K.3751
Description
A clay tablet inscribed with a summary of the first seventeen years of Tiglath-Pileser III (reigned 745-727 BC), discovered in 1873 by George Smith at Nimrud and now in the British Museum. Although catalogued with the Kouyunjik (K) series, it was marked as found in the South-East Palace at Nimrud. Its text contains the first known archaeological reference to Judah (Yaudaya) and names Jeho-ahaz - Ahaz, king of Judah - among the western kings who paid tribute, alongside Bit-Humri (the House of Omri, Israel) and deportations from Gilead.
Historical Period
Iron Age II
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The tablet anchors Ahaz of Judah and the northern kingdom in a contemporary Assyrian record, confirming the tribute diplomacy of 2 Kings 16 and the deportations of 2 Kings 15:29. The readings 'Yaudaya' as Judah and 'Jeho-ahaz' as Ahaz are broadly accepted; the tablet's deportation figures belong to summary passages covering several campaigns and should not be tied to a single biblical verse.
Alternative Names
Kalhu Palace Summary Inscription 7
Related Places
Samaria
Related Books

Scripture Illuminated
2 Kings 15:29; 2 Kings 16:7-9; 1 Chronicles 5:26
Biblical Significance
It is the earliest extra-biblical mention of Judah by name, and it corroborates Ahaz's appeal to Tiglath-Pileser and the Assyrian deportations from Israel's north.
