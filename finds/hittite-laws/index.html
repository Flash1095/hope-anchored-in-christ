Hittite Laws

Find Name
Hittite Laws
Description
The Code of the Nesilim, preserved on Hittite cuneiform tablets from Hattusa (CTH 291-292) listing about 200 laws, copied in Old, Middle, and Late Hittite across the empire's life (c. 1650-1100 BC). The laws are case laws ('if anyone...'), covering assault, theft, murder, marriage, divorce, witchcraft, and sexual offences, and are notably milder than Hammurabi's code, preferring fines and compensation to death. Unlike the Mesopotamian codes, the collection has no divine prologue attributing it to the gods.
Historical Period
Bronze Age
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
Scholars agree on the corpus and its long use; the parallels with biblical law are real but must not be overstated, since the Hittite code's penalties and theology differ, and a third tablet series may once have existed.
Alternative Names
Code of the Nesilim

Biblical Significance
The Hittite Laws are the closest legal neighbours to biblical law: their case-law form matches Exodus 21-22, and individual provisions, such as the rape law of section 197, recall Deuteronomy 22. They show that Israel's laws were given in a world full of law codes, and the contrasts, above all that biblical law comes from God alone and never allows money to buy out a murderer's life (Numbers 35:31), stand out all the more sharply.
