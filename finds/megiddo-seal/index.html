Megiddo Seal (Shema Seal)

Find Name
Megiddo Seal (Shema Seal)
Date Discovered
1904
Artifact Date
8th century BC
Discovery Location
Megiddo, Israel
Current Location
Original lost after transfer to Istanbul; bronze cast at the Rockefeller Museum, Jerusalem
Timeline Era
Divided Monarchy
Description
A jasper seal found by Gottlieb Schumacher's team at Megiddo, carved with a roaring lion and the Paleo-Hebrew inscription 'Belonging to Shema, servant of Jeroboam.' It belonged to a high official of King Jeroboam II of Israel. The original was sent to the Ottoman sultan in Istanbul and disappeared; a bronze cast made beforehand survives in Jerusalem.
Biblical Significance
Jeroboam II gets only seven verses in 2 Kings, yet he ruled Israel for forty-one years — and here is his administration in miniature: a minister's seal, a lion, a name. It is the earliest seal naming a biblical king's servant, and a reminder that the kings the Bible sketches briefly governed real, busy kingdoms.
