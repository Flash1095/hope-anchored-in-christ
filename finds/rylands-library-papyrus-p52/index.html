Description
A credit-card-sized papyrus fragment of John's Gospel (John 18:31-33 and 37-38), purchased in Egypt in 1920 and published in 1935. Written on both sides, it comes from a codex and is the earliest known surviving fragment of the New Testament.
Biblical Significance
Within a generation of John's Gospel being written, a copy was already circulating in provincial Egypt, far from Ephesus. P52 rebuts the idea that the Gospels were late inventions and reminds us how carefully and widely the good news was copied and carried, so that hope in Christ reached the ends of the earth.
