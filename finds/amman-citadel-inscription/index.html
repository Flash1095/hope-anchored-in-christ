Amman Citadel Inscription

Find Name
Amman Citadel Inscription
Description
A fractured basalt block inscribed in early Ammonite script, discovered in 1961 at Jabal al-Qal'a (Amman Citadel, ancient Rabbath-Ammon) and published by Siegfried H. Horn in 1969. Dated to the late 9th century BC, it mentions a king whose name ends '...king of Ammon' and a building dedication to the god Milkom. It is the earliest known text in the Ammonite language. It is now in the on-site Jordan Archaeological Museum.
Historical Period
Iron Age II; late 9th century BC
Evidence Class
Contextual
Interpretations
The inscription attests an Ammonite kingdom with its own language and a temple-building king devoted to Milkom at Rabbath-Ammon, the Ammonite capital that appears in Deuteronomy and 2 Samuel. The king's full name is lost, so no identification with a biblical Ammonite king should be made.
Alternative Names
Amman Citadel Text; Jabal al-Qal'a Inscription
Related Books
Deuteronomy; 2 Samuel
Scripture Illuminated
Deuteronomy 3:11; 2 Samuel 12:26-29
Biblical Significance
It confirms Rabbath-Ammon as a real Ammonite royal capital with its own language and temple cult in the period of the divided monarchy.
