Songs of the Sabbath Sacrifice (4Q400-407)

Find Name
Songs of the Sabbath Sacrifice (4Q400-407)
Description
A liturgical work of thirteen songs, one for each of the first thirteen Sabbaths of the year on the sect's solar calendar, describing an angelic priesthood worshipping in the heavenly temple and throne room. Ten fragmentary copies survive: eight or nine at Qumran (4Q400–407, 11Q17) and one at Masada (Mas1k). Composition is thought to be around 100 BC, with copies ranging from the late Hasmonean period (about 75–50 BC) to the Herodian period (about 25–50 AD).
Historical Period
Hellenistic–Roman
Evidence Class
Direct
Interpretations
The Songs are the fullest Second Temple vision of heavenly worship: earthly liturgy joining the praise of seven angelic orders around God's throne, with the seventh song as the peak. The work is generally not considered sectarian — it lacks Yahad language and uses 'Elohim', rare in sectarian texts — and the Masada copy shows it circulated beyond Qumran, so it witnesses to wider Jewish mysticism, not only to one sect.
Alternative Names

Scripture Illuminated

Biblical Significance
It opens a window onto how Second Temple Jews imagined worship in heaven, the same throne-room imagery that shapes Revelation's worship scenes.
