Summary
Darius the Mede, son of Ahasuerus, took the kingdom of Babylon at about sixty-two years of age after Belshazzar's death; he set Daniel over the whole realm, was tricked by his satraps into the lions' den decree, and found Daniel unharmed at dawn, then decreed that men should tremble before Daniel's God. Scholars debate whether Darius the Mede is a throne name, a governor, or another figure of the period; the entry follows the text's own account.
Key Story
Darius sealed the lions' den with his own signet, spent the night fasting, and at dawn cried to Daniel whether his God had delivered him — and Daniel answered that God had shut the lions' mouths.
