Summary
Satan, the adversary, appears as the accuser in Job, as the accuser of Joshua the high priest in Zechariah, as the tempter of Jesus, as the one who desired to sift Peter, and as the dragon of Revelation, called 'that old serpent, the Devil, and Satan.' His other biblical titles are noted here, not given separate profiles: 'Devil' is the same figure; 'Lucifer' in Isaiah 14:12 is traditionally applied to him, but that identification is debated and is not presented as settled; 'Beelzebub' is a title traditionally associated with him. This profile covers the named adversary from Job onward; the identification of the Genesis serpent with Satan is traditional and is noted as such, not asserted from Genesis itself.
