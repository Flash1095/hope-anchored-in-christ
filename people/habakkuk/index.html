Summary
Habakkuk prophesied in Judah around 609–605 BC, as Babylon rose to power. Unusually, his book is a dialogue: he questioned God about violence and injustice, God answered that He would raise the Chaldeans, Habakkuk questioned again, and God answered with a vision — the just shall live by faith — ending in a psalm of trust even when the fields yield no food.
Key Story
Habakkuk cried out over violence in Judah, wrestled with God's answer that Babylon would be His instrument, and finished by trusting God even if fig tree and flock should fail.
Key Verses
Habakkuk 1:1; Habakkuk 2:4

Life Lesson
Habakkuk teaches that honest questions are not unbelief. Bring them to God, wait for His answer, and trust Him even when the harvest fails.
