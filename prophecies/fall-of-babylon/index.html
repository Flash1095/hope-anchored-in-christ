The Fall of Babylon

Prophecy
The Fall of Babylon
Prophet
John
Date Approx
c. AD 95
Timeline Era
Early Church
Prophecy Summary
John saw a woman, Babylon the great, seated on a scarlet beast and drunk with the blood of the saints, a city of wealth, luxury, and spiritual adultery that had made all nations drink of her fornication. Her fall was announced with a mighty voice — Babylon is fallen, is fallen — and merchants, kings, and seafarers lamented her burning in a single hour, while heaven rejoiced because God had avenged the blood of his servants upon her. God's people were called to come out of her, lest they share in her sins and receive of her plagues, for her sins had reached unto heaven and her judgment would be sudden and complete.
Fulfillment
Babylon's identity is debated — Rome, a future rebuilt city, or a symbol of the world system opposed to God — and interpreters differ over whether her fall is past, future, or both in pattern and climax. The prophecy's moral core is not debated in the text: a persecuting, seducing world power will fall under God's judgment, and God's people must separate from her before that judgment comes. This entry covers Revelation 17-18 as one prophecy of that fall, distinct from the bowl judgments that execute it.
