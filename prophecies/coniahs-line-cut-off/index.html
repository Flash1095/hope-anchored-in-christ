Coniah's Line Cut Off from the Throne

Coniah's Line Cut Off from the Throne

Prophecy Summary

Jeremiah foretold that Coniah, also called Jeconiah, king of Judah, would be cast out with his mother into a land where he was not born, and would die there, never to return. God swore that no man of his seed would prosper sitting upon the throne of David or ruling any more in Judah, and that Coniah would be written as childless in the sense of having no royal heir. It is a prophecy about a named king's dynasty, distinct from the promise of a righteous Branch in the same book.

Fulfillment

Jeconiah was carried to Babylon in 597 BC, lived out his life in exile, and never returned to rule, while no descendant of his sat on the throne of David in Judah after him. Matthew's genealogy still traces Jesus' legal line through Jeconiah, which has led interpreters to distinguish the royal succession in Judah from the messianic line, but the throne prediction itself held. The prophecy was therefore fulfilled in the end of Jeconiah's royal line.
