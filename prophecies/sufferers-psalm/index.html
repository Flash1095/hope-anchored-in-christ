The Sufferer's Psalm

Prophecy
The Sufferer's Psalm
Prophet
David
Date Approx
c. 1000 BC
Timeline Era
United Monarchy
Prophecy Summary
The psalm speaks in the voice of a sufferer consumed by zeal for God's house, bearing reproach for God's sake, and hated without a cause. He is given gall for his meat and vinegar to drink in his thirst, as one set apart and afflicted. The portrait is not a single complaint but a pattern of suffering that Scripture later recognises in the Messiah.
Fulfillment
John records that the disciples remembered 'the zeal of thine house hath eaten me up' when Jesus cleansed the temple, and that He was hated without a cause. At the cross soldiers offered Him vinegar in His thirst, matching the psalm's gall and vinegar. These distinct details, fulfilled in separate moments of Jesus's life, mark the psalm as a genuine messianic portrait rather than a restatement of Psalm 22 or Isaiah 53.
