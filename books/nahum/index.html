Summary
Nahum proclaims the coming fall of Nineveh, the brutal Assyrian capital, about a century after Jonah. It comforts Judah by declaring God slow to anger yet a mighty avenger against His people's oppressors.
Key Verses
Nahum 1:7; Nahum 1:15
Key People
Nahum
Themes
Judgment on Nineveh; God's wrath and refuge; Comfort for Judah
Purpose
To announce Nineveh's destruction and comfort Judah under Assyrian domination.
Outline
God's character and wrath (1); Nineveh's fall described (2-3)
Historical Context
Prophesied shortly before Nineveh fell to Babylon and Media in 612 BC, when Assyria still oppressed Judah.
Related Places
Nineveh; Jerusalem
Life Application
Nahum assures you that God does not ignore cruelty forever, even when the powerful seem untouchable for a century. If you are oppressed or watching injustice go unanswered, trust that God's patience is not indifference, and refuse to become like the oppressors you are waiting for Him to judge.
Cultural Background
Nineveh was one of the ancient world's largest cities, ringed by massive walls and fed by canals, and its fall in 612 BC shocked the region because Assyria had seemed permanent. Nahum's vivid description of siege, flood, and plunder matches how ancient cities actually fell, and why Judah found comfort in it.
