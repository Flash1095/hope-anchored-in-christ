1 Peter

Peter encourages Christians suffering as strangers in the world to rejoice in their living hope through Christ's resurrection. He calls them to holiness, submission, and humble trust as God's chosen people.

Themes

Living hope; Suffering; Holiness; Identity in Christ

Purpose

To strengthen persecuted believers in Asia Minor to stand firm in grace while living honorably among unbelievers.

Outline

Living hope and holiness (1-2); Submission in relationships (3); Suffering and shepherding (4-5)

Historical Context

Written around AD 62-64, likely from Rome, to churches across Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia in modern Turkey.

Life Application

First Peter teaches you to live as a stranger with hope: suffer without retaliation, submit without surrendering conscience, and answer hostility with good conduct and gentle words. Your exile status is not a disadvantage but a calling, because a holy life among unbelievers is itself a witness to Christ.

Cultural Background

Peter's readers lived in the provinces of Asia Minor, where Christians were a suspect minority facing social exclusion and slander rather than empire-wide persecution. His language of strangers, exiles, and a chosen people gave those scattered believers an identity stronger than the shame their neighbors tried to impose.
