Summary
Leviticus gives the laws for sacrifices, priesthood, purity, and holy living that Israel received at Mount Sinai. It teaches that a holy God makes a way for sinful people to live in His presence through atonement.
Key Verses
Leviticus 11:45; Leviticus 17:11; Leviticus 19:2; Leviticus 19:18
Key People
Moses; Aaron; Nadab; Abihu; Eleazar
Themes
Holiness; Sacrifice; Atonement; Priesthood; Purity
Purpose
To instruct priests and people how to worship and live as God's holy nation set apart from surrounding nations.
Outline
Sacrifices and priesthood (1-10); Purity laws (11-15); Day of Atonement and holiness code (16-27)
Historical Context
Delivered at Mount Sinai during Israel's wilderness encampment, in the year after the Exodus.
Related Places
Mount Sinai
Related Events
The Ten Commandments
Life Application
Leviticus teaches that holiness is not about being impressive but about belonging wholly to God in ordinary things: honest work, clean speech, generous neighbors, and regular rest. Offer God your everyday conduct, not just your Sunday worship, and let small acts of obedience mark you as set apart for Him.
Cultural Background
Israel lived among peoples whose worship often involved magic, bribery, and appeasing angry gods. Leviticus instead gives a system of sacrifices and purity laws that taught one clear idea: a holy God provides the way for imperfect people to live near Him, and access to Him comes by His provision, not by human bargaining.
