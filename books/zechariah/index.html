Zechariah

Zechariah encourages the Temple builders with eight night visions of restoration and prophesies the coming King who rides in humbly and is pierced. Its later chapters look to God's final victory and the Shepherd-King.

Themes

Visions; Restoration; Coming King; Holy Spirit; Repentance

Purpose

To encourage the returned exiles to rebuild and to reveal God's messianic plans for Jerusalem and the world.

Outline

Night visions (1-6); Fasting question (7-8); Oracles about the coming King (9-14)

Historical Context

Began in Jerusalem in 520 BC alongside Haggai, during Darius's reign, as the Second Temple was being rebuilt.

Life Application

Zechariah encourages you to keep building when the work looks small and the opposition looks large, because God sees the day of small things and finishes what He starts. Measure progress by faithfulness rather than by spectacle, and trust that the same God who gave the vision will supply the strength to complete it.

Cultural Background

Zechariah's eight night visions use the symbolic language of Persian-period prophecy: lampstands, measuring lines, flying scrolls, and horsemen patrolling the earth. That apocalyptic imagery, common in the post-exilic period, let him speak hope to a small, discouraged province by picturing God's unseen activity behind ordinary events.
