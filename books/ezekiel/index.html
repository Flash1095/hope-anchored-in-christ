Ezekiel

Ezekiel, a priest exiled to Babylon, receives dramatic visions of God's glory departing and one day returning to a new Temple. He prophesies judgment on Judah and the nations, and promises resurrection-like restoration for Israel.

Themes

God's glory; Judgment; Restoration; New heart; Dry bones revived

Purpose

To explain to the exiles that Jerusalem's fall was God's judgment, and to promise a future restoration by His Spirit.

Outline

Judgment on Judah (1-24); Judgment on nations (25-32); Restoration of Israel (33-48)

Historical Context

Prophesied among the Jewish exiles by the Kebar River in Babylon, 593-571 BC, before and after Jerusalem's fall.

Life Application

Ezekiel's valley of dry bones speaks to places in your life that look dead beyond repair: a broken relationship, a lost dream, a hardened heart. God's Spirit, not your effort, breathes life into what is dead, so pray for renewal where you have given up, and trust Him to do what only He can do.

Cultural Background

Ezekiel prophesied among exiles settled by the Kebar River, a canal in Babylon where deported Judeans lived as a displaced community. His visions of a portable divine glory and a rebuilt temple answered their deepest fear: that God had stayed behind in Jerusalem and abandoned them in a foreign land.
