Summary
Jonah flees God's call to preach to Nineveh, is swallowed by a great fish, then reluctantly obeys - and Nineveh repents. The book exposes the prophet's anger at God's mercy and reveals God's compassion for the nations.
Key Verses

Purpose
To teach Israel that God's mercy extends beyond Israel and to challenge narrow-hearted exclusiveness.

Life Application
Jonah exposes how easily you can love God's mercy for yourself while resenting it for people you dislike. If you catch yourself wanting grace for your group and judgment for another, remember that God's compassion is wider than your borders, and ask Him to enlarge your heart toward the Nineveh you would rather avoid.
Cultural Background
Nineveh was the capital of Assyria, an empire famous for cruelty toward conquered peoples, which is why Jonah's mission there sounded absurd and offensive to his audience. The book's humor and shock depend on that reputation: the last people Israel expected to repent were the ones who did.
