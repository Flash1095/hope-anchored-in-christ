Joshua

Joshua leads Israel to conquer and divide the Promised Land after Moses' death. The book shows God fulfilling His promise to Abraham as Israel takes possession of Canaan tribe by tribe.

Themes

Conquest; Inheritance; Obedience; God's faithfulness; Courage

Purpose

To record the fulfillment of God's land promise and call Israel to serve the Lord alone.

Outline

Entering Canaan (1-5); Conquest (6-12); Division of the land (13-22); Joshua's farewell (23-24)

Historical Context

Set during the conquest of Canaan in the late 15th to early 14th century BC, as Israel settled among Canaanite city-states.

Life Application

Joshua calls you to courage that is not self-confidence but trust in God's promises when the task looks too big. Take the next step into what God has already promised, whether a hard conversation, a new responsibility, or a fresh start, and let remembrance of His past faithfulness steady you for the ground ahead.

Cultural Background

Canaan in Joshua's day was a patchwork of small fortified city-states, each with its own king and gods, rather than one great empire. The book's account of a united people entering that fractured land explains how Israel understood its origins: not as conquest for its own glory but as God keeping a promise made generations earlier.
