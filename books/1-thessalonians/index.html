1 Thessalonians

Book
1 Thessalonians
Testament
New Testament
Category
Epistles
Traditional Author
Paul
Date Approx
c. AD 51
Timeline Era
Early Church
Key Theme
Encouragement and the return of Christ
Summary
Paul encourages a young persecuted church, commending their faith and clarifying what happens to believers who die before Christ returns. He urges holy, diligent living while they wait for the Lord.
Key Verses
1 Thessalonians 4:16-17; 1 Thessalonians 5:16-18; 1 Thessalonians 5:11
Key People
Paul; Silas; Timothy
Themes
Hope; Return of Christ; Holiness; Encouragement
Purpose
To encourage new believers under persecution and correct confusion about Christ's return and the resurrection.
Outline
Thanksgiving for their faith (1-3); Holy living and the Lord's return (4-5)
Historical Context
One of Paul's earliest letters, written from Corinth around AD 51, soon after he founded the church in Thessalonica.
Related Places
Thessalonica; Corinth
Life Application
First Thessalonians encourages steady faithfulness while you wait: work diligently, love your brothers and sisters, and live quietly in a watching world. If anxiety about the future tempts you to idleness or panic, hold to the hope of Christ's return and let it make you more faithful today, not less.
Cultural Background
Thessalonica was a free city and provincial capital on the great Via Egnatia highway, where the imperial cult and trade guilds pressed Christians to conform. Paul's young converts faced social and economic pressure for refusing those loyalties, which is why his earliest letter stresses endurance, holiness, and hope.
