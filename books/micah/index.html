Micah

Micah denounces corrupt leaders and false prophets in Judah and Israel, warns of judgment, and promises a ruler born in Bethlehem. He sums up God's requirement: to do justice, love mercy, and walk humbly with God.

Themes

Justice; Judgment; Mercy; Bethlehem ruler; Remnant

Purpose

To indict Samaria and Jerusalem for injustice and to announce both judgment and messianic restoration.

Outline

Judgment announced (1-3); Future kingdom (4-5); God's case and requirements (6-7)

Historical Context

Prophesied in Judah during the reigns of Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah in the late 8th century BC, as Assyria threatened.

Life Application

Micah reduces godliness to three practices you can live today: do justice in your dealings, love mercy toward people who cannot repay you, and walk humbly with God instead of performing religion. When faith feels complicated, return to those three, and let them judge whether your worship is real.

Cultural Background

Micah spoke to small-town Judah while Jerusalem's leaders and prophets sold justice to the highest bidder, seizing fields and houses from the vulnerable. His own origin in the countryside town of Moresheth explains his angle: he saw the corruption of the capital from the perspective of the farmers it exploited.
