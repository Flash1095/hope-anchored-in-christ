Daniel

Daniel and his friends remain faithful to God while serving in Babylonian and Persian courts, and God delivers them from the fiery furnace and lions' den. Daniel's visions reveal God's sovereign plan for successive kingdoms and the coming Son of Man.

Themes

Sovereignty; Faithfulness in exile; Prophecy; Kingdoms; Son of Man

Purpose

To encourage God's people that He rules over empires and will establish His everlasting kingdom.

Outline

Stories of faithfulness (1-6); Daniel's visions (7-12)

Historical Context

Set in Babylon and Persia during the exile, from Daniel's deportation in 605 BC through the early Persian period.

Life Application

Daniel shows how to stay faithful when the surrounding culture asks you to compromise in small, daily ways: food, prayer habits, and loyalty. Decide your convictions before the pressure arrives, keep praying openly even when it costs you, and trust that God rules over every empire and employer above you.

Cultural Background

Daniel's world was the Babylonian and Persian court, where officials were trained in the king's language and literature and expected to serve the empire's gods. His refusal of royal food and his open prayers were not private quirks but public acts of loyalty to God inside a system designed to absorb conquered peoples.
