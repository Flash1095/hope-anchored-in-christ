Malachi

Book
Malachi
Testament
Old Testament
Category
Minor Prophets
Traditional Author
Malachi
Date Approx
c. 440-400 BC
Timeline Era
Return and Second Temple
Key Theme
Call to faithfulness and the coming messenger
Summary
Malachi, the last Old Testament prophet, confronts a discouraged post-exile people and their priests for half-hearted worship and broken covenants. He promises God will send a messenger before the great Day of the Lord.
Key Verses
Malachi 3:10; Malachi 4:2; Malachi 3:6
Key People
Malachi; The coming messenger (Elijah)
Themes
Faithfulness; Worship; Tithing; Marriage covenant; Day of the Lord
Purpose
To call a complacent generation back to wholehearted devotion and to prepare them for God's coming messenger.
Outline
God's love affirmed (1); Priests and people rebuked (2); Robbing God and the Book of Remembrance (3); Day of the Lord (4)
Historical Context
Addressed to Jerusalem in the mid-5th century BC, a generation after the Temple was rebuilt, under Persian rule.
Related Places
Jerusalem
Life Application
Malachi exposes half-hearted worship: giving God leftovers of time, money, and attention while expecting full blessing in return. Examine what you offer Him first and best, whether your morning, your budget, or your energy, and return to wholehearted devotion before cynicism hardens into the habit of your heart.
Cultural Background
Malachi addresses a community a generation after the temple's rebuilding, when enthusiasm had faded into routine and priests accepted blemished sacrifices. His courtroom style, with the people answering back at each charge, reflects a weary congregation arguing with God, which is why his rebukes sound like a debate rather than a sermon.
