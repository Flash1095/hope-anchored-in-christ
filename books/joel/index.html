Joel

Book
Joel
Testament
Old Testament
Category
Minor Prophets
Traditional Author
Joel
Date Approx
c. 835-800 BC
Timeline Era
Divided Monarchy
Key Theme
The Day of the Lord and the outpouring of the Spirit
Summary
After a devastating locust plague, Joel calls Judah to repent and warns of the coming Day of the Lord. He also promises God will pour out His Spirit on all people and restore His repentant nation.
Key Verses
Joel 2:13; Joel 2:28-29; Joel 2:25
Key People
Joel
Themes
Day of the Lord; Repentance; Outpouring of the Spirit; Restoration
Purpose
To summon God's people to heartfelt repentance in crisis and to promise the Spirit's future outpouring, fulfilled at Pentecost.
Outline
Locust plague (1); Day of the Lord and repentance (2); Judgment on nations and blessing (3)
Historical Context
Addressed to Judah in Jerusalem, likely around a locust disaster; its exact date is debated, often placed in the 9th or 5th century BC.
Related Places
Jerusalem
Life Application
Joel calls you to treat disaster as a summons, not just a setback: when a crisis strips away comfort, rend your heart rather than your garments, and turn to God with honest repentance. Crisis can become the place where you finally listen, and Joel promises that God restores what locusts and losses have eaten.
Cultural Background
Locust plagues were among the most feared disasters in the ancient Near East, stripping fields bare in hours and threatening famine for an entire region. Joel uses a real agricultural catastrophe his audience would have recognized instantly as the image of a greater invasion, the Day of the Lord, that demanded urgent repentance.
