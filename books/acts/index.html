Key Theme

Summary
Acts follows the Holy Spirit empowering the apostles to spread the gospel from Jerusalem to Rome. It records the birth of the Church at Pentecost, Peter's ministry to Jews and Gentiles, and Paul's missionary journeys.
Key Verses
Acts 1:8; Acts 2:38; Acts 4:12; Acts 16:31
Key People
Peter; Paul; Stephen; Philip; Barnabas; James; The Holy Spirit
Themes
Holy Spirit; Church growth; Missions; Witness; Perseverance
Purpose
To show how the gospel advanced from Jerusalem to the heart of the Roman Empire under the Spirit's power.
Outline
Church in Jerusalem (1-7); Judea and Samaria (8-12); Paul's journeys (13-21); Paul to Rome (22-28)
Historical Context
Covers roughly AD 30-62, from Jesus' ascension through Paul's imprisonment in Rome under Nero.
Related Places
Jerusalem; Samaria; Antioch; Ephesus; Corinth; Philippi; Thessalonica; Rome
Related Events
Pentecost; The Ascension; Martyrdom of Stephen; Peter and Cornelius; Jerusalem Council; The Conversion of Paul; Paul's Missionary Journeys; Paul in Rome; Paul Shipwrecked on Malta
Life Application
Acts reminds you that the Church's power is the Holy Spirit, not budgets, buildings, or strategies. If your witness feels weak, pray for boldness, speak about Jesus where you are, and expect God to open doors across cultural lines, because the gospel's advance in Acts always outran the plans of its messengers.
Cultural Background
Acts moves through the Roman Empire's roads, ships, and cities, from Jerusalem to Rome, and its story depends on that infrastructure: Roman citizenship, common Greek language, and synagogues in every major city gave the gospel ready-made networks. Paul's journeys trace the empire's highways, not a map of isolated villages.
