Ruth

During the time of the judges, a Moabite widow named Ruth loyally follows her mother-in-law Naomi back to Bethlehem. God provides for them through Boaz, a kinsman-redeemer, and Ruth becomes the great-grandmother of King David.

Themes

Loyalty; Redemption; Providence; Kindness; David's lineage

Purpose

To show God's providence in ordinary faithfulness and to trace the family line that leads to David and ultimately to Jesus.

Outline

Naomi and Ruth return (1); Ruth gleans in Boaz's field (2); At the threshing floor (3); Marriage and birth of Obed (4)

Historical Context

Set in the period of the judges, in Bethlehem and Moab, generations before David's kingship.

Life Application

Ruth honors quiet faithfulness: staying loyal to family, working an honest day in someone else's field, and trusting God when no rescue is in sight. Your most important work may be unglamorous and unseen, but God weaves ordinary loyalty into His larger story, often in ways you will only recognize years later.

Cultural Background

In the ancient world, a widow without sons had almost no economic protection, and gleaning leftover grain was a legal provision for the poor. Ruth's story turns on two Israelite institutions, the kinsman-redeemer and levirate duty, which let a relative rescue a family's land and name when no one else could.
