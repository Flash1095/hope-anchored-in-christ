Amos

Book
Amos
Testament
Old Testament
Category
Minor Prophets
Traditional Author
Amos
Date Approx
c. 760-750 BC
Timeline Era
Divided Monarchy
Key Theme
God's call for justice and righteousness
Summary
Amos, a shepherd from Judah, confronts wealthy Israel for oppressing the poor while maintaining empty worship. He proclaims God's justice like a mighty river and warns of coming exile, ending with a promise of restoration.
Key Verses
Amos 5:24; Amos 7:14-15; Amos 9:11
Key People
Amos; Amaziah; Jeroboam II
Themes
Justice; Judgment; Social righteousness; True Worship
Purpose
To call prosperous Israel to justice and repentance before Assyrian judgment falls.
Outline
Judgments on nations (1-2); Sermons against Israel (3-6); Visions (7-9)
Historical Context
Prophesied at Bethel in northern Israel around 760 BC, during Jeroboam II's prosperous but unjust reign.
Related Places
Samaria; Bethel
Related Events
Fall of Samaria
Life Application
Amos confronts comfortable religion that sings loudly on Sunday while exploiting workers and ignoring the poor on Monday. Examine whether your worship is matched by justice in your spending, hiring, and treatment of vulnerable people, because God measures devotion not by religious noise but by righteousness in daily dealings.
Cultural Background
Amos preached at Bethel, the northern kingdom's royal sanctuary, during a boom time when trade enriched the wealthy while the poor fell into debt slavery. His oracles against luxurious houses and rigged scales hit home because his hearers were living in that prosperity, and his background as a shepherd made him an outsider to the court.
