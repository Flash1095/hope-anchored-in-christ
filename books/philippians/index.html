Philippians

Paul's letter of joy thanks the Philippians for their partnership and urges humility modeled on Christ, who emptied Himself. Even from prison Paul rejoices and presses on toward the goal in Christ.

Themes

Joy; Humility; Partnership; Contentment; Christ's example

Purpose

To thank the Philippians for their gift, encourage unity and joy, and warn against false teaching.

Outline

Thanksgiving and Paul's chains (1); Christ's humility and examples (2); Pressing on; warnings (3); Joy and peace (4)

Historical Context

Written from prison, likely in Rome around AD 61-62, to the church Paul founded in Philippi, a Roman colony in Macedonia.

Life Application

Philippians teaches joy that does not depend on circumstances: Paul rejoices from prison, urges humility modeled on Christ's self-emptying, and finds contentment in plenty and in want. Practice that joy by refusing to grumble, by serving others first, and by praying your anxieties into God's hands today.

Cultural Background

Philippi was a Roman colony in Macedonia, settled by retired soldiers, where citizenship was prized and loyalty to Rome was a civic religion. Paul's call to live as citizens of heaven and his portrait of Christ emptying Himself would have sounded politically charged to people proud of their Roman status.
