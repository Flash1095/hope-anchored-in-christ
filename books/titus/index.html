Summary
Paul tells Titus how to organize the churches on Crete: appoint godly elders, teach sound doctrine to every age group, and insist that grace produces good works. It pairs right belief with right living.
Key Verses
Titus 2:11-14; Titus 3:5
Key People
Paul; Titus
Themes
Sound doctrine; Good works; Godly leadership; Grace
Purpose
To instruct Titus in ordering Cretan churches and linking doctrine to transformed lives.
Outline
Elders appointed (1); Teaching all groups (2); Grace and good works (3)
Historical Context
Written around AD 64-65, after Paul left Titus on the island of Crete to strengthen its young churches.
Life Application
Titus pairs belief with behavior: sound doctrine must show up in how older and younger believers speak, work, marry, and serve. If your theology never reaches your Monday conduct, it is incomplete, because grace trains people to say no to ungodliness and to be zealous for good works that bless their neighbors.
Cultural Background
Crete was notorious in the ancient world for dishonesty and disorder, a reputation Paul himself quotes, and its churches needed structure badly. His instructions to appoint elders in every town and teach each age group reflect an island culture where the church's credibility depended on visible, orderly, transformed lives.
