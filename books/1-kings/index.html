Summary
1 Kings begins with Solomon's glorious reign and the building of the Temple, then records the kingdom dividing into Israel and Judah. It follows the kings of both nations and the prophet Elijah's confrontation with idolatry.
Key Verses
1 Kings 8:23; 1 Kings 18:39
Key People
Solomon; Rehoboam; Jeroboam; Elijah; King Ahab; Jezebel
Themes
Wisdom; Temple; Division; Idolatry; Prophetic witness
Purpose
To show how Solomon's successors turned from God, leading to division, and to evaluate kings by covenant faithfulness.
Outline
Solomon's reign (1-11); Divided kingdom to Elijah (12-17); Elijah and Ahab (18-22)
Historical Context
Covers Solomon's reign in the 10th century BC through the divided monarchy into the 9th century BC under Assyrian shadow.
Related Places
Jerusalem; Samaria; Mount Carmel; Tyre; Gibeon
Related Events
Building of Solomon's Temple; The Kingdom Divides; Elijah on Mount Carmel
Life Application
First Kings traces how small compromises harden into a divided life: Solomon's wisdom could not save him once foreign alliances and idols took root. Guard the small decisions about money, marriage, friendship, and worship, because the direction of a life is set less by one dramatic fall than by a hundred quiet ones.
Cultural Background
Solomon's temple was built with Phoenician timber and craftsmen, and its dedication drew on the grandeur of neighboring royal sanctuaries. The book measures that splendor against covenant loyalty, so its readers see that impressive buildings and wealth never substitute for a king who keeps God's commands.
