Summary
Psalms is Israel's hymnbook of 150 songs covering praise, lament, thanksgiving, wisdom, and royal themes. Attributed largely to David, it gives God's people words for every season of life before God.
Key Verses
Psalm 23:1; Psalm 19:14; Psalm 46:1; Psalm 100:4-5; Psalm 119:105
Key People
David; Asaph; Sons of Korah; Solomon; Moses
Themes
Worship; Praise; Lament; Trust; Messianic hope
Purpose
To provide God's people with prayers and songs for worship, teaching them to bring every emotion honestly to God.
Outline
Book I (1-41); Book II (42-72); Book III (73-89); Book IV (90-106); Book V (107-150)
Historical Context
Composed over centuries, from Moses to the post-exile period, and used in Temple and synagogue worship in Jerusalem.
Related Places
Jerusalem
Life Application
Psalms gives you words when your own run out: praise when you are grateful, lament when you are hurt, and confession when you have failed. Bring every emotion to God honestly instead of only the presentable ones, and let the habit of praying through the psalms slowly reshape how you see your troubles and your blessings.
Cultural Background
The psalms were sung and prayed in temple worship and later in synagogue gatherings, accompanied by instruments and organized into five books that mirror the five books of Moses. That liturgical setting explains their range: they were not private poems but a community's shared language for worship across centuries of joy and exile.
