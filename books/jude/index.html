Jude

Book
Jude
Testament
New Testament
Category
Epistles
Traditional Author
Jude, brother of James
Date Approx
c. AD 65-80
Timeline Era
Early Church
Key Theme
Contend for the faith against false teachers
Summary
Jude urgently warns the church against ungodly intruders who twist God's grace into license and deny Christ. He calls believers to contend for the faith once delivered and to build themselves up in love and prayer.
Key Verses
Jude 1:3; Jude 1:24-25
Key People
Jude; Michael the archangel; Enoch
Themes
Contending for the faith; Judgment on false teachers; Mercy; Perseverance
Purpose
To expose false teachers in the church and call believers to stand firm and rescue others with mercy.
Outline
Greeting and purpose (1-4); Examples of judgment (5-16); Call to persevere (17-23); Doxology (24-25)
Historical Context
Written in the later first century AD (c. AD 65-80) to a church infiltrated by immoral false teachers.
Life Application
Jude calls you to contend for the faith without becoming contentious: recognize false teaching that turns grace into license, build yourself up in prayer and love, and rescue doubters with mercy. Standing firm and showing mercy are not opposites; Jude insists the faithful church does both at once.
Cultural Background
Jude draws on Jewish traditions about rebellious angels, Sodom, and wandering stars, images his readers would have known from popular Jewish writings of the day. His short, urgent letter assumes a church familiar with those stories, using them to unmask teachers who promised freedom while practicing immorality.
