Job

Job, a righteous man, loses everything and wrestles with his friends over why the righteous suffer. In the end God answers from the whirlwind, affirming His wisdom and sovereignty, and restores Job.

Themes

Suffering; Sovereignty; Wisdom; Faith; Restoration

Purpose

To explore innocent suffering and teach trust in God's wisdom when His purposes are beyond human understanding.

Outline

Prologue (1-2); Dialogues with friends (3-31); Elihu (32-37); God's speeches and restoration (38-42)

Historical Context

Set in the land of Uz, likely in the patriarchal era, outside Israel, with no reference to the Law or Exodus.

Life Application

Job gives you permission to grieve and question without pretending to have answers, and it warns friends against explaining away another person's pain. When suffering makes no sense, resist tidy explanations, keep speaking honestly to God, and trust His wisdom even when you cannot trace His hand in your circumstances.

Cultural Background

Job is set outside Israel, in the land of Uz, with no mention of the Law, the temple, or the Exodus, and its dialogue form resembles ancient wisdom debates about justice and suffering. That setting signals its universal question: it asks why the righteous suffer, a problem every culture has wrestled with, not only Israel.
