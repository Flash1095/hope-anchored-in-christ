Summary
Zephaniah warns Judah and the nations of the sweeping Day of the Lord, calling for humility and repentance. It ends with joyful promises that God will gather a humble remnant and rejoice over them with singing.
Key Verses
Zephaniah 3:17; Zephaniah 2:3; Zephaniah 1:7
Key People
Zephaniah; King Josiah
Themes
Day of the Lord; Judgment; Humble remnant; God's joy over His people
Purpose
To warn of imminent judgment and summon Judah to seek the Lord before it comes.
Outline
Judgment on Judah and nations (1-2); Woe and restoration (3)
Historical Context
Prophesied in Jerusalem during King Josiah's reign in the late 7th century BC, before his reforms fully took hold.
Related Places
Jerusalem
Related Events
Fall of Jerusalem and the Exile
Life Application
Zephaniah calls you to seek the Lord before the storm rather than after it: humility, repentance, and quiet trust are not last resorts but the safest place to stand. When judgment feels abstract and life feels stable, let the warning sharpen your priorities instead of letting comfort dull them.
Cultural Background
Zephaniah preached during Josiah's reign, when Judah's worship still mixed devotion to the Lord with the worship of Baal, Molech, and the stars, practices imported from Assyria. His sweeping Day of the Lord language targeted that syncretism directly, naming the foreign gods whose altars still stood in Jerusalem.
