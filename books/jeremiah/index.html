Summary
Jeremiah, the weeping prophet, warns Judah for forty years that Babylon will bring judgment for idolatry and injustice. He also proclaims God's promise of a new covenant written on the heart and a future restoration.
Key Verses
Jeremiah 1:5; Jeremiah 29:11; Jeremiah 31:33; Jeremiah 33:3
Key People
Jeremiah; Baruch; Zedekiah; King Nebuchadnezzar; Jehoiakim
Themes
Judgment; New covenant; Repentance; God's faithfulness; Lament
Purpose
To announce Judah's coming exile as covenant judgment and to hold out hope of restoration under a new covenant.
Outline
Jeremiah's call and sermons (1-24); Controversies and letters (25-38); Fall of Jerusalem and aftermath (39-52)
Historical Context
Ministered in Jerusalem from 627 BC through the city's fall in 586 BC and the early exile to Babylon and Egypt.
Related Places
Jerusalem; Babylon; Egypt
Related Events
Fall of Jerusalem and the Exile
Life Application
Jeremiah shows faithfulness that keeps speaking truth even when no one listens and nothing changes for forty years. If your obedience feels fruitless, measure it by faithfulness rather than results, and hold on to the promise of a new covenant written on the heart, which God fulfills in ways you cannot yet see.
Cultural Background
Jeremiah ministered while Jerusalem's politics swung between submission to Babylon and reckless rebellion, with court officials, false prophets, and kings pulling in opposite directions. His confessions and confrontations reflect that pressure cooker: a prophet caught between a collapsing nation and a message of judgment no one wanted to hear.
