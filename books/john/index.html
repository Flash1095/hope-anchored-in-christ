John

John presents Jesus as the eternal Word made flesh and the Son of God, through seven signs and deep discourses like the Good Shepherd and the Vine. It was written so readers may believe in Jesus and have life in His name.

Themes

Son of God; Belief; Eternal Life; Light; Love

Purpose

Stated in John 20:31 - so that readers may believe Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life in His name.

Outline

Prologue (1); Book of Signs (2-12); Upper Room and Farewell Discourse (13-17); Passion and resurrection (18-21)

Historical Context

Written late in the first century AD, traditionally at Ephesus, decades after the Synoptic Gospels.

Life Application

John invites you to believe, not merely to admire Jesus from a distance: trust that the Word made flesh is the Son of God, and let that belief become life. When doubts come, return to His signs and His words, and let abiding in Him, like a branch in a vine, shape your prayers, your love, and your obedience.

Cultural Background

John was written decades after the other Gospels, in a world where Greek philosophy asked deep questions about the Word, light, and life. His prologue answers those questions in Jewish terms, presenting Jesus as the eternal Word, so readers in Ephesus and beyond would hear both the philosophy of their age and the fulfillment of Israel's story.
