Galatians

Paul passionately defends the gospel that sinners are justified by faith in Christ alone, not by works of the Law. He warns the Galatians against returning to slavery and calls them to live by the Spirit.

Themes

Justification by faith; Freedom; Fruit of the Spirit; Gospel purity

Purpose

To refute teachers adding circumcision and Law-keeping to faith, and to secure the Galatians' freedom in Christ.

Outline

Personal defense (1-2); Doctrinal argument from Abraham (3-4); Freedom and life by the Spirit (5-6)

Historical Context

Written to churches in Galatia (in modern Turkey) Paul founded on his journeys, likely AD 48-55, as Judaizers followed behind him.

Life Application

Galatians guards the freedom you have in Christ: do not add rules, rituals, or achievements to faith as the price of belonging. If your conscience is weighed down by requirements Jesus never imposed, return to justification by faith alone, and let the Spirit, not a checklist, produce the fruit in your life.

Cultural Background

Galatia was a region in central Asia Minor settled by Celtic tribes, and its churches were visited by teachers insisting that Gentile converts be circumcised and keep the Law. Paul's appeal to Abraham, who believed before circumcision existed, struck at the heart of that demand with an argument his readers could follow.
