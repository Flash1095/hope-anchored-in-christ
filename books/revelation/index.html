Revelation

Book
Revelation
Testament
New Testament
Category
Prophecy
Traditional Author
John the Apostle
Date Approx
c. AD 95
Timeline Era
Early Church
Key Theme
Christ's victory and the new heaven and new earth
Summary
Revelation unveils Jesus Christ in glory, with letters to seven churches and symbolic visions of judgment, the Lamb's victory over evil, and Satan's final defeat. It ends with the new heaven, new earth, and New Jerusalem where God dwells with His people.
Key Verses
Revelation 1:8; Revelation 21:4; Revelation 22:12-13; Revelation 5:12
Key People
John the Apostle; Jesus the Lamb; The seven churches
Themes
Victory of Christ; Perseverance; Judgment; New Creation; Worship
Purpose
To encourage persecuted churches to overcome by revealing Christ's sovereignty and the certain triumph of God's kingdom.
Outline
Letters to seven churches (1-3); Visions of judgment - seals, trumpets, bowls (4-18); Christ's return and final judgment (19-20); New creation (21-22)
Historical Context
Written around AD 95 while John was exiled on Patmos under Domitian, addressed to seven real churches in Roman Asia.
Related Places
Patmos; Ephesus; Jerusalem
Life Application
Revelation calls you to overcome by faithful witness rather than by force: refuse to worship what the world worships, endure suffering without surrendering, and fix your hope on the Lamb who has already won. When evil looks victorious, remember the end of the story, and live today as a citizen of the coming city.
Cultural Background
Revelation was written to seven real churches in Roman Asia, where emperor worship, trade guilds, and the threat of persecution pressed believers to compromise. Its symbols of beasts, harlots, and a heavenly city spoke in coded apocalyptic language that insiders would recognize and Roman authorities would find opaque.
