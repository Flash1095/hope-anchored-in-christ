1 Corinthians

Paul addresses divisions, immorality, lawsuits, and confusion about worship and spiritual gifts in the young Corinthian church. He calls them to unity, holiness, and love, and gives the great resurrection chapter.

Themes

Unity; Love; Holiness; Resurrection; Spiritual gifts

Purpose

To correct problems in the Corinthian church and answer their questions with gospel-centered order and love.

Outline

Divisions and morality (1-6); Marriage and food offered to idols (7-10); Worship and gifts (11-14); Resurrection (15-16)

Historical Context

Written from Ephesus around AD 55 to a church Paul founded in Corinth, a wealthy and morally lax Greek port city.

Life Application

First Corinthians calls a divided church to unity around the cross rather than around favorite leaders, gifts, or opinions. When your church or family splits over personalities, remember that love is the measure of every gift, and choose the unglamorous work of reconciliation over winning the argument.

Cultural Background

Corinth was a wealthy port city rebuilt as a Roman colony, famous for trade, temples, and a permissive moral climate, where status was displayed through lawsuits, banquets, and public speaking. Paul's answers about divisions, sex, idolatry, and worship only make sense in that competitive, status-conscious port culture.
