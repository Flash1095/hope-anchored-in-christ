Obadiah

Obadiah, the shortest Old Testament book, pronounces judgment on Edom for rejoicing over and looting Jerusalem in its disaster. It promises the Day of the Lord for all nations and Israel's eventual restoration.

Themes

Judgment on pride; Day of the Lord; Israel's restoration

Purpose

To assure Judah that God will judge Edom's pride and violence and restore His people.

Outline

Judgment on Edom (1-14); Day of the Lord (15-16); Israel restored (17-21)

Historical Context

Issued after an attack on Jerusalem in which Edom participated, commonly linked to the Babylonian destruction in 586 BC.

Life Application

Obadiah warns against the quiet pleasure of watching a rival fall, and the worse step of profiting from their disaster. When someone who hurt you stumbles, refuse both gloating and revenge, and leave judgment to God, who sees both their wrong and yours with perfect fairness.

Cultural Background

Edom lived in the rocky highlands southeast of Judah and controlled desert trade routes, and its people were kin to Israel through Esau. Obadiah's charge stings precisely because of that kinship: Edom did not merely watch strangers suffer but stood by, looted, and handed over refugees when brother Judah fell.
