Luke

Luke gives an orderly, detailed account of Jesus' birth, ministry, death, and resurrection, stressing His compassion for the poor, women, and outsiders. Written for Theophilus, it presents Jesus as Savior of all people.

Themes

Savior for all; Compassion; Prayer; Holy Spirit; Joy

Purpose

To give a careful, verified account so readers can know the certainty of what they were taught about Jesus.

Outline

Birth and preparation (1-4); Ministry in Galilee (5-9); Journey to Jerusalem (10-19); Passion and resurrection (20-24)

Historical Context

Written around AD 60-62 by the physician Luke, companion of Paul, for a largely Gentile audience in the Roman world.

Life Application

Luke widens your circle of compassion: notice the poor, the outsider, the woman, and the person your community overlooks, because Jesus consistently did. Let His welcome reshape who you eat with, give to, and pray for, and measure your faith by whether the marginalized find a place at your table.

Cultural Background

Luke writes as a careful historian in the style of Greco-Roman histories, addressing Theophilus and claiming to have investigated everything from eyewitness sources. That orderly, researched approach suited a Gentile audience that valued documented accounts, and it explains why Luke alone preserves so many parables about the lost and the lowly.
