2 Corinthians

In his most personal letter, Paul defends his apostleship, shares his sufferings, and urges reconciliation and generous giving. He shows that God's power is made perfect in weakness.

Themes

Ministry; Suffering; Reconciliation; Generosity; Strength in weakness

Purpose

To reconcile with the Corinthians, defend Paul's ministry against critics, and prepare the collection for Jerusalem.

Outline

Paul's ministry explained (1-7); Generous giving (8-9); Defense against false apostles (10-13)

Historical Context

Written from Macedonia around AD 56, after a painful visit and a severe letter to Corinth.

Life Application

Second Corinthians redefines strength: God's power shows up most clearly in weakness, suffering, and honest need rather than in impressive success. Stop hiding your wounds to appear strong, and let your scars become the place where others meet God's comfort and where generosity grows out of gratitude.

Cultural Background

Paul's relationship with Corinth had broken down through a painful visit, a severe letter, and rival teachers who boasted of credentials and fees. His defense of a ministry marked by suffering and a collection for Jerusalem's poor reflects that raw history, which is why this letter reads more personally than any other he wrote.
