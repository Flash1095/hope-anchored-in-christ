1 John

John writes to assure believers that they have eternal life, testing true faith by belief in Jesus, obedience, and love for one another. He declares that God is light and God is love, and that perfect love casts out fear.

Themes

Fellowship; Love; Assurance; Truth; Light

Purpose

To give believers assurance of salvation and to counter false teaching denying that Jesus came in the flesh.

Outline

Walk in the light (1-2); Children of God love one another (3-4); Faith and assurance (5)

Historical Context

Written late in the first century AD, likely from Ephesus, to churches in Asia Minor troubled by early Gnostic-like teaching.

Life Application

First John gives you three tests for assurance: believe truly in Jesus, obey His commands, and love your brothers and sisters. If doubt troubles you, examine your life by those three marks rather than by your feelings, and rest in the promise that those born of God have eternal life.

Cultural Background

First John was written against teachers who denied that Jesus truly came in the flesh, an early form of teaching that separated spirit from matter. John's insistence on what was seen, heard, and touched, and his tests of obedience and love, answer a community split by claims to special spiritual knowledge.
