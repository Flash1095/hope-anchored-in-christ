Summary
The Song of Solomon is a lyrical celebration of romantic love between a bridegroom and bride. Through poetic dialogue it honors the beauty, exclusivity, and joy of marital love as God designed it.
Key Verses
Song of Solomon 2:16; Song of Solomon 8:6-7
Key People
Solomon; The Shulamite bride; Daughters of Jerusalem
Themes
Love; Marriage; Beauty; Devotion
Purpose
To celebrate human love and marriage, often also read as a picture of God's love for His people.
Outline
Courtship (1-3); Wedding (3-4); Married love and longing (5-8)
Historical Context
Set in Solomon's Jerusalem and the countryside of Israel in the 10th century BC.
Related Places
Jerusalem
Life Application
The Song dignifies married love as a good gift from God, not a concession to be embarrassed about: delight, exclusivity, and tenderness belong in marriage. Guard that intimacy from neglect and from outside voices, and let the poetry remind you that love is meant to be celebrated, protected, and enjoyed within its covenant.
Cultural Background
The Song's imagery draws on ancient Near Eastern wedding poetry, with gardens, vineyards, spices, and city gates standing for beauty and desire. Its lovers speak in the language of pastoral life and royal courts, which is why its metaphors feel lush and strange to modern readers but would have sounded like love songs to its first hearers.
