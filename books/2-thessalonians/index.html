2 Thessalonians

Book
2 Thessalonians
Testament
New Testament
Category
Epistles
Traditional Author
Paul
Date Approx
c. AD 51-52
Timeline Era
Early Church
Key Theme
Stand firm while awaiting Christ's return
Summary
Paul corrects the claim that the Day of the Lord had already come, explaining events that must precede it. He urges the church to stand firm, hold to the teaching they received, and not grow idle while waiting.
Key Verses
2 Thessalonians 3:3; 2 Thessalonians 2:16-17
Key People
Paul; Silas; Timothy
Themes
Perseverance; Day of the Lord; Standing firm; Diligent work
Purpose
To calm fears about the end times, encourage persecuted believers, and address idleness in the church.
Outline
Encouragement in persecution (1); The man of lawlessness (2); Prayer and diligence (3)
Historical Context
Written shortly after 1 Thessalonians, around AD 51-52, while Paul was still in Corinth on his second journey.
Related Places
Thessalonica; Corinth
Life Application
Second Thessalonians corrects two opposite errors about the end: panicked speculation and lazy idleness. Stay alert without becoming obsessed, keep working, and hold fast to what you were taught, because faithful waiting looks like ordinary diligence, not frantic predictions or passive withdrawal.
Cultural Background
A forged letter claiming Paul's authority had unsettled Thessalonica by announcing that the Day of the Lord had already come, which explains the church's confusion and idleness. Second Thessalonians answers that forgery with a reminder of apostolic teaching, a pattern of authentication, and a call to orderly work.
