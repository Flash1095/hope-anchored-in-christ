Haggai

Haggai urges the returned exiles, who had stalled for years, to resume rebuilding the Temple and put God first. His brief dated messages promise God's presence and greater future glory for the house.

Themes

Priorities; Temple rebuilding; God's presence; Encouragement

Purpose

To stir the post-exile community to finish the Temple and trust God's promised blessing.

Outline

Call to build (1); Greater glory and blessings (2)

Historical Context

Delivered in Jerusalem over four months in 520 BC, eighteen years after the first exiles returned under Cyrus.

Life Application

Haggai confronts the polite excuse of waiting for a better season while your own house gets paneled and God's house lies in ruins. If you keep postponing obedience until life calms down, start with the task you have been avoiding this week, and trust that God's blessing follows putting Him first, not the other way around.

Cultural Background

Haggai's messages are precisely dated to the second year of Darius, a rare feature that ties them to the Persian administration's calendar and records. The returned exiles had stalled for eighteen years partly because Persian politics and local opposition made building risky, which is why his call to resume work took real courage.
