Esther

In the Persian capital of Susa, the Jewish queen Esther and her cousin Mordecai expose Haman's plot to destroy the Jews. God providentially preserves His people, and the feast of Purim commemorates the deliverance.

Themes

Providence; Courage; Preservation; Reversal; Purim

Purpose

To explain the origin of Purim and show God's unseen providence protecting His people in exile.

Outline

Esther becomes queen (1-2); Haman's plot (3-5); Reversal and deliverance (6-10)

Historical Context

Set in Susa under King Ahasuerus (Xerxes I, 486-465 BC), when many Jews remained in Persia after the exile.

Life Application

Esther teaches that God's providence often works without fanfare: no miracle is named, yet every coincidence lines up to save a people. When you cannot see God acting, be faithful in the position you hold, speak up when silence would be safer, and trust that He may have placed you there for exactly this moment.

Cultural Background

The Persian court ran on elaborate protocol, royal banquets, and irreversible decrees sealed with the king's ring, which is why Esther's approach to the king risked her life and why Haman's edict could not simply be canceled. The story's tension depends on that court system, where one rash decree could endanger an entire people.
