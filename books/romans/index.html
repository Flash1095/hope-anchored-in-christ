Romans

Romans is Paul's most systematic letter, explaining that all have sinned and that God justifies sinners by grace through faith in Christ. It unfolds the Christian life, Israel's place in God's plan, and practical living in love.

Themes

Righteousness of God; Faith; Grace; Salvation; Christian living

Purpose

To present the gospel fully to a church Paul hoped to visit and to unite Jewish and Gentile believers in Rome.

Outline

Sin and justification (1-5); Sanctification (6-8); Israel in God's plan (9-11); Practical living (12-16)

Historical Context

Written from Corinth around AD 57 to the house churches in Rome, capital of the empire, before Paul's journey to Jerusalem.

Life Application

Romans frees you from both pride and despair by grounding your standing in Christ's finished work rather than your performance. When you are tempted to earn God's favor or to doubt it, return to justification by faith, and let that security produce a life of love toward neighbors, including those very different from you.

Cultural Background

Paul wrote to house churches in Rome, the empire's capital, where Jewish and Gentile believers were divided over food, holy days, and the Law after the emperor's expulsion of Jews. Romans addresses that divided community directly, arguing that one gospel makes one people out of groups that the empire itself kept apart.
