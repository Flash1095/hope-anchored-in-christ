Romans

Book
Romans
Testament
New Testament
Category
Epistles
Traditional Author
Paul
Date Approx
c. AD 57
Timeline Era
Early Church
Key Theme
Salvation by faith and the righteousness of God
Summary
Romans is Paul's most systematic letter, explaining that all have sinned and that God justifies sinners by grace through faith in Christ. It unfolds the Christian life, Israel's place in God's plan, and practical living in love.
Key Verses
Romans 1:16; Romans 3:23; Romans 5:8; Romans 8:28; Romans 12:1-2
Key People
Paul; Phoebe; Priscilla and Aquila
Themes
Righteousness of God; Faith; Grace; Salvation; Christian living
Purpose
To present the gospel fully to a church Paul hoped to visit and to unite Jewish and Gentile believers in Rome.
Outline
Sin and justification (1-5); Sanctification (6-8); Israel in God's plan (9-11); Practical living (12-16)
Historical Context
Written from Corinth around AD 57 to the house churches in Rome, capital of the empire, before Paul's journey to Jerusalem.
Related Places
Rome; Corinth
