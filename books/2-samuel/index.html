Summary
2 Samuel covers David's reign as king over all Israel, his military victories, and his establishment of Jerusalem as capital. It also records his sin with Bathsheba and the painful consequences in his family.
Key Verses
2 Samuel 7:16; 2 Samuel 7:22; 2 Samuel 22:31

Purpose
To record David's reign and God's everlasting covenant promise that his house and kingdom would endure forever.
Outline
David's triumphs (1-10); David's sin and its consequences (11-20); Appendix (21-24)
Historical Context
Set in the 10th century BC during David's forty-year reign from Hebron and Jerusalem.

Life Application
Second Samuel refuses to hide David's greatest failure, showing that even a person after God's own heart can fall badly and still be restored through honest repentance. When you sin, do not minimize it or hide it; confess it plainly, accept the consequences, and trust that God's covenant mercy is bigger than your worst chapter.
Cultural Background
Ancient kings normally published only their victories and erased their defeats from the record. Second Samuel does the opposite, preserving David's adultery, murder, and family disasters alongside his triumphs, which tells its readers that this history is measured by covenant faithfulness rather than royal propaganda.
