Kosher

Kosher law governs what observant Jews eat and how food is prepared, marking out a life set apart by God's commands.

Jewish Background

Leviticus and Deuteronomy list clean and unclean animals, forbid blood and certain mixtures, and require humane slaughter and the separation of meat and dairy. Keeping kosher is both obedience and daily identity, a reminder at every meal that God's people are holy.

Points To Christ

The New Testament records Peter's vision of unclean animals with the command not to call unclean what God has made clean — a vision chiefly about welcoming Gentiles into the people of God. Many Messianic Jews continue to keep kosher as a matter of identity and witness, not as a means of salvation.
