The Veil

Topic
The Veil
Category
Custom
Summary
The veil was the woven curtain that separated the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place, marking the boundary between Israel and God's immediate presence.
Jewish Background
Woven of blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and fine linen with cherubim worked into it, the veil hung from four gold-covered pillars and screened the ark from view. Only the high priest passed beyond it, and only once a year, on the Day of Atonement, carrying blood for himself and the people. The veil taught Israel both that God dwelt among them and that sin barred free approach to him; the Temple that stood in Jesus' day likewise kept its innermost room veiled.
Points To Christ
The New Testament link is explicit: the Gospels record that the Temple veil was torn from top to bottom at Jesus' death, and Hebrews reads the veil as his flesh, the new and living way through which believers now enter the Most Holy Place with confidence. The tearing is presented not as damage to a curtain but as the opening of access God himself had guarded.
