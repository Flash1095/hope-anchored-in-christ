The Ark of the Covenant

Topic
The Ark of the Covenant
Category
Custom
Summary
The Ark of the Covenant was the gold-covered chest that held the tablets of the covenant and marked God's dwelling presence among Israel.
Jewish Background
Built to the pattern given at Sinai, the ark was an acacia-wood chest overlaid with gold, topped by the mercy seat with two cherubim whose wings overshadowed it. It held the tablets of the covenant, and Hebrews also names a jar of manna and Aaron's rod that budded. Carried by Levites on poles, the ark went before Israel through the wilderness, across the Jordan, and to Shiloh; it was captured by the Philistines, returned to Beth-shemesh and then to Kirjath-jearim, brought to Jerusalem by David, and placed in Solomon's Temple. Scripture does not record its fate after the Babylonian destruction, and the Second Temple's Most Holy Place stood empty of it.
Points To Christ
The New Testament link is explicit: Romans calls Christ the mercy seat, using the ark's own cover as the place where atonement is made, and Hebrews describes the ark and its contents as part of the earthly sanctuary that pointed to the heavenly reality. In Christ, Messianic Jews note, God's presence is no longer localized above a golden chest but dwells among his people.
