Qumran's Two Messiahs

Topic
Qumran's Two Messiahs
Category
Messianic Reading
Summary
The Dead Sea Scrolls community expected not one Messiah but two: a Messiah of Aaron, a priest, and a Messiah of Israel, a king — with a prophet like Moses alongside them.
Jewish Background
The Community Rule and the Testimonia scroll describe a community waiting for two anointed figures: one priestly, from Aaron's line, and one royal, from Israel, until 'the prophet and the Messiahs of Aaron and Israel' come. This was that community's expectation, shaped by its priestly character and its quarrel with the Jerusalem Temple establishment; it was not the expectation of all Jews. The two figures divide the roles that Israel's history had kept apart: priest and king.
Points To Christ
The site's reading is that Jesus unites what Qumran kept apart: priest and king in one person, after the order of Melchizedek, who was both. Where Qumran waited for two messiahs, Christians hold that one Messiah fulfils both offices, and that the prophet like Moses is the same Jesus.
