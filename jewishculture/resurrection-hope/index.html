Resurrection Hope

Topic
Resurrection Hope
Category
History
Summary
Resurrection hope is the Pharisaic belief, widespread by the first century, that God will raise the dead to life at the last day.
Jewish Background
The Hebrew Scriptures hint at life beyond death — Daniel's 'many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake' (Daniel 12:2) and Isaiah's 'thy dead men shall live' (Isaiah 26:19) — and by the Second Temple period the Pharisees taught resurrection openly, alongside angels and spirits, while the Sadducees denied it. Martha voices the common hope at Lazarus's tomb: 'I know that he shall rise again in the resurrection at the last day' (John 11:24). This entry covers the positive hope; the Sadducees' denial is covered in their own entry and is not repeated here.
Points To Christ
Jesus affirms the resurrection against the Sadducees from the Torah itself — God is the God of the living — and then embodies the hope: he raises Lazarus and rises himself as 'the firstfruits of them that slept' (1 Corinthians 15:20). For the New Testament, the general resurrection Israel hoped for has begun in Christ and will be completed when all are raised.
