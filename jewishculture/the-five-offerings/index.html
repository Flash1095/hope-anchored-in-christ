The Five Offerings

Topic
The Five Offerings
Category
Custom
Summary
The five offerings of Leviticus were the burnt, grain, peace, sin, and guilt offerings, each with its own purpose, procedure, and meaning.
Jewish Background
Leviticus sets out five distinct offerings. The burnt offering was wholly consumed by fire as atonement and complete dedication. The grain offering, of fine flour, oil, and frankincense with no leaven or honey, was a bloodless tribute acknowledging God's provision. The peace offering was shared, part burned, part given to the priests, and part eaten by the offerer in a fellowship meal. The sin offering dealt with unintentional sins and cleansed the sanctuary, with the blood applied according to the offerer's standing. The guilt offering added restitution, with repayment plus a fifth, for wrongs against God or neighbour that could be repaid, and a ram as the offering itself.
Points To Christ
The New Testament link is explicit: Hebrews reads the whole sacrificial system as a shadow that could never perfect the worshipper, and presents Christ's single offering as the reality that takes away sins once for all, while Paul calls Christ's self-giving an offering and sacrifice to God. Messianic reading therefore keeps the five distinct rather than collapsing them: dedication, tribute, fellowship, cleansing, and restitution are all gathered up in the one person who fulfils what each offering pictured.
