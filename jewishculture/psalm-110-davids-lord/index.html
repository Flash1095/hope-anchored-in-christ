Psalm 110: David's Lord

Topic
Psalm 110: David's Lord
Category
Messianic Reading
Summary
'The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand' — a royal psalm in which David's Lord is also a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek.
Jewish Background
Psalm 110 is a royal psalm, and Jewish tradition has read it in more than one way: of David himself, of Abraham, or of a coming Davidic king. The Melchizedek priesthood in verse 4 is distinctive, since kingship and priesthood were normally kept apart in Israel. Some ancient Jewish readings are messianic; mainstream rabbinic tradition largely reads the psalm of a past king rather than of the future Messiah. The difference is stated plainly here rather than smoothed over.
Points To Christ
The site's reading follows Jesus' own argument: when he asked how David could call the Messiah 'Lord' if the Messiah were merely David's son (Matthew 22:41-46), no one could answer him. Hebrews builds the whole priesthood of Christ on this psalm: a priest forever after Melchizedek's order, not Aaron's, seated at God's right hand.
