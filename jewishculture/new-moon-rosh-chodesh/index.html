New Moon (Rosh Chodesh)

Topic
New Moon (Rosh Chodesh)
Category
Custom
Summary
Rosh Chodesh marked the first day of each lunar month with special offerings, trumpet blasts, and a holy assembly, setting Israel's calendar by the new moon.
Jewish Background
In ancient Israel the month began when the new moon was sighted, so Rosh Chodesh was the calendar's hinge: Numbers 28 prescribes burnt offerings, a grain offering, and a sin offering for the day, and Psalm 81 calls for the trumpet to be blown at the new moon. It was a day for seeking God and for gathering — David's absence from Saul's table at the new moon feast in 1 Samuel 20 shows it was a recognised occasion — and Ezekiel pictures the sanctuary gates opened for worship on new moons. Isaiah looks forward to a time when all flesh will come to worship from one new moon to another.
Points To Christ
EXPLICIT New Testament mention: Paul names new moons alongside festivals and Sabbaths in Colossians 2:16-17, saying these are a shadow of things to come, and the substance belongs to Christ. That is Paul's own classification; it is not presented here as a claim that every detail of the new-moon offerings pictures a single event in Jesus' life.
