Tithing

Topic
Tithing
Category
Custom
Summary
Tithing is the giving of a tenth of produce and income to God, supporting the Levites, the Temple, and the poor.
Jewish Background
Leviticus declares the tithe of the land holy to the Lord, and Deuteronomy sets out its uses: the annual tithe eaten in rejoicing before the Lord, the tithe supporting the Levites who had no land of their own, and the tithe every third year stored up for the Levite, the stranger, the fatherless, and the widow. The pattern reaches back to Abraham, who gave a tenth to Melchizedek, and Malachi challenges Israel to bring the whole tithe into the storehouse and test God's blessing. Tithing was thus not a single tax but a system tying worship, priestly support, and care for the vulnerable together.
Points To Christ
EXPLICIT New Testament link: Jesus told the Pharisees they tithed mint, dill, and cumin but neglected the weightier matters of the Law, justice, mercy, and faith, adding that these they ought to have done, without leaving the others undone (Matthew 23:23). He affirms the tithe while subordinating it to justice and mercy. Hebrews takes up Abraham's tithe to Melchizedek as evidence of a priesthood greater than Levi's (Hebrews 7:1-10).
