Hospitality

Topic
Hospitality
Category
Custom
Summary
Hospitality in Jewish life is a sacred duty: the stranger is welcomed, fed, and sheltered, because Israel herself was once a stranger in Egypt.
Jewish Background
Abraham running to meet three visitors at Mamre, preparing bread, curds, and a calf, is the model — urgent, generous, and personal, with the host serving rather than delegating. Lot shelters strangers in Sodom at risk to himself. Torah turns the memory of Egypt into law: love the stranger, for ye were strangers in Egypt, and leave the corners of the field for the poor and the sojourner. Hospitality is therefore not politeness but covenant behaviour, owed especially to travellers, the poor, and the outsider.
Points To Christ
Hebrews sums the tradition up as a command to the church: 'Be not forgetful to entertain strangers: for thereby some have entertained angels unawares' (Hebrews 13:2), and Jesus makes hospitality a mark of his own people — 'I was an hungred, and ye gave me meat: I was a stranger, and ye took me in' (Matthew 25:35). He himself practises it, eating in the homes of friends, tax collectors, and Pharisees alike.
