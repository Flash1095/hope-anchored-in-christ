The Altar of Incense

Topic
The Altar of Incense
Category
Custom
Summary
The altar of incense was the small golden altar before the veil where priests burned fragrant incense morning and evening.
Jewish Background
Standing in the Holy Place directly before the veil, the gold-covered altar received a special incense blend each morning and evening, when the lamps were tended, and its horns were touched with blood on the Day of Atonement. Burning incense was a priestly task alone: unauthorized offering was a grave offence, as the deaths of Nadab and Abihu showed. In the Second Temple period the daily incense offering was assigned by lot, which is how Zechariah, father of John the Baptist, came to be in the Temple when the angel appeared to him.
Points To Christ
The New Testament link is explicit in Revelation's own imagery: the prayers of the saints rise with the incense before God's throne, and the angel's golden censer pictures intercession offered through a mediator. Luke's account of Zechariah at the altar also sets the birth of the forerunner inside the Temple's daily worship, on the threshold of the gospel story.
