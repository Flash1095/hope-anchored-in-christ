Fast of Gedaliah

Topic
Fast of Gedaliah
Category
Custom
Summary
The Fast of Gedaliah, on 3 Tishri, mourns the assassination of Gedaliah, governor of the remnant left in Judah after Jerusalem's fall.
Jewish Background
After Jerusalem fell, Gedaliah son of Ahikam was made governor over the poor remnant left in the land, and he urged them to serve Babylon and live. He was murdered at Mizpah by Ishmael, an act Jeremiah 41 recounts in detail, and the remnant, fearing Babylonian reprisal, fled to Egypt — ending organised Jewish life in the land for the exile. Zechariah names the fast of the seventh month among the fasts kept after the exile, and later tradition fixed it on 3 Tishri, the day after the New Year feast. It is a fast of political mourning: not for a Temple destroyed, but for a last chance at remaining in the land, lost to internal violence.
Points To Christ
No direct New Testament link to the Fast of Gedaliah is drawn. No New Testament passage names this fast, and none is cited here as if it did.
