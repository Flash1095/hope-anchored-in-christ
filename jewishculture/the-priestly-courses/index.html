The Priestly Courses

Topic
The Priestly Courses
Category
History
Summary
The priestly courses were the twenty-four divisions of priests who served in the Temple by rotation, a week at a time.
Jewish Background
King David organized the descendants of Aaron into twenty-four divisions, sixteen from Eleazar's line and eight from Ithamar's, with their order fixed by lot, and each division served for a week, with all divisions serving together at the great feasts. The system survived exile and return: Ezra records the priests and Levites set in their divisions for the service of the Second Temple, and Nehemiah lists the priestly heads by course. By the first century the courses were still in force, so that Zechariah, of the division of Abijah, was on duty by lot when he entered the Temple to burn incense.
Points To Christ
No direct Messianic link is drawn from the courses themselves, and none is claimed here. Their gospel significance is historical setting rather than type: Luke deliberately dates the announcement of John the Baptist's birth to Zechariah's week of service in Abijah's course, anchoring the forerunner's story inside the Temple's working order just before the birth of Jesus.
