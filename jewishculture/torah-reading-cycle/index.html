Torah Reading Cycle

Topic
Torah Reading Cycle
Category

Summary
The Torah reading cycle is the ordered schedule by which the five books of Moses are read aloud in the synagogue, portion by portion, until the whole Torah has been heard.
Jewish Background
The weekly portion is called a parashah, and there are 54 of them, one for each Sabbath of the year, with readings also on Monday and Thursday mornings. Each Torah portion is paired with a haftarah, a passage from the Prophets chosen to echo its theme. Two schedules existed in antiquity: the annual cycle, completed in one year, which became standard in Babylonian Judaism, and a triennial cycle, spread over about three years, used in the land of Israel. The cycle ends and begins again at Simchat Torah, when the last verses of Deuteronomy are read and Genesis is started afresh. The public reading itself goes back to Ezra, who read the Law aloud to the returned exiles, and by the first century it was the fixed centre of synagogue worship.
Points To Christ
EXPLICIT New Testament link: Jesus stood up to read in the Nazareth synagogue on the Sabbath, was handed the scroll of Isaiah, and announced that the passage was fulfilled in their hearing (Luke 4:16-21). In Pisidian Antioch the synagogue rulers invited Paul and Barnabas to speak after the reading of the Law and the Prophets (Acts 13:15), and James notes at the Jerusalem council that Moses has been read in every city from ancient generations, every Sabbath (Acts 15:21). TRADITIONAL reading: Messianic congregations that follow the weekly parashah read the same portions as the wider Jewish community, and often pair each portion with a New Testament passage that echoes its theme.
