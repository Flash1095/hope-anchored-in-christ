Blessing and Curse

Topic
Blessing and Curse
Category
Custom
Summary
Blessing and curse are the spoken words of favour and judgment that carry real weight in Israel's story: to bless is to invoke God's good upon someone, to curse is to call down his judgment.
Jewish Background
Blessings are spoken by fathers to sons, by priests over the people, and by God himself over Abraham — 'I will bless thee, and make thy name great' — and they are treated as effective, not merely wishful: Isaac's blessing, once given to Jacob, cannot be taken back. Curses are the mirror image, pronounced at Sinai and in Deuteronomy 28, where obedience brings blessing on city, field, and family, and disobedience brings curse in the same places. Daily Jewish life is ringed with short blessings (berakhot) over food, wine, and ordinary moments, acknowledging God as the source of every good.
Points To Christ
Paul argues explicitly that 'Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us' (Galatians 3:13), so that the blessing promised to Abraham might come to the Gentiles through faith. TRADITIONAL reading: Messianic readers also hear the priestly blessing of Numbers 6 — 'The LORD bless thee, and keep thee' — as fulfilled in the blessing Christ speaks over his people.
