Feast of Unleavened Bread

Topic
Feast of Unleavened Bread
Category
Feast
Summary
Unleavened Bread was the seven-day feast from 15 to 21 Nisan on which Israel ate no leavened bread, remembering the haste of the departure from Egypt.
Jewish Background
Leviticus 23 lists Unleavened Bread as a feast in its own right, beginning the day after Passover: for seven days no leaven was to be found in Israelite homes, and the first and seventh days were holy assemblies with no regular work. Exodus 12 ties the practice to the night of departure, when there was no time for dough to rise, and Deuteronomy 16 repeats the command for the pilgrimage gathering. Although Passover and Unleavened Bread are often spoken of together, the Torah gives them separate days and separate commands: Passover is the lamb on 14 Nisan, Unleavened Bread is the week that follows.
Points To Christ
EXPLICIT New Testament link: Paul writes that Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed, and therefore believers are to keep the feast with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth (1 Corinthians 5:7-8). The link is Paul's own, not a later reading imposed on the text.
