Sabbatical Year (Shemitah)

Topic
Sabbatical Year (Shemitah)
Category
Custom
Summary
The Sabbatical Year was every seventh year, when the land lay fallow, debts were released, and Israel relearned that the land and its produce belonged to God.
Jewish Background
Exodus 23 commands that the land rest in the seventh year so the poor may eat from it, and Leviticus 25 lays out the full pattern: six years of sowing and pruning, then a year in which the land keeps a Sabbath to the Lord. Deuteronomy 15 adds the release of debts at the end of every seven years, warning against withholding a loan because the release year is near. Nehemiah 10 shows the returned exiles recommitting themselves to the practice, including forgoing the produce of the seventh year and cancelling debts. The year was therefore both agricultural and social: rest for the soil, relief for the debtor, and a reminder that Israel held the land as tenants, not owners.
Points To Christ
No direct New Testament link to the Sabbatical Year is drawn. No New Testament passage names Shemitah, and none is cited here as if it did.
