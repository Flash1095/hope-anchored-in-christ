The Laver

Topic
The Laver
Category
Custom
Summary
The laver was the bronze basin where priests washed their hands and feet before entering the tabernacle or serving at the altar.
Jewish Background
Placed between the tent of meeting and the bronze altar, the laver held water for the priests' washing, and Scripture warns that serving unwashed meant death, so that cleansing was a condition of approaching God, not a courtesy. It was made from the bronze mirrors of the women who served at the entrance, turning instruments of self-regard into a vessel of preparation. Solomon's Temple later held the far larger Bronze Sea, carried on twelve oxen, alongside smaller basins, but the daily washing of the priests remained the laver's purpose.
Points To Christ
No direct New Testament link to the laver is drawn, and none is claimed here. TRADITIONAL reading: Messianic readers hear an echo in Hebrews' picture of hearts sprinkled clean and bodies washed with pure water as believers draw near, the same twofold cleansing, inward and outward, that the laver enacted for the priests.
