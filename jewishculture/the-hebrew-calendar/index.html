The Hebrew Calendar

Topic
The Hebrew Calendar
Category
Custom
Summary
The Hebrew calendar is the lunar calendar that sets Jewish time: months beginning with the new moon, the year beginning in spring with Passover, and feasts marking the seasons of the Exodus and the harvest.
Jewish Background
Months run from new moon to new moon, twelve in most years, with an extra month added when needed to keep Passover in spring. Exodus commands that the month of the Exodus become the first month, so the sacred year opens with deliverance, while the civil year turns in autumn around the Feast of Trumpets. Leviticus 23 lays out the whole round — weekly Sabbath, Passover, Firstfruits, Weeks, Trumpets, Atonement, Tabernacles — so that the year itself retells Israel's story. Days begin at sunset, which is why Sabbath and feasts run from evening to evening. Individual feasts have their own entries; this entry covers the calendar as a whole and does not fragment into per-month entries.
Points To Christ
The Gospels deliberately set Jesus' ministry inside this calendar: he dies at Passover, rises at Firstfruits season, and the Spirit falls at the Feast of Weeks (Pentecost). No direct Messianic link is drawn for the calendar structure itself beyond that timing, which the New Testament itself observes.
