The Bronze Altar

Topic
The Bronze Altar
Category
Custom
Summary
The bronze altar stood in the Temple court as the place where burnt offerings and sacrifices were made by fire.
Jewish Background
Set at the entrance to the tabernacle court and later before Solomon's Temple, the bronze altar was an acacia-wood frame overlaid with bronze, with horns at its four corners and a bronze grating. Here the burnt offerings, grain offerings, and peace offerings were presented, and its fire was never allowed to go out. Solomon's Temple kept the same pattern on a far larger scale, and at its dedication the altar received so many sacrifices that the king consecrated the middle of the court for the overflow.
Points To Christ
Hebrews draws the contrast explicitly in general terms: 'We have an altar' from which those serving the tabernacle have no right to eat, and Jesus suffered outside the gate, bearing reproach beyond the camp like the sin offerings burned outside. The bronze altar, where life was given for atonement, points to the one sacrifice offered once, outside Jerusalem's walls.
