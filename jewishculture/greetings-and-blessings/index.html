Greetings and Blessings

Topic
Greetings and Blessings
Category
Custom
Summary
Greetings and blessings in Jewish life run on shalom — peace, wholeness, well-being — spoken at meeting and parting, and on short blessings over the ordinary gifts of each day.
Jewish Background
Shalom is far more than the absence of quarrel: it wishes the other person completeness, safety, and God's favour. Boaz greets his reapers, 'The LORD be with you,' and they answer, 'The LORD bless thee' (Ruth 2:4) — greeting itself is a blessing. Daily life adds berakhot, brief blessings before eating, drinking wine, seeing wonders of nature, or hearing good or bad news, each naming God as blessed and as the source of the gift. The priestly blessing of Numbers 6 stands behind them all, and the kiss of peace among kin and community seals fellowship.
Points To Christ
Jesus sends his disciples with a greeting of peace — 'into whatsoever house ye enter, first say, Peace be to this house' (Luke 10:5) — and rises to greet his followers with 'Peace be unto you.' Paul's letters open and close with grace and peace, carrying the Jewish greeting into the church. TRADITIONAL reading: Messianic readers hear in 'Shalom' the peace Christ himself gives, distinct from a mere farewell.
