Ketubah

Topic
Ketubah
Category
Custom
Summary
The ketubah is the written Jewish marriage contract, setting out the husband's financial obligations to his wife and her protection if the marriage ends by death or divorce.
Jewish Background
The ketubah has roots in the biblical bride-price, the mohar, and in written marriage settlements already known from the Jewish community at Elephantine in Egypt in the fifth century BC, but the document in its standard form was fixed by the rabbis of the Mishnah. It names the couple, the date and place, and the sums pledged: traditionally two hundred zuz for a bride marrying for the first time and one hundred for a widow, together with any additional settlement the groom adds. The wife holds the document, and it must be produced to claim the settlement, which is why it functions as a real legal protection rather than a keepsake. It is read aloud at the wedding and kept by the wife throughout the marriage.
Points To Christ
No direct Messianic link is drawn for the ketubah itself: no New Testament passage mentions or alludes to this document, and the marriage imagery of Ephesians 5 and Revelation 19 concerns marriage as a covenant picture, not this contract. The entry is included as a distinct object of Jewish life, and no Messianic connection is forced onto it.
