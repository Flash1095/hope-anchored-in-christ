The Shema

The Shema is Judaism's central confession, recited morning and evening: Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is one.

Jewish Background

Deuteronomy 6:4-9 commands Israel to love God with heart, soul, and strength, to teach these words to children, and to bind them on hand and forehead and write them on doorposts. The Shema anchors Jewish prayer and identity, confessing one God and one covenant.

Points To Christ

Jesus himself affirmed the Shema as the first and greatest commandment. Mainstream Jewish tradition holds God's oneness in a way that excludes the Trinity, while Messianic readers confess the same one God, revealed in the Messiah Jesus. The difference is stated here plainly, not as a point of mockery: the Shema is holy ground for both.
