Tefillin

Topic
Tefillin
Category
Custom
Summary
Tefillin, also called phylacteries, are small black leather boxes containing Scripture passages, worn on the arm and forehead during weekday morning prayer.
Jewish Background
Deuteronomy commands God's words to be bound as a sign on the hand and as frontlets between the eyes, and the passages placed inside the boxes are Exodus 13, Deuteronomy 6:4-9, and Deuteronomy 11:13-21, written by hand on parchment. One box is bound on the upper arm, facing the heart, and one on the forehead, between the eyes, so that the wearer's strength and thought are both turned toward God's commandments. Tefillin are worn on weekdays but not on Sabbaths or festivals, when the day itself is the sign. Archaeological finds, including tefillin from the Judean Desert, show the practice was established by the Second Temple period.
Points To Christ
EXPLICIT New Testament link: Jesus criticised religious leaders who broadened their phylacteries to be seen by others (Matthew 23:5). His criticism was not of the practice itself but of wearing it for display, which assumes the practice was normal and familiar in his day. TRADITIONAL reading: Messianic Jews who wear tefillin describe the arm box as binding the heart and the head box as binding the mind to the Messiah who kept the Law perfectly.
