Tallit

Topic
Tallit
Category
Custom
Summary
The tallit is the four-cornered garment worn in prayer, fringed with tzitzit at its corners, which grew out of the ordinary outer cloak of biblical times into a distinct prayer shawl.
Jewish Background
In biblical and Second Temple times the tallit was not yet a special liturgical object: it was the everyday outer cloak, and because it had four corners it carried the fringes commanded in Numbers 15 and Deuteronomy 22. The same Hebrew word for corner or edge, kanaph, also means wing, which is why Ruth could ask Boaz to spread his kanaph over her as a pledge of protection. Only in later rabbinic Judaism did the tallit become a distinct, usually striped prayer shawl with a decorated neckband, worn for morning prayer. The garment itself, then, is biblical; the prayer shawl in its familiar modern form is a later development, and the two stages should not be confused.
Points To Christ
EXPLICIT New Testament link: crowds begged to touch even the hem of Jesus' garment, and as many as touched it were made well (Matthew 14:36), the hem again being the fringed edge of his cloak. TRADITIONAL reading: Messianic readers connect the kanaph, the wing or corner of the garment, with Malachi's promise of the Sun of Righteousness rising with healing in his wings, reading the healing at Jesus' hem as the wings of the Messiah.
