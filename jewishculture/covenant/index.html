Covenant

Covenant

History
Summary
Covenant is the framework of Israel's relationship with God: a binding bond, sealed by promises, signs, and obligations, running from Noah and Abraham through Sinai to David.
Jewish Background
In Jewish thought God relates to his people not by vague goodwill but by covenant (berit): a sworn, binding relationship with both promise and responsibility. The covenant with Abraham promised descendants and land, sealed by circumcision; the covenant at Sinai bound the redeemed people to Torah, sealed with sacrifice; the covenant with David promised an enduring throne. Each covenant has a sign, a story, and a demand: God keeps his word, and Israel is called to keep hers.
Points To Christ
The New Testament presents Jesus as the mediator of a new covenant, promised in Jeremiah 31 and sealed by his blood at the Last Supper: 'This cup is the new testament in my blood' (Luke 22:20), and Hebrews sets out how the new covenant fulfils what Sinai foreshadowed. TRADITIONAL reading: Messianic readers also see the Abrahamic promise — 'in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed' — as reaching its full scope in the inclusion of the Gentiles through Christ.
