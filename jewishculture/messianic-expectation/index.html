Messianic Expectation

Topic
Messianic Expectation
Category
Messianic Reading
Summary
Messianic expectation is what first-century Jews hoped for in the Messiah: an anointed king from David's line who would free Israel, judge its enemies, and restore God's reign.
Jewish Background
By the first century, expectation was widespread but not uniform. Many looked for a Davidic warrior-king who would throw off Rome, rebuild or purify the Temple, and gather the scattered tribes; others, shaped by Daniel, hoped for a heavenly Son of Man; still others expected a priestly or prophetic figure. The hope was fed by Isaiah's Prince of Peace, Micah's ruler from Bethlehem, and Zechariah's humble king on a donkey — texts that did not all point the same way, which is why Jesus was recognised by some and rejected by others. This entry describes the expectation itself; it does not claim all Jews held one picture.
Points To Christ
The Gospels show Jesus deliberately taking up these expectations and reshaping them: he enters Jerusalem on a donkey as Zechariah's king, yet is crucified rather than crowned by Rome; after the resurrection he explains from the Scriptures that the Christ had to suffer before entering glory (Luke 24:25-27). The disciples' question after the resurrection — 'wilt thou at this time restore again the kingdom to Israel?' (Acts 1:6) — shows the political expectation still alive in them. Messianic readers hold that Jesus fulfilled the prophetic hope in his first coming and will complete the political and cosmic restoration at his return; mainstream Jewish tradition holds that the Messiah has not yet come, and the difference is stated plainly here.
