The World to Come

Topic
The World to Come
Category
History
Summary
The World to Come is the age to come in Jewish hope: a renewed creation where God reigns, the dead are raised, and sorrow gives way to everlasting life.
Jewish Background
The Hebrew prophets picture a new heavens and a new earth where the wolf and lamb feed together and former troubles are forgotten, and Daniel joins that renewal to the awakening of the dead — some to everlasting life, some to shame. Rabbinic teaching later named this hope Olam Ha-Ba, the World to Come, and debated who has a share in it, but the core picture predates the rabbis: history is heading somewhere, and God's future will undo the brokenness of the present. This entry covers the coming age itself; resurrection as the event within it has its own entry.
Points To Christ
Jesus speaks of the regeneration, when the Son of Man sits on his throne (Matthew 19:28), and of the age to come in which the righteous are like angels and children of the resurrection (Luke 20:34-36). Revelation closes the canon with the same vision — a new heaven and a new earth, and God wiping away all tears (Revelation 21:1-4) — which Christians read as the World to Come inaugurated by Christ and completed at his return.
