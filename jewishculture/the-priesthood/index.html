The Priesthood

Topic
The Priesthood
Category
History
Summary
The priesthood was the hereditary Aaronic institution that mediated between God and Israel through sacrifice, teaching, and blessing.
Jewish Background
The priesthood was restricted to Aaron and his sons, consecrated by washing, clothing, anointing, and sacrifice, and passed from father to son. Priests offered the sacrifices, tended the lamp and incense, pronounced the blessing over the people, taught the law, and judged matters of purity, while the rest of Levi assisted them. They received no territorial inheritance, living instead from the tithes and offerings, since the Lord himself was their portion. By the Second Temple period the priesthood had also become a political aristocracy, and prophets and apostles alike charged its leaders with corruption when office served wealth and power rather than the people.
Points To Christ
The New Testament link is explicit twice over: Hebrews argues that the Aaronic priesthood, being changeable and mortal, gives way to a priest after the order of Melchizedek who lives forever, and Peter reapplies the priestly calling to all believers, a royal priesthood offering spiritual sacrifices. The institution that once mediated through one family is thus both fulfilled in Christ and extended to his whole people.
