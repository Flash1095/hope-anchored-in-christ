The Daily Offerings

Topic
The Daily Offerings
Category
Custom
Summary
The daily offerings, the Tamid, were the two lambs offered every morning and evening, with grain and drink offerings, as Israel's continual burnt offering.
Jewish Background
The Tamid, meaning continual, was the fixed heartbeat of Temple worship: one lamb each morning and one each evening, with fine flour, oil, and wine, offered on the bronze altar, accompanied by the morning and evening incense and the tending of the lamps. Extra offerings were added on Sabbaths and feasts, but the Tamid itself never paused while the Temple stood. Daniel's prophecy of a time when the daily sacrifice would be taken away was read by later Jews against the Temple's desecration and, after AD 70, against its destruction, when the daily offering ceased for want of a Temple.
Points To Christ
The New Testament link is explicit: Hebrews contrasts the priests who stand daily offering the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins, with Christ, who offered one sacrifice for sins for all time and then sat down. The daily rhythm that could only repeat becomes, in Hebrews' reading, the measure of what Christ's single offering finished.
