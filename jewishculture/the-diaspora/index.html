The Diaspora

Topic
The Diaspora
Category
History
Summary
The Diaspora is the scattering of Jews beyond the Land of Israel: communities formed by exile, trade, and migration that kept Torah and synagogue life far from Jerusalem.
Jewish Background
The Diaspora began with the Assyrian and Babylonian exiles and grew through the Persian and Greek periods, so that by the first century large Jewish communities lived in Babylon, Egypt — above all Alexandria, where the Scriptures were translated into Greek — Asia Minor, and Rome. Jeremiah told the exiles to seek the welfare of the cities where they lived, and diaspora Jews balanced loyalty to Jerusalem, with pilgrimage and Temple tax, against life in Greek and Roman cities. This entry covers that scattered life itself, which the Second Temple Judaism overview does not treat in its own right; it does not repeat that overview's account of sects and Temple politics.
Points To Christ
The Diaspora is the setting of much of the New Testament: Jews from every nation are in Jerusalem at Pentecost (Acts 2:5-11), Paul preaches first in diaspora synagogues on every journey, and James and Peter address their letters to the scattered tribes and the strangers dispersed abroad (James 1:1; 1 Peter 1:1). The scattering that began in judgment becomes, in the apostles' hands, the network along which the Gospel travels.
