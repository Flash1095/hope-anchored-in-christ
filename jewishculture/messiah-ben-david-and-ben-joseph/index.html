Messiah ben David and Messiah ben Joseph

Topic
Messiah ben David and Messiah ben Joseph
Category
Messianic Reading
Summary
Rabbinic tradition speaks of two messianic figures: Messiah ben David, the victorious king from David's line, and Messiah ben Joseph, a suffering messiah who is slain in battle before the final redemption.
Jewish Background
The two-messiah idea appears in rabbinic literature, most famously in the Babylonian Talmud (Sukkah 52a), where Zechariah 12:10 is read of mourning for Messiah ben Joseph who was slain. Messiah ben Joseph, from the tribe of Joseph, was understood to fight the wars of God and fall, after which Messiah ben David would come in victory and restore Israel. The tradition wrestles honestly with a tension in the prophetic texts: some pictures show a conquering king, others a suffering figure. Not all Jewish thinkers held the two-messiah scheme; some read all the promises of one Davidic Messiah. This entry describes the tradition, not a unanimous Jewish doctrine.
Points To Christ
The site's reading is that the two pictures belong to one Messiah in two comings: Jesus was slain like the suffering figure and will return as the victorious king. The disciples themselves wrestled with the same tension until the resurrection, when Jesus explained from the Scriptures that the Christ had to suffer before entering glory (Luke 24:25-27). Where Jewish tradition holds two figures apart, Christians hold one Messiah fulfilling both roles.
