Targumic Messianic Renderings

Topic
Targumic Messianic Renderings
Category
Messianic Reading
Summary
The Aramaic Targums — paraphrases of Scripture read in the synagogue — often make the Messiah explicit where the Hebrew text leaves him unnamed.
Jewish Background
When Hebrew ceased to be the people's everyday tongue, the Targums (Onkelos on the Torah, Jonathan on the Prophets) rendered Scripture into Aramaic, weaving explanation into the text. In messianic passages they frequently name the Messiah outright: Targum Jonathan on Isaiah 11 reads the Branch as the Messiah, and Targum Onkelos on Genesis 49 reads 'until Shiloh come' as 'until the Messiah comes, to whom the kingdom belongs.' These paraphrases reached final form later than the biblical text, but the traditions they carry are ancient, and they show how Jewish hearers already heard these passages messianically.
Points To Christ
The site's reading is that the Targums are valuable witnesses: the New Testament's messianic claims did not appear in a vacuum but landed among readers who already heard Messiah in these texts. Where the Hebrew is ambiguous, the Targums show one faithful Jewish way of hearing it — the way the apostles' claims echo.
