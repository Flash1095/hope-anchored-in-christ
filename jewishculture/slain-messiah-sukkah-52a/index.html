The Slain Messiah (Sukkah 52a)

Topic
The Slain Messiah (Sukkah 52a)
Category
Messianic Reading
Summary
The Babylonian Talmud pictures mourning for 'Messiah ben Joseph who was slain' — a tradition that wrestles with a Messiah who dies before the final victory.
Jewish Background
In Sukkah 52a, the rabbis read Zechariah 12:10 — 'they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn' — of Messiah ben Joseph, slain in the wars that precede redemption. The passage is late (Talmudic period) and its interpretation is debated even within the tradition, and it is presented here as tradition, not as early proof of anything. What it shows is that Judaism itself wrestled with the figure of a slain messiah, not only with a victorious one.
Points To Christ
The site's reading is that this tradition, however late, preserves the pattern the Gospels proclaim: a Messiah pierced and mourned, then vindicated. Christians hold that ben Joseph and ben David are not two figures but one Messiah, slain at his first coming and returning in glory at his second.
