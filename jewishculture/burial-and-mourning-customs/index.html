Burial and Mourning Customs

Topic
Burial and Mourning Customs
Category
Custom
Summary
Jewish burial and mourning customs centre on prompt, honourable burial, mourning that is both bodily and communal, and the tomb as a place of ongoing respect for the dead.
Jewish Background
The dead were buried promptly, usually the same day, washed, anointed with spices, wrapped in linen, and laid in a tomb, often a rock-cut cave like the one Abraham bought for Sarah. In the Second Temple period a second stage became common: after the flesh decayed, the bones were gathered into a small stone box, an ossuary, often inscribed with the name. Mourning was expressed bodily, by tearing garments, wearing sackcloth, and sitting in dust and ashes, and communally: seven days of intense mourning are already recorded for Jacob, and thirty days for Moses and Aaron. The formal seven-day observance later called shiva was systematised in rabbinic Judaism after the Temple period, so its later form should not be read back unchanged into biblical times.
Points To Christ
EXPLICIT New Testament link: Jesus was buried according to Jewish custom, wrapped in linen with spices by Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus and laid in a new rock-cut tomb (John 19:38-42), and Lazarus was raised from a cave tomb with his grave clothes still binding him (John 11:38-44). The burial customs of the Gospels are the customs described here, practised without alteration for the Messiah himself.
