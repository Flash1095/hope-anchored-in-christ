Shemini Atzeret (Eighth Day Assembly)

Topic
Shemini Atzeret (Eighth Day Assembly)
Category
Feast
Summary
Shemini Atzeret is the eighth-day sacred assembly that closes the Tabernacles season, a separate holy day after the seven days of booths.
Jewish Background
Leviticus 23 gives Shemini Atzeret its own command after the seven days of Tabernacles: on the eighth day there is a holy assembly, an offering made by fire, and no regular work. Numbers 29 repeats the prescription with its own offerings, distinct from the offerings of the seven days. The Hebrew name means an assembly or a holding back — the people linger one more day before dispersing after the pilgrimage feast. It is not simply the last day of Tabernacles, and it is not the same observance as the later Simchat Torah celebration, which grew up around it in post-biblical times.
Points To Christ
TRADITIONAL reading: some Messianic readers connect Jesus' cry on the last day, that great day of the feast, inviting the thirsty to come to him for living water (John 7:37-38), with this closing assembly. Whether John's 'last day' is the seventh day of Tabernacles or the eighth-day assembly is debated among commentators, so this is presented as a traditional reading, not as an explicit New Testament identification.
Scripture
