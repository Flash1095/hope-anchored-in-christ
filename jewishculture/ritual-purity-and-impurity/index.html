Ritual Purity and Impurity

Topic
Ritual Purity and Impurity
Category
Custom
Summary
Ritual purity is the system, set out in Leviticus, by which states of cleanness and uncleanness govern access to worship, the Temple, and holy things.
Jewish Background
Impurity in Torah is not sin: childbirth, skin disease, bodily discharges, and contact with a corpse all make a person ritually unclean for a time, and the remedy is waiting, washing, and in some cases sacrifice. The system guards the boundary between the common and the holy, so that nothing unclean approaches the sanctuary. By the Second Temple period purity rules shaped daily life far beyond the Temple — in food, in washing hands, in who could eat together — and the Pharisees extended priestly care for purity to ordinary homes. This entry covers the system; the mikveh bath itself is covered in its own entry and is not redrafted here.
Points To Christ
Jesus clashes with purity teachers not by dismissing holiness but by relocating defilement: 'there is nothing from without a man, that entering into him can defile him' — evil comes from within (Mark 7:15, 20-23). Peter's vision of unclean animals (Acts 10:9-16) extends the same point to table fellowship with Gentiles, and Hebrews presents Christ's blood as the cleansing the old washings pointed toward (Hebrews 9:13-14).
