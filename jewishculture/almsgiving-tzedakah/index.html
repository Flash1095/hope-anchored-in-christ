Almsgiving

Topic
Almsgiving
Category
Custom
Summary
Almsgiving, in Hebrew tzedakah, is giving to the poor as an act of righteousness and justice, not optional charity, and it stood beside prayer and fasting as a core discipline of Jewish piety.
Jewish Background
Tzedakah literally means righteousness: the poor have a claim, and the giver discharges a duty. Deuteronomy commands an open hand to the needy brother, warning against a grudging heart, and Proverbs says that whoever is kind to the poor lends to the Lord. In Second Temple Judaism, alms were given at the Temple, at synagogue collection boxes, and directly to beggars, and later tradition ranked its forms, with helping a person become self-supporting counted highest. The discipline was public enough to be abused for display, which is exactly the abuse Jesus addresses.
Points To Christ
EXPLICIT New Testament link: Jesus taught that when giving alms, the left hand should not know what the right hand is doing, so that giving is in secret and the Father who sees in secret rewards it (Matthew 6:2-4). He likewise told his hearers to sell their possessions and give alms, providing purses that do not grow old (Luke 12:33). Cornelius, a God-fearing Gentile, is presented as a man whose prayers and alms had come up as a memorial before God (Acts 10:2,4).
