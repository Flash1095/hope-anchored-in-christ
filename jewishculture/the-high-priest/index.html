The High Priest

Topic
The High Priest
Category
History
Summary
The high priest was the head of the Aaronic priesthood, the only man who entered the Most Holy Place, once a year on the Day of Atonement.
Jewish Background
The office passed from Aaron to his eldest son, marked by a special anointing and by garments no other priest wore: the ephod, the breastpiece bearing the twelve tribes over his heart, and the gold plate engraved 'Holiness to the Lord.' He alone entered the Most Holy Place, on the Day of Atonement, after offerings for himself and the people, and he bore the Urim and Thummim for seeking God's guidance. In the Second Temple period the high priest was also Israel's leading political figure, and by Jesus' day the office was filled by appointment of the ruling power rather than by simple hereditary succession, so that families such as that of Annas held influence across several high priesthoods, including that of Caiaphas.
Points To Christ
The New Testament link is explicit: Hebrews presents Jesus as the great high priest who has passed through the heavens, sinless and therefore needing no offering for himself, who offered himself once for all and now intercedes continually. This entry covers the office as an institution, its succession, garments, and duties; the typology collection's 'High Priest' entry covers the same figure as a type fulfilled in Christ.
