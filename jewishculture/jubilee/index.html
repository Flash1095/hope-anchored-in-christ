Jubilee

Topic
Jubilee
Category
Custom
Summary
The Jubilee was the fiftieth year, proclaimed with a trumpet on the Day of Atonement, when debts were cancelled, slaves were freed, and sold land returned to its original family.
Jewish Background
Leviticus 25 sets the Jubilee after seven Sabbatical cycles — seven times seven years, with the fiftieth year hallowed. On the Day of Atonement a trumpet sounded through the land, liberty was proclaimed, every person returned to their possession, and every family returned to its own. Land could not be sold permanently, because, as the chapter puts it, the land is God's and Israel are strangers and sojourners with him. Numbers 36 shows the Jubilee already shaping inheritance law, since a daughter's marriage could move land between tribes until the Jubilee restored it. Whether the Jubilee was ever fully kept in practice is not claimed in the Torah's account; the command itself is the witness to God's economics of restoration.
Points To Christ
TRADITIONAL reading: Messianic readers hear an echo of Jubilee in Jesus reading Isaiah 61 in the synagogue at Nazareth — good news to the poor, liberty to the captives, and the acceptable year of the Lord (Luke 4:18-19). Luke does not use the word Jubilee, and Isaiah 61 does not name it either, so this is presented as a traditional correspondence of language and theme, not as an explicit New Testament identification of Jesus' ministry with the Jubilee year.
