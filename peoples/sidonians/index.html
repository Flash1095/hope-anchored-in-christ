Sidonians

People Group
Sidonians
Time Period
From Deuteronomy through Ezekiel (Late Bronze Age to exile)
Region
The Phoenician coast around Sidon, north of Tyre
History
Sidon was Canaan's firstborn (Genesis 10:15), and the Sidonians are the distinct city-people of Sidon, not Phoenicians in general: the Phoenicia entry covers Phoenicians broadly, while this entry covers Sidon itself, on the same pattern as Samaritans beside Samaria. Deuteronomy 3:9 notes that the Sidonians call Hermon Sirion. Judges 3:3 and 10:12 count Sidonians among Israel's oppressors, and Judges 18:7 describes the Zidonians as 'quiet and secure.' Solomon hired Sidonians as skilled timbermen for the temple (1 Kings 5:6), then married Sidonian wives and followed Ashtoreth, goddess of the Zidonians (1 Kings 11:1, 33). Jezebel was the daughter of Ethbaal, king of the Zidonians (1 Kings 16:31). Ezekiel 32:30 counts the Zidonians among the slain gone down to the pit. The King James Version uses both Sidonians and Zidonians for this people.
Culture
Seafarers and timbermen of the Phoenician coast, famed for cedar work and trade.
Religion
Ashtoreth, goddess of the Zidonians, carried into Israel by Solomon's Sidonian wives and by Jezebel.
Bible Connection
Sidonians touch Israel at every turn: oppressors in Judges, temple workmen under Solomon, the source of Jezebel, and the slain of Ezekiel.
Key Scriptures
Deuteronomy 3:9; Judges 3:3; Judges 10:12; Judges 18:7; 1 Kings 5:6; 1 Kings 11:1; Ezekiel 32:30
Related People

Related Places
Sidon; Tyre; Zarephath
