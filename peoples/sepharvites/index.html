Sepharvites

The Sepharvites were one of the Mesopotamian peoples Assyria resettled in Samaria after Israel's fall (2 Kings 17:24). The Sepharvites burnt their children in fire to Adrammelech and Anammelech, the gods of Sepharvaim (2 Kings 17:31), and the kings of Sepharvaim were named by Assyria's king when he mocked the gods of the nations (2 Kings 18:34; 19:13).

Culture

As a Mesopotamian people, the Sepharvites shared the urban culture of the Euphrates cities.

Bible Connection

The Sepharvites stand in the Bible as a warning example twice over: a people whose gods could not save their cities from Assyria, and whose worship in Samaria showed how far the resettled land had fallen.

Details

People Group: Sepharvites
Time Period: c. 700 BC (Assyrian resettlement)
Region: Sepharvaim, on the Euphrates in Mesopotamia
Religion: The Sepharvites worshipped Adrammelech and Anammelech, to whom they offered child sacrifice — the Bible's darkest note on their religion.
Key Scriptures: 2 Kings 17:24; 2 Kings 17:31; 2 Kings 18:34; 2 Kings 19:13
Related Places: Samaria
Related Events: Fall of Samaria
