Philistines

People Group
Philistines
Time Period
c. 1200-600 BC (settlement to the Babylonian period); named already in Genesis
Region
The southern coastal plain: Gaza, Ashkelon, Ashdod, Ekron, Gath
History
This entry covers the Philistines as a people; the five-city league and its wars as a polity are covered by the Philistia entry in Civilizations. The Philistines are usually identified with the Peleset of the Egyptian Sea Peoples records, who settled the southern coast around 1200 BC, but that identification is a scholarly proposal, not a certainty. Genesis already names Philistines in Abraham's day (Genesis 21:34), before the Sea Peoples settlement, a point scholars debate, whether as an early presence or a later name applied backward. In the judges era they oppressed Israel (Judges 13:1) and Samson fought them single-handed; they captured the ark and set it in Dagon's temple before returning it (1 Samuel 5:1). Goliath of Gath challenged Israel's army (1 Samuel 17:1), and David fought them as outlaw, king, and old man (2 Samuel 5:17). Amos 9:7 traces them to Caphtor. Under the empires that followed, the Philistine cities were absorbed and the people disappear from the record.
Culture
Aegean settlers turned coastal city-dwellers, famed for iron-working and for the bichrome pottery that marks their towns; their five cities shared a league but kept their own lords.
Religion
Dagon, god of Ashdod, and Ashtaroth, goddess of the Philistine cities; the ark stood in Dagon's temple before it was returned.
Bible Connection
The Philistines are Israel's great early rival: Samson's enemy, the captors of the ark, Goliath's people, and the power David fought before and after becoming king.
Key Scriptures
Genesis 21:34; Judges 13:1; 1 Samuel 5:1; 1 Samuel 17:1; 2 Samuel 5:17; Amos 9:7
Related People
Samson; Delilah; Goliath; King Saul; King David
Related Places
Gath; Gaza; Ashkelon; Ashdod; Ekron
Related Events
David and Goliath; Samson and Delilah
