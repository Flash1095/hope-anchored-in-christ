Israelites

People Group
Israelites
Time Period
c. 1900 BC-first century AD
Region
Canaan, then Egypt, then the land of Israel; after the exile, Judah and the Dispersion
History
This entry covers the Israelites as a people; the kingdoms and the whole national story are covered by the Israel entry in Civilizations, and the individual tribes by their own entries. The name comes from Jacob, renamed Israel after wrestling with God (Genesis 32:28); his twelve sons became the twelve tribes. At Sinai the Exodus nation became 'a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation' (Exodus 19:5), 'a holy people unto the LORD thy God' (Deuteronomy 7:6). After Solomon the kingdom split (1 Kings 12:16) and Assyria carried the northern tribes away (2 Kings 17:6), but a remnant returned and the people endured. In the New Testament Paul still opens his anguish with 'my kinsmen according to the flesh: who are Israelites' (Romans 9:4). Whether Paul's 'Israel of God' (Galatians 6:16) means ethnic Israel or the whole church is debated, and is left debated here.
Culture
A tribal people become a nation under Torah: twelve tribes under one covenant, gathered at the tabernacle and then the temple, scattered in exile and regathered under Cyrus.
Religion
The worship of the LORD under the covenant: one God, one Torah, one temple in Jerusalem, the identity that made Israel a people rather than only a kingdom.
Bible Connection
The Israelites are the covenant people of the whole Bible: Jacob's twelve tribes, the Exodus nation, the exiles who returned, and the name Paul still claims in Romans.
Key Scriptures
Genesis 32:28; Exodus 19:5; Deuteronomy 7:6; 1 Kings 12:16; 2 Kings 17:6; Romans 9:4
