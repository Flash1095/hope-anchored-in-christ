Arameans

People Group
Arameans
Time Period
c. 1200-700 BC as kingdoms; Aramaic remained the region's common language into the New Testament period
Region
Syria, centered on Damascus, with Aramean settlements east and south
History
This entry covers the Arameans as a people; the Damascus-centered kingdoms and their wars are covered by the Aram entry in Civilizations. The Arameans descend from Aram, son of Shem (Genesis 10:22). The King James Version usually calls this people the Syrians: Rebekah was 'the Syrian' (Genesis 25:20), David struck 'the Syrians of Damascus' (2 Samuel 8:5), Ben-Hadad of Syria warred with Ahab (1 Kings 20:1), and Naaman, captain of Syria's army, was healed of leprosy by Elisha (2 Kings 5:1). Amos 9:7 traces the Syrians to Kir. Their language outlived their kingdoms: Aramaic became the Near East's common tongue and remained the everyday speech of Judea in the New Testament period.
Culture
Traders and farmers of the Syrian interior, whose language, Aramaic, spread across the Near East under the empires and was still spoken in Jesus' day.
Religion
Hadadrimmon is named in Zechariah 12:11, though whether the name is a god or a place is debated; Scripture records no Aramean god directly.
Bible Connection
The Arameans are Israel's northern neighbors and rivals: the Syrians of Damascus in Kings, Naaman's people, and the speakers of the Aramaic that became the region's common tongue.
Key Scriptures
Genesis 10:22; Genesis 25:20; 2 Samuel 8:5; 1 Kings 20:1; 2 Kings 5:1; Amos 9:7
Related People
Hazael, King of Syria; Ben-Hadad I, King of Syria; Naaman; Elisha; King Ahab
Related Places
Damascus; Aram
Related Finds
Melqart Stele; Tel Dan Stele (House of David)
