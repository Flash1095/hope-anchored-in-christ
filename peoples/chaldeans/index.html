Chaldeans

People Group
Chaldeans
Time Period
9th-6th centuries BC (Neo-Babylonian ascendancy); the name also survives as a learned caste in Daniel
Region
Southern Mesopotamia, centred on Babylon
History
The Chaldeans are the people of Chaldea who rose under Nabopolassar and Nebuchadnezzar to rule the Neo-Babylonian empire. Habakkuk 1:6 calls them 'that bitter and hasty nation, which shall march through the breadth of the land, to possess the dwellingplaces that are not theirs.' Jeremiah names them as the besiegers of Jerusalem again and again (Jeremiah 21:4), and Job 1:17 has Chaldean raiders carrying off Job's camels. In Daniel the same word also denotes a learned caste: the 'tongue of the Chaldeans' (Daniel 1:4) and the Chaldeans summoned as wise men and astrologers (Daniel 2:2). Both uses are kept distinct here: the nation and the caste. Note on placement: the October 27 civilizations merge deliberately folded Chaldeans into the Babylon entry; this draft treats the people and the caste distinctly from the empire, and publishing it reverses that fold without touching the Babylon entry.
Culture
Imperial administrators, soldiers, and scholars: the ruling people of Nebuchadnezzar's Babylon, and in Daniel a caste of astrologers and wise men.
Religion
The gods of Babylon, including Bel and Nebo; in Daniel the Chaldeans serve as the court diviners whose wisdom Daniel's God surpasses.
Bible Connection
The Chaldeans are the instrument of Judah's exile in Jeremiah and Habakkuk, the raiders of Job's prologue, and the court wise men of Daniel.
Key Scriptures
Job 1:17; Habakkuk 1:6; Jeremiah 21:4; Daniel 1:4; Daniel 2:2
Related People
Daniel
Related Places
Babylon; Chaldea
Related Events
