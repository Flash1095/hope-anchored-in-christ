Phoenicians

People Group
Phoenicians
Time Period
c. 1500-300 BC, with a New Testament presence in the Gospels
Region
The northern Levantine coast around Tyre and Sidon, with colonies across the Mediterranean
History
This entry covers the Phoenicians as a people; the trading city-states and their colonies are covered by the Phoenicia entry in Civilizations. The Phoenicians are the coast people of Tyre, Sidon, and Byblos, distinct as a whole from the Sidonians entry, which covers Sidon itself. Hiram, king of Tyre, sent cedar and craftsmen for Solomon's temple (1 Kings 5:1), and a later Tyrian alliance gave Israel Jezebel, daughter of Ethbaal, king of the Zidonians, whose marriage to Ahab imported Baal worship (1 Kings 16:31). Ezekiel's lament over Tyre (Ezekiel 27:2) is the fullest portrait of their trade. In the Gospels Jesus withdrew to the region of Tyre and Sidon (Matthew 15:21), where a Syrophoenician woman's faith drew his praise (Mark 7:26).
Culture
Seafarers, shipbuilders, and dyers of purple, whose ships carried cedar, metals, and glass across the Mediterranean; their alphabet spread with their trade.
Religion
Baal and Ashtoreth, carried into Israel by Jezebel; Melqart of Tyre, the city god of sailors.
Bible Connection
The Phoenicians are the seafaring traders of the northern coast: Hiram's workmen built Solomon's temple, Jezebel carried their gods into Israel, and Jesus praised a Syrophoenician woman's faith.
Key Scriptures
1 Kings 5:1; 1 Kings 16:31; Ezekiel 27:2; Matthew 15:21; Mark 7:26
Related People
Hiram, King of Tyre; Jezebel; King Solomon; King Ahab; Elijah
Related Places
Tyre; Sidon; Zarephath
Related Events
