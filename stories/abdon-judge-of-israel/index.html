Abdon, Judge of Israel

Abdon, Judge of Israel

Retelling

After Elon, Abdon, son of Hillel, a Pirathonite, judged Israel for eight years. He had forty sons and thirty grandsons who rode on seventy donkey colts. Then Abdon died and was buried at Pirathon in the land of Ephraim, in the hill country of the Amalekites. No enemy, battle, or specific deed is recorded in his account. He is the last of the minor judges named before Samson, closing the book's list of judges with a household at peace in Ephraim.

Discussion

Abdon's record is a household on the move: seventy grandsons and sons riding in a procession that displayed peace and prosperity in Ephraim. His judgeship ends the list of minor judges with an image of a flourishing family rather than a battlefield. Reflect: 1) What does flourishing look like in a time of peace? 2) How does a large, stable household witness to God's blessing? 3) What will your own household's procession show the next generation?

Significance

Abdon's eight years close the minor judges with a picture of household prosperity in Ephraim. Doubt: the text records no enemy, battle, or specific deed, so this row preserves a notice of judgeship rather than a narrated deliverance.
