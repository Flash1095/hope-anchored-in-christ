Philip and the Ethiopian Eunuch

Philip and the Ethiopian Eunuch

Retelling

An angel told Philip to go south to the desert road from Jerusalem to Gaza, where he met an Ethiopian eunuch, a court official of the queen of Ethiopia in charge of her treasury, returning from worship in Jerusalem. The man sat in his chariot reading the prophet Isaiah. The Spirit told Philip to join the chariot, and Philip ran up and heard him reading, He was led like a sheep to the slaughter. Philip asked whether he understood what he was reading, and the official answered, How can I, unless someone guides me? He invited Philip to sit with him, and beginning from that very passage, Philip told him the good news about Jesus. As they travelled they came to some water, and the official said, See, here is water; what prevents me from being baptised? Philip baptised him, and when they came up out of the water, the Spirit carried Philip away to Azotus, while the official went on his way rejoicing. Philip was later found at Azotus and preached through the towns until he reached Caesarea.

Discussion

The Gospel reached Africa through one guided Bible reading: a question, an open scroll, and water on a desert road. No barrier — race, office, or bodily condition — kept the official from baptism. Reflect: Who is reading Scripture without a guide, waiting for someone to run up alongside? What water is in front of you that you are treating as a barrier? How does this story widen your map of who the Gospel is for?

Significance

The Ethiopian eunuch's baptism shows the Gospel crossing ethnic and social boundaries to the ends of the earth, through Scripture explained and immediately obeyed.
