The Disciples Pluck Grain on the Sabbath

The Disciples Pluck Grain on the Sabbath

Retelling

One Sabbath, as Jesus and his disciples walked through grainfields, the hungry disciples began to pick heads of grain and eat them. The Pharisees objected that this was unlawful on the Sabbath. Jesus answered from Scripture: David, when hungry, entered the house of God and ate the consecrated bread, which only priests might eat, and the priests themselves work in the temple on the Sabbath yet are innocent. He added that something greater than the temple was here, that God desires mercy, not sacrifice, and concluded that the Son of Man is Lord of the Sabbath.

Discussion

The Sabbath was made for human flourishing, not as a trap for the hungry: Jesus reads the Law through mercy. Reflect: 1) Where do religious rules in your life hinder mercy instead of serving it? 2) How does Jesus' appeal to David challenge proof-texting without context? 3) What does it mean to live under the Lord of the Sabbath rather than under Sabbath anxiety?

Significance

Jesus claimed authority over the Sabbath itself, grounding that claim in Scripture and in God's desire for mercy, and so set the pattern for his Sabbath controversies.
