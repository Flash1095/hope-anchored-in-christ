Joshua's Southern Campaign

Story
Joshua's Southern Campaign
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Joshua 10:28-43
Retelling
After the victory at Gibeon, Joshua turned south city by city. He took Makkedah and struck its king and all its people with the sword, leaving none remaining. The same pattern followed at Libnah, at Lachish, where the king of Gezer came to help and was also defeated, at Eglon, at Hebron, and at Debir. In each city Joshua utterly destroyed every person, as the LORD God of Israel had commanded, and he struck the whole hill country, the south, the lowland, and the slopes, with all their kings. Then he returned with all Israel to the camp at Gilgal, having taken the entire southern region in a single campaign.
Discussion
The southern campaign shows the conquest as sustained obedience rather than a single miracle. Gibeon was the sign; Makkedah to Debir was the steady work that followed it. Reflect: 1) What does faithful follow-through look like after a dramatic victory? 2) How do you read passages of total destruction honestly, as judgment rather than as a model for our wars? 3) Where has God asked you for steady obedience rather than one great act?
Significance
The southern campaign completed in the field what began at Gibeon, bringing the hill country and the south under Israel's control in one continuous campaign of judgment.
