The Man of God from Judah

Story
The Man of God from Judah
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
1 Kings 13
Retelling
By the word of the LORD, a man of God came from Judah to Bethel while Jeroboam stood by the altar to burn incense. He cried against the altar, 'O altar, altar, thus saith the LORD; Behold, a child shall be born unto the house of David, Josiah by name; and upon thee shall he offer the priests of the high places that burn incense upon thee, and men's bones shall be burnt upon thee.' He gave a sign: the altar would be rent and its ashes poured out. When Jeroboam stretched out his hand and ordered the man seized, his hand dried up so that he could not pull it back, and the altar was rent as the man had said. Jeroboam begged the man to entreat the LORD, his hand was restored, and the king offered him a reward, but the man refused, for the LORD had commanded him to eat no bread, drink no water, and return by another way. On the road home, an old prophet of Bethel, who had heard what the man had done, rode after him, found him sitting under an oak, and lied to him, claiming an angel had told him to bring him back to eat and drink. The man of God believed him, returned, ate, and drank, and the word of the LORD came to him as he sat at table: because he had disobeyed the commandment of the LORD, his carcase would not come to the sepulchre of his fathers. On the way home a lion met him and slew him, but the lion did not eat the body or tear the ass, and the old prophet, finding them standing together, brought the man's body back and buried him in his own grave, saying, 'Alas, my brother.'
Discussion
The man of God obeyed God before a king and disobeyed him before a fellow prophet's lie, and the lion that killed but did not devour him became the sign that his death was judgment, not accident. Reflect: 1) Why is a lie from a religious voice harder to resist than a threat from a king? 2) How do you test a message that claims angelic authority against God's plain command? 3) What does the old prophet's grief suggest about the cost of misleading others?
Significance
The man of God from Judah condemned Jeroboam's altar, then died on the road home for disobeying the LORD's own command at another prophet's lying word.
