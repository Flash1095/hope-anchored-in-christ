The Rich Fool

Story
The Rich Fool
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Luke 12:16-21
Retelling
When a man asked Jesus to make his brother divide an inheritance with him, Jesus refused to be their arbitrator and warned the crowd to guard against all covetousness, for life does not consist in the abundance of possessions. He told of a rich man whose land produced plentifully. With no room to store it all, he said to himself, I will tear down my barns and build larger ones, and there I will store all my grain and goods. Then he said to his soul, Soul, you have ample goods laid up for many years; relax, eat, drink, be merry. But God said to him, Fool, this night your soul is required of you, and the things you have prepared, whose will they be? So is the one who lays up treasure for himself and is not rich toward God.
Discussion
The fool's failure was not farming but soliloquy: he talked only to himself, planned only for himself, and died the night his barns were full. Wealth promised years it could not deliver. Reflect: Who are you talking to in your financial plans — yourself, or God? What barn are you building larger right now? What does being 'rich toward God' require you to do with this year's surplus?
Significance
The rich fool warns that possessions cannot secure life, and that treasure kept for self alone is folly before God.
