The Good Shepherd

Story
The Good Shepherd
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
John 10:1-21
Retelling
Jesus told the Pharisees that whoever does not enter the sheepfold by the door but climbs in another way is a thief and a robber, while the shepherd enters by the door, calls his own sheep by name, and leads them out, going before them, and they follow because they know his voice. When his hearers did not understand, he explained: I am the door of the sheep; whoever enters through me will be saved and find pasture. The thief comes only to steal, kill, and destroy; I came that they may have life, and have it abundantly. I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep, unlike the hired hand who flees when the wolf comes. He knows his own and is known by them, and he has other sheep not of this fold, whom he must also bring, so there will be one flock and one shepherd.
Discussion
The shepherd is known by two marks: his sheep know his voice, and he lays down his life for them. Hired hands perform; shepherds die. Reflect: Whose voice are you actually following — the shepherd's, or a hired hand's? What does it mean to be known by name, not just counted in a flock? How does the promise of 'other sheep' widen your view of who belongs?
Significance
The good shepherd discourse presents Jesus as the door to salvation and the shepherd who dies for his sheep, gathering one flock from many folds. Doubt: This is an extended metaphorical discourse rather than a narrated past event, so its standing as a story row is debated.
