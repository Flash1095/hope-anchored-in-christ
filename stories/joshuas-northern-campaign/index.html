Joshua's Northern Campaign

Story
Joshua's Northern Campaign
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Joshua 11
Retelling
When Jabin, king of Hazor, heard of Israel's southern victories, he sent word to the kings of the north, and a vast host gathered at the waters of Merom, with horses and chariots in great number. The LORD told Joshua not to fear them, for by the next day he would deliver them all slain before Israel, and Joshua was to hamstring their horses and burn their chariots. Joshua fell on them suddenly at Merom, and the LORD delivered them into Israel's hand; they were pursued to Sidon and to the valley of Mizpeh until none remained. Joshua then turned back and took Hazor itself, its king, and burned the city with fire, for Hazor had been the head of all those kingdoms. So Joshua took the whole land, and the land had rest from war.
Discussion
The northern campaign answers the largest army Israel had yet faced with the same command as the first: do not fear, for the battle is the LORD's. Joshua's obedience even extended to destroying the captured horses and chariots, refusing to trust in the weapons of his enemies. Reflect: 1) What 'horses and chariots' are you tempted to keep as security? 2) How does sudden obedience differ from delayed obedience? 3) What does rest after war suggest about the goal of God's judgments?
Significance
The victory at Merom and the fall of Hazor broke the northern coalition and brought the conquest of Canaan to its close, leaving the land at rest from war.
