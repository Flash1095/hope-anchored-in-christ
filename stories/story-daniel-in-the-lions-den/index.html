Daniel in the Lions' Den

Story
Daniel in the Lions' Den
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Daniel 6; Hebrews 11:33
Retelling
Daniel was an exile who rose to the top of the Persian empire because of his integrity, and jealous officials plotted against the one thing they could attack: his prayer life. They tricked King Darius into signing a law that for thirty days no one could pray to anyone but the king, on pain of the lions' den. Daniel went home, opened his windows toward Jerusalem, and prayed three times a day just as he always had. Thrown to the lions, he spent the night unharmed — God shut the lions' mouths — and at dawn the king found him alive and threw his accusers in instead.
Discussion
Daniel's courage was built in private before it was tested in public: he did not start praying when the law passed; he simply kept praying as he always had. Faithfulness in small, unseen habits prepares you for the lions' den. Reflect: 1) What private habit of faith sustains you? 2) Where does pressure tempt you to hide your faith? 3) How does Daniel's calm trust challenge your anxiety?
Significance
Decades of quiet faithfulness prepared Daniel for one public night of testing. His story anchors hope that God honours open, steady devotion — in Babylon, and in any culture that pressures believers to hide it.
