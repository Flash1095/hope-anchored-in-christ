Paul Addresses the Jerusalem Crowd

Story
Paul Addresses the Jerusalem Crowd
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Acts 22:1-21
Retelling
Standing on the stairs, Paul asked the chief captain for leave to speak to the people, and beckoning with the hand, he addressed them in the Hebrew tongue: Men, brethren, and fathers, hear ye my defence which I make now unto you. When they heard that he spake in the Hebrew tongue, they kept the more silence. He told them he was a Jew, born in Tarsus, brought up in Jerusalem at the feet of Gamaliel, taught according to the perfect manner of the law of the fathers, zealous toward God as they all were that day, and that he had persecuted this way unto the death, binding and delivering into prisons both men and women, as the high priest and all the estate of the elders could bear witness, and had received letters to Damascus to bring those in bonds to Jerusalem to be punished. He told of the journey at midday, when suddenly there shone from heaven a great light round about him, and he fell to the ground and heard a voice saying, Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me? And he answered, Who art thou, Lord? And the voice said, I am Jesus of Nazareth, whom thou persecutest. Those with him saw the light and were afraid, but heard not the voice. He asked, What shall I do, Lord? And the Lord said, Arise, and go into Damascus, and there it shall be told thee of all things which are appointed for thee to do. Being blinded by the glory of that light, he was led by the hand into Damascus. There Ananias, a devout man according to the law, having a good report of all the Jews which dwelt there, came and said, Brother Saul, receive thy sight, and the same hour he looked up upon him. Ananias said, The God of our fathers hath chosen thee, that thou shouldest know his will, and see that Just One, and shouldest hear the voice of his mouth, for thou shalt be his witness unto all men of what thou hast seen and heard. And now why tarriest thou? Arise, and be baptized, and wash away thy sins, calling on the name of the Lord. Paul told how, when he had returned to Jerusalem and was praying in the temple in a trance, he saw Jesus saying, Make haste, and get thee quickly out of Jerusalem, for they will not receive thy testimony concerning me. He answered that they knew he had imprisoned and beaten believers, and had consented to Stephen's death and kept the raiment of them that slew him. And Jesus said, Depart, for I will send thee far hence unto the Gentiles.
Discussion
Paul's defence is his own story told in the crowd's own tongue: a Pharisee's training, a persecutor's zeal, a light at noon, Ananias's welcome, and a commission. The crowd listens in silence until one word, Gentiles, and then the shouting resumes. Reflect: 1) Why does Paul tell his conversion as a defence rather than arguing his case? 2) What does Ananias's role show about how God uses ordinary believers in great conversions? 3) Where does your own story meet the hearers' prejudice, and what word stops their listening?
Significance
The crowd speech turns Paul's arrest into a witness: his conversion, retold from the stairs in Hebrew, becomes the defence of the gospel itself, and the crowd's breaking point reveals that the Gentile mission is the true charge against him.
