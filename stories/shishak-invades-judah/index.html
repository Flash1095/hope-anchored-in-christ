Shishak Invades Judah

Story
Shishak Invades Judah
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Chronicles 12; 1 Kings 14:25-28
Retelling
In the fifth year of Rehoboam, because Judah had forsaken the law of the LORD, Shishak, king of Egypt, came up against Jerusalem with twelve hundred chariots, sixty thousand horsemen, and a people without number from Egypt, Libya, Sukkim, and Ethiopia. He took the fenced cities of Judah and came to Jerusalem. Then Shemaiah the prophet came to Rehoboam and the princes, who had gathered at Jerusalem for fear of Shishak, and said, 'Thus saith the LORD, Ye have forsaken me, and therefore have I also left you in the hand of Shishak.' The princes and the king humbled themselves and said, 'The LORD is righteous.' When the LORD saw that they humbled themselves, he said they would not be destroyed, but would be given some deliverance, and Shishak would not pour out his wrath on Jerusalem, though they would become his servants, to know the difference between serving God and serving foreign kings. Shishak took the treasures of the house of the LORD and the king's house, including the shields of gold Solomon had made, and Rehoboam made shields of brass in their place, which the guard carried when the king went to the house of the LORD.
Discussion
Shishak's invasion is the first foreign humiliation of the divided kingdom, and it came with a lesson: Judah would learn what it meant to serve a foreign king after refusing to serve God. Humility did not recover the gold shields, but it saved the city. Reflect: 1) How does God use foreign pressure to teach what disobedience costs? 2) What brass shields, cheaper substitutes, have you accepted after losing the gold? 3) Why did humbling themselves save Jerusalem but not restore its treasures?
Significance
Shishak's invasion stripped Solomon's gold shields from Jerusalem, but Rehoboam's humility saved the city from destruction.
