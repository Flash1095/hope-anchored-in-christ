Simon of Cyrene Carries Jesus' Cross

Story
Simon of Cyrene Carries Jesus' Cross
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 27:32; Mark 15:21; Luke 23:26-32
Retelling
As the soldiers led Jesus out to be crucified, they seized a man named Simon from Cyrene, who was coming in from the country, and put the cross on him to carry behind Jesus. Mark names him as the father of Alexander and Rufus. A large number of people followed, including women who mourned and wailed for him, and Jesus turned to them and said, Daughters of Jerusalem, do not weep for me; weep for yourselves and for your children, warning of days when people would say blessed are the childless, and would call on the mountains to fall on them. If people do these things when the tree is green, he asked, what will happen when it is dry? Simon, a passer-by pressed into service, walked the road to Calvary carrying the cross behind Jesus.
Discussion
Simon did not volunteer; he was seized and made to carry a condemned man's cross, and following Jesus behind him became the literal picture of discipleship. Reflect: 1) What cross have you been pressed to carry that you did not choose? 2) How does Jesus' warning to the weeping women reframe pity into repentance? 3) Where does following behind Jesus, rather than ahead of him, change your direction?
Significance
A bystander from Cyrene was compelled to carry Jesus' cross, and on the way Jesus warned Jerusalem's mourners that judgment, not mere pity, was what the city needed.
