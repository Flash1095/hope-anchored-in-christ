Solomon's Dream at Gibeon

Solomon's Dream at Gibeon

Retelling

Solomon made an alliance with Pharaoh, king of Egypt, and took Pharaoh's daughter as wife, bringing her to the city of David until he had finished building his house, the house of the LORD, and Jerusalem's wall. Then Solomon went to Gibeon to sacrifice, for that was the great high place, and offered a thousand burnt offerings on the altar there. At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night and said, 'Ask what I shall give thee.' Solomon answered by remembering God's mercy to David his father, confessing that he was but a little child who did not know how to go out or come in, and asking for an understanding heart to judge God's great people and to discern between good and bad. The speech pleased the LORD, who said that because Solomon had asked for wisdom rather than long life, riches, or the lives of his enemies, he would give him a wise and understanding heart such as none before or after him would have, and would also give him riches and honour he had not asked for, so that no king would be like him all his days, with long life added if he walked in God's ways as David had. Solomon awoke, and behold, it was a dream, and he returned to Jerusalem and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings before the ark and made a feast for all his servants.

Discussion

Solomon's wisest moment was admitting he was a child who did not know how to lead. God rewarded the request for discernment with everything Solomon had not asked for. Reflect: 1) If God asked you tonight what you wanted, what would you actually answer? 2) How does admitting you do not know how to lead change the way you lead? 3) What gifts have come to you precisely because you asked for something else?

Significance

At Gibeon, Solomon's request for an understanding heart won him wisdom, riches, and honour, and set the terms for his whole reign.
