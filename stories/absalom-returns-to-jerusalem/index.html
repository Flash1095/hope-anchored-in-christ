Absalom Returns to Jerusalem

Story
Absalom Returns to Jerusalem
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Samuel 14
Retelling
Joab, seeing that David's heart was toward Absalom, sent a wise woman from Tekoa to David with a parable: she claimed to be a widow whose two sons had fought in the field, one killing the other, and whose family now demanded the survivor's life, which would quench her last coal. David swore the woman's son would be safe, and she turned the parable on him, asking why he, who had sworn to protect a guilty son, still kept his own banished son away. David recognised Joab's hand in the story and told Joab to bring Absalom back, but for two full years Absalom lived in Jerusalem without seeing the king's face. At last Absalom, desperate to be seen, set Joab's barley field on fire to force a meeting. Joab came, and Absalom said he would rather have stayed in Geshur than live in Jerusalem unseen, and asked Joab to plead for him: if there was iniquity in him, let the king kill him. Joab told David, David called Absalom, and Absalom bowed with his face to the ground before the king, and the king kissed Absalom.
Discussion
Absalom's return is reconciliation without repentance: a clever parable, a kiss, and no confession from either father or son. The embrace closes the chapter but resolves nothing, and rebellion follows. Reflect: 1) What is the difference between a real reconciliation and a formal kiss? 2) How does avoiding hard truth for two years make a later explosion more likely? 3) When has a clever story helped you see your own situation more clearly?
Significance
Absalom's return to Jerusalem brought a kiss but no repentance, leaving father and son formally reconciled and inwardly estranged.
