The Quail and the Craving at Kibroth-hattaavah

Story
The Quail and the Craving at Kibroth-hattaavah
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Numbers 11:4-15; Numbers 11:31-35
Retelling
The mixed multitude among Israel fell a lusting, and Israel wept again, asking who would give them flesh to eat, and remembering the fish, cucumbers, melons, leeks, onions, and garlic of Egypt, while their soul was dried away and there was nothing but manna before their eyes. Moses, weighed down by carrying the whole people alone, asked God why he had laid this burden on him. God promised flesh, not for a day but for a whole month, until it came out of their nostrils and became loathsome, because they had despised the LORD who was among them. A wind from the LORD brought quails from the sea and let them fall around the camp, a day's journey on every side. The people gathered quails all day, all night, and all the next day, but while the flesh was still between their teeth, the LORD smote them with a very great plague. The place was called Kibroth-hattaavah, the graves of lust, because there they buried the people who lusted.
Discussion
Craving rewrites the past: Egypt's slavery becomes a menu, and manna becomes nothing. God gives what is demanded, and the gift itself becomes judgment. Reflect: 1) How does nostalgia for Egypt distort your memory of bondage? 2) What does it mean to despise the LORD by despising his provision? 3) Why does Kibroth-hattaavah keep its warning name?
Significance
The quail at Kibroth-hattaavah shows craving turning God's gift into judgment, and gives the wilderness its most sobering place-name: the graves of those who lusted.
