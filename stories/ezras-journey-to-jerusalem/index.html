Ezra's Journey to Jerusalem

Story
Ezra's Journey to Jerusalem
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Ezra 8
Retelling
After Artaxerxes gave Ezra a letter authorising his return, Ezra gathered the heads of families at the river Ahava and found no Levites among them. He sent for ministers for the house of God from Casiphia, and by the good hand of God they brought Sherebiah and Hashabiah with their brethren, Levites who could serve. At Ahava, Ezra proclaimed a fast, that they might afflict themselves before God and seek of him a right way for themselves, their little ones, and all their substance. He was ashamed to ask the king for soldiers and horsemen to protect them, because he had told the king that God's hand was upon all who sought him for good. So they fasted and besought God, and he was intreated of them. Ezra then weighed out the silver, gold, and vessels for the house of God into the hands of twelve priests, charging them, 'Ye are holy unto the LORD; the vessels are holy also; and the silver and the gold are a freewill offering unto the LORD God of your fathers. Watch ye, and keep them, until ye weigh them before the chief of the priests and the Levites in the chambers of the house of the LORD at Jerusalem.' They departed from Ahava on the twelfth day of the first month, and the hand of God was upon them, delivering them from the enemy and from ambush by the way. They came to Jerusalem on the first day of the fifth month, rested three days, and weighed the treasure in the house of God, where it was found complete, and offered burnt offerings to the God of Israel.
Discussion
Ezra's journey pairs fasting with responsibility: he refused a royal escort because he had boasted of God's protection, and then he weighed every talent of treasure into priests' hands and weighed it again in Jerusalem. Faith and accounting went together. Reflect: 1) When has your own testimony made you refuse an easy safeguard? 2) How does fasting before a journey change the journey itself? 3) What treasure has God entrusted to you that must arrive weighed and complete?
Significance
Ezra's journey to Jerusalem brought the exiles and the temple treasure safely home by fasting, prayer, and careful stewardship, without a royal escort.
