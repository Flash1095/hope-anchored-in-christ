The Burning Bush

The Burning Bush

Retelling

Moses was eighty years old, tending sheep in the wilderness, a fugitive who had failed as a prince and settled for obscurity. Then he saw a bush burning without being consumed, and when he turned aside to look, God called his name and told him to take off his sandals, for he stood on holy ground. God had seen His people's suffering in Egypt and was sending Moses — stammering, reluctant Moses — to lead them out. Moses offered every excuse he could think of, and God answered each one, giving him a brother to speak for him and signs to prove he was sent.

Discussion

God calls the unlikely, the reluctant, and the past-their-prime — and His answer to 'I can't' is 'I will be with you.' Your excuses are not news to God, and they are not disqualifiers. Reflect: 1) What excuse do you most often give God? 2) How does God's name — 'I AM' — speak to your situation? 3) What would change if you believed God's presence, not your ability, is what qualifies you?

Significance

God heard the cries of His suffering people and came down to deliver them. The burning bush anchors hope that God sees, knows our names, and can use even reluctant, ordinary people.

Calling; God's Holiness; Deliverance
