Diotrephes Refuses the Brethren

Story
Diotrephes Refuses the Brethren
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
3 John 1:1-14
Retelling
The elder writes unto the wellbeloved Gaius, whom he loves in the truth, rejoicing greatly because brethren came and testified of the truth that is in him, even as he walketh in the truth, and because of his hospitality toward the brethren and strangers, whom he has brought forward on their journey after a godly sort. John writes that he wrote unto the church, but Diotrephes, who loveth to have the preeminence among them, receiveth us not. Wherefore, if John comes, he will remember his deeds which he doeth, prating against them with malicious words, and not content therewith, neither doth he himself receive the brethren, and forbiddeth them that would, and casteth them out of the church. Beloved, John writes, follow not that which is evil, but that which is good. He that doeth good is of God, but he that doeth evil hath not seen God. Demetrius hath good report of all men, and of the truth itself, yea, and John and his companions also bear record, and Gaius knows that their record is true.
Discussion
Three men stand in one short letter: Gaius, who receives travelling brethren; Diotrephes, who loves preeminence, refuses the elder, talks maliciously, and casts out those who would receive; and Demetrius, who has a good report from all. The occasion is a church's hospitality, and the issue is who rules it. Reflect: 1) How does love of preeminence show itself in a church today? 2) Why is receiving travelling brethren a test of truth, not just of manners? 3) What does it mean to follow good rather than evil when the evil is done by a church leader?
Significance
Diotrephes's refusal is the Epistles' clearest picture of a leader who puts himself above the church: hospitality refused, dissenters cast out, and the elder answered with a commendation of Gaius and Demetrius instead.
