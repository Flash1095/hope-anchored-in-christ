Elisha's Last Prophecy to Joash

Retelling

When Elisha was fallen sick with the sickness of which he died, Joash, king of Israel, came down to him, wept over his face, and cried, 'O my father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof.' Elisha told him to take bow and arrows, and when the king had taken them, Elisha put his hands on the king's hands and told him to open the east window and shoot. Elisha said, 'The arrow of the LORD's deliverance, and the arrow of deliverance from Syria: for thou shalt smite the Syrians in Aphek, till thou have consumed them.' Then he told Joash to take the arrows and smite upon the ground. Joash smote three times and stayed. Elisha was angry and said, 'Thou shouldest have smitten five or six times; then hadst thou smitten Syria till thou hadst consumed it: whereas now thou shalt smite Syria but thrice.' Joash did smite Syria three times and recovered the cities of Israel, as Elisha had said.

Discussion

Elisha's last prophecy was acted, not just spoken: a shot arrow, arrows struck on the ground, and a king whose half-hearted obedience limited his own victory. Three strikes meant three victories, not total deliverance. Reflect: 1) How does half-hearted obedience limit the victory God intended? 2) What arrows have you stopped striking too soon? 3) Why does a dying prophet's word still carry authority for a king?

Significance

Elisha's last prophecy to Joash gave Israel three victories over Syria, limited by the king's three half-hearted strikes on the ground.
