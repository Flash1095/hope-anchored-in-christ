The Road to Emmaus

Story
The Road to Emmaus
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Luke 24:13-35; Mark 16:12-13
Retelling
On the first day of the week, two disciples were walking to Emmaus, about seven miles from Jerusalem, talking about all that had happened. Jesus himself came up and walked along with them, but they were kept from recognising him. He asked what they were discussing, and Cleopas answered, Are you the only one visiting Jerusalem who does not know the things that have happened there? They told him about Jesus of Nazareth, a prophet powerful in word and deed, whom they had hoped was the one to redeem Israel, but who had been crucified, and how some women had astounded them that morning with a vision of angels saying he was alive. Then Jesus explained, beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, what was said in all the Scriptures concerning himself, though their hearts burned without yet recognising him. At Emmaus they urged the stranger to stay, and as he took bread, gave thanks, broke it, and gave it to them, their eyes were opened and they recognised him, and he disappeared from their sight. At once they returned to Jerusalem to tell the Eleven that the Lord had truly risen and had appeared to Simon.
Discussion
The risen Jesus walked seven miles unrecognised, opening the Scriptures before opening their eyes: understanding comes through the word before the breaking of bread. Reflect: 1) Where have you walked with Jesus unrecognised in disappointment? 2) How does Jesus' reading of Moses and the Prophets reshape what the Messiah had to suffer? 3) What makes hearts burn, and what sends witnesses hurrying back the same hour?
Significance
On the Emmaus road the risen Jesus taught two disappointed disciples from the whole of Scripture and was recognised in the breaking of bread, turning their flight from Jerusalem into an urgent return with the news.
