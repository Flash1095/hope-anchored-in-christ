The Death of John the Baptist

Story
The Death of John the Baptist
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 14:1-12; Mark 6:14-29
Retelling
John the Baptist had publicly rebuked Herod Antipas for marrying Herodias, his brother's wife, and Herod had him arrested and imprisoned. Herod respected John and feared the crowds, but Herodias nursed a grudge. At Herod's birthday banquet, Herodias's daughter danced and pleased him, and he rashly promised her anything she asked, swearing an oath before his guests. Prompted by her mother, she asked for John's head on a platter. Herod was sorry, but because of his oath and his guests he ordered it done. John was beheaded in prison, and his disciples came, took his body, buried it, and told Jesus.
Discussion
A prophet died because a king valued his oath and his image more than justice. John's courage cost him everything; Herod's cowardice cost him his conscience. Reflect: Where do you stay silent to keep the room comfortable? What oath, promise, or reputation is pressuring you toward a wrong choice? How does John's end steady you when faithfulness has no visible reward?
Significance
John's death marks the cost of prophetic truth-telling and closes the forerunner's ministry, as Jesus' own path toward rejection comes into view.
