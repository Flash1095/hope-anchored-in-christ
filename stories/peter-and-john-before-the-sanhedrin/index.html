Peter and John Before the Sanhedrin

Story
Peter and John Before the Sanhedrin
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Acts 4:1-22
Retelling
While Peter and John were still speaking to the people, the priests, the captain of the temple, and the Sadducees came upon them, grieved that they taught the people and preached through Jesus the resurrection from the dead. They laid hands on them and held them until the next day. The next day the rulers, elders, scribes, Annas, Caiaphas, John, Alexander, and others of the high priest's kindred gathered in Jerusalem and asked, By what power, or by what name, have ye done this? Peter, filled with the Holy Ghost, answered that if they were examined for the good deed done to the impotent man, by what means he was made whole, then let it be known that by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom they crucified and whom God raised from the dead, this man stood before them whole. Jesus was the stone set at nought by the builders which had become the head of the corner, and there was salvation in no other name under heaven given among men. When the council saw the boldness of Peter and John, perceived they were unlearned and ignorant men, and took knowledge that they had been with Jesus, and saw the healed man standing with them, they could say nothing against it. They commanded them to go aside, conferred, and threatened them that they speak no more in the name of Jesus. Peter and John answered, Whether it be right in the sight of God to hearken unto you more than unto God, judge ye: for we cannot but speak the things which we have seen and heard. The council further threatened them and let them go, finding no way to punish them because all men glorified God for what was done, for the man was above forty years old on whom this miracle of healing was shewed.
Discussion
This is the church's first trial, and its defence is a person, not an argument: a healed man standing there and a name that saves. Peter obeys God rather than men, but he does so without abuse or evasion. Reflect: 1) What evidence of Jesus do people see standing beside your words? 2) When human authority forbids speaking of Christ, where is the line between boldness and recklessness? 3) How does Peter's answer keep both respect and refusal together?
Significance
The gospel's first clash with authority establishes the principle the church has lived by ever since: when commanded to be silent about Jesus, believers must speak, because they cannot but speak what they have seen and heard.
