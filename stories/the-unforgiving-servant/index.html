The Unforgiving Servant

The Unforgiving Servant

Retelling

When Peter asked how often he must forgive his brother — as many as seven times? — Jesus answered, not seven times, but seventy times seven. Then he told of a king settling accounts whose servant owed him ten thousand talents, an unpayable fortune. Unable to pay, the servant begged for patience, and the king, moved with compassion, forgave the whole debt. But that same servant found a fellow servant who owed him a hundred denarii, a small sum, seized him by the throat, and had him thrown into prison until he paid. When the king heard, he summoned the servant and, angered that one forgiven so much had forgiven so little, handed him over to the jailers until he paid all. So, Jesus concluded, will the Father do to those who do not forgive their brother from the heart.

Discussion

The mathematics are the sermon: ten thousand talents against a hundred coins. Forgiven people who refuse to forgive prove they never grasped the first forgiveness. Reflect: What hundred-denarii debt are you strangling someone over? Have you truly received the ten-thousand-talent forgiveness? What would forgiving from the heart — not just from the lips — require this week?

Significance

The unforgiving servant warns that receiving God's immeasurable forgiveness obliges believers to forgive others, or forfeit it.
