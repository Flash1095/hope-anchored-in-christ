The Plague of Frogs

Story
The Plague of Frogs
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Exodus 8:1-15
Retelling
God told Moses to warn Pharaoh that if he refused to let Israel go, the LORD would smite all Egypt's borders with frogs. Aaron stretched out his hand over the waters, and frogs came up and covered the land of Egypt, invading houses, bedrooms, beds, ovens, and kneading troughs. The magicians did the same and brought up frogs as well. This time Pharaoh asked Moses to entreat the LORD to take the frogs away, promising to let the people go and sacrifice. Moses set a time, tomorrow, and at the appointed time the frogs died and were gathered in heaps, and the land stank. But when Pharaoh saw respite, he hardened his heart and did not let the people go.
Discussion
Frogs in every room turn a national contest into an unbearable domestic invasion, and Pharaoh's bargain collapses the moment relief comes. Reflect: 1) Why do promises made under pressure so often fail when pressure lifts? 2) What does Moses' 'tomorrow' show about trusting God's timing? 3) Where do you bargain with God instead of obeying him?
Significance
The frogs show God's judgment reaching into every Egyptian home, and expose a repentance that lasts only until the discomfort passes.
