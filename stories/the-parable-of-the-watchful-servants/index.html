The Parable of the Watchful Servants

Story
The Parable of the Watchful Servants
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Luke 12:35-48; Matthew 24:45-51
Retelling
Jesus said, Be dressed ready for service and keep your lamps burning, like servants waiting for their master to return from a wedding banquet, so that when he comes and knocks they can immediately open the door. It will be good for those servants whose master finds them watching when he comes; truly, he will dress himself to serve, have them recline at table, and come and wait on them. But if a servant says to himself, My master is taking a long time in coming, and begins to beat the other servants and to eat and drink and get drunk, the master will come on a day he does not expect and cut him to pieces, assigning him a place with the unbelievers. Matthew's parallel tells the same story as the faithful and wise servant put in charge of the household, who gives food at the proper time, contrasted with the wicked servant who abuses his charge. The one who knew the master's will and did not prepare will be beaten with many blows; the one who did not know, with few.
Discussion
Readiness is not anxiety but faithful work with lamps burning: the master rewards watchful servants by serving them himself. Reflect: 1) What does dressed ready and lamps burning look like in your daily work? 2) How does delay tempt servants to abuse rather than serve? 3) What charge has your Master left you that faithfulness means carrying out on time?
Significance
Disciples must live as servants with lamps burning for their master's return, faithful in their charge, because he will come at an hour they do not expect.
