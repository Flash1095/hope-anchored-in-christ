Asa's Victory over Zerah

Story
Asa's Victory over Zerah
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Chronicles 14:9-15
Retelling
Asa, king of Judah, had done what was good and right in the eyes of the LORD, removing foreign altars, high places, and images, and commanding Judah to seek the LORD and keep his law. Then Zerah the Ethiopian came out against Judah with a host of a thousand thousand men and three hundred chariots, and reached Mareshah. Asa went out against him and set his army in the valley of Zephathah, with five hundred and eighty thousand men. Asa cried to the LORD, 'LORD, it is nothing with thee to help, whether with many, or with them that have no power: help us, O LORD our God; for we rest on thee, and in thy name we go against this multitude. O LORD, thou art our God; let not man prevail against thee.' The LORD smote the Ethiopians before Asa and Judah, and they fled, and Judah pursued them to Gerar, taking much spoil, and smote all the cities around Gerar, for the fear of the LORD came upon them, and they returned to Jerusalem with exceeding great spoil.
Discussion
Asa's prayer is one of the finest in Scripture: help is nothing to God, whether with many or with those who have no power. A million-man army fled before a king who rested on God rather than on numbers. Reflect: 1) What overwhelming force in your life makes Asa's prayer your own? 2) How does resting on God differ from passivity? 3) Why does Asa's reform, removing idols, come before his victory?
Significance
Asa's victory over Zerah's million-man army answered a prayer that God's help does not depend on numbers, and brought Judah great spoil.
