The Great Commission

The Great Commission

Retelling

After his resurrection, Jesus met his disciples in Galilee and on the Mount of Olives and gave them their standing orders. All authority in heaven and on earth had been given to him, so they were to go and make disciples of all nations, baptising them in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey everything he had commanded. They were to preach repentance and forgiveness in his name to all nations, beginning at Jerusalem, and to wait there until clothed with power from on high. He promised to be with them always, to the end of the age, and that they would receive power when the Holy Spirit came upon them, and be his witnesses in Jerusalem, Judea, Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.

Discussion

The commission is bigger than evangelism alone: making disciples means baptising and teaching obedience, and its fuel is Jesus' authority and presence, not the disciples' competence. Reflect: Where is your witness stopping — at Jerusalem, at Samaria, or short of the ends of the earth? Are you making converts, or disciples who obey? How does 'I am with you always' change the task you are avoiding?

Significance

The Great Commission sends the church to all nations with Jesus' authority and presence, and it is the hinge between the resurrection appearances and the Acts mission. Note: its chapters overlap the live Resurrection and Ascension rows, but it is drafted as a distinct commissioning unit, not as another telling of either event.
