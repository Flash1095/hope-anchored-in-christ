The Sign to Ahaz

The Sign to Ahaz

Retelling

When Rezin king of Syria and Pekah king of Israel marched against Jerusalem, the heart of Ahaz king of Judah and his people was moved, as the trees of the wood are moved with the wind. The LORD sent Isaiah, with his son Shear-jashub, to meet Ahaz at the end of the conduit of the upper pool, on the highway of the fuller's field, with this word: Take heed, and be quiet; fear not, neither be fainthearted for the two tails of these smoking firebrands. Ahaz was told to ask a sign of the LORD his God, in the depth or in the height above, but he refused, saying, I will not ask, neither will I tempt the LORD. Isaiah answered that the Lord Himself would give a sign: Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel. Before the child knew to refuse the evil and choose the good, the land of the two kings Ahaz dreaded would be forsaken — and Assyria, the power Ahaz trusted instead, would come upon Judah.

Discussion

Ahaz was offered any sign he wanted and refused it, preferring his own alliance to God's word. Reflect: 1) When has fear pushed you to trust a human alliance rather than God? 2) Why is refusing a sign sometimes a form of unbelief, not humility? 3) How does God's giving a sign anyway show grace to a king who would not ask?

Significance

God gives His sign even to an unwilling king, and the sign points beyond Ahaz's crisis to a child whose name — God with us — outlasts every empire. Doubt: this is a narrative scene wrapped around an oracle, so the sign itself dominates the episode.
