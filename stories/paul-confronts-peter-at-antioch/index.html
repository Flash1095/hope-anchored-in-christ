Paul Confronts Peter at Antioch

Story
Paul Confronts Peter at Antioch
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Galatians 2:11-14
Retelling
Paul writes that when Peter was come to Antioch, he withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed. For before that certain came from James, Peter did eat with the Gentiles, but when they were come, he withdrew and separated himself, fearing them which were of the circumcision. And the other Jews dissembled likewise with him, insomuch that Barnabas also was carried away with their dissimulation. But when Paul saw that they walked not uprightly according to the truth of the gospel, he said unto Peter before them all, If thou, being a Jew, livest after the manner of Gentiles, and not as do the Jews, why compellest thou the Gentiles to live as do the Jews?
Discussion
This is the only apostolic confrontation narrated in the Epistles: Peter's withdrawal from Gentile tables, out of fear, pressures Gentiles to live as Jews, and Paul rebukes him publicly because the truth of the gospel is at stake. The letter gives no record of Peter's reply. Reflect: 1) Why does table fellowship become a gospel issue rather than a manners issue? 2) How does fear make even leaders act against what they believe? 3) When is a public rebuke right, and what does it cost a friendship?
Significance
The Antioch confrontation guards the gospel's freedom: if Peter's fear had stood, Gentiles would have had to become Jews to belong, and Paul's public challenge keeps the table, and the church, open.
