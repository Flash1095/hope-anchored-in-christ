Jephthah's Vow

Story
Jephthah's Vow
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Judges 11
Retelling
Jephthah was a mighty warrior, but he was the son of a prostitute, and his half-brothers drove him out of his father's house. When Ammon attacked Israel, the elders of Gilead begged him to come back and lead them, and he agreed, on condition that he would remain their head if God gave him victory. Before the battle he tried to reason with the king of Ammon, recounting how Israel had come to the land, but the king would not listen. Then Jephthah made a rash vow: if God delivered Ammon into his hands, whatever first came out of his house to meet him on his return would be the LORD's. He won a great victory, but his only child, his daughter, came out dancing to meet him. She asked for two months in the mountains to mourn that she would never marry, and afterwards, the text says, he did with her according to his vow.
Discussion
Jephthah's story is a warning against bargaining with God and against vows made in the heat of battle. God had not asked for this vow, and its cost fell on an innocent young woman. Reflect: 1) Have you ever tried to bargain with God instead of simply trusting him? 2) What promises should you refuse to make in anger or excitement? 3) How does this story shape the way you read vows and commitments in Scripture?
Significance
Jephthah's victory was real, but his rash vow turned triumph into tragedy, warning readers never to trade with God for what he already promises freely. Doubt: interpreters disagree over whether the vow ended in his daughter's death or in lifelong devoted service apart from marriage; the text states the vow was kept and she remained unmarried, and this draft does not settle the question.
