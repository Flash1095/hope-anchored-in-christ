The Fall

The Fall

Retelling

God placed Adam and Eve in a garden of abundance with one boundary: they must not eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. The serpent came with a question that planted doubt — did God really mean what He said? — and promised that eating would make them like God. Eve ate, Adam ate, and in that moment everything broke: shame replaced innocence, blame replaced trust, and humanity was sent from the garden. Yet even in judgment, God promised that the offspring of the woman would one day crush the serpent's head.

Discussion

Temptation rarely arrives as an obvious lie; it arrives as a doubt about God's goodness and a promise that disobedience will make us free. The Fall explains why the world feels broken — and why we cannot fix ourselves. Reflect: 1) What doubts about God's goodness do you wrestle with? 2) Where do you see the pattern of blame and shame in your own life? 3) How does God's first promise of rescue give you hope?

Significance

The Fall explains why the world is broken, but God's promise in Genesis 3:15 is the first whisper of the gospel. Hope is anchored here: God did not abandon fallen people, He came looking for them.
