Ibzan, Judge of Israel

Story
Ibzan, Judge of Israel
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Judges 12:8-10
Retelling
After Jephthah, Ibzan of Bethlehem judged Israel for seven years. He had thirty sons and thirty daughters: he sent his thirty daughters out in marriage and brought in thirty daughters from abroad for his sons. Then Ibzan died and was buried at Bethlehem. No enemy, battle, or specific deed is recorded in his account. His seven years fall between Jephthah and Elon in the closing list of judges, and his marriages joined his house to families beyond his own.
Discussion
Ibzan is remembered through his family alliances: sixty marriages binding his house to other families across Israel. In a tribal age, such bonds were a form of peace-making. Reflect: 1) How do family and friendship ties hold a divided people together? 2) What does seven years of quiet leadership suggest about God's work between crises? 3) Are you content to build bridges rather than win battles?
Significance
Ibzan's seven years of judgeship are remembered through his family alliances rather than any recorded deed. Doubt: the text records no enemy, battle, or specific deed, so this row preserves a notice of judgeship rather than a narrated deliverance.
