The Persistent Widow

Story
The Persistent Widow
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Luke 18:1-8
Retelling
Jesus told his disciples a parable to show they ought always to pray and not lose heart. In a certain city there was a judge who neither feared God nor respected man, and a widow who kept coming to him saying, Give me justice against my adversary. For a while he refused, but at last he said to himself that, though he feared neither God nor man, he would give her justice because she kept bothering him, lest she wear him out. Jesus drew the contrast: if even an unjust judge answers persistence, how much more will God bring justice for his elect who cry to him day and night? Will he delay long over them? He will give them justice speedily — though when the Son of Man comes, will he find faith on earth?
Discussion
The parable is an argument from the lesser to the greater: God is not the unjust judge, so persistence is not about wearing God down but about refusing to lose heart. Reflect: What justice or answer have you stopped praying for because of delay? Do you picture God as reluctant, needing persuasion? What does praying 'day and night' look like in your actual week?
Significance
The persistent widow teaches persevering prayer grounded in God's character, not in the hope of exhausting him.
