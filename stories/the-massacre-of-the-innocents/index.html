The Massacre of the Innocents

Story
The Massacre of the Innocents
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 2:16-18
Retelling
When Herod saw that the wise men had outwitted him, he flew into a rage and gave orders to kill all the boys in Bethlehem and its vicinity who were two years old and under, in accordance with the time he had learned from the wise men. The slaughter fulfilled Jeremiah's lament: a voice heard in Ramah, weeping and great mourning, Rachel weeping for her children and refusing to be comforted, because they are no more. Herod's cruelty could not reach the child he hunted, who was already safe in Egypt, but Bethlehem's families bore the cost of his fear.
Discussion
This is the darkest page of the nativity, and Scripture does not soften it: the Saviour's birth provoked murder, and innocent families grieved. Reflect: 1) How do you hold God's protection of Jesus alongside the grief of Bethlehem's mothers? 2) Where does power still answer fear with violence against the defenceless? 3) What comfort does Rachel's uncomforted weeping, held inside God's story, offer mourners?
Significance
Herod's massacre shows the world's violent resistance to the newborn king from the very beginning, and it anchors the Christmas story in real grief rather than sentiment.
