The Parable of the Barren Fig Tree

Story
The Parable of the Barren Fig Tree
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Luke 13:6-9
Retelling
Jesus told this parable: a man had a fig tree growing in his vineyard, and when he came looking for fruit on it he found none, so he said to the keeper of the vineyard, For three years now I've been coming to look for fruit on this fig tree and haven't found any. Cut it down! Why should it use up the soil? The keeper answered, Sir, leave it alone for one more year, and I'll dig around it and fertilise it. If it bears fruit next year, fine! If not, then cut it down. The parable is distinct from the later cursing of the fig tree: here the tree is given one more year of patient cultivation, not withered at once.
Discussion
Judgment is delayed by a gardener's plea for one more year of digging and fertilising: patience has a purpose, and the purpose is fruit. Reflect: 1) How does the keeper's intercession shape your prayers for fruitless people, including yourself? 2) What does one more year of cultivation look like practically? 3) Where does patience end and the axe fall, and who decides?
Significance
A fruitless fig tree is granted one more year of digging and fertilising before the axe, picturing God's patience that seeks fruit rather than immediate judgment.
