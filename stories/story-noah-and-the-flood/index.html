Noah and the Flood

Noah and the Flood

Retelling

The world had grown so violent and corrupt that God grieved over what He had made. But Noah found favor with God, and God told him to build an enormous ark, even though there was no sea in sight. Noah obeyed, gathering his family and pairs of every kind of animal, and when the rains came the waters covered the earth for forty days and nights. Months later the ark rested on dry ground, Noah sent out a dove that returned with an olive leaf, and God set a rainbow in the sky as the sign of His promise never again to flood the whole earth.

Discussion

Noah obeyed for years before he saw a single drop of rain. Faith often means building the ark before the flood — doing the right thing because God said so, not because the results are visible yet. Reflect: 1) What is one thing God has asked you to do that you have been delaying? 2) How does the rainbow remind you of God's faithfulness after judgment? 3) What does Noah's story teach about God's patience with a broken world?

Significance

The Flood holds judgment and mercy together: God takes sin seriously, yet He saves and starts again. The rainbow still preaches that God's covenant promises — not the storms — have the final word.
