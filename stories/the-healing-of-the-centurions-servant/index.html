The Healing of the Centurion's Servant

Story
The Healing of the Centurion's Servant
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 8:5-13; Luke 7:1-10
Retelling
In Capernaum, a Roman centurion whose servant lay paralysed and suffering sent Jewish elders to ask Jesus for help. They urged him, saying the centurion loved Israel and had built their synagogue. As Jesus drew near the house, the centurion sent friends to say he was not worthy for Jesus to enter under his roof, but only to speak the word, and his servant would be healed. He understood authority: as a man under authority himself, he knew a command was enough. Jesus marvelled at his faith, saying he had not found such faith in Israel, and promised that many would come from east and west to sit with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob in the kingdom, while the servant was healed that very hour.
Discussion
A Gentile soldier understood Jesus' authority better than many insiders, and his humility — 'I am not worthy' — became the model of faith. He trusted a word at a distance. Reflect: Do you need Jesus to come to your house, or can you trust his word from afar? Where does your sense of unworthiness keep you from asking at all? How does this outsider's faith challenge your assumptions about who believes?
Significance
The centurion's servant is healed by Jesus' word alone, and the episode opens the kingdom to Gentiles whose faith outshines Israel's.
