The Woman Caught in Adultery

The Woman Caught in Adultery

Retelling

Early one morning, as Jesus taught in the temple, the scribes and Pharisees dragged before him a woman caught in adultery and set her in the midst. They reminded him that Moses commanded such women to be stoned, and asked what he said, testing him so they could accuse him. Jesus bent down and wrote with his finger on the ground. When they pressed him, he straightened and said, Let him who is without sin among you be the first to throw a stone. He bent down and wrote again, and one by one, beginning with the eldest, the accusers slipped away until only Jesus and the woman remained. He asked where her accusers were and whether no one had condemned her. She said no one, Lord. He answered, Neither do I condemn you; go, and sin no more.

Discussion

Jesus refused both traps: he neither excused the sin nor joined the stoning. Mercy came first, and the command to leave sin followed it. Reflect: When are you the accuser, using someone else's sin to test Jesus? Do you offer grace that costs nothing and changes nothing, or grace that says 'go and sin no more'? How does being uncondemned change how you treat the condemned?

Significance

The episode displays Jesus' wisdom under testing and his pattern of grace that neither condemns nor excuses, but sends the sinner to a new life. Doubt: this passage is absent from the earliest surviving manuscripts of John's Gospel and appears in different places in later ones, so its original position in John is uncertain, though the story itself is ancient and widely received.

Mercy; Forgiveness; Hypocrisy; Grace
