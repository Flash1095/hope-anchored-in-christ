Nadab and Abihu

Story
Nadab and Abihu
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Leviticus 10
Retelling
On the day God's glory filled the tabernacle and fire consumed the offerings, Aaron's eldest sons, Nadab and Abihu, took their censers, put fire in them, and offered incense before the Lord, but with fire God had not commanded. Fire came out from the Lord and consumed them, and they died before him. Moses reminded Aaron that God had said he would be honoured as holy by those who draw near to him. Aaron held his peace. Their cousins carried their bodies outside the camp, and Aaron and his remaining sons were told not to mourn in the usual way, because the holiness of God's house came before even a father's grief, and the priesthood must not treat God's worship as common.
Discussion
Access to God is grace, and grace does not make holiness optional. Worship is not sincere improvisation; it is drawing near on God's terms, and Nadab and Abihu's story is severe because holiness is serious. Reflect: 1) How do you treat worship as common or casual? 2) What does unauthorised fire look like today? 3) How does God's holiness deepen, rather than diminish, your gratitude for access to him?
Significance
At the very start of the tabernacle priesthood, God struck down unauthorised worship, teaching Israel that nearness to a holy God is a gift governed by his command, not by priestly invention.
