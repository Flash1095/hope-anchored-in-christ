Sheba's Rebellion

Story
Sheba's Rebellion
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Samuel 20
Retelling
Just as David returned to Jerusalem after Absalom's rebellion, Sheba, son of Bichri, a Benjamite, blew a trumpet and cried, 'We have no part in David, neither have we inheritance in the son of Jesse: every man to his tents, O Israel.' All the men of Israel deserted David and followed Sheba, while the men of Judah stayed with their king from the Jordan to Jerusalem. David told Amasa, his new captain, to assemble Judah within three days, but Amasa delayed, so David sent Abishai with Joab's men after Sheba. At Gibeon, Joab met Amasa, greeted him as brother, took his beard as if to kiss him, and smote him in the belly with a sword, leaving him wallowing in blood in the highway until a man dragged his body aside and covered it. Joab then pursued Sheba to Abel-beth-maachah and besieged the city, building a siege mound against its wall. A wise woman of the city called out to Joab, asking why he would swallow up a mother city in Israel and destroy the LORD's inheritance, and proposed a bargain: if Sheba's head were thrown over the wall, the siege would end. The people of Abel cut off Sheba's head and threw it to Joab, the trumpet was blown, the army dispersed, and Joab returned to Jerusalem.
Discussion
Sheba's rebellion shows a kingdom still fractured after Absalom: one trumpet blast nearly undid David's restoration, and Joab's murder of Amasa poisoned the victory before it was won. Only a wise woman's negotiation saved a whole city. Reflect: 1) How quickly can a restored relationship fracture again? 2) What does Joab's kiss-and-kill of Amasa say about ambition dressed as loyalty? 3) How can one wise voice stop a siege that armies could not?
Significance
Sheba's rebellion nearly split the restored kingdom, and ended when the wise woman of Abel traded one rebel's head for her city's life.
