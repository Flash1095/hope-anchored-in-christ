The Lesson of the Fig Tree

Story
The Lesson of the Fig Tree
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 24:32-35; Mark 13:28-31; Luke 21:29-33
Retelling
As part of his teaching about the coming of the Son of Man, Jesus said, Now learn this lesson from the fig tree: as soon as its twigs get tender and its leaves come out, you know that summer is near. Even so, when you see all these things, you know that it is near, right at the door. Truly I tell you, he said, this generation will certainly not pass away until all these things have happened. Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will never pass away. Luke adds that he told them this parable about the fig tree and all the trees: when they sprout leaves, you can see for yourselves that summer is near. The lesson is a sign-reading illustration inside the Olivet teaching, distinct from the cursing of the fig tree, which was an acted event. Doubt: this is an illustrative saying within a discourse rather than a standalone story-parable, so its standing as a separate parable row is debatable.
Discussion
Leaves on the fig tree mean summer is near, and the signs Jesus named mean his coming is near: discernment reads the season instead of predicting the day. Reflect: 1) How does the fig tree teach readiness without date-setting? 2) What signs in your own life show a season changing? 3) What does it mean to trust words that will never pass away when heaven and earth will?
Significance
Learn from the fig tree: when its twigs get tender and leaves come out, summer is near, and so the signs Jesus named show his coming is near, right at the door. Doubt: this is an illustrative saying within a discourse rather than a standalone story-parable, so its standing as a separate parable row is debatable.
