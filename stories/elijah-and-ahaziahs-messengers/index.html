Elijah and Ahaziah's Messengers

Story
Elijah and Ahaziah's Messengers
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Kings 1
Retelling
After Ahab's death, Ahaziah, his son, reigned over Israel, and fell down through a lattice in his upper chamber at Samaria and was sick. He sent messengers to inquire of Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron, whether he would recover from his disease. The angel of the LORD told Elijah the Tishbite to meet the messengers and ask them, 'Is it not because there is not a God in Israel, that ye go to enquire of Baal-zebub?' Elijah declared that Ahaziah would not come down from his bed but would surely die. When the messengers reported this, Ahaziah sent a captain with fifty men to seize Elijah, who sat on a hill. The captain ordered, 'Thou man of God, the king hath said, Come down.' Elijah answered, 'If I be a man of God, then let fire come down from heaven, and consume thee and thy fifty.' Fire fell and consumed them. A second captain with fifty came with the same order and met the same fire. A third captain came, fell on his knees before Elijah, and begged him to spare his life and the lives of his fifty men, saying their predecessors had been consumed. The angel of the LORD told Elijah to go down with him and not be afraid, and Elijah went to the king and repeated the word: because Ahaziah had sent to inquire of Baal-zebub, as if there were no God in Israel, he would not recover but would die. So Ahaziah died according to the word of the LORD, and Jehoram reigned in his stead.
Discussion
Ahaziah's question revealed his theology: he looked for answers in Ekron because he acted as if there were no God in Israel. Elijah's fire was not a show of temper but a sign that the God of Israel still answered. Reflect: 1) Where do you look for answers when you are sick or afraid, and what does that reveal? 2) How does the third captain's humility change the outcome? 3) What does it mean to act as if there is a God in Israel?
Significance
Elijah's confrontation with Ahaziah's messengers showed that seeking Baal-zebub was itself the sin, and the king died by the word he refused to hear.
