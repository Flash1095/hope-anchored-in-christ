Ehud and Eglon

Ehud and Eglon

Retelling

After Israel again did evil, God strengthened Eglon, king of Moab, who joined with Ammon and Amalek and took the city of palm trees, oppressing Israel for eighteen years. When Israel cried out, God raised up Ehud, a left-handed man from Benjamin, as their deliverer. Ehud made a double-edged dagger about a cubit long and strapped it to his right thigh, where a guard would not expect a weapon. He brought tribute to Eglon, a very fat man, then sent his attendants away and returned alone, saying he had a secret message from God. When they were alone, Ehud drew the dagger with his left hand and struck Eglon down, locked the doors behind him, and escaped. He rallied Israel, seized the fords of the Jordan, and defeated Moab, giving the land rest for eighty years.

Discussion

God used an unlikely deliverer and an unexpected plan to free his people, reminding us that he is not limited by our weaknesses or by an enemy's size. Reflect: 1) What weakness of yours might God want to use rather than remove? 2) How do you respond when deliverance comes in an unexpected form? 3) What oppression or habit do you need God to break today?

Significance

Ehud's victory opened the age of the judges with a plain lesson: when Israel cried out, God raised a deliverer, however unlikely.
