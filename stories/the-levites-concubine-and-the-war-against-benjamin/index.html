The Levite's Concubine and the War against Benjamin

Story
The Levite's Concubine and the War against Benjamin
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Judges 19-21
Retelling
In a time when there was no king in Israel, a Levite living in the hill country of Ephraim went to Bethlehem to bring back his concubine. On the journey home they stopped for the night at Gibeah, in Benjamin, where no one would take them in except an old man. That night wicked men of the city surrounded the house and demanded the Levite; the old man and the Levite instead sent the concubine out to them, and she was abused through the night and found dead at the door by morning. The Levite cut her body into twelve pieces and sent one to each tribe, and all Israel was horrified. The tribes demanded that Benjamin hand over the guilty men, Benjamin refused, and a terrible civil war followed. Benjamin was nearly wiped out, and the other tribes then scrambled to find wives for the survivors so that a tribe of Israel would not be lost.
Discussion
This is one of the darkest passages in Scripture, and it is told without praise for anyone in it. It shows a society without godly leadership collapsing into cruelty, revenge, and more wrongs committed to fix the first. Reflect: 1) Why does Judges repeat, 'there was no king in Israel'? 2) How should we read a passage that describes evil without endorsing it? 3) What does this story say about where a culture goes when everyone does what is right in their own eyes?
Significance
The Gibeah outrage and the war that followed expose the bottom of the judges cycle: without God as king, Israel turned its violence on itself. Doubt: these appendix stories are undated and may belong earlier in the judges period than their position at the book's end suggests.
