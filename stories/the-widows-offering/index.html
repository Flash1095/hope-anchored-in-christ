The Widow's Offering

Story
The Widow's Offering
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Mark 12:41-44; Luke 21:1-4
Retelling
Sitting opposite the temple treasury, Jesus watched people putting money into the offering boxes. Many rich people put in large amounts, making a show of it. Then a poor widow came and put in two very small copper coins, worth almost nothing. Jesus called his disciples and told them she had put in more than all the others, for they gave out of their abundance, but she, out of her poverty, had put in everything she had — all she had to live on. No one praised her gift in the temple that day; only Jesus measured it, and his measure was not the size of the gift but the size of the sacrifice.
Discussion
God's accounting differs from the treasury's: the smallest gift was the largest because it cost everything. Generosity is measured by what remains, not by what is given. Reflect: Do you give from abundance while keeping security untouched? What would giving 'all she had to live on' mean in your budget — and what would it not mean? How do you honour quiet givers no one else notices?
Significance
The widow's offering redefines generosity as sacrifice, not amount, and commends total trust in God over impressive giving.
