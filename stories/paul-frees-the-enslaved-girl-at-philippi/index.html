Paul Frees the Enslaved Girl at Philippi

Paul Frees the Enslaved Girl at Philippi

Retelling

As Paul's company went to prayer, they were met by a damsel possessed with a spirit of divination, which brought her masters much gain by soothsaying. The same followed Paul and the missionaries, and cried, saying, These men are the servants of the most high God, which shew unto us the way of salvation. And this did she many days. But Paul, being grieved, turned and said to the spirit, I command thee in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her. And he came out the same hour.

Discussion

The girl's words were true, but Paul refuses a testimony from a spirit of divination and frees her in the name of Jesus Christ, ending her masters' profit from her. Deliverance here is both spiritual and economic: a person stops being a source of gain. Reflect: 1) Why would Paul silence a true statement from a wrong source? 2) How does freeing one exploited person threaten an entire business? 3) What does grief, rather than anger, tell you about Paul's motive?

Significance

The girl's deliverance shows the gospel confronting exploitation directly: a spirit is cast out, a trafficked gift ends, and the mission's next step is prison, because freedom has a price for those who profited from bondage.

Deliverance; Human Trafficking and Exploitation; Spiritual Conflict; Compassion
