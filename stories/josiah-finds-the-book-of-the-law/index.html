Josiah Finds the Book of the Law

Story
Josiah Finds the Book of the Law
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Kings 22-23; 2 Chronicles 34-35
Retelling
Josiah became king at eight, and in the eighteenth year of his reign he sent Shaphan the scribe to the temple for temple repairs. There Hilkiah the high priest found the book of the Law, and Shaphan read it before the king. When Josiah heard its words, he tore his clothes, at how far Judah had strayed. He sent to inquire of the LORD, and the prophetess Huldah answered that judgment was certain, but Josiah, whose heart was tender, would not see it. Josiah then gathered all Judah, read the whole book aloud in the temple, and made a covenant to walk after the LORD and keep his commandments. He removed idols, altars, and vessels of Baal from the temple and throughout the land, broke down the high places, defiled Topheth so no one would sacrifice children there, and kept a Passover such as had not been kept since the days of the judges.
Discussion
A forgotten book, found during repairs, reformed a nation, because one young king took its words personally instead of filing them away. Reform begins with reading and trembling. Reflect: 1) What has grown dusty or forgotten in your own reading of Scripture? 2) How does Josiah's tearing of his clothes challenge casual responses to God's word? 3) What idols or habits would a Josiah-like reform remove from your life?
Significance
Josiah's discovery of the Law sparked Judah's last great reform, turning a temple repair into national repentance, covenant renewal, and a Passover without precedent since the judges.
