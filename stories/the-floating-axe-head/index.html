The Floating Axe Head

The Floating Axe Head

Retelling

The sons of the prophets told Elisha that their dwelling was too small, and asked to go to the Jordan, cut beams, and build a larger place. Elisha agreed and went with them. As one man was cutting down a tree, the iron head of his borrowed axe flew off and fell into the water. He cried out in distress, because the axe was borrowed, and losing another man's tool would have left him in debt. Elisha asked where it had fallen, cut a stick, threw it in at that spot, and the iron axe head floated to the surface. Elisha told the man to take it up, and he reached out his hand and took it. A small, everyday disaster, a borrowed tool lost in a river, was met by the same God who parted the Jordan, in a miracle as quiet as it was complete.

Discussion

God cares about borrowed axe heads as well as battles and kings. The man was honest about a small loss, and Elisha treated it as worth a miracle. Reflect: 1) What small loss are you tempted to think is too trivial for prayer? 2) How does honesty about borrowed things reflect faith? 3) Where has God met you in an ordinary problem lately?

Significance

The floating axe head is Elisha's smallest miracle, and its point is large: no honest need is too small for God's attention.
