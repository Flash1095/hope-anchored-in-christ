Naaman Healed of Leprosy

Story
Naaman Healed of Leprosy
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Kings 5
Retelling
Naaman, Syria's army commander, was a great man, but a leper. A young Israelite girl, taken captive and serving Naaman's wife, said that if her master were with the prophet in Samaria, he would heal him. Naaman travelled to Israel with gifts and a letter from Syria's king, who tore his robes in panic. Elisha sent word that Naaman should come to him. When Naaman arrived, Elisha did not even come out, but sent a messenger telling him to wash seven times in the Jordan and his flesh would be restored. Naaman was furious: he had expected ceremony, and Damascus's rivers seemed better. His servants persuaded him to try it, and after seven dips his flesh became like a child's. He returned, confessed that there is no God in all the earth but in Israel, but Elisha refused his gifts. But Gehazi, Elisha's servant, ran after him, lied for silver and garments, and was struck with leprosy instead.
Discussion
Naaman wanted a healing that matched his importance; God gave him a muddy river and seven dips. Healing came when pride gave way to obedience, and the story's warning falls on Gehazi, who wanted the prophet's reward without the prophet's integrity. Reflect: 1) What simple command of God are you resisting because it seems too ordinary? 2) How does the captive girl's witness challenge the way you speak about God? 3) Where might greed be quietly following your obedience, as it did Gehazi?
Significance
Naaman's healing shows God's grace reaching a foreign general through a captive girl's word, and his cleansing comes by humble obedience, not by status or payment.
