Aaron's Rod Buds

Story
Aaron's Rod Buds
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Numbers 17
Retelling
After Korah's rebellion, God ordered a test to end the grumbling over Aaron's priesthood. Each tribe's leader brought a staff with his name on it, and Aaron's staff represented Levi. Moses laid all twelve staffs before the Lord in the tent of meeting overnight. In the morning, Aaron's staff alone had budded, blossomed, and produced ripe almonds. Moses brought out all the staffs so Israel could see, and God ordered Aaron's staff kept before the testimony as a sign against rebels, so that grumbling would cease and the people would not die by approaching holy things on their own terms. The budding staff confirmed, by life from dead wood, the priesthood God had chosen.
Discussion
God answered grumbling not with another argument but with a sign of life. A sign can settle what argument cannot. Chosen service is confirmed by God, not claimed by a faction. Reflect: 1) What grumbling in your life needs a sign of God's choice, not more debate? 2) How does life from dead wood picture God's calling? 3) Why keep a warning sign before the testimony?
Significance
A dead staff budding, blossoming, and bearing almonds confirmed Aaron's priesthood by God's own sign. Doubt: this is a sign-act confirming Korah's aftermath rather than a fully standalone narrative, so its status as a separate row is debatable.
