The Angels Who Sinned

Story
The Angels Who Sinned
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
2 Peter 2:4; Jude 6
Retelling
Canonical Scripture narrates no fall of angels as a story; it gives two brief statements, and this row retells only those. Peter writes: For if God spared not the angels that sinned, but cast them down to hell, and delivered them into chains of darkness, to be reserved unto judgment. Jude writes: And the angels which kept not their first estate, but left their own habitation, he hath reserved in everlasting chains under darkness unto the judgment of the great day. Neither passage says when the angels sinned, what their sin was, how many fell, or how the casting down happened. Both use the angels as an example in a warning: Peter, that God knows how to reserve the unjust unto the day of judgment, and Jude, that those who reject authority and speak evil of dignities follow a pattern of rebellion. Readers have often connected these verses with Genesis 6:1-4, where the sons of God took wives from the daughters of men, but neither Peter nor Jude says that this is the sin meant, and Genesis itself tells no angelic fall. The connection is an interpretation, not a statement of either passage, and it is not presented here as fact.
Discussion
What Scripture says is two sentences of judgment, and what tradition adds is a whole mythology of a primeval angelic rebellion. The honest reading keeps them apart: the passages assert that angels who sinned were not spared but are reserved for judgment, and they use that certainty to warn against false teachers. They do not narrate the sin. Reflect: 1) Why do both letters use the angels as a warning example rather than telling their story? 2) What is lost when an allusion is expanded into a full narrative? 3) How does the certainty of judgment for angels who sinned bear on human teachers who promise liberty while serving corruption?
Significance
The angels who sinned stand in Scripture as a warning, not a story: God did not spare angels who rebelled but reserved them in chains of darkness for judgment, and the same God will judge false teachers. Doubt: what the angels' sin was, when it happened, and whether Genesis 6:1-4 describes it, since neither passage says so and no canonical narrative of an angelic fall exists.
