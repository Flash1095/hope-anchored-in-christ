Absalom's Rebellion

Story
Absalom's Rebellion
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Samuel 15-18
Retelling
Absalom, David's handsome and popular son, stole the hearts of Israel by standing at the city gate, listening to people's cases, and hinting that the king had no one to hear them. After four years he asked leave to go to Hebron, then had himself proclaimed king there, and the conspiracy grew strong. David fled Jerusalem, leaving ten concubines to keep the house, while loyal friends, priests, and soldiers went with him. His counsellor Ahithophel joined Absalom, but David's friend Hushai stayed behind in the city and, by clever counsel, delayed Ahithophel's plan to strike at once, giving David time to escape across the Jordan. The armies met in the forest of Ephraim, where twenty thousand men died. Absalom, riding his mule, was caught by his long hair in an oak tree, and Joab killed him against David's orders. When the news reached David, he wept, crying, 'O my son Absalom, would God I had died for thee.'
Discussion
Absalom's rebellion grew from charm, grievance, and a father's failure to deal with wrongs in his own house. David's grief shows that even a just victory can break a parent's heart. Reflect: 1) How do small resentments grow into open rebellion in families and churches? 2) What does Hushai's loyal cunning say about serving a king in danger? 3) How do you grieve when someone you love chooses a destructive path?
Significance
Absalom's rebellion nearly destroyed David's kingdom from within, and David's lament over his rebellious son remains one of Scripture's most human cries of parental grief.
