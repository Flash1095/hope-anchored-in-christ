The Altar by the Jordan

Story
The Altar by the Jordan
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Joshua 22
Retelling
When the conquest was over, Joshua sent the Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh back across the Jordan to their inheritance, blessing them for keeping their pledge to fight for their brothers. On their way home they built a great altar by the Jordan, at the border of Canaan. When the rest of Israel heard of it, they gathered at Shiloh to go to war against them, fearing rebellion and idolatry like the sin at Peor. Phinehas the priest and ten princes were sent first to ask what they meant. The eastern tribes answered that the altar was not for sacrifice at all, but a witness between them and their brothers that they too worshipped the LORD, so that future generations could not say they had no part in him. The answer pleased Israel, and the war was averted.
Discussion
A near civil war was stopped by asking before attacking. The eastern tribes built a witness, not a rival altar, and Israel's leaders investigated rather than assuming the worst. Reflect: 1) When have you assumed rebellion where there was only misunderstanding? 2) What witnesses do you leave for the next generation that you belong to God's people? 3) How do you handle a brother whose loyalty you doubt?
Significance
The altar by the Jordan became a witness of shared faith rather than a cause of civil war, because Israel asked, listened, and believed the explanation.
