The Parable of the Mustard Seed

The Parable of the Mustard Seed

Retelling

Jesus asked what the kingdom of God is like, and answered with the smallest of seeds. The kingdom is like a grain of mustard seed, which a man took and sowed in his field. Though it is the smallest of all seeds, when it grows it becomes larger than all the garden plants and becomes a tree, so that the birds of the air come and nest in its branches. A movement that began with a handful of Galilean followers, despised and unimpressive, would grow beyond all expectation into shelter for the nations. The parable answers the disappointment of small beginnings with the promise of disproportionate growth.

Discussion

The kingdom does not arrive impressive; it grows. Small, hidden, and slow is not the same as failing. Reflect: What small beginning are you despising in your life or church? Are you measuring God's work by its present size or by its promised growth? Who is finding shelter in the branches of something you planted small?

Significance

The mustard seed promises that the kingdom's tiny, unimpressive beginning will grow into shelter for the nations.
