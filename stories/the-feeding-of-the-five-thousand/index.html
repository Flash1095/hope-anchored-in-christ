The Feeding of the Five Thousand

The Feeding of the Five Thousand

Retelling

A crowd of five thousand men, plus women and children, had followed Jesus into a remote place, and evening was coming with no food in sight. Philip calculated that even a year's wages could not buy enough bread, but Andrew brought a boy who had five barley loaves and two small fish. Jesus took the loaves, gave thanks, and broke them, and the disciples distributed food until everyone had eaten their fill — and then gathered twelve baskets of leftovers. The crowd wanted to make Him king by force, but Jesus withdrew: He had come to give a greater bread, His own life, for the world.

Themes

Provision; Compassion; Abundance

Discussion

Jesus took a child's small lunch and fed a multitude — and then pointed beyond full stomachs to Himself as the bread of life. What you offer God, however small, is never too small in His hands. Reflect: 1) What 'five loaves' do you have to offer God? 2) Where do you need to trust His provision instead of your calculations? 3) How does this miracle point to Jesus as more than a provider of things?

Significance

Little placed in Jesus' hands becomes more than enough. This miracle anchors hope for every 'not enough' — of money, strength, or time — surrendered to Him.
