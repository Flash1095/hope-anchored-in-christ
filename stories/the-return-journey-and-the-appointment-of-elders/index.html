The Return Journey and the Appointment of Elders

Story
The Return Journey and the Appointment of Elders
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Acts 14:21-28
Retelling
After preaching the gospel at Derbe and teaching many, Paul and Barnabas returned again to Lystra, and to Iconium, and to Antioch, confirming the souls of the disciples, and exhorting them to continue in the faith, and that we must through much tribulation enter into the kingdom of God. And when they had ordained them elders in every church, and had prayed with fasting, they commended them to the Lord, on whom they believed. And after they had passed throughout Pisidia, they came to Pamphylia. And when they had preached the word in Perga, they went down into Attalia, and thence sailed to Antioch, from whence they had been recommended to the grace of God for the work which they fulfilled. And when they were come, and had gathered the church together, they rehearsed all that God had done with them, and how he had opened the door of faith unto the Gentiles. And there they abode long time with the disciples.
Discussion
The mission does not end with converts but with churches: the apostles retrace their steps through the very cities that stoned and expelled them, strengthen the disciples, and appoint elders with prayer and fasting. Then they report back to the church that sent them. Reflect: 1) Why return to dangerous places instead of moving on to new ground? 2) What does appointing elders, rather than leaving converts to themselves, protect? 3) How does a mission report honour both God and the sending church?
Significance
The return journey completes the first mission: new churches are not left alone but confirmed, taught that tribulation is the road to the kingdom, given elders, commended to the Lord, and reported to Antioch as the door of faith opened to the Gentiles.
