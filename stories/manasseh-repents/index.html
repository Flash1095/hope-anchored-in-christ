Manasseh Repents

Story
Manasseh Repents
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Chronicles 33
Retelling
Manasseh became king of Judah at twelve and reigned fifty-five years, he undid his father Hezekiah's reforms. He rebuilt the high places, raised altars to Baal, worshipped the host of heaven, set carved images in the temple itself, practised sorcery, and, the text says, made his children pass through the fire. He shed much innocent blood, and would not listen to God's warnings. At last the captains of Assyria took him with hooks, bound him in chains, and carried him to Babylon. There, in his distress, Manasseh humbled himself greatly before the God of his fathers and prayed. God was entreated by him, heard his supplication, and brought him back to Jerusalem and his kingdom. Then Manasseh knew that the LORD was God. He removed the foreign gods and the idol from the temple, repaired the altar, and offered peace offerings and thank offerings, and commanded Judah to serve the LORD, though the high places were not fully removed.
Discussion
Manasseh's repentance is the Bible's most extreme picture of grace: a king who did every evil named in the Law was heard when he humbled himself in chains. No past, however long, is beyond prayer. Reflect: 1) What does Manasseh's story say about the limits of grace, or the lack of them? 2) How does his return to Jerusalem differ from a mere escape from trouble? 3) What unfinished reforms in your own life still need attention after repentance?
Significance
Manasseh's repentance in Babylon shows that even the worst king of Judah could be heard when he humbled himself, and his return changed the last years of his reign. Doubt: this repentance account appears only in Chronicles, not in Kings, where Manasseh's reign is told without it.
