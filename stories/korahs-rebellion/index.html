Korah's Rebellion

Story
Korah's Rebellion
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Numbers 16; Jude 1:11
Retelling
Korah, a Levite, gathered Dathan, Abiram, and 250 respected leaders and accused Moses and Aaron of exalting themselves, claiming the whole congregation was holy. Moses answered that God himself had chosen Aaron's priesthood and challenged the rebels to offer incense before the Lord, letting God show whom he had chosen. The next morning God's glory appeared, the ground opened beneath Korah, Dathan, and Abiram and swallowed their households, and fire consumed the 250 men offering incense. Eleazar gathered their bronze censers and hammered them into a covering for the altar, a lasting warning against seizing a priesthood God had not given.
Discussion
Korah's claim sounded democratic and spiritual, but it rejected God's appointed order. Grasping for a role God has not given harms the whole community, and the congregation suffered for a claim it applauded. Reflect: 1) Where do you confuse wanting a role with being called to it? 2) How can ambition be tested honestly before God? 3) What warning does the altar covering still carry?
Significance
God confirmed Aaron's priesthood by judgment, not by vote, and turned the rebels' censers into a memorial, so Israel would remember that holy service is received by God's choice, not seized by human claim.
