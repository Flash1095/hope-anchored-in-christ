David's War with Ammon

Retelling

When Nahash, king of Ammon, died, David sent servants to comfort his son Hanun, remembering the kindness Nahash had shown him. But Hanun's princes persuaded him that David's servants were spies come to search out the city, so Hanun seized them, shaved off half of each man's beard, cut their garments to the buttocks, and sent them away in shame. When David heard, he told them to stay at Jericho until their beards had grown, for they were greatly ashamed. The Ammonites, knowing they had made themselves odious to David, hired twenty thousand Syrian footmen and the kings of Maacah and Tob to help them. Joab, seeing battle set against him both before and behind, divided his army, giving the Syrians to his brother Abishai and taking the Ammonites himself, with the charge, 'Be of good courage, and let us play the men for our people, and for the cities of our God: and the LORD do that which seemeth him good.' Israel drove back both armies, the Syrians fled, and the Ammonites fled into their city. When the Syrians regrouped under Hadadezer, David crossed the Jordan, fought them at Helam, and slew their captain Shobach with seven hundred chariots and forty thousand horsemen, so that all the kings who served Hadadezer made peace with Israel.

Discussion

A gesture of comfort became a war through suspicion: Hanun humiliated David's envoys, and Joab's courage carried the day on two fronts at once. Reflect: 1) How does suspicion turn goodwill into conflict in your relationships? 2) What does Joab's charge, leaving the outcome to God, teach about courage? 3) When has a small insult escalated into something far larger than anyone intended?

Significance

David's war with Ammon began with a humiliated embassy and ended with Syria suing for peace, after Joab fought on two fronts and left the outcome to God.
