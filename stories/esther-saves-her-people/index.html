Esther Saves Her People

Esther Saves Her People

Retelling

Esther was a Jewish orphan who became queen of Persia without anyone knowing her people. When the royal official Haman plotted to destroy all the Jews in the empire, Esther's cousin Mordecai urged her to speak to the king — though approaching him uninvited could mean death. Esther asked her people to fast for three days, then risked her life, saying, 'If I perish, I perish.' She exposed Haman's plot at a banquet, the king had Haman hanged on the gallows he built for Mordecai, and the Jews were saved. God's name never appears in the book, but His providence is on every page.

Themes

Courage; Providence; Deliverance

Discussion

Esther reminds us that God works through ordinary people in their ordinary positions — and that courage often means risking comfort for others. You may be exactly where you are 'for such a time as this.' Reflect: 1) Where has God placed you that you might be underestimating? 2) What would courage cost you right now? 3) How do you see God's hidden hand in your life, even when He seems silent?

Significance

God is never named in Esther, yet His fingerprints are on every page. For seasons when heaven seems silent, Esther anchors hope that God is working behind the scenes for His people's good.
