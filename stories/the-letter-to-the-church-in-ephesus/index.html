The Letter to the Church in Ephesus

Story
The Letter to the Church in Ephesus
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Revelation 2:1-7
Retelling
In a vision, John was told to write unto the angel of the church of Ephesus: These things saith he that holdeth the seven stars in his right hand, who walketh in the midst of the seven golden candlesticks. I know thy works, and thy labour, and thy patience, and how thou canst not bear them which are evil, and thou hast tried them which say they are apostles, and are not, and hast found them liars, and hast borne, and hast patience, and for my name's sake hast laboured, and hast not fainted. Nevertheless I have somewhat against thee, because thou hast left thy first love. Remember therefore from whence thou art fallen, and repent, and do the first works, or else I will come unto thee quickly, and will remove thy candlestick out of his place, except thou repent. But this thou hast, that thou hatest the deeds of the Nicolaitans, which I also hate. He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches. To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life, which is in the midst of the paradise of God.
Discussion
This is a vision-message, not a historical visit: Christ walks among the candlesticks and knows Ephesus's works, patience, and testing of false apostles, yet charges it with leaving its first love. Orthodoxy without love is the rebuke, and the remedy is to remember, repent, and do the first works. Reflect: 1) How can a church be right about false teachers and still lose its first love? 2) What are the first works in your own life? 3) What does it mean for a church's candlestick to be removed?
Significance
The Ephesian letter warns every orthodox church that labour and patience cannot replace love: remember, repent, and return to the first works, or the light itself is at stake, and the overcomer is promised the tree of life.
