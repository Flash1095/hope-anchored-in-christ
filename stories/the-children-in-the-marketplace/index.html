The Children in the Marketplace

The Children in the Marketplace

Retelling

Jesus asked, To what can I compare this generation? They are like children sitting in the marketplaces and calling out to others: We played the pipe for you, and you did not dance; we sang a dirge, and you did not mourn. For John came neither eating nor drinking, and they say, He has a demon. The Son of Man came eating and drinking, and they say, Here is a glutton and a drunkard, a friend of tax collectors and sinners. But wisdom is proved right by her deeds. The generation refused both John and Jesus, faulting the ascetic and faulting the feaster, and the brief marketplace picture exposes a refusal that no approach could satisfy. Doubt: this is a short simile rather than a story-parable with characters and plot, so its standing as a parable is debatable.

Discussion

A generation that would neither dance nor mourn faulted John for fasting and Jesus for feasting: refusal can disguise itself as criticism of style. Reflect: 1) Where do you fault God's messengers for opposite reasons and miss the message? 2) How does wisdom proved right by her deeds answer the marketplace critics? 3) What would dancing or mourning appropriately look like in response to Jesus?

Significance

This generation is like children in the marketplace who would neither dance to the pipe nor mourn to the dirge, rejecting both John's austerity and Jesus' feasting. Doubt: this is a short simile rather than a story-parable with characters and plot, so its standing as a parable is debatable.
