Job's Affliction and His Wife's Counsel

Story
Job's Affliction and His Wife's Counsel
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Job 2:1-10
Retelling
The adversary came again before the LORD, and the LORD pointed to Job, who still held fast his integrity despite being moved against him without cause. The adversary answered, Skin for skin, yea, all that a man hath will he give for his life — touch his bone and his flesh, and he will curse Thee to Thy face. The LORD allowed Job's body to be touched, only his life spared, and Job was struck with sore boils from the sole of his foot to his crown, so that he sat among the ashes scraping himself with a potsherd. His wife said to him, Dost thou still retain thine integrity? curse God, and die. Job answered, Thou speakest as one of the foolish women speaketh. What? shall we receive good at the hand of God, and shall we not receive evil? In all this Job did not sin with his lips.
Discussion
Job's suffering moved from his possessions to his body, and from strangers' blows to his own wife's counsel. Reflect: 1) How do you respond when those closest to you urge you to give up? 2) Is your trust in God steady when the pain is in your own body, not just your circumstances? 3) What does it mean to receive both good and evil from God's hand without charging Him foolishly?
Significance
Faithfulness is tested most sharply when suffering turns personal and the counsel around you turns bitter; Job held his integrity without pretending his pain was small. Doubt: Job is a poetic book and the historicity and genre of its prose frame are debated; only the prose frame is drafted here, not the poetic dialogues.
