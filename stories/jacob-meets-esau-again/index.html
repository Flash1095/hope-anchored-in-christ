Jacob Meets Esau Again

Story
Jacob Meets Esau Again
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Genesis 32:1-21; Genesis 33
Retelling
As Jacob went on his way, the angels of God met him, and he called that place Mahanaim, God's host. He sent messengers before him to Esau his brother in the land of Seir, the country of Edom, saying that he had sojourned with Laban until now and had oxen, asses, flocks and servants, and asking to find grace in Esau's sight. The messengers returned saying Esau was coming to meet him with four hundred men. Jacob was greatly afraid and distressed, divided his people, flocks, herds and camels into two bands so that one might escape if the other were attacked, and prayed to the God of Abraham and Isaac, confessing he was not worthy of the least of God's mercies, since he had crossed the Jordan with only his staff and now had become two bands, and pleading to be delivered from Esau's hand. That night he prepared a present for Esau: two hundred she goats and twenty he goats, two hundred ewes and twenty rams, thirty milch camels with their colts, forty kine and ten bulls, twenty she asses and ten foals, sent in separate droves with space between them, each servant to say the present was Jacob's and that Jacob himself was behind them. Then Jacob lifted up his eyes and saw Esau coming with four hundred men. He put the handmaids and their children foremost, Leah and her children after, and Rachel and Joseph hindermost, and passed over before them, bowing himself to the ground seven times until he came near to his brother. But Esau ran to meet him, embraced him, fell on his neck, kissed him, and they wept. Esau at first refused the present, saying he had enough, but Jacob urged him, saying he had seen Esau's face as though he had seen the face of God, and Esau took it. Esau offered to journey together and to leave some of his men with Jacob, but Jacob asked him to pass on before, saying he would lead on softly as the tender children and the flocks with young could endure, until he came to Esau in Seir. So Esau returned that day to Seir, and Jacob journeyed to Succoth, where he built a house and made booths for his cattle. From there he came to Shalem, a city of Shechem in the land of Canaan, pitched his tent before the city, bought a parcel of ground from the children of Hamor, Shechem's father, for a hundred pieces of money, and erected an altar there called El-elohe-Israel.
Key People
Jacob; Esau; Rachel; Leah
Themes
Forgiveness; Reconciliation; Humility; God's Providence
Discussion
Jacob prepared for the worst, prayed honestly about his fear, and then walked toward his brother in humility, and grace met him in an embrace he had not dared to expect. Reconciliation often begins before the meeting, in prayer and in a willingness to go first. Reflect: 1) Is there a broken relationship where fear is keeping you from taking the first step? 2) How does Jacob's prayer shape the way you bring your own fears to God? 3) What does Esau's welcome teach us about forgiveness that is freely given, not earned?

Significance
After twenty years apart, the brothers who had parted in anger met in tears and parted in peace. The reunion shows that God can heal even long-standing family wounds, and it brought Jacob safely home to Canaan.
