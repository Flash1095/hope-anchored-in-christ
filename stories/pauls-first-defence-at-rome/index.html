Paul's First Defence at Rome

Story
Paul's First Defence at Rome
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
2 Timothy 4:16-18
Retelling
Paul writes to Timothy from prison that at his first answer no man stood with him, but all men forsook him, and he prays that it may not be laid to their charge. Notwithstanding, he writes, the Lord stood with him, and strengthened him, that by him the preaching might be fully known, and that all the Gentiles might hear, and he was delivered out of the mouth of the lion. And the Lord shall deliver him from every evil work, and will preserve him unto his heavenly kingdom, to whom be glory for ever and ever. This is Paul's own first-person report of a hearing at Rome, written when he expects his course to be finished, and it is the only account of this defence: Acts ends before it, and no other passage describes the hearing itself.
Discussion
This is not a court transcript but a prisoner's report: deserted by all, strengthened by the Lord, delivered out of the mouth of the lion, and still confident of preservation unto the heavenly kingdom. The letter's context is Paul's expectation of death, which makes the confidence more striking, not less. Reflect: 1) What does it mean to be stood with by the Lord when all men forsake you? 2) How does Paul pray for those who deserted him rather than blaming them? 3) What is the difference between deliverance from death and deliverance unto the heavenly kingdom?
Significance
Paul's first defence, known only from this letter, closes his public witness: deserted at his hearing, strengthened to preach fully to the Gentiles, delivered once from the lion's mouth, and confident of a final deliverance into the heavenly kingdom.
