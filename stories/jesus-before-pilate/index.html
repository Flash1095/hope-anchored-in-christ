Jesus Before Pilate

Story
Jesus Before Pilate
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 27:11-26; Mark 15:1-15; Luke 23:1-25; John 18:28-19:16
Retelling
At daybreak the Jewish leaders bound Jesus and handed him to Pontius Pilate, the Roman governor, charging him with subverting the nation, opposing taxes to Caesar, and claiming to be Messiah, a king. Pilate asked, Are you the king of the Jews? Jesus answered, You have said so, and otherwise remained silent before the accusations, to Pilate's amazement. Pilate found no basis for a charge, and learning Jesus was a Galilean, sent him to Herod, who returned him. Pilate, warned by his wife's dream and convinced of Jesus' innocence, tried to release him under the Passover custom, but the chief priests stirred the crowd to demand Barabbas, a rebel and murderer, instead. Pilate asked what he should do with Jesus, the crowd shouted, Crucify him! Pilate washed his hands, declared himself innocent of this man's blood, and handed Jesus over to be flogged and crucified.
Discussion
Pilate knew Jesus was innocent and still handed him over to keep the crowd quiet: a verdict can be true and still be betrayed. Reflect: 1) Where does pressure from a crowd or a career tempt you to wash your hands of a true verdict? 2) How does Jesus' near-silence before Pilate speak louder than defence? 3) What does Barabbas' release say about substitution at the heart of the cross?
Significance
The Roman governor three times found no guilt in Jesus yet sentenced him to death under crowd pressure, and the guilty Barabbas walked free in his place.
