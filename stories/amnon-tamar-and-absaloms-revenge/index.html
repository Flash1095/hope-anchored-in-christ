Amnon, Tamar, and Absalom's Revenge

Story
Amnon, Tamar, and Absalom's Revenge
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Samuel 13
Retelling
Amnon, David's firstborn, fell sick with love for his half-sister Tamar, Absalom's sister, and his friend Jonadab advised him to feign illness and ask that Tamar come to feed him. When Tamar brought him food in his chamber, Amnon seized her, and though she pleaded with him not to force her, reminding him of the shame and folly of such a thing in Israel, he was stronger than she and violated her. Then Amnon hated her with a hatred greater than the love he had felt, and had her cast out and the door bolted behind her. Tamar, in her torn robe of a virgin princess with ashes on her head, cried aloud and went to her brother Absalom, who told her to hold her peace. David heard of it and was very angry, but did nothing. Absalom said nothing to Amnon, good or bad, but hated him for two full years. Then, at a sheep-shearing feast at Baal-hazor, Absalom ordered his servants to strike Amnon down when his heart was merry with wine, and they killed him. Absalom fled to Geshur, where he remained three years, and David mourned for Amnon every day, while his soul longed to go out to Absalom.
Discussion
This chapter contains no hero: Amnon's lust, David's inaction, and Absalom's long-planned revenge each compound the last wrong. Tamar's voice, pleading against folly and shame, is the chapter's moral centre, and it is ignored by every man in power. Reflect: 1) Why does David's anger without action make things worse? 2) How does unaddressed wrong harden into revenge over two years? 3) What does Tamar's story demand of communities that hear victims too late?
Significance
Amnon's crime, David's inaction, and Absalom's revenge set in motion the family tragedy that would become open rebellion.
