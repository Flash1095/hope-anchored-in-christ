Ezekiel's Divided Hair

Story
Ezekiel's Divided Hair
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Ezekiel 5
Retelling
The LORD told Ezekiel, Thou, son of man, take thee a sharp knife, take thee a barber's razor, and cause it to pass upon thine head and upon thy beard: then take thee balances to weigh, and divide the hair. Thou shalt burn with fire a third part in the midst of the city, when the days of the siege are fulfilled: and thou shalt take a third part, and smite about it with a knife: and a third part thou shalt scatter in the wind; and I will draw out a sword after them. Thou shalt also take thereof a few in number, and bind them in thy skirts. Then take of them again, and cast them into the midst of the fire, and burn them in the fire; for thereof shall a fire come forth into all the house of Israel. For thus saith the Lord GOD: This is Jerusalem: I have set it in the midst of the nations and countries that are round about her. And she hath changed My judgments into wickedness more than the nations. Therefore a third part of thee shall die with the pestilence, and with famine shall they be consumed in the midst of thee: and a third part shall fall by the sword round about thee; and I will scatter a third part into all the winds, and I will draw out a sword after them.
Discussion
Ezekiel shaved his head and divided the hair into thirds — burned, sword-struck, scattered. Reflect: 1) Why divide one man's hair to preach one city's fate? 2) What does the few hairs kept in the prophet's skirt say about the remnant? 3) How does a city set 'in the midst of the nations' fall further than the nations around it?
Significance
One head of hair, weighed and divided three ways, told Jerusalem its whole future: famine, sword, and scattering, with only a few kept. Doubt: this is a prophetic sign-act, a staged picture or lived sign, rather than an ordinary historical episode.
