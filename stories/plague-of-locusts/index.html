The Plague of Locusts

Story
The Plague of Locusts
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Exodus 10:1-20
Retelling
God told Moses to go to Pharaoh, for he had hardened Pharaoh's heart so that his signs might be shown. Moses warned that if Pharaoh refused, locusts would cover the face of the earth so that it could not be seen, and would eat what the hail had left, every tree and herb. Pharaoh's own servants pleaded with him, asking how long this man would be a snare, and urging him to let the men go before Egypt was destroyed. Pharaoh offered to let only the men go, and Moses refused to leave women, children, and livestock behind. Then an east wind brought locusts over all Egypt, very grievous, covering the land and eating every herb and tree. Pharaoh called Moses in haste, confessed sin, and asked forgiveness, and a mighty west wind carried the locusts into the Red Sea, leaving not one. But the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he did not let Israel go.
Discussion
Locusts finish what hail left, and even Pharaoh's court sees Egypt is destroyed. Yet Pharaoh again tries to divide the people, keeping children and flocks as hostages. Reflect: 1) Why does Pharaoh keep negotiating who may go? 2) What does Moses' refusal to leave anyone behind teach about wholehearted obedience? 3) How do you respond when those around you see the truth before a leader does?
Significance
The locusts strip Egypt bare and show Pharaoh bargaining over the people themselves, while Moses insists that deliverance must include families and flocks alike.
