Abram Rescues Lot

Story
Abram Rescues Lot
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Genesis 14
Retelling
In the days of Amraphel king of Shinar, Arioch king of Ellasar, Chedorlaomer king of Elam, and Tidal king of nations, these made war with Bera king of Sodom, and with Birsha king of Gomorrah, Shinab king of Admah, and Shemeber king of Zeboiim, and the king of Bela, which is Zoar. All these were joined together in the vale of Siddim, which is the salt sea. Twelve years they served Chedorlaomer, and in the thirteenth year they rebelled, and in the fourteenth year came Chedorlaomer and the kings that were with him, and they smote many peoples on their march, and the kings of Sodom and Gomorrah and their allies joined battle with them in the vale of Siddim, four kings with five. The vale of Siddim was full of slimepits, and the kings of Sodom and Gomorrah fled and fell there, and they that remained fled to the mountain. The victors took all the goods of Sodom and Gomorrah, and all their victuals, and went their way, and they took Lot, Abram's brother's son, who dwelt in Sodom, and his goods, and departed. One that had escaped came and told Abram the Hebrew, for he dwelt in the plain of Mamre the Amorite, brother of Eshcol, and brother of Aner, and these were confederate with Abram. When Abram heard that his brother was taken captive, he armed his trained servants, born in his own house, three hundred and eighteen, and pursued them unto Dan. He divided himself against them, he and his servants, by night, and smote them, and pursued them unto Hobah, which is on the left hand of Damascus. He brought back all the goods, and also brought again his brother Lot, and his goods, and the women also, and the people. The king of Sodom went out to meet him after his return from the slaughter of Chedorlaomer and of the kings that were with him, at the valley of Shaveh, which is the king's dale. Melchizedek, king of Salem, brought forth bread and wine, and he was the priest of the most high God. He blessed Abram, saying, Blessed be Abram of the most high God, possessor of heaven and earth, and blessed be the most high God, which hath delivered thine enemies into thy hand. And Abram gave him tithes of all. The king of Sodom said to Abram, give me the persons, and take the goods to thyself, but Abram said to the king of Sodom that he had lift up his hand unto the LORD, the most high God, the possessor of heaven and earth, that he would not take from a thread even to a shoelatchet, nor anything that was the king's, lest the king should say, I have made Abram rich, save only that which the young men had eaten, and the portion of the men which went with him, Aner, Eshcol, and Mamre, and let them take their portion.

Discussion
Abram risked his household to rescue Lot, then refused the king of Sodom's reward so that no one could say a king had made him rich, and gave tithes to Melchizedek, priest of the most high God. True victory leaves the glory with God. Reflect: 1) Who is a 'Lot' in your life that you could go after, even at cost to yourself? 2) When success comes, how do you keep the credit with God rather than with people? 3) What does Abram's tithe to Melchizedek teach about worship after a victory?

Significance
Abram's rescue of Lot shows a faith that fights for family and then refuses worldly reward, honouring God alone as the giver of victory — a pattern crowned by his meeting with Melchizedek, priest of the most high God.
