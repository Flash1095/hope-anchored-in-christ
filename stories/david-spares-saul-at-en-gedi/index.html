David Spares Saul at En Gedi

David Spares Saul at En Gedi

Retelling

Saul was hunting David with three thousand chosen men when he went alone into a cave at En Gedi to rest. Unknown to him, David and his men were hiding in the very back of that cave. David's men whispered that this was the day God had promised, when his enemy would be delivered into his hand. David crept forward and quietly cut off the corner of Saul's robe, but his conscience struck him even for that, and he refused to stretch out his hand against the LORD's anointed. When Saul left the cave, David called after him, bowed with his face to the earth, and showed the piece of cloth, proving he could have killed Saul and had not. Saul wept, admitted David was more righteous than he was, and acknowledged that David would surely be king.

Discussion

David had the perfect chance to seize the throne, and he refused it, because he would not take by murder what God had promised by anointing. Waiting on God's timing is often harder than fighting. Reflect: 1) What shortcut to a good thing are you tempted to take? 2) How does David's respect for 'the LORD's anointed' challenge the way you treat difficult leaders? 3) Where do you need to trust God's timing instead of forcing an outcome?

Significance

At En Gedi, David proved his kingship before he wore a crown: he would rather wait for God's timing than take the throne by killing God's anointed.
