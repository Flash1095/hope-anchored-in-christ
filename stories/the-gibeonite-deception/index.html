The Gibeonite Deception

The Gibeonite Deception

Retelling

When the kings west of the Jordan joined forces against Israel, the people of Gibeon chose a different plan. They sent men dressed as travellers from a far country, with worn-out clothes, patched sandals, and dry, mouldy bread, claiming to have heard of Israel's God and asking for a treaty. The leaders of Israel examined the provisions but did not ask counsel of the LORD, and they swore a covenant of peace with the strangers. Three days later they discovered the truth: the Gibeonites were neighbours, living within the land. Israel could not break its sworn oath, so the Gibeonites were spared, but they were made woodcutters and water carriers for the congregation and for the altar of the LORD.

Discussion

A decision made in a hurry, without prayer, can bind us for years. The Gibeonites were clever, but Israel's real failure was acting without asking God first. Reflect: 1) When are you most tempted to decide without praying? 2) How do you handle a promise you wish you had not made? 3) What does God's mercy to the Gibeonites say about outsiders who seek him?

Significance

The treaty with Gibeon shows both the danger of deciding without God's counsel and the seriousness of a sworn oath, even one gained by trickery.
