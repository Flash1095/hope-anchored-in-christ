Reuben and Gad Settle East of the Jordan

Story
Reuben and Gad Settle East of the Jordan
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Numbers 32
Retelling
The children of Reuben and Gad had a very great multitude of cattle, and when they saw the land of Jazer and Gilead, taken from Sihon and Og, they saw it was a place for cattle. They came to Moses, Eleazar, and the princes and asked for this land as a possession, saying they need not go over the Jordan. Moses answered sharply, asking whether their brothers should go to war while they sat there, and warning that discouraging Israel would repeat the sin of the spies at Kadesh. The tribes answered that they would build sheepfolds and cities for their families, then go armed before Israel until the land was conquered, and not return until every Israelite had his inheritance. Moses agreed: if they kept this promise, they would have the Transjordan as their possession, and half the tribe of Manasseh was settled with them. So Reuben, Gad, and half-Manasseh received the conquered kingdoms east of the Jordan, on condition that their warriors cross first.
Discussion
Reuben and Gad's request looks like settling short of the promise, and Moses hears it as another Kadesh. Their pledge to fight first turns a self-interested request into shared warfare. Reflect: 1) When does a reasonable request discourage others? 2) How does the tribes' pledge change Moses' judgment? 3) What does fighting first, before enjoying your own land, teach about community?
Significance
Reuben and Gad's settlement east of the Jordan fixes the Transjordan tribes on condition of first fighting for their brothers, binding possession to shared warfare.
