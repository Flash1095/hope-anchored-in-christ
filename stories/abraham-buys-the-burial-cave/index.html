Abraham Buys the Burial Cave

Date Approx
c. 1900 BC
Discussion
Abraham grieved openly for Sarah, yet he insisted on buying — not accepting as a gift — a permanent burial place in the land God had promised. A grave in Canaan was a stake in the ground for a promise he would not live to see fulfilled. Reflect: 1) How does Abraham's grief sit alongside his faith in God's promise? 2) What does his insistence on paying the full price show about honour and integrity? 3) What 'stake in the ground' might faith ask of you before you see the outcome?

Retelling
Sarah was an hundred and seven and twenty years old, and she died in Kirjath-arba, the same is Hebron in the land of Canaan. Abraham came to mourn for Sarah, and to weep for her. He stood up from before his dead and spoke unto the sons of Heth, saying he was a stranger and a sojourner with them, and asking them to give him a possession of a buryingplace, that he might bury his dead out of his sight. The children of Heth answered that he was a mighty prince among them, and that in the choice of their sepulchres he might bury his dead, for none of them would withhold his sepulchre from him. Abraham bowed himself to the people of the land, and asked them to intreat for him to Ephron the son of Zohar, that he might give him the cave of Machpelah, which was in the end of his field, for as much money as it was worth, for a possession of a buryingplace amongst them. Ephron the Hittite answered in the audience of the children of Heth, and of all that went in at the gate of his city, offering Abraham the field and the cave therein freely, in the presence of the sons of his people. Abraham bowed down himself before the people of the land, and said to Ephron that he would give money for the field, and asked him to take it, so that he might bury his dead there. Ephron answered that the land was worth four hundred shekels of silver, and asked what that was betwixt him and Abraham. Abraham hearkened unto Ephron, and weighed to him the silver which he had named in the audience of the sons of Heth, four hundred shekels of silver, current money with the merchant. The field of Ephron, which was in Machpelah, which was before Mamre, the field, and the cave which was therein, and all the trees that were in the field, in all the borders round about, were made sure unto Abraham for a possession, in the presence of the children of Heth, before all that went in at the gate of his city. After this, Abraham buried Sarah his wife in the cave of the field of Machpelah before Mamre, the same is Hebron in the land of Canaan. The field, and the cave that was therein, were made sure unto Abraham for a possession of a buryingplace by the sons of Heth.
Scripture
Genesis 23
Significance
The cave of Machpelah became the family's burial place in Canaan — the first piece of the promised land owned outright, holding Abraham's hope beyond his own lifetime.
