Job's Losses in a Single Day

Job's Losses in a Single Day

Retelling

Job was a blameless and upright man in the land of Uz, who feared God and turned away from evil, the greatest of all the men of the east. In the heavenly court the adversary asked whether Job feared God for nothing, pointing to the hedge God had put around him and his household, and the LORD allowed all Job had to be touched, only not Job himself. In a single day messengers came one after another: the Sabeans took his oxen and donkeys and killed his servants, the fire of God burned his sheep and servants, the Chaldeans took his camels and killed his servants, and a great wind struck the house where his sons and daughters were feasting, killing them all. Job tore his robe, shaved his head, fell to the ground and worshipped, saying, Naked came I out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return thither: the LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD.

Discussion

Job lost everything without an explanation, and still worshipped. Reflect: 1) What would remain of your faith if your blessings were taken away? 2) How do you speak to God when loss comes all at once? 3) What does Job's worship in grief teach you about who God is, apart from what He gives?

Significance

Faith is not a bargain for blessing; Job's worship when he had nothing left shows trust in God Himself, not in His gifts. Doubt: Job is a poetic book and the historicity and genre of its prose frame are debated; only the prose frame is drafted here, not the poetic dialogues.
