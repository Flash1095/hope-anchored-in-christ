Samson and Delilah

Story
Samson and Delilah
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Judges 13-16; Hebrews 11:32
Retelling
Samson was set apart from birth as a Nazirite, gifted with supernatural strength to begin delivering Israel from the Philistines. He tore a lion apart, killed a thousand men with a donkey's jawbone, and carried off the gates of Gaza — but he toyed with his calling, and with Delilah he toyed with his secret. Three times she asked where his strength lay and three times he lied, until at last he told her the truth: his uncut hair was the sign of his vow. While he slept she cut it, the Philistines seized and blinded him, but in his final act — praying for strength one last time — Samson brought down the temple of Dagon on his captors and himself.
Key People
Samson; Delilah; Manoah
Themes
Strength; Weakness; Repentance
Discussion
Samson's strength was never really his; it was God's gift, and he treated it as a toy until it was gone. Yet even after failure, God heard his final prayer. No one is beyond the reach of repentance. Reflect: 1) What gift or calling are you toying with? 2) Where does small compromise lead in your life? 3) How does Samson's ending give hope to someone who has failed badly?

Significance
Samson's wasted gifts are a warning, but his final prayer is pure hope: God heard a broken man in the dark. It is never too late to cry out to the God who restores.
