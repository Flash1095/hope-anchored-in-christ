Abimelech's Rise and Fall

Retelling

After Gideon's death, his son Abimelech went to Shechem, where his mother's family lived, and persuaded the leaders to make him king, arguing that one ruler was better than Gideon's seventy sons. With money from the temple of Baal-berith he hired reckless men, went to his father's house at Ophrah, and murdered all seventy of his brothers on a single stone. Only the youngest, Jotham, escaped, and from Mount Gerizim he told a fable: the trees asked the olive, the fig, and the vine to be king, and each refused, but the thornbush accepted, promising shade it could never give. Abimelech ruled for three years, but God set division between him and Shechem, the city rebelled, and in the fighting at Thebez a woman dropped an upper millstone from a tower and crushed his skull. Dying, he ordered his armour-bearer to kill him so that no one would say a woman had slain him.

Discussion

Abimelech shows what happens when leadership is seized by murder and paid for by idolatry: the thornbush king gives no real shade, and violence returns on the violent. Reflect: 1) What does Jotham's fable say about leaders who promise what they cannot give? 2) Where do you see ambition outrunning character today? 3) How does God judge cruelty even when it wears a crown?

Significance

Abimelech, Israel's first would-be king, is a cautionary tale: a crown won by murder cannot hold, and God repays treachery in kind.
