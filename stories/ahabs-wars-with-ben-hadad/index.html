Ahab's Wars with Ben-Hadad

Story
Ahab's Wars with Ben-Hadad
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
1 Kings 20
Retelling
Ben-hadad, king of Syria, gathered all his host, thirty-two kings with him, with horses and chariots, and besieged Samaria, demanding Ahab's silver, gold, wives, and children. Ahab first agreed, but when Ben-hadad demanded the right to search his house and take whatever pleased him, Ahab refused, and the elders of Israel backed him. A prophet told Ahab that the LORD would deliver the great Syrian multitude into his hand that day, so that he would know he was the LORD, and that the young men of the princes of the provinces would lead the attack. At noon, when Ben-hadad was drinking himself drunk in the pavilions with the thirty-two kings, the young men went out, each slew his man, and the Syrians fled, with Ben-hadad escaping on a horse. The next year Ben-hadad returned, advised that Israel's gods were gods of the hills and that Syria would win on the plain, and the two armies camped opposite each other for seven days. On the seventh day Israel slew one hundred thousand Syrian footmen in one day, and the rest fled to Aphek, where a wall fell on twenty-seven thousand of them. Ben-hadad hid in an inner chamber, and his servants, with sackcloth on their heads and ropes on their heads, came to Ahab begging for his life. Ahab called Ben-hadad his brother, made a covenant with him, and let him go, for which a prophet condemned him: because he had let go a man appointed to utter destruction, his life would go for that man's life.
Discussion
Twice God gave Ahab victories he had not earned, and twice the point was stated: that Ahab would know that he was the LORD. The second victory was squandered when Ahab treated God's appointed enemy as a brother and a business partner. Reflect: 1) How do you respond to victories you know you did not deserve? 2) When does mercy toward an enemy become disobedience to God? 3) What covenants have you made that traded away what God told you to hold?
Significance
Ahab's wars with Ben-hadad gave Israel two undeserved victories and one condemned peace, when Ahab spared the Syrian king God had appointed to destruction.
