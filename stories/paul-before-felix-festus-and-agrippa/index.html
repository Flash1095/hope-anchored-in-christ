Paul Before Felix, Festus, and Agrippa

Story
Paul Before Felix, Festus, and Agrippa
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Acts 24:1-27; Acts 25:1-27; Acts 26:1-32
Retelling
After his arrest in Jerusalem, Paul was held at Caesarea for two years of hearings. Before the governor Felix, Paul faced charges of stirring up riots and profaning the temple; he answered that he worshipped the God of his fathers, believing all the Law and the Prophets, and that his hope was the resurrection. Felix kept Paul in custody, hoping for a bribe. His successor Festus, wanting to please the Jewish leaders, proposed a trial in Jerusalem, but Paul, knowing the danger, appealed to Caesar. Before King Agrippa and Bernice, Paul told his whole story, from persecutor to the light on the Damascus road. Festus cried that much learning had driven him mad; Paul answered he spoke true and rational words, and asked Agrippa if he believed the prophets. Agrippa said, In a short time you would persuade me to be a Christian. All agreed he had done nothing deserving death, but his appeal meant he must go to Rome.
Discussion
Paul turned every courtroom into a pulpit, using trials to preach the resurrection to governors and kings. Two years of delay became two years of witness. Reflect: What delay in your life could become a platform for witness? Do you use your defence to clear yourself, or to preach Christ? How do you answer when someone says you almost persuade them?
Significance
Paul's Caesarean trials show the Gospel proclaimed before Roman power and explain, legally and theologically, how the apostle to the Gentiles reached Rome.
