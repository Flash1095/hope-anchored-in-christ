Saul Rescues Jabesh-Gilead

Story
Saul Rescues Jabesh-Gilead
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
1 Samuel 11
Retelling
Nahash the Ammonite came up and encamped against Jabesh-gilead, and the men of Jabesh asked for a covenant. Nahash answered that he would covenant only on condition that he thrust out all their right eyes, as a reproach upon all Israel. The elders asked for seven days' respite to send messengers through Israel. When the messengers came to Gibeah, Saul was coming after his oxen from the field, and the Spirit of God came upon him when he heard the news. He cut a yoke of oxen into pieces and sent them throughout Israel, saying whoever did not follow Saul and Samuel would have his oxen treated the same way, and the fear of the LORD fell on the people. Saul mustered three hundred thousand men, marched by night, and in the morning watch smote the Ammonites until the heat of the day, scattering them so that no two remained together. The people wanted to kill those who had questioned Saul's kingship, but Saul refused, saying the LORD had wrought salvation in Israel that day, and Samuel led them to Gilgal, where they renewed the kingdom before the LORD.
Discussion
Saul's first act as king was rescue, not revenge: he saved Jabesh-gilead and then spared his own critics. The Spirit's coming turned a farmer behind oxen into a deliverer by morning. Reflect: 1) What crisis in front of you needs action today rather than discussion? 2) How do you treat critics after a success? 3) What does it mean to renew a calling, as Israel renewed the kingdom at Gilgal?
Significance
Saul's rescue of Jabesh-gilead proved his kingship in battle and ended in mercy, with the kingdom renewed at Gilgal instead of settled by revenge.
