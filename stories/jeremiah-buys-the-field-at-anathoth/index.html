Jeremiah Buys the Field at Anathoth

Jeremiah Buys the Field at Anathoth

Retelling

In the tenth year of Zedekiah king of Judah, the eighteenth year of Nebuchadnezzar, when the king of Babylon's army besieged Jerusalem, Jeremiah was shut up in the court of the prison in the king of Judah's house, because he had prophesied that the city would be given into the hand of the king of Babylon. The word of the LORD came to him: Behold, Hanamel the son of Shallum thine uncle shall come unto thee, saying, Buy thee my field that is in Anathoth, for the right of redemption is thine to buy it. Hanamel came, just as the LORD had said, and Jeremiah knew it was the word of the LORD. He bought the field for seventeen shekels of silver, subscribed the evidence, sealed it, took witnesses, and gave the evidence of the purchase to Baruch the son of Neriah in the sight of Hanamel and of all the Jews who sat in the court of the prison. He charged Baruch to put both the sealed and the open evidence in an earthen vessel, that they might continue many days. Then Jeremiah prayed, and the LORD answered: Behold, I will bring upon this city all the evil I have pronounced, and I will gather them out of all countries and bring them again to this place — and fields shall be bought in this land again, for houses and fields and vineyards shall be possessed again in this land.

Discussion

With the enemy at the walls, Jeremiah bought a field he could not use — the strangest investment in the Bible. Reflect: 1) What does it mean to invest in a future you will not live to enjoy? 2) How does a sealed deed in an earthen jar preach hope during a siege? 3) Where is God asking you to act as if His promise were already true?

Significance

Buying land in a besieged city is hope made legal: judgment is real, but God will bring His people back to fields they can own again.
