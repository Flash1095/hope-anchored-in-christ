Jeremiah's Trial

Story
Jeremiah's Trial
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Jeremiah 26
Retelling
In the beginning of the reign of Jehoiakim, Jeremiah stood in the court of the LORD's house and warned all the cities of Judah that if they would not listen and walk in God's law, He would make Jerusalem a curse to all the nations of the earth, as He had made Shiloh. The priests, the prophets, and all the people seized him, saying, Thou shalt surely die, because thou hast prophesied that this house shall be like Shiloh and this city desolate. The princes of Judah heard, came up from the king's house, and sat in the entry of the new gate of the LORD's house. The priests and prophets pressed the charge, but Jeremiah answered, The LORD sent me to prophesy against this house and this city all the words that ye have heard. Therefore now amend your ways and your doings, and obey the voice of the LORD your God, and the LORD will repent Him of the evil that He hath pronounced against you. As for me, behold, I am in your hand: do with me as seemeth good and meet unto you. But know ye for certain, that if ye put me to death, ye shall surely bring innocent blood upon yourselves, and upon this city, and upon the inhabitants thereof. The princes and all the people said, This man is not worthy to die, for he hath spoken to us in the name of the LORD our God. Elders reminded them that Micah had prophesied the same in Hezekiah's days and Hezekiah had not killed him, and Ahikam the son of Shaphan stood by Jeremiah so that he was not given into the hand of the people to put him to death.
Discussion
Jeremiah was tried for his life in the Temple court for preaching against the Temple. Reflect: 1) How do you defend a hard word without defending yourself? 2) Why did the princes, not the priests, acquit Jeremiah — and what does that say about who listens? 3) What does 'I am in your hand' teach about courage that leaves the outcome to God?
Significance
A prophet on trial for his life rests his defence on one fact — the LORD sent me — and leaves his fate in his judges' hands rather than softening the word.
