War against Midian

Retelling

After Peor, the LORD told Moses to avenge Israel against the Midianites, and Moses sent a thousand from each tribe to war, with Phinehas son of Eleazar carrying the holy instruments and trumpets. Israel warred against Midian as commanded, slew all the males, including the five kings of Midian, Evi, Rekem, Zur, Hur, and Reba, and slew Balaam son of Beor with the sword. They took the women, children, cattle, and spoil, and burnt their cities. Moses was angry that the women who had caused Israel's sin at Peor had been kept alive, and commanded that the Midianite boys and the women who had known a man be slain, while young girls were kept alive. The spoil was then divided: half to the warriors and half to the congregation, with a tribute to the LORD, and the officers offered the gold they had taken as an offering, because not one man of their army was missing.

Discussion

The Midian war closes the Peor story in judgment and in accounting: Balaam, who could not curse Israel, dies by the sword, and the spoil is divided by rule, not by plunder. Reflect: 1) How does this war complete the judgment begun at Peor? 2) Why does Scripture record the exact division of spoil and the officers' offering? 3) How do you read a hard war text honestly without softening it?

Significance

War against Midian is the wilderness generation's last campaign, executing judgment on Midian for Peor, ending Balaam's story, and modelling spoil handled by God's rules rather than by greed.
