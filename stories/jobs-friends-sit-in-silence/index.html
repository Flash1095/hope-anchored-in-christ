Job's Friends Sit in Silence

Job's Friends Sit in Silence

Retelling

When Job's three friends heard of all the evil that had come upon him, they came, each from his own place: Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite. They had agreed together to come to mourn with him and to comfort him. When they lifted up their eyes from afar and did not recognise him, they wept, tore their robes, and sprinkled dust on their heads toward heaven. Then they sat down with him on the ground seven days and seven nights, and none spoke a word to him, for they saw that his grief was very great.

Discussion

Before any of them offered an answer, Job's friends simply sat with him for a week in silence. Reflect: 1) When have words made someone's grief worse, and silence helped? 2) How do you sit with a suffering friend without rushing to explain their pain? 3) What does the size of Job's grief — greater than any speech — teach you about the limits of our answers?

Significance

Presence can be the first mercy to a sufferer; Job's friends were at their best before they began to argue, when they shared his grief in silence. Doubt: Job is a poetic book and the historicity and genre of its prose frame are debated; only the prose frame is drafted here, not the poetic dialogues.
