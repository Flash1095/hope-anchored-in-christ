The Parable of the Wheat and the Weeds

Story
The Parable of the Wheat and the Weeds
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 13:24-30; Matthew 13:36-43
Retelling
The kingdom of heaven is like a man who sowed good seed in his field, but while everyone slept, his enemy came and sowed weeds among the wheat and went away. When the plants grew, the weeds appeared too, and the servants asked whether they should pull them up. The master refused: pulling the weeds would uproot the wheat as well, so let both grow together until the harvest. At harvest time the reapers would gather the weeds first, bind them for burning, and gather the wheat into the barn. Privately, Jesus explained that the sower is the Son of Man, the field is the world, the good seed are the children of the kingdom, the weeds are the children of the evil one, and the harvest is the end of the age, when the righteous will shine like the sun.
Discussion
The parable forbids the church's favourite shortcut: purifying the field by force before harvest. Good and evil grow together, and judgment belongs to the harvest, not to us. Reflect: Where are you tempted to pull up weeds — to purge, exclude, or settle accounts early? Can you live with a mixed field without calling evil good? How does harvest patience change your urgency?
Significance
The wheat and weeds teach patience with evil's persistence and reserve final separation for God's harvest at the end of the age.
