Render to Caesar

Story
Render to Caesar
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 22:15-22; Mark 12:13-17; Luke 20:20-26
Retelling
The Pharisees and Herodians, enemies who agreed on nothing else, joined forces to trap Jesus with a question about the poll tax: was it lawful to pay taxes to Caesar or not? If he said yes, he would lose the crowds who hated Rome; if no, he could be reported to Rome as a rebel. Jesus saw their hypocrisy and asked for a denarius. Whose image and inscription is this, he asked. Caesar's, they answered. Then he said, Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's. Amazed at the answer, they left him and went away, having caught nothing.
Discussion
Jesus refused the binary his questioners imposed and answered with a distinction that still governs Christian citizenship: the coin bears Caesar's image, but you bear God's. Reflect: Where are you being forced into a false either-or by people who want a slogan, not an answer? What in your life bears Caesar's image, and what bears God's? How do you honour lawful authority without worshipping it?
Significance
Render to Caesar gives the classic Christian distinction between civic duty and ultimate allegiance, which belongs to God alone.
