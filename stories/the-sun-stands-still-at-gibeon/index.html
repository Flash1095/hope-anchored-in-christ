The Sun Stands Still at Gibeon

The Sun Stands Still at Gibeon

Retelling

Because Gibeon had made peace with Israel, five Amorite kings joined together and attacked it. The Gibeonites sent an urgent message to Joshua at Gilgal, and Joshua marched all night with his army to their rescue. The LORD threw the enemy into confusion, and as they fled, great hailstones fell on them. Then, in the sight of Israel, Joshua prayed that the sun would stand still over Gibeon and the moon over the Valley of Aijalon, so that the day would be long enough to finish the battle. The sun stopped in the middle of the sky and did not hurry to go down for about a whole day. Israel won a complete victory, the five kings were captured and put to death, and the southern towns fell one after another.

Discussion

When God's people are in a battle he has called them to, he fights for them in ways beyond their strength. Joshua's bold prayer came from trusting that God keeps his promises. Reflect: 1) What battle are you facing that is bigger than your strength? 2) Do you pray boldly, or only for what seems possible? 3) How does this story change the way you see God's power over nature and history?

Significance

The long day at Gibeon is Scripture's most dramatic picture of God fighting for his people, lengthening a day so that his promise of the land could stand.
