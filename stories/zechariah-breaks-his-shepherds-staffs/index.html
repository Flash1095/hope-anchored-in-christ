Zechariah Breaks His Shepherd's Staffs

Story
Zechariah Breaks His Shepherd's Staffs
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Zechariah 11:7-17
Retelling
Zechariah fed the flock of the slaughter, whose possessors slew them and held themselves not guilty, and they that sold them said, Blessed be the LORD, for I am rich, and their own shepherds pitied them not. He took unto him two staves, the one he called Beauty, and the other he called Bands, and fed the flock. Three shepherds also he cut off in one month, and his soul loathed them, and their soul also abhorred him. Then said he, I will not feed you: that that dieth, let it die; and that that is to be cut off, let it be cut off; and let the rest eat every one the flesh of another. And he took his staff, even Beauty, and cut it asunder, that he might break his covenant which he had made with all the people. And it was broken in that day, and so the poor of the flock that waited upon him knew that it was the word of the LORD. And he said unto them, If ye think good, give me my price; and if not, forbear. So they weighed for his price thirty pieces of silver. And the LORD said unto him, Cast it unto the potter: a goodly price that I was prised at of them. And Zechariah took the thirty pieces of silver, and cast them to the potter in the house of the LORD. Then he cut asunder his other staff, even Bands, that he might break the brotherhood between Judah and Israel. And the LORD said, Take unto thee yet the instruments of a foolish shepherd. For, lo, I will raise up a shepherd in the land, which shall not visit those that be cut off, neither shall seek the young one, nor heal that that is broken, nor feed that that standeth still: but he shall eat the flesh of the fat, and tear their claws in pieces. Woe to the idol shepherd that leaveth the flock! the sword shall be upon his arm, and upon his right eye: his arm shall be clean dried up, and his right eye shall be utterly darkened.
Discussion
Zechariah broke two staffs and was paid thirty pieces of silver — the price of a shepherd, thrown to the potter. Reflect: 1) What do Beauty and Bands stand for, and what breaks when both are cut? 2) Why is thirty pieces of silver a 'goodly price' said with bitter irony? 3) How does the worthless shepherd at the end warn every leader who feeds himself instead of the flock?
Significance
A shepherd's broken staffs, a contemptible wage cast to the potter, and a worthless shepherd under woe together picture a flock abandoned by its leaders and a covenant broken. Doubt: this is a prophetic sign-act, a staged picture or lived sign, rather than an ordinary historical episode.
