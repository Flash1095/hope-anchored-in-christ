Enoch

Story
Enoch
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Genesis 5:21-24; Hebrews 11:5; Jude 1:14-15
Retelling
In the genealogy from Adam to Noah, Enoch stands out for what did not happen to him. He lived sixty-five years and became the father of Methuselah, then walked with God for three hundred years and had other sons and daughters. His life totalled three hundred and sixty-five years, yet unlike every other name in the list, his entry does not end with 'and he died.' Instead Scripture says Enoch walked with God, and he was not, for God took him. Hebrews adds that by faith Enoch was translated so that he should not see death, and Jude records that Enoch prophesied judgment against the ungodly.
Discussion
Enoch's whole story is four verses, yet it has shaped hope about life with God ever since. Walking with God is presented as an ordinary, long obedience, not a single dramatic deed. Reflect: 1) What does a long, quiet walk with God look like in your daily routine? 2) How does Enoch's translation encourage you about death? 3) Why does Scripture link Enoch's walk with his warning about judgment?
Significance
Enoch shows that a life spent walking with God ends not in mere death but in God's own taking, a first glimpse of life beyond the grave. Doubt: this is a four-verse genealogy notice, not a fully narrated story; it is included only because the task expressly required Enoch, and its brevity means it should be weighed as a borderline entry rather than a standard story row.
