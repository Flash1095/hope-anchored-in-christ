Israel Renews the Covenant

Israel Renews the Covenant

Retelling

On the twenty-fourth day of the seventh month, after the Feast of Tabernacles, the children of Israel assembled with fasting, sackcloth, and earth upon them, and separated themselves from all strangers. They stood and read in the book of the law of the LORD for a fourth part of the day, and for another fourth part they confessed and worshipped. The Levites stood on the stairs and cried with a loud voice to the LORD, and then led the people through the whole story of God's dealings: his choice of Abram, the Exodus, the giving of the law at Sinai, the manna and water in the wilderness, the conquest, and the long cycle in which Israel rebelled, God delivered them, and they rebelled again. The prayer confessed, 'Thou art just in all that is brought upon us; for thou hast done right, but we have done wickedly,' and yet ended with God's great mercies. Then the people made a sure covenant and wrote it, sealing it with the names of Nehemiah, the priests, the Levites, and the princes. In the covenant they pledged not to give their daughters to the peoples of the land nor take their daughters for their sons, to keep the sabbath and the holy days without buying or selling on them, to observe the sabbatical year and remit debts, and to bring the firstfruits, tithes, and wood offerings to the house of God, promising, 'We will not forsake the house of our God.'

Discussion

Covenant renewal in Nehemiah is history retold as confession: a quarter of the day reading the law, a quarter confessing, and then a written, sealed promise. The people did not merely feel sorry; they signed specific commitments about marriage, sabbath, debt, and temple support. Reflect: 1) How does retelling God's story change the way you confess your own? 2) What specific, signed commitments would your repentance require? 3) Why does the covenant end with the house of God rather than with personal resolve?

Significance

Israel's covenant renewal turned a day of fasting and confession into a sealed, specific pledge to keep the sabbath, support the temple, and forsake foreign marriages.
