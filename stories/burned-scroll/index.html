The Burned Scroll

The Burned Scroll

Retelling

In the fourth year of Jehoiakim, the word came to Jeremiah: Take thee a roll of a book, and write therein all the words that I have spoken unto thee against Israel, and against Judah, and against all the nations, from the day I spake unto thee, from the days of Josiah, even unto this day. Jeremiah called Baruch the son of Neriah, and Baruch wrote from the mouth of Jeremiah all the words of the LORD in the roll. Because Jeremiah was shut up and could not go into the house of the LORD, Baruch went on the fasting day and read the roll in the house of the LORD, in the chamber of Gemariah the son of Shaphan. Micaiah the son of Gemariah heard the words and declared them to the princes in the scribe's chamber. The princes sent Jehudi to fetch the roll, and Jehudi read it in their ears; when they heard all the words they were afraid, and told Baruch to go and hide himself and Jeremiah, and laid the roll up in the chamber of Elishama the scribe. They told the king, who was sitting in the winterhouse with a fire burning on the hearth before him. As Jehudi read three or four leaves, the king cut the roll with a penknife and cast it into the fire, until all the roll was consumed — and yet the king and his servants were not afraid, nor rent their garments, though Elnathan, Delaiah, and Gemariah made intercession that he would not burn the roll. The king commanded the arrest of Baruch and Jeremiah, but the LORD hid them. Then the word came to Jeremiah again: Take thee again another roll, and write in it all the former words that were in the first roll, which Jehoiakim hath burned — with this added: Jehoiakim shall have none to sit upon the throne of David, and his dead body shall be cast out in the day to the heat, and in the night to the frost.

Discussion

A king burned God's word leaf by leaf — and God's word came back longer. Reflect: 1) Why did the princes tremble at the scroll while the king cut it up calmly? 2) What do you do with a word from God you do not want to hear — hide it, argue it, or burn it? 3) How does the rewritten scroll show that destroying the message never destroys the Sender?

Significance

Burning the scroll did not silence the word; it came back rewritten, with judgment added for the king who cut it up.
