Isaiah Walks Naked and Barefoot

Isaiah Walks Naked and Barefoot

Retelling

In the year that Tartan came to Ashdod, when Sargon the king of Assyria sent him and he fought against Ashdod and took it, the LORD spoke by Isaiah the son of Amoz, saying, Go and loose the sackcloth from off thy loins, and put off thy shoe from thy foot. Isaiah did so, walking naked and barefoot. The LORD said that as His servant Isaiah had walked naked and barefoot three years for a sign and wonder upon Egypt and upon Ethiopia, so would the king of Assyria lead away the Egyptians prisoners and the Ethiopians captives, young and old, naked and barefoot, even with their buttocks uncovered, to the shame of Egypt. And the people of Judah, who had looked to Egypt and Ethiopia for help, would be afraid and ashamed of the very powers they had trusted.

Discussion

Isaiah preached for three years in the dress of a prisoner of war. Reflect: 1) Why would God make His prophet look like a captive to warn against trusting Egypt? 2) How do you react when God's warning embarrasses the respectable plans around you? 3) What alliances are you trusting that might be led away captive themselves?

Significance

Trust in foreign powers is exposed as shameful hope: the prophet wore the fate of Egypt so Judah could see where its trust was heading. Doubt: this is a prophetic sign-act, a staged picture or lived sign, rather than an ordinary historical episode.
