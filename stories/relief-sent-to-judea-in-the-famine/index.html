Relief Sent to Judea in the Famine

Relief Sent to Judea in the Famine

Retelling

In those days came prophets from Jerusalem unto Antioch. And there stood up one of them named Agabus, and signified by the Spirit that there should be great dearth throughout all the world, which came to pass in the days of Claudius Caesar. Then the disciples, every man according to his ability, determined to send relief unto the brethren which dwelt in Judaea. Which also they did, and sent it to the elders by the hands of Barnabas and Saul.

Discussion

A prophecy of famine becomes a collection, not a panic: Gentile believers at Antioch send help to Jewish believers in Judaea, each according to ability, carried by trusted hands. Generosity here is fellowship made practical. Reflect: 1) How does your church turn warnings about need into concrete help? 2) What does giving according to ability, rather than equally, require of each giver? 3) Why does it matter that Barnabas and Saul, not strangers, carried the gift?

Significance

The first recorded relief gift binds the young Gentile church to the Jewish mother church in practical love, and it gives Barnabas and Saul their first joint mission before they are sent out as missionaries.
