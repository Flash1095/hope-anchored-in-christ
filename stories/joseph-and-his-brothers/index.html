Joseph and His Brothers

Story
Joseph and His Brothers
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Genesis 37; 39-45
Retelling
Joseph was his father Jacob's favorite son, and his brothers hated him for it — for his coat of many colors and for his dreams of ruling over them. They sold him to traders bound for Egypt, told their father he was dead, and Joseph spent years as a slave and a prisoner, forgotten by everyone except God. But God raised him up to interpret Pharaoh's dreams and govern Egypt through seven years of famine, and when his brothers came begging for grain, Joseph wept and forgave them, saying, 'You intended to harm me, but God intended it for good.'
Key People
Joseph; Jacob; Judah; Reuben
Themes
Forgiveness; God's Providence; Dreams
Discussion
Joseph's life is the long proof that God works through betrayal, delay, and injustice without ever endorsing them. Forgiveness is not pretending the harm did not happen; it is trusting God's larger purpose while releasing revenge. Reflect: 1) Who do you need to forgive, and what makes that hard? 2) How has God used a painful season in your life for good? 3) Where do you need to trust God's providence when you cannot see the ending?
