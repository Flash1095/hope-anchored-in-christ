Ahaz and the Syro-Ephraimite War

Story
Ahaz and the Syro-Ephraimite War
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Kings 16; 2 Chronicles 28
Retelling
In the days of Ahaz, king of Judah, Rezin, king of Syria, and Pekah, son of Remaliah, king of Israel, came up to Jerusalem to war against it, but could not prevail against it. The Syrians and Israelites instead ravaged Judah: the Syrians carried away captives to Damascus, and Pekah slew one hundred and twenty thousand men of Judah in one day, because they had forsaken the LORD God of their fathers, and carried away two hundred thousand women, sons, and daughters as spoil to Samaria. But a prophet of the LORD, Oded, met the returning army at Samaria and warned them that their rage had reached up to heaven and that keeping their brothers as slaves would add guilt to their sin, so the captors, at the princes' urging, released the captives, clothed and fed them, and carried the feeble on asses back to Jericho. Ahaz, instead of trusting God, sent messengers to Tiglath-pileser, king of Assyria, with silver and gold from the house of the LORD and the king's house, saying, 'I am thy servant and thy son: come up, and save me out of the hand of the king of Syria, and out of the hand of the king of Israel.' Assyria came, took Damascus, slew Rezin, and carried its people captive, but also distressed Ahaz rather than strengthening him. Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Tiglath-pileser, saw an altar there, sent its pattern to Uriah the priest, and had a copy built in Jerusalem, replacing the LORD's altar and remodelling the temple courts after the Assyrian fashion.
Discussion
The Syro-Ephraimite war shows Judah attacked by its own brothers and rescued by a prophet's rebuke, while Ahaz answered by buying Assyria's help and copying a Damascus altar into the temple. Reflect: 1) How does fear drive you to copy the solutions of powerful neighbours? 2) What does Oded's intervention say about mercy toward captives who are your own kin? 3) When has a rescue you paid for become a new master?
Significance
Ahaz's Syro-Ephraimite war ended with Judah's captives freed by prophetic rebuke and its king enslaved to Assyria and its altar.
