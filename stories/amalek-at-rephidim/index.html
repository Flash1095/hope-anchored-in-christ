Amalek at Rephidim

Retelling

At Rephidim, after water had come from the rock, Amalek came and fought with Israel. Moses told Joshua to choose men and go out and fight, while he himself would stand on the top of the hill with the rod of God in his hand. As long as Moses held up his hand, Israel prevailed; when he let it down, Amalek prevailed. When Moses' hands grew heavy, Aaron and Hur sat him on a stone and held up his hands, one on each side, until the sun went down. Joshua defeated Amalek with the edge of the sword. God told Moses to write this as a memorial and to tell Joshua that he would utterly blot out the remembrance of Amalek, and Moses built an altar called The LORD My Banner, saying the LORD would have war with Amalek from generation to generation.

Discussion

Israel's first battle after the Exodus is won not by the sword alone but by upheld hands on the hill and steady friends beside a weary leader. Victory belongs to God, and the memorial matters. Reflect: 1) Who holds up your hands when you grow weary? 2) How does this battle show that prayer and action belong together? 3) Why does God command a written memorial of Amalek?

Significance

Amalek at Rephidim is Israel's first war as a free people, won through Joshua's sword and Moses' upheld hands, and remembered by altar and book as a standing sign that God fights for his people.
