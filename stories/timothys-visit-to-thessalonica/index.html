Timothy's Visit to Thessalonica

Timothy's Visit to Thessalonica

Retelling

Paul writes that when he could no longer forbear, he thought it good to be left at Athens alone, and sent Timotheus, his brother and minister of God and fellowlabourer in the gospel of Christ, to establish the Thessalonians and to comfort them concerning their faith, that no man should be moved by their afflictions. But now when Timotheus came from Thessalonica unto Paul, he brought him good tidings of their faith and charity, and that they had good remembrance of Paul always, desiring greatly to see him, as Paul also desired to see them. Therefore, Paul writes, brethren, we were comforted over you in all our affliction and distress by your faith, for now we live, if ye stand fast in the Lord. He asks what thanks they can render to God for all the joy wherewith they joy for their sakes before God, night and day praying exceedingly that they might see their face and perfect that which is lacking in their faith.

Discussion

This is a mission told by letter: a sending from Athens, a visit to a persecuted young church, and a return carrying good news that comforts the sender. Paul's anxiety and joy are both pastoral, not strategic. Reflect: 1) Why does Paul send Timothy rather than going himself? 2) What does it mean to be established and comforted concerning faith in affliction? 3) How does good news from a young church become comfort for its founder?

Significance

Timothy's visit holds the Thessalonian church together after Paul's sudden departure: the young church stands fast, and the report of its faith and love becomes the occasion for Paul's first letter to any church.

Encouragement; Faith under Affliction; Mission; Pastoral Care
