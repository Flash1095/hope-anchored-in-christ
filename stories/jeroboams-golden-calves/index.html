Jeroboam's Golden Calves

Story
Jeroboam's Golden Calves
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
1 Kings 12:25-33
Retelling
After the kingdom divided, Jeroboam built Shechem in Ephraim and lived there, and went out and built Penuel. Then he said in his heart, 'Now shall the kingdom return to the house of David: if this people go up to do sacrifice in the house of the LORD at Jerusalem, then shall the heart of this people turn again unto their lord, even unto Rehoboam king of Judah, and they shall kill me.' So the king took counsel and made two calves of gold, and said to the people, 'It is too much for you to go up to Jerusalem: behold thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt.' He set one calf in Bethel and the other in Dan, and the thing became a sin, for the people went to worship before the one, even unto Dan. Jeroboam made a house of high places and priests from the lowest of the people, who were not of the sons of Levi, and he ordained a feast in the eighth month, on the fifteenth day, like the feast in Judah, offering upon the altar at Bethel and burning incense.
Discussion
Jeroboam's calves were political religion: a new worship designed to keep a new kingdom from drifting back to Jerusalem. He kept the Exodus language, 'which brought thee up out of Egypt,' and pointed it at golden calves. Reflect: 1) How does fear for a position lead to reshaping worship? 2) When is convenience, 'it is too much for you to go up,' used to justify disobedience? 3) What modern calves carry God's own language while pointing away from him?
Significance
Jeroboam's golden calves at Bethel and Dan made political convenience into state idolatry and fixed the sin that would define the northern kingdom.
