David Recovers Ziklag

David Recovers Ziklag

Retelling

When David and his men returned to Ziklag after being dismissed by the Philistines, they found the city burned with fire and their wives, sons, and daughters taken captive by the Amalekites. David and his men wept until they had no more power to weep, and the people spoke of stoning David, for every man's soul was grieved for his sons and daughters. But David encouraged himself in the LORD his God, asked Abiathar to bring the ephod, and inquired of the LORD whether he should pursue. The LORD answered, 'Pursue: for thou shalt surely overtake them, and without fail recover all.' Two hundred men, too faint to cross the brook Besor, stayed with the supplies, while four hundred pressed on. An abandoned Egyptian servant, left sick by his Amalekite master, led them to the raiders, who were spread over the land eating, drinking, and dancing over their spoil. David smote them from twilight until the evening of the next day, recovered all that the Amalekites had taken, including every wife and child, and lost nothing. When some of his men refused to share the spoil with those who had stayed behind, David ruled that the share of those who stayed with the supplies would equal the share of those who went to battle, and he made it a statute in Israel.

Discussion

Ziklag is David's lowest point turned by one decision: he encouraged himself in God when his own men talked of stoning him, and then he shared the spoil equally with those too faint to fight. Reflect: 1) Where do you turn when even your own people blame you? 2) How does David's equal-share ruling challenge the way you reward visible effort? 3) What would pursuing, rather than grieving, look like in your situation?

Significance

David's recovery of Ziklag turned grief and mutiny into complete restoration, and his equal sharing of the spoil became a lasting statute in Israel.
