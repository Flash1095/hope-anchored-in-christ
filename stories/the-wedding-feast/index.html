The Wedding Feast

Story
The Wedding Feast
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 22:1-14; Luke 14:15-24
Retelling
The kingdom of heaven is like a king who gave a wedding feast for his son and sent servants to call the invited guests, but they would not come. He sent more servants with the message that all was ready, but the guests made light of it: one went to his farm, another to his business, and the rest seized the servants and killed them. Enraged, the king sent troops against the murderers and burned their city. Then he told his servants to go into the streets and invite everyone they found, bad and good, and the hall filled with guests. But when the king saw a man without a wedding garment, he had him bound and thrown into outer darkness. Luke tells the same story as a great banquet, where the invited guests excuse themselves over a field, oxen, and a new wife, and the master fills his house from the highways and hedges.
Discussion
The feast is free, but it is not casual: the invited refused for ordinary reasons — farm, business, marriage — and the uninvited came in from the streets. The garment warns that grace still expects readiness. Reflect: What ordinary excuse — work, property, family — is keeping you from the feast? Are you treating God's invitation as optional? How do you hold together free grace and the wedding garment?
Significance
The wedding feast warns that God's invitation, refused by the invited, goes out to the streets, while still requiring the guest to come prepared.
