The Defeat of Sihon and Og

The Defeat of Sihon and Og

Retelling

From the wilderness Israel sent messengers to Sihon, king of the Amorites, asking to pass through his land by the king's highway, without turning into field or vineyard or drinking well water. Sihon refused, gathered all his people, and fought against Israel at Jahaz. Israel smote him with the edge of the sword and possessed his land from Arnon to Jabbok. They took all the Amorite cities, including Heshbon, and dwelt in them. Then Israel turned toward Bashan, and Og, king of Bashan, came out against them at Edrei with all his people. God told Moses not to fear him, for he had delivered Og into Israel's hand. Israel smote Og and his sons until none remained, and possessed his land. These two victories gave Israel the whole Transjordan before it ever crossed the Jordan.

Discussion

Sihon and Og are the first kingdoms Israel defeats and possesses, and their land becomes the home of two and a half tribes. A refused highway becomes a conquered territory. Reflect: 1) How does God turn a refused request into provision? 2) Why does Moses recount these victories again in Deuteronomy? 3) What does it mean that Israel possessed land before crossing the Jordan?

Significance

The defeat of Sihon and Og is Israel's first territorial conquest, giving it the Transjordan and proving, before Jericho, that God delivers kings into his people's hands.
