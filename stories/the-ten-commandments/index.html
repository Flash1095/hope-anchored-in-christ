The Ten Commandments

Story
The Ten Commandments
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Exodus 19-20; Exodus 24; Deuteronomy 5
Retelling
Three months after leaving Egypt, Israel camped at Mount Sinai, and God descended on the mountain in thunder, lightning, and a thick cloud. He reminded them that He had carried them out of Egypt on eagles' wings, and then He spoke ten words that would shape His covenant people: no other gods, no idols, honor God's name, keep the Sabbath, honor father and mother, and no murder, adultery, theft, lies, or coveting. The people trembled at the mountain and asked Moses to speak to God for them, and Moses received the commandments written on tablets of stone.
Key People
Moses; Aaron; Joshua
Themes
Covenant; Law; Holiness
Discussion
The commandments are not the price of God's love; they are the shape of life for people already rescued. They show us God's character, expose our need, and point us to the One who kept them perfectly. Reflect: 1) Which commandment most convicts you, and why? 2) How does the law as a gift differ from the law as a burden? 3) How does Jesus fulfill the law rather than abolish it?

Significance
The Law was a gift to a rescued people — God saved them first, then showed them how to live. It anchors hope by revealing God's holy character and pointing us to the Saviour who fulfils it.
