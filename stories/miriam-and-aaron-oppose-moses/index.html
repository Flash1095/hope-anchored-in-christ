Miriam and Aaron Oppose Moses

Story
Miriam and Aaron Oppose Moses
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Numbers 12
Retelling
Miriam and Aaron, Moses' own sister and brother, spoke against him, first over the Cushite woman he had married, then over his unique authority, asking whether God had spoken only through Moses. God summoned all three to the tent of meeting and defended Moses, saying he spoke with Moses face to face, not in riddles, while other prophets received visions and dreams. When the cloud lifted, Miriam was leprous, white as snow. Aaron pleaded for her, and Moses cried out for her healing. God ordered her shut outside the camp for seven days, and the whole camp waited until she was restored before moving on.
Discussion
Envy can hide behind a reasonable complaint, and it strikes closest to home. God defends humble leadership, yet Moses intercedes for the sister who opposed him. Reflect: 1) What resentment toward a leader have you dressed up as a principle? 2) How does Moses' prayer for Miriam challenge your response to critics? 3) Why does God combine discipline with restoration?
Significance
God vindicated Moses' calling and disciplined Miriam, yet restored her after seven days, showing that opposition to God's appointed leadership is serious and that discipline aims at restoration, not rejection.
