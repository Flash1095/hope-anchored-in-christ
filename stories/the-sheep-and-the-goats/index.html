The Sheep and the Goats

The Sheep and the Goats

Retelling

When the Son of Man comes in his glory with all the angels, Jesus said, he will sit on his glorious throne and separate all nations as a shepherd separates sheep from goats. To those on his right he will say, Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you, for I was hungry and you gave me food, thirsty and you gave me drink, a stranger and you welcomed me, naked and you clothed me, sick and you visited me, in prison and you came to me. The righteous will ask when they did these things for him, and he will answer that as they did it to one of the least of his brothers, they did it to him. To those on his left, who did none of these things, he will say, Depart from me, into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels.

Discussion

The judgment surprises both groups: neither knew they were serving or neglecting Christ himself. The test is not theology recited but mercy practised on the least. Reflect: Who are 'the least' in your daily path — hungry, stranger, prisoner — whom you pass without seeing? Would your last month's mercy pass this test? How does serving Christ in disguise change ordinary kindness?

Significance

The sheep and goats make mercy to the needy the criterion of final judgment and identify Christ with the least of his brothers. Doubt: This is a discourse about final judgment rather than a narrated past event, so its standing as a story row is debated.
