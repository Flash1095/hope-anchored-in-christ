Paul Teaches in the School of Tyrannus

Story
Paul Teaches in the School of Tyrannus
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Acts 19:8-10
Retelling
Paul went into the synagogue at Ephesus, and spake boldly for the space of three months, disputing and persuading the things concerning the kingdom of God. But when divers were hardened, and believed not, but spake evil of that way before the multitude, he departed from them, and separated the disciples, and disputed daily in the school of one Tyrannus. And this continued by the space of two years, so that all they which dwelt in Asia heard the word of the Lord Jesus, both Jews and Greeks.
Discussion
After three months in the synagogue, Paul moves to a rented lecture hall and teaches daily for two years, until a whole province hears the word. The Ephesian ministry is not a campaign but a school: steady, daily, public reasoning. Reflect: 1) When is it right to leave a hostile room and find another? 2) What does two years of daily teaching do that a single visit cannot? 3) How does a local school reach a whole province?
Significance
The school of Tyrannus is the mission's longest classroom: from one hall in Ephesus, the word spreads through all Asia, and the churches of the province trace their hearing to these two years.
