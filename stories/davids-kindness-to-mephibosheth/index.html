David's Kindness to Mephibosheth

Story
David's Kindness to Mephibosheth
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Samuel 9
Retelling
David asked whether anyone remained of the house of Saul to whom he could show the kindness of God for Jonathan's sake. Ziba, a former servant of Saul, told him of Mephibosheth, Jonathan's son, who was lame in both feet and living at Lo-debar in the house of Machir. David sent for him, and Mephibosheth fell on his face in fear, calling himself a dead dog. David said, 'Fear not: for I will surely shew thee kindness for Jonathan thy father's sake, and will restore thee all the land of Saul thy father; and thou shalt eat bread at my table continually.' He gave Ziba charge of the land, to work it for Mephibosheth, and Mephibosheth himself ate at the king's table like one of the king's sons, though he was lame in both feet.
Discussion
Mephibosheth expected execution and received a seat at the table. David's kindness was not earned by the lame prince but inherited from a covenant with his father, Jonathan. Reflect: 1) Who in your life expects judgment and needs unexpected kindness? 2) How does covenant loyalty differ from kindness that waits to be earned? 3) What does a permanent seat at the table, not a one-time gift, say about grace?
Significance
David's kindness to Mephibosheth turned covenant loyalty into a lifelong seat at the king's table for a lame and fearful prince.
