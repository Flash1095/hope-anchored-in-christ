Shamgar and the Ox Goad

Story
Shamgar and the Ox Goad
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Judges 3:31
Retelling
After Ehud came Shamgar, son of Anath. The whole account of his deliverance is a single verse: he slew six hundred Philistines with an ox goad, and he also delivered Israel. No oppression is named, no battle is described, and no years of rest are counted. A farmer's tool in an ordinary man's hand became the weapon by which God broke Philistine power over his people for a season. The verse places him after Ehud in the sequence of judges, and before Deborah and Barak, who are the next deliverers the book names.
Discussion
Shamgar's story is told in one verse, and that brevity is itself the point worth noticing: God is not limited to trained soldiers or proper weapons. An ox goad, the tool of a working man, was enough. Reflect: 1) What ordinary tool or skill in your hands might God use? 2) Why do you think Scripture preserves a one-verse deliverance at all? 3) How do you value faithful people whose whole story is never told?
Significance
Shamgar's single-verse deliverance shows God using an unlikely man and an ordinary tool to save Israel. Doubt: the account is one verse with no oppression, battle, or period of rest named, so its place and scale in the judges sequence cannot be fixed from the text alone.
