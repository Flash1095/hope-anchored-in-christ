Isaac and Rebekah

Date Approx
c. 1900 BC
Discussion
Every step of this journey — the oath, the prayer at the well, Rebekah's willing answer, 'I will go' — shows God's hand guiding ordinary decisions toward his promise. Isaac was comforted after his mother's death through the wife God provided. Reflect: 1) Where do you see God's providence in small, everyday details, as the servant did at the well? 2) What does Rebekah's willing 'I will go' teach about faith as a personal choice? 3) How do you seek God's guidance before a life-changing decision?

Retelling
Abraham was old, and well stricken in age, and the LORD had blessed Abraham in all things. He said unto his eldest servant of his house, that ruled over all that he had, to put his hand under his thigh, and he made him swear by the LORD, the God of heaven and the God of the earth, that he would not take a wife unto his son Isaac of the daughters of the Canaanites, among whom Abraham dwelt, but would go unto Abraham's country and kindred and take a wife unto his son Isaac. When the servant asked whether, if the woman would not follow him, he must bring Isaac back to that land, Abraham said he must beware that he bring not his son thither again, for the LORD God of heaven, who took Abraham from his father's house and from the land of his kindred and sware that unto his seed he would give this land, would send his angel before the servant. If the woman would not be willing to follow, the servant would be clear from the oath, but only bring not his son thither again. The servant took ten camels of his master's camels, and departed, for all the goods of his master were in his hand, and went to Mesopotamia, unto the city of Nahor. He made his camels kneel down without the city by a well of water at the time of the evening, the time that women go out to draw water. He prayed, O LORD God of my master Abraham, send me good speed this day, and shew kindness unto my master Abraham, and asked that the damsel to whom he would say, Let down thy pitcher, that I may drink, and who would say, Drink, and I will give thy camels drink also, would be she whom the LORD had appointed for his servant Isaac. Before he had done speaking, Rebekah came out, who was born to Bethuel, son of Milcah, the wife of Nahor, Abraham's brother, with her pitcher upon her shoulder. The damsel was very fair to look upon, a virgin, neither had any man known her, and she went down to the well, filled her pitcher, and came up. The servant ran to meet her and asked to drink a little water of her pitcher. She said, Drink, my lord, hasted, let down her pitcher upon her hand, and gave him drink. When she had done giving him drink, she said she would draw water for his camels also, until they had done drinking. She hasted, emptied her pitcher into the trough, and ran again unto the well to draw water, and drew for all his camels. As the camels had done drinking, the man took a golden earring of half a shekel weight, and two bracelets for her hands of ten shekels weight of gold, and asked whose daughter she was, and whether there was room in her father's house for them to lodge in. She said she was the daughter of Bethuel the son of Milcah, which she bare unto Nahor, and that they had both straw and provender enough, and room to lodge in. The man bowed down his head and worshipped the LORD, saying, Blessed be the LORD God of my master Abraham, who had not left destitute his master of his mercy and his truth, for the LORD had led him to the house of his master's brethren. Rebekah ran and told her mother's house these things. Rebekah had a brother, whose name was Laban, and Laban ran out unto the man, unto the well, when he saw the earring and bracelets upon his sister's hands and heard her words. Laban welcomed him as the blessed of the LORD, and the man came into the house, where his camels were ungirded and given straw and provender, and water was given to wash his feet and the feet of the men with him. When meat was set before him, he said he would not eat until he had told his errand, and he told them he was Abraham's servant, that the LORD had blessed his master greatly, that Sarah his master's wife bare a son to his master when she was old, and unto him Abraham had given all that he had, and he repeated the oath and the sign at the well. Then Laban and Bethuel answered and said, The thing proceedeth from the LORD: we cannot speak unto thee bad or good. Behold, Rebekah is before thee, take her, and go, and let her be thy master's son's wife, as the LORD hath spoken. When Abraham's servant heard their words, he worshipped the LORD, bowing himself to the earth. He brought forth jewels of silver, and jewels of gold, and raiment, and gave them to Rebekah, and gave also to her brother and to her mother precious things. They did eat and drink, and tarried all night, and rose up in the morning, and the servant said, Send me away unto my master. Her brother and her mother said, Let the damsel abide with us a few days, at the least ten, and after that she shall go. He said, Hinder me not, seeing the LORD hath prospered my way, and send me away that I may go to my master. They said, We will call the damsel, and enquire at her mouth. They called Rebekah, and said unto her, Wilt thou go with this man? And she said, I will go. They sent away Rebekah their sister, and her nurse, and Abraham's servant, and his men. They blessed Rebekah, and said unto her, Thou art our sister, be thou the mother of thousands of millions, and let thy seed possess the gate of those which hate them. Rebekah arose, and her damsels, and they rode upon the camels, and followed the man. Isaac came from the way of the well Lahai-roi, for he dwelt in the south country. Isaac went out to meditate in the field at the eventide, and lifted up his eyes, and saw the camels coming. Rebekah lifted up her eyes, and when she saw Isaac, she lighted off the camel, for she had said unto the servant, What man is this that walketh in the field to meet us? And the servant had said, It is my master: therefore she took a vail, and covered herself. The servant told Isaac all things that he had done. Isaac brought her into his mother Sarah's tent, and took Rebekah, and she became his wife, and he loved her, and Isaac was comforted after his mother's death.
Scripture
Genesis 24
Significance
Rebekah's marriage to Isaac carried the covenant promise into the next generation, showing God's providence at work through prayer, hospitality, and a willing heart.
