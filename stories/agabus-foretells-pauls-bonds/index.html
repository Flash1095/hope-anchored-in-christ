Agabus Foretells Paul's Bonds

Story
Agabus Foretells Paul's Bonds
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Acts 21:8-14
Retelling
Paul's company came unto Caesarea, and entered into the house of Philip the evangelist, which was one of the seven, and abode with him. And the same man had four daughters, virgins, which did prophesy. And as they tarried there many days, there came down from Judaea a certain prophet, named Agabus. And when he was come unto them, he took Paul's girdle, and bound his own hands and feet, and said, Thus saith the Holy Ghost, So shall the Jews at Jerusalem bind the man that owneth this girdle, and shall deliver him into the hands of the Gentiles. Then when they heard these things, both they of that place, and others, besought him not to go up to Jerusalem. Then Paul answered, What mean ye to weep and to break mine heart? For I am ready not to be bound only, but also to die at Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus. And when he would not be persuaded, they ceased, saying, The will of the Lord be done.
Discussion
Agabus acts the prophecy with Paul's own girdle, binding his hands and feet, and everyone hears it as a reason not to go. Paul hears it differently: as a warning to be faced, not a prohibition to be obeyed, and he is ready to die at Jerusalem for the name of Jesus. Reflect: 1) How do you tell a prophetic warning from a prophetic command? 2) Why do Paul's friends weep, and why does he call their weeping heart-breaking? 3) When does submitting to the Lord's will mean walking into the danger you were warned about?
Significance
Agabus's acted prophecy gives Paul his clearest warning and his clearest resolve: Jerusalem will bind him and hand him to the Gentiles, and he will go anyway, ready for bonds and for death, because the will of the Lord is to be done.
