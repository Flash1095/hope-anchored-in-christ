Micah's Idol and the Danites

Story
Micah's Idol and the Danites
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Judges 17-18
Retelling
In the hill country of Ephraim, a man named Micah returned eleven hundred shekels of silver he had taken from his mother, and she dedicated the silver to the LORD to make a carved image and a molten image. Micah set up a house of gods, made an ephod and teraphim, and consecrated one of his own sons as priest. Then a young Levite from Bethlehem came seeking a place to stay, and Micah hired him as his priest for ten shekels of silver a year, convinced the LORD would now do him good. Meanwhile the tribe of Dan, still seeking an inheritance, sent five spies who lodged with Micah and recognised the Levite's voice. On their report, six hundred armed Danites marched north, seized Micah's idol, ephod, and teraphim, and persuaded the Levite to go with them as priest of a whole tribe rather than one man's house. They conquered Laish, a quiet and unsuspecting city, burned it, rebuilt it, and called it Dan, setting up Micah's carved image there, where it remained through the time of the captivity of the land.
Discussion
Micah's story shows religion gone wrong from the inside: a stolen fortune, a mother's blessing on an idol, a hired priest, and a tribe that steals both god and priest for its own city. No one in the story consults the LORD; everyone does what is right in his own eyes. Reflect: 1) How can sincere-sounding religion still be idolatry? 2) What happens when spiritual leadership is hired rather than called? 3) Where do you see private compromise becoming a whole community's worship?
Significance
Micah's idol and the Danite migration expose the anarchy of the judges period: household idolatry, a mercenary priesthood, and a tribe that founded its city on stolen gods.
