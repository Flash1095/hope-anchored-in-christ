Victory over Arad at Hormah

Story
Victory over Arad at Hormah
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Numbers 21:1-3
Retelling
When the Canaanite king of Arad, who dwelt in the south, heard that Israel came by the way of the spies, he fought against Israel and took some of them prisoners. Israel vowed a vow to the LORD, saying, If you will indeed deliver this people into my hand, I will utterly destroy their cities. The LORD listened to Israel's voice and delivered up the Canaanites, and they utterly destroyed them and their cities. And the place was called Hormah, meaning destruction.
Discussion
Arad is a small victory with a large pattern: attack, vow, answer, and a place renamed for what God did. It is the first Canaanite defeat on the road to the promised land. Reflect: 1) How does a vow shape Israel's warfare differently from revenge? 2) Why does Scripture record small victories alongside great ones? 3) What place in your life deserves renaming for what God did there?
Significance
Victory over Arad at Hormah is Israel's first named defeat of a Canaanite king, won by vow and answer, and remembered in the name Hormah.
