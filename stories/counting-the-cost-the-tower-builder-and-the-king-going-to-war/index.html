Counting the Cost: The Tower Builder and the King Going to War

Counting the Cost: The Tower Builder and the King Going to War

Retelling

As large crowds travelled with Jesus, he turned and said that no one could be his disciple without hating, by comparison, father, mother, wife, children, brothers, sisters, and even their own life, and carrying their cross. Then he gave two pictures of counting the cost. Suppose one of you wants to build a tower: won't you first sit down and estimate the cost, to see whether you have enough to complete it? Otherwise, after laying the foundation and failing to finish, everyone will ridicule you, saying, This person began to build and wasn't able to finish. Or suppose a king about to go to war against another king: won't he first sit down and consider whether he is able with ten thousand men to oppose the one coming with twenty thousand? If not, he will send a delegation while the other is still a long way off and ask for terms of peace. In the same way, Jesus concluded, those who do not give up everything they have cannot be his disciples.

Discussion

Discipleship is begun with eyes open, like a builder estimating or a king counting troops: following Jesus costs everything, and he says so before you start. Reflect: 1) What have you not yet counted in the cost of following Jesus? 2) How does the tower's unfinished foundation warn against enthusiastic beginnings? 3) What does giving up everything mean concretely for your possessions, family, and plans?

Significance

Before following him, disciples must count the cost like a tower builder estimating his funds and a king counting his troops, for no one who will not give up everything can be his disciple.
