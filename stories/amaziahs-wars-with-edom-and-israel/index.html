Amaziah's Wars with Edom and Israel

Story
Amaziah's Wars with Edom and Israel
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Kings 14:1-22; 2 Chronicles 25
Retelling
Amaziah, son of Joash, king of Judah, did what was right in the sight of the LORD, but not with a perfect heart. After securing his kingdom and executing his father's murderers, he mustered Judah and hired one hundred thousand mighty men of Israel for one hundred talents of silver to fight Edom. A man of God warned him not to take Israel's army, for the LORD was not with Israel, and Amaziah, though grumbling at the lost silver, dismissed them, and they returned home in fierce anger. Amaziah then led Judah to the Valley of Salt, slew ten thousand Edomites, and took ten thousand more alive, casting them from the top of a rock. But the dismissed Israelite soldiers, angry at their dismissal, raided Judah's cities on their way home, killing three thousand and taking much spoil. Flushed with his Edomite victory, Amaziah took the gods of Edom, set them up as his own gods, bowed before them, and burned incense to them, and the LORD's anger was kindled against him. Amaziah then challenged Jehoash, king of Israel, to face him in battle, and Jehoash answered with a parable of a thistle demanding a cedar's daughter, warning that a wild beast had trodden the thistle down. Amaziah would not hear, and at Beth-shemesh Israel smote Judah, Amaziah was captured, and Jehoash broke down four hundred cubits of Jerusalem's wall, took the temple and palace treasures and hostages, and returned to Samaria. Amaziah lived fifteen years after Jehoash's death, but a conspiracy was formed against him at Jerusalem, and he fled to Lachish, where he was slain.
Discussion
Amaziah obeyed God about Edom's army and then worshipped Edom's gods: victory over an enemy became surrender to its idols, and pride after victory became a lost war with Israel. Reflect: 1) How does victory over one enemy become worship of that enemy's gods? 2) Why is it hard to accept a prophet's warning after a success? 3) What does Jehoash's thistle parable say about pride that challenges stronger powers?
Significance
Amaziah's wars with Edom and Israel turned a victory won by obedience into defeat, after he worshipped Edom's gods and challenged Israel in pride.
