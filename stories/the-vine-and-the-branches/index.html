The Vine and the Branches

Story
The Vine and the Branches
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
John 15:1-17
Retelling
On the night before his death, Jesus told his disciples, I am the true vine, and my Father is the vinedresser. Every branch in me that does not bear fruit he takes away, and every branch that bears fruit he prunes, that it may bear more fruit. He told them to abide in him, as a branch cannot bear fruit by itself unless it abides in the vine, and neither can they unless they abide in him. Apart from me, he said, you can do nothing. Whoever abides in him and he in them bears much fruit; whoever does not is thrown away like a withered branch. If his words abide in them, they may ask whatever they wish and it will be done. His command was that they love one another as he had loved them, for no one has greater love than to lay down his life for his friends.
Discussion
Fruit is not produced by straining but by abiding: the branch's job is connection, and the Father's is pruning. Even fruitful branches are cut back so they bear more. Reflect: Are you straining to produce fruit while neglecting abiding? What pruning — painful but purposeful — might the Father be doing in you? How does 'apart from me you can do nothing' sit with your self-sufficiency?
Significance
The vine teaches that fruitfulness flows from abiding in Christ, and that his command to love one another is the fruit itself. Doubt: This is a metaphorical discourse from the farewell teaching rather than a narrated past event, so its standing as a story row is debated.
