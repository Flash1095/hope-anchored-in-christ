The Calling of the First Disciples

Story
The Calling of the First Disciples
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 4:18-22; Mark 1:16-20; Luke 5:1-11; John 1:35-51
Retelling
Beside the Sea of Galilee, Jesus called Simon Peter and Andrew as they fished, promising to make them fishers of men, and they left their nets at once. He called James and John as they mended nets with their father, and they followed too. In John's account, Andrew first brought his brother Simon to Jesus after hearing John the Baptist point to the Lamb of God, and Philip brought Nathanael, who came doubting whether anything good could come from Nazareth and stayed after Jesus showed he already knew him. In Luke's account, after a night of catching nothing, Peter obeyed Jesus' word to cast again and hauled in a catch so large the nets broke; overwhelmed, he fell at Jesus' knees, and Jesus answered his fear with the same call: do not be afraid; from now on you will catch men.
Discussion
The call came in the middle of ordinary work and demanded an immediate break with it. Peter's catch shows obedience preceding understanding: the nets filled after he acted on Jesus' word, not before. Reflect: What ordinary work is Jesus interrupting in your life right now? What net — income, security, identity — are you being asked to leave? Where do you need to obey before you see the result?
Significance
The calling of the first disciples founds the apostolic circle and sets the pattern of discipleship: Jesus calls, and followers leave everything to follow.
