The Conversion of Paul

Story
The Conversion of Paul
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Acts 9; Acts 22; Galatians 1:13-24
Retelling
Saul of Tarsus was the church's fiercest enemy — a Pharisee who approved Stephen's stoning and hunted Christians with arrest warrants. On the road to Damascus, a light from heaven brighter than the sun knocked him to the ground, and the risen Jesus spoke: 'Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?' Blinded, Saul was led into the city, where the disciple Ananias — who feared him — laid hands on him at the Lord's command. Scales fell from his eyes, he was baptized, and within days the man who came to destroy the church was preaching that Jesus is the Son of God. The persecutor had become an apostle.
Key People
Paul; Ananias; Barnabas; Saul (Paul); Jesus
Themes
Grace; Transformation; Calling
Discussion
Paul's conversion proves that no one is too far gone — not the zealous opponent, not the person who has done the worst. Grace does not improve the old life; it raises a new one. Reflect: 1) Who in your life seems 'too far gone,' and how does Paul challenge that? 2) How has Christ transformed you, and where is He still transforming you? 3) What does Ananias' obedience teach about welcoming unlikely converts?
