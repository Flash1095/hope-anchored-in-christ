Jesus Before Herod

Story
Jesus Before Herod
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Luke 23:6-12
Retelling
When Pilate learned that Jesus was under Herod's jurisdiction, he sent him to Herod Antipas, who happened to be in Jerusalem at the time. Herod was greatly pleased, because for a long time he had wanted to see Jesus, having heard about him, and he hoped to see him perform a sign. He plied Jesus with many questions, but Jesus gave him no answer, while the chief priests and scribes stood by vehemently accusing him. Herod and his soldiers ridiculed and mocked Jesus, dressing him in an elegant robe, and then sent him back to Pilate. That day Herod and Pilate, who had been enemies, became friends.
Discussion
Herod wanted a performance, not a king, and Jesus answered curiosity with silence: the gospel is not entertainment for the powerful. Reflect: 1) What is the difference between wanting to see Jesus and wanting to be entertained by him? 2) How does Jesus' silence before Herod challenge the urge to justify yourself to every accuser? 3) What does the new friendship of Herod and Pilate say about opposition uniting against Jesus?
Significance
Herod, who had killed John the Baptist, met Jesus only as a curiosity, mocked him, and returned him to Pilate, and the two rulers became friends over the prisoner neither would acquit.
