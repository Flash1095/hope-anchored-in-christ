The Famine and the Gibeonites

The Famine and the Gibeonites

Retelling

There was a famine in the days of David for three years, year after year, and David inquired of the LORD. The LORD answered that it was for Saul and for his bloody house, because he had slain the Gibeonites, breaking the oath Israel had sworn to them in Joshua's day. David asked the Gibeonites what atonement they required, and they asked for seven of Saul's sons to be hanged before the LORD in Gibeah of Saul. David spared Mephibosheth, Jonathan's son, because of his oath to Jonathan, but delivered two sons of Rizpah, Saul's concubine, and five sons of Merab, Saul's daughter, to the Gibeonites, who hanged them at the beginning of barley harvest. Rizpah, daughter of Aiah, took sackcloth and spread it on a rock, and stayed with the bodies from the beginning of harvest until water dropped on them from heaven, keeping birds away by day and beasts by night, never leaving them. When David was told what Rizpah had done, he went and took the bones of Saul and Jonathan from Jabesh-gilead, and buried them with the bones of the seven in the sepulchre of Kish, Saul's father. Then God was intreated for the land, and the famine ended.

Discussion

A three-year famine traced back to a broken oath shows that old wrongs do not expire with a new king. Rizpah's lonely vigil, guarding her sons' bodies for months, moved David to give Saul's house a proper burial and the land its healing. Reflect: 1) What old promises, made by others, still bind your community today? 2) How does Rizpah's persistence change a king's mind? 3) What does it mean that God was entreated for the land only after honour was restored?

Significance

The famine ended when David honoured the Gibeonite oath and gave Saul's house burial, moved by Rizpah's months-long vigil over the dead.
