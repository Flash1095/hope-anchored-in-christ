The Healing at the Pool of Bethesda

Story
The Healing at the Pool of Bethesda
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
John 5:1-15
Retelling
At Jerusalem, beside the Sheep Gate, lay a pool called Bethesda, where crowds of sick people waited for the water to be stirred, believing the first one in would be healed. Among them was a man who had been ill for thirty-eight years. Jesus saw him lying there, knew how long he had suffered, and asked, Do you want to be made well? The man answered that he had no one to put him into the pool, and someone always stepped in ahead of him. Jesus told him to rise, take up his bed, and walk. At once the man was healed, picked up his bed, and walked — but it was the Sabbath, and the religious leaders objected not to the healing but to the carrying of the bed. When they asked who had healed him, the man at first did not know; later, finding Jesus in the temple, he reported him.
Discussion
Thirty-eight years of waiting had left the man without hope or helper, and Jesus' question cut through his excuses to his actual desire. Healing came as a command to act, not as a technique. Reflect: What long-standing condition have you stopped expecting to change? Are you explaining why help cannot reach you instead of answering whether you want it? What 'bed' is Jesus telling you to pick up and walk with?
Significance
Bethesda shows Jesus healing on his own authority, even on the Sabbath, and exposes a religion that polices rules while ignoring restored lives.
