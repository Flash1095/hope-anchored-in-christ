Hosea's Marriage

Story
Hosea's Marriage
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Hosea 1-3
Retelling
The LORD told Hosea to marry Gomer, a wife whose unfaithfulness would picture Israel's unfaithfulness to the LORD, and the children born to them were given names that spoke judgment: Jezreel, Lo-Ruhamah, meaning no mercy, and Lo-Ammi, meaning not my people. The book then lays out Israel's adultery in chasing other gods, and God's grief and determination not to abandon her. At last the LORD told Hosea to love his wife again, even though she had been loved by another, just as the LORD loved Israel though she turned to other gods. Hosea bought her back for fifteen pieces of silver and a measure of barley, and told her she must live faithfully with him for many days.
Discussion
Hosea's marriage makes God's pain and God's love painfully personal. Israel's sin is pictured as adultery, and God's response as a husband who buys back his wife. Reflect: 1) How does picturing sin as unfaithfulness change the way you see your own wandering? 2) What does it cost God to love a people who keep leaving Him? 3) How does Hosea's costly love picture what God has done for you?
Significance
God's love pursues an unfaithful people at great cost, and His discipline aims at restoration, not rejection. Doubt: this is a lived parable — a marriage used as a prophetic sign — so whether it is read as straightforward biography or prophetic sign-act is debated; the narrative is drafted as borderline.
