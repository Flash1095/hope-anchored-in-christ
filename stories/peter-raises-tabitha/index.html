Peter Raises Tabitha

Story
Peter Raises Tabitha
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Acts 9:36-43
Retelling
At Joppa there was a disciple named Tabitha, called Dorcas in Greek, who was full of good works and acts of charity. She fell sick and died, and the believers washed her body and laid it in an upper room. Hearing that Peter was nearby at Lydda, they sent two men urging him to come without delay. When Peter arrived, the widows stood weeping and showed him the tunics and garments Tabitha had made while she was with them. Peter sent everyone out, knelt down and prayed, then turned to the body and said, Tabitha, arise. She opened her eyes, saw Peter, and sat up. He gave her his hand, raised her, called the saints and widows, and presented her alive. The news became known throughout Joppa, and many believed in the Lord, and Peter stayed there many days with Simon the tanner.
Discussion
Tabitha's resurrection honoured an ordinary life: the evidence at her deathbed was coats she had sewn for widows. Great faithfulness in small mercies became the stage for a great miracle. Reflect: What would the widows show at your deathbed as evidence of your life? Do you value quiet, practical charity as highly as public ministry? How does Peter's prayer-first pattern shape your response to impossible news?
Significance
Tabitha's raising honours a life of practical mercy and extends the resurrection power of Jesus through Peter to the coastal church.
