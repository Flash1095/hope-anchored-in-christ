Job's Restoration

Story
Job's Restoration
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Job 42:7-17
Retelling
After the long debate, the LORD spoke to Eliphaz the Temanite and said His wrath was kindled against him and his two friends, because they had not spoken of Him what was right, as His servant Job had. They were told to take seven bullocks and seven rams, go to Job, and offer a burnt offering for themselves, while Job prayed for them, and the LORD would accept him and not deal with them after their folly. They did so, and the LORD accepted Job. Then the LORD turned Job's captivity when he prayed for his friends, and gave him twice as much as he had before. His brothers, sisters, and former friends came and comforted him, each giving him a piece of money and an earring of gold. The LORD blessed Job's latter end more than his beginning: fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, a thousand yoke of oxen, and a thousand she asses, seven sons and three daughters — Jemima, Kezia, and Keren-happuch, the fairest in the land — and Job lived a hundred and forty years after this, saw his sons and his sons' sons to four generations, and died old and full of days.
Discussion
Job's ending is restoration, not an explanation — and it comes through praying for the friends who had wronged him. Reflect: 1) Why does God give Job's restoration only after Job prays for his friends? 2) How do you hold together God's gift of twice as much with the children who were never replaced? 3) What does it mean to trust God when the book ends without answering why you suffered?
Significance
Restoration is God's gift, not a reward Job earned or a bargain he struck, and it arrives alongside a silence about why he suffered that is never filled. Doubt: Job is a poetic book and the historicity and genre of its prose frame are debated; only the prose frame is drafted here, not the poetic dialogues.
