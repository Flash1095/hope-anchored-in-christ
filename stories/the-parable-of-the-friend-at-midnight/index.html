The Parable of the Friend at Midnight

Story
The Parable of the Friend at Midnight
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Luke 11:5-8
Retelling
Jesus said, Suppose you have a friend, and you go to him at midnight and say, Friend, lend me three loaves of bread; a friend of mine on a journey has come to me, and I have no food to offer him. And suppose the one inside answers, Don't bother me. The door is already locked, and my children and I are in bed. I can't get up and give you anything. I tell you, Jesus said, even though he will not get up and give you the bread because of friendship, yet because of your shameless audacity he will surely get up and give you as much as you need. The parable follows the Lord's Prayer and precedes the teaching to ask, seek, and knock.
Discussion
Midnight boldness is commended, not condemned: prayer may be shamelessly persistent, because the need is real and the neighbour's door is not God's door. Reflect: 1) Where has politeness kept you from asking God boldly at midnight? 2) How does this parable differ from the persistent widow's plea to an unjust judge? 3) What need of a travelling friend, someone else's need, drives your boldest prayer?
Significance
A friend at midnight receives bread not by friendship alone but by shameless persistence, teaching disciples to pray boldly and without giving up.
