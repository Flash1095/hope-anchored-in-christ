The Plague of Flies

Story
The Plague of Flies
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Exodus 8:20-32
Retelling
God warned Pharaoh through Moses that if he would not let Israel go, swarms of flies would fill Egyptian houses and the land itself. God did so, and grievous swarms came into Pharaoh's house, his servants' houses, and all Egypt, and the land was corrupted by the flies. For the first time God made a separation: in the land of Goshen, where Israel lived, there were no swarms, so that Pharaoh would know that the LORD was in the midst of the land. Pharaoh offered a compromise, sacrifice in the land, then sacrifice not too far away, and Moses refused both half-measures. The flies were removed at Moses' prayer, but Pharaoh hardened his heart again and did not let the people go.
Discussion
The flies introduce both distinction and compromise: God separates his people in Goshen, while Pharaoh offers worship on his own terms. Moses refuses a shortened obedience. Reflect: 1) What compromises with obedience look reasonable but keep you in Egypt? 2) How does God's protection of Goshen encourage his people? 3) Why does Pharaoh negotiate the terms of worship instead of surrendering?
Significance
The flies show God distinguishing Israel from Egypt in the midst of judgment, and expose Pharaoh's strategy of offering partial obedience in place of release.
