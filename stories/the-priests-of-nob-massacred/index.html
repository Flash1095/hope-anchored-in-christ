The Priests of Nob Massacred

Story
The Priests of Nob Massacred
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
1 Samuel 22:6-23
Retelling
When Saul heard that David and his men had been discovered, he sat under a tree at Gibeah with his spear in his hand and accused his servants of conspiracy, saying none of them had told him his son had made a league with David. Doeg the Edomite, who stood by, reported that he had seen David come to Nob, where Ahimelech the priest had inquired of the LORD for him and given him bread and the sword of Goliath. Saul summoned Ahimelech and all his father's house, the priests of Nob, and charged them with treason. Ahimelech answered that David was the king's faithful son-in-law, and that he had done nothing he did not do before. Saul commanded his footmen to slay the priests, but they refused to put forth their hand against the priests of the LORD. Then Saul told Doeg to do it, and Doeg fell upon them and slew eighty-five priests who wore a linen ephod that day, and then smote Nob itself, men and women, children and sucklings, oxen, asses, and sheep, with the edge of the sword. Only Abiathar, son of Ahimelech, escaped, and he fled to David, who said, 'I knew it that day, when Doeg the Edomite was there, that he would surely tell Saul: I have occasioned the death of all the persons of thy father's house. Abide thou with me, fear not.'
Discussion
Saul's paranoia turned a king's court into a massacre: the priests who had served David in innocence died because Saul could no longer distinguish loyalty from treason, and only Doeg would carry out the order. Reflect: 1) How does suspicion, left unchecked, become violence? 2) What do you do when an order you are given is plainly wrong? 3) How does David's taking responsibility for Abiathar's loss speak to the cost of our choices on others?
Significance
The massacre at Nob marks Saul's descent from jealous king to murderer of priests, and it drove Abiathar, the last priest of Eli's line, into David's camp.
