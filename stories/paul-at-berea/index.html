Paul at Berea

Story
Paul at Berea
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Acts 17:10-15
Retelling
The brethren immediately sent away Paul and Silas by night unto Berea, who coming thither went into the synagogue of the Jews. These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so. Therefore many of them believed, also of honourable women which were Greeks, and of men, not a few. But when the Jews of Thessalonica had knowledge that the word of God was preached of Paul at Berea, they came thither also, and stirred up the people. And then immediately the brethren sent away Paul to go as it were to the sea, but Silas and Timotheus abode there still. And they that conducted Paul brought him unto Athens, and receiving a commandment unto Silas and Timotheus for to come to him with all speed, they departed.
Discussion
The Bereans are Luke's model hearers: they receive the word eagerly and check it daily against the Scriptures, and their nobility is shown by study, not by suspicion. Even so, opposition follows Paul from Thessalonica, and he leaves alone for Athens. Reflect: 1) What does readiness of mind plus daily searching look like in your own hearing of preaching? 2) How do you test a message without hardening against it? 3) Why does Luke praise the Bereans rather than the crowds who believed at once?
Significance
Berea gives the church its pattern for discernment: welcome the word, then search the Scriptures daily to see whether these things are so, and believe on evidence rather than on excitement.
