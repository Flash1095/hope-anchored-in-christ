The Thousand Years

Story
The Thousand Years
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Revelation 20:1-6
Retelling
In a vision, John saw an angel come down from heaven, having the key of the bottomless pit and a great chain in his hand. And he laid hold on the dragon, that old serpent, which is the Devil, and Satan, and bound him a thousand years, and cast him into the bottomless pit, and shut him up, and set a seal upon him, that he should deceive the nations no more, till the thousand years should be fulfilled, and after that he must be loosed a little season. And John saw thrones, and they sat upon them, and judgment was given unto them, and he saw the souls of them that were beheaded for the witness of Jesus, and for the word of God, and which had not worshipped the beast, neither his image, neither had received his mark upon their foreheads, or in their hands, and they lived and reigned with Christ a thousand years. But the rest of the dead lived not again until the thousand years were finished. This is the first resurrection. Blessed and holy is he that hath part in the first resurrection: on such the second death hath no power, but they shall be priests of God and of Christ, and shall reign with him a thousand years.
Discussion
This is a vision, and its thousand years have been read in sharply different ways by faithful Christians: as a future earthly reign, as the present reign of the martyrs with Christ, and in other ways. This row reports the vision as John saw it, a bound dragon, thrones, beheaded witnesses living and reigning with Christ, and a first resurrection, without settling which reading is correct. What the vision states plainly is that the second death has no power over those who share in the first resurrection. Reflect: 1) Why does the vision bind Satan before describing the reign? 2) Who are those who live and reign, and what has their witness cost them? 3) How do you hold a debated vision with conviction about its centre and humility about its details?
Significance
The thousand years vision gives the martyrs their reign: the dragon is bound and sealed, the beheaded witnesses live and reign with Christ, and the second death has no power over those who share the first resurrection.
