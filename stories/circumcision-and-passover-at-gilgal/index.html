Circumcision and Passover at Gilgal

Retelling

When the kings of Canaan heard how the LORD had dried up the Jordan, their hearts melted with fear. At Gilgal, God told Joshua to circumcise the men of Israel, for the generation born in the wilderness had not been circumcised on the journey. After the whole nation was circumcised and had healed, the LORD said the reproach of Egypt was rolled away, and the place was called Gilgal. On the fourteenth day of the month the people kept the Passover on the plains of Jericho, and the next day they ate unleavened bread and parched grain from the produce of the land. On that very day the manna ceased, for Israel now ate the fruit of Canaan.

Discussion

Before any battle, Israel was marked and fed as God's covenant people. Circumcision renewed the covenant sign and Passover remembered the deliverance, so the conquest began with identity before achievement. Reflect: 1) What reminds you of who you belong to before you face a hard task? 2) How do you mark the end of a wilderness season in your own life? 3) Why does God feed his people from the land before asking them to fight for it?

Significance

At Gilgal Israel stopped living on wilderness provision and started living as a covenant nation in the land, with the reproach of Egypt rolled away and the Passover kept in Canaan for the first time.
