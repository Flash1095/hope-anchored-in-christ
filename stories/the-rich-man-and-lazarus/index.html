The Rich Man and Lazarus

Story
The Rich Man and Lazarus
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Luke 16:19-31
Retelling
There was a rich man who dressed in purple and fine linen and feasted sumptuously every day, and at his gate lay a beggar named Lazarus, covered with sores, longing to be fed with the crumbs from the rich man's table, while dogs licked his sores. Both died. Lazarus was carried to Abraham's side; the rich man, in torment, looked up and saw Abraham far off with Lazarus beside him, and begged for a drop of water and for Lazarus to be sent to warn his five brothers. Abraham answered that a great chasm was fixed between them, and that his brothers had Moses and the Prophets: if they would not hear them, they would not be persuaded even if someone rose from the dead. This Lazarus is a figure of the parable, not Lazarus of Bethany, whom Jesus raised.
Discussion
The rich man's sin was not cruelty in the story's telling but indifference: a gate between feast and beggar. After death, the gate became a chasm. Reflect: Who lies at your gate, visible daily and unhelped? Do you listen to Moses and the Prophets, or are you waiting for a more dramatic sign? What would hearing Scripture actually change in your spending?
Significance
The rich man and Lazarus warn that wealth used without mercy fixes its own divide, and that Scripture, not spectacle, is God's sufficient warning.
