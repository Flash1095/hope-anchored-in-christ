Judah and Tamar

Judah and Tamar

Retelling

At that time Judah went down from his brothers and stayed with Hirah, an Adullamite, and married a daughter of a Canaanite named Shuah. She bore him three sons: Er, Onan and Shelah. Judah took a wife for Er, his firstborn, whose name was Tamar. Er was wicked in the sight of the LORD, and the LORD took his life. Judah then told Onan to marry his brother's widow and raise up a child for his brother, but Onan refused to carry out that duty, and what he did displeased the LORD, and the LORD took his life also. Judah told Tamar to remain a widow in her father's house until Shelah was grown, saying he feared Shelah might die as his brothers had, and Tamar went to live in her father's house. In time Judah's wife, the daughter of Shuah, died, and when Judah was comforted he went up to Timnath to his sheepshearers with his friend Hirah. Tamar was told her father-in-law was going to Timnath, and seeing that Shelah was grown and she had not been given to him as a wife, she laid aside her widow's garments, covered herself with a veil, and sat in an open place by the way to Timnath. Judah saw her there and, not recognising her as his daughter-in-law, made an arrangement with her, giving her his signet, his bracelets and his staff as a pledge until he sent her a kid from the flock, and she conceived by him. When Judah sent the kid by Hirah to recover the pledge, the woman could not be found, and the men of the place said no such woman had been there. About three months later Judah was told that Tamar was with child, and he ordered her to be brought out and burned. As she was brought out, she sent the signet, bracelets and staff to her father-in-law, saying, "By the man, whose these are, am I with child," and asking him to discern whose they were. Judah acknowledged them and said, "She hath been more righteous than I; because that I gave her not to Shelah my son." He did not approach her again. When Tamar gave birth, twins were in her womb: one put out his hand, and the midwife bound a scarlet thread on it, but he drew his hand back and his brother came out first, and was called Pharez, because of the breach; afterwards came his brother with the scarlet thread, called Zarah. Through Pharez the family line runs on to David and to Christ.

Themes

Grace; Redemption; God's Providence; Messiah

Discussion

Judah and Tamar is a story of failure on every side, yet God worked through it to preserve the line of promise. Grace does not excuse wrongdoing, but it does mean that no family's failures are beyond God's redeeming purpose. Reflect: 1) How does God's grace working through flawed people change the way you see your own family story? 2) What does Judah's confession, "She hath been more righteous than I," teach us about owning our part honestly? 3) How does seeing Tamar and Pharez in the line to Christ deepen your hope that God keeps his promises?

Significance

From this tangled family story comes Pharez, through whom the line of Judah runs to David and to Christ. It is a striking proof that God's Messianic promise advances by grace through flawed people, not through perfect ones.
