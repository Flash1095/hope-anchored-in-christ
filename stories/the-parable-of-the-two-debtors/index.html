The Parable of the Two Debtors

Story
The Parable of the Two Debtors
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Luke 7:41-43
Retelling
While Jesus reclined at a Pharisee's table and a sinful woman anointed his feet, he told his host Simon this parable: Two people owed money to a certain moneylender. One owed him five hundred denarii, and the other fifty. Neither of them had the money to pay him back, so he forgave the debts of both. Now which of them will love him more? Simon replied, I suppose the one who had the bigger debt forgiven. You have judged correctly, Jesus said, and he turned the parable on Simon: the woman's many sins were forgiven, as her great love showed, while Simon, who had shown little love, presumed a smaller debt. The parable is told inside the anointing episode, but it is a complete parable of its own, with its own question and answer.
Discussion
Forgiven much, love much: the size of love reveals the size of debt we admit, and the Pharisee's small love betrayed a small sense of debt. Reflect: 1) Do you see yourself as the fifty-denarii debtor or the five-hundred debtor, and what does that do to your love? 2) How does the woman's love prove forgiveness rather than earn it? 3) Where does respectable religion hide an unpaid debt?
Significance
Two debtors, one forgiven five hundred and one fifty, show that the one forgiven more loves more, exposing Simon's cold welcome beside the sinful woman's lavish love.
