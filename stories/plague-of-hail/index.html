The Plague of Hail

Story
The Plague of Hail
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Exodus 9:13-35
Retelling
Moses warned Pharaoh that God would send grievous hail, such as had never been in Egypt, and that man and beast left in the field would die. Those servants of Pharaoh who feared the word of the LORD brought their servants and cattle indoors; those who disregarded it left them in the field. Moses stretched out his rod toward heaven, and the LORD sent thunder, hail, and fire running along the ground. The hail smote every herb and tree and every man and beast in the field, but in Goshen there was no hail. Pharaoh confessed, I have sinned this time, and asked Moses to pray, promising release. Moses prayed, the thunder and hail ceased, but when Pharaoh saw the rain and hail stop, he sinned yet more and hardened his heart.
Discussion
Hail is the first plague with a way of escape announced in advance: fear the LORD's word and shelter. Some Egyptians do, and live. Pharaoh confesses under thunder and retracts in sunshine. Reflect: 1) What does it mean to fear God's word before judgment falls? 2) Why does Pharaoh's confession not last? 3) How does Goshen's shelter picture God's protection in judgment?
Significance
The hail shows both God's mercy in warning and his power in judgment, separating those who heed his word from those who disregard it, and exposing a confession made only under pressure.
