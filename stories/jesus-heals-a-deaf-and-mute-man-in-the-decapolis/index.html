Jesus Heals a Deaf and Mute Man in the Decapolis

Jesus Heals a Deaf and Mute Man in the Decapolis

Retelling

After leaving Tyre and Sidon, Jesus went through the Decapolis to the Sea of Galilee, where some people brought to him a man who was deaf and could hardly talk, and begged Jesus to place his hand on him. Jesus took him aside, away from the crowd, put his fingers into the man's ears, spat and touched his tongue, looked up to heaven and with a deep sigh said to him, Ephphatha! which means, Be opened! At this the man's ears were opened, his tongue was loosened, and he began to speak plainly. Jesus commanded them not to tell anyone, but the more he did so, the more they kept talking about it, and people were overwhelmed with amazement, saying, He has done everything well. He even makes the deaf hear and the mute speak.

Discussion

Jesus took the man aside and healed him privately, with touch, sigh, and a word: Be opened. Reflect: 1) What does Jesus' private, tactile care say about dignity for those who cannot hear or speak? 2) How does Ephphatha speak to closed ears and tied tongues in your own life? 3) Why does amazement overflow even a command to silence?

Significance

In the Decapolis Jesus opened the ears and loosened the tongue of a deaf and mute man with the word Ephphatha, and the Gentile crowds declared he had done everything well.
