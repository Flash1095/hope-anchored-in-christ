Apollos Taught at Ephesus

Story
Apollos Taught at Ephesus
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Acts 18:24-28
Retelling
A certain Jew named Apollos, born at Alexandria, an eloquent man, and mighty in the scriptures, came to Ephesus. This man was instructed in the way of the Lord, and being fervent in the spirit, he spake and taught diligently the things of the Lord, knowing only the baptism of John. And he began to speak boldly in the synagogue, whom when Aquila and Priscilla had heard, they took him unto them, and expounded unto him the way of God more perfectly. And when he was disposed to pass into Achaia, the brethren wrote, exhorting the disciples to receive him, who, when he was come, helped them much which had believed through grace, for he mightily convinced the Jews, and that publicly, shewing by the scriptures that Jesus was Christ.
Discussion
Apollos is gifted, eloquent, and incomplete, and Aquila and Priscilla correct him privately rather than shaming him publicly. Their quiet teaching turns a partial preacher into a powerful defender of the faith in Achaia. Reflect: 1) How do you correct a gifted person without crushing them? 2) What does it mean to know the Scriptures mightily and still need the way expounded more perfectly? 3) Why does private correction produce public fruit?
Significance
Apollos shows that zeal needs completion: a learned preacher who knows only John's baptism is gently taught the fuller way, and then mightily convinces the Jews from the Scriptures that Jesus is Christ.
