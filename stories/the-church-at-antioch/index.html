The Church at Antioch

Story
The Church at Antioch
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Acts 11:19-26
Retelling
They which were scattered abroad upon the persecution that arose about Stephen travelled as far as Phenice, and Cyprus, and Antioch, preaching the word to none but unto the Jews only. But some of them were men of Cyprus and Cyrene, which, when they were come to Antioch, spake unto the Grecians, preaching the Lord Jesus. And the hand of the Lord was with them, and a great number believed, and turned unto the Lord. Then tidings of these things came unto the ears of the church which was in Jerusalem, and they sent forth Barnabas, that he should go as far as Antioch. When he came and had seen the grace of God, he was glad, and exhorted them all that with purpose of heart they would cleave unto the Lord, for he was a good man, and full of the Holy Ghost and of faith, and much people was added unto the Lord. Then departed Barnabas to Tarsus, for to seek Saul, and when he had found him, he brought him unto Antioch. And it came to pass that a whole year they assembled themselves with the church, and taught much people. And the disciples were called Christians first in Antioch.
Discussion
Antioch is where the gospel crosses to Gentiles without a council first, and where Jerusalem's response is not suspicion but Barnabas's gladness. A whole year of teaching follows, and a new name is born. Reflect: 1) How do you respond when God works beyond the lines you expected? 2) What does a year of steady teaching do that a moment of excitement cannot? 3) What does it mean to be called a Christian before the world gives you any other name?
Significance
Antioch becomes the missionary church: the first place where Gentiles are reached in numbers, where Barnabas and Saul teach together for a year, and where the disciples first bear the name Christian.
