The Coin in the Fish's Mouth

The Coin in the Fish's Mouth

Retelling

After Jesus and his disciples arrived in Capernaum, the collectors of the temple tax came to Peter and asked, Doesn't your teacher pay the temple tax? Peter answered, Yes, he does, and when he entered the house Jesus spoke first, asking from whom the kings of the earth collect duty and taxes, from their own children or from others? Peter answered, From others, and Jesus said, Then the children are exempt. But so that we may not cause offence, he told Peter to go to the lake, throw out a line, and take the first fish he caught; opening its mouth, he would find a four-drachma coin, enough for the tax for both of them. Peter did so, and paid the tax for Jesus and himself.

Discussion

The Son, exempt as a child of the King, still paid the tax to avoid needless offence, and provided it by a miracle: freedom exercised as consideration for others. Reflect: 1) Where does insisting on your rights cause offence that love would avoid? 2) How does a coin in a fish's mouth show God's provision in ordinary work? 3) What does it mean that Jesus paid a temple tax for the temple of his own Father?

Significance

Jesus, though exempt as the King's Son, miraculously provided the temple tax for himself and Peter from a fish's mouth, so as not to give offence.
