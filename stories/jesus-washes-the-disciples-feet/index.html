Jesus Washes the Disciples' Feet

Story
Jesus Washes the Disciples' Feet
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
John 13:1-17
Retelling
On the evening before Passover, Jesus, knowing the Father had put all things under his power and that he had come from God and was returning to God, rose from the meal, took off his outer clothing, wrapped a towel around his waist, poured water into a basin, and began to wash his disciples' feet, drying them with the towel. When he came to Simon Peter, Peter protested, Lord, are you going to wash my feet? Jesus answered that unless he washed him, Peter would have no part with him, and Peter then asked for his hands and head as well. After washing them all, Jesus put on his clothes and asked whether they understood: he, their Lord and Teacher, had washed their feet, and they ought to wash one another's feet, for he had set them an example to do as he had done.
Discussion
The Lord of all took the slave's task on the night of betrayal: greatness in his kingdom is measured by washing feet. Reflect: 1) Where does pride keep you from accepting Jesus' cleansing service? 2) What foot-washing task, literal or figurative, is yours toward someone who may betray you? 3) How does this scene reframe leadership in your home, church, or work?
Significance
On his last evening Jesus enacted servanthood as the shape of discipleship, cleansing his followers and commanding them to serve one another as he had served them.
