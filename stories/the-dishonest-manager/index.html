The Dishonest Manager

The Dishonest Manager

Retelling

There was a rich man whose manager was accused of wasting his goods. The master told him he would lose his job and demanded an account. The manager, too weak to dig and too proud to beg, acted quickly: he called his master's debtors one by one and cut their bills — a hundred measures of oil to fifty, a hundred measures of wheat to eighty — so that when he was dismissed, they would welcome him into their homes. Surprisingly, the master commended the dishonest manager for his shrewdness, and Jesus drew the lesson: the children of this world are more shrewd in dealing with their own generation than the children of light. He added that believers should make friends with unrighteous wealth, so that when it fails, they will be welcomed into eternal dwellings, and that whoever is faithful with very little will be faithful with much, while no one can serve both God and money.

Discussion

Jesus praised the manager's shrewdness, not his dishonesty: he used a closing window wisely, while believers often drift. The parable asks whether eternity shapes today's money at all. Reflect: Are you as deliberate about eternal things as this manager was about his next meal? What would using wealth to make eternal friends look like for you? Which master — God or money — does your ledger say you serve?

Significance

The dishonest manager teaches shrewd, faithful use of worldly wealth for eternal ends, and the impossibility of serving both God and money.

Themes

Stewardship; Wealth; Shrewdness; Faithfulness
