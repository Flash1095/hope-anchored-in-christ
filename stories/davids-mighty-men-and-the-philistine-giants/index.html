David's Mighty Men and the Philistine Giants

Story
David's Mighty Men and the Philistine Giants
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Samuel 21:15-22
Retelling
In David's later wars with the Philistines, four brief encounters with giants are recorded. At Gob, David grew faint in battle against the Philistines, and Ishbi-benob, a giant whose spear weighed three hundred shekels of brass and who wore a new sword, thought to slay him, but Abishai, son of Zeruiah, rescued David and killed the giant. After that David's men swore he would go out no more to battle, lest he quench the light of Israel. At Gob again, Sibbechai the Hushathite slew Saph, another giant. In another battle at Gob, Elhanan, son of Jaare-oregim, slew the brother of Goliath of Gath, whose spear staff was like a weaver's beam. And at Gath there was a man of great stature with six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot, twenty-four in number, who defied Israel, and Jonathan, son of Shimea, David's brother, slew him. These four were born to the giant in Gath, and they fell by the hand of David and his servants.
Discussion
The giant-killings of David's old age are told as a roll of honour, not a single battle: the next generation, Abishai, Sibbechai, Elhanan, and Jonathan, finished what David's youth had begun. Reflect: 1) How does a leader hand the hardest fights to the next generation? 2) What does it mean that David's men protected the light of Israel by keeping him from battle? 3) Which giants in your life belong to others to face, not to you?
Significance
The four giant-killings of David's later wars show his mighty men carrying on the fight David began as a youth. Doubt: the passage is a series of four brief notices rather than one narrated battle, so the encounters cannot be dated or ordered beyond the text's sequence.
