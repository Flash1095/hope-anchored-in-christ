Hezekiah's Illness and Recovery

Retelling

In those days Hezekiah, king of Judah, fell sick unto death, and the prophet Isaiah came to him with a hard message: set your house in order, for you shall die and not live. Hezekiah turned his face to the wall and prayed, weeping, reminding God of his faithful walk. Before Isaiah had left the court, God told him to return and say God had heard his prayer and seen his tears, and would heal him, adding fifteen years to his life and delivering Jerusalem from Assyria. As a sign, Isaiah asked whether the shadow on the sundial of Ahaz should go forward ten degrees or back ten degrees. Hezekiah said it was easy for a shadow to go forward, so let it go backward. Isaiah cried to the LORD, and the shadow returned backward ten degrees. Hezekiah recovered, and later wrote a psalm praising God for his healing.

Discussion

Hezekiah prayed honestly in the face of a death sentence, and God answered with healing and a sign in the sky. Prayer here is not a formula but a relationship, and the king's tears are taken seriously. Reflect: 1) How do you pray when the news is final? 2) What does it mean that God heard Hezekiah's prayer and saw his tears? 3) How do you thank God after a deliverance, as Hezekiah did in writing?

Significance

Hezekiah's recovery is distinct from his deliverance from Sennacherib: it shows the same king turning from a death sentence to fifteen more years by prayer, with a backward shadow as its sign.
