The Feeding of the Four Thousand

The Feeding of the Four Thousand

Retelling

A great crowd had followed Jesus for three days in the region of the Decapolis and had nothing left to eat. Jesus told his disciples he had compassion on them and would not send them away hungry, lest they faint on the road. The disciples, who had already seen five thousand fed, still asked where enough bread could be found in such a remote place. Jesus asked what they had: seven loaves and a few small fish. He gave thanks, broke the food, and had the disciples distribute it. Four thousand men, besides women and children, ate and were satisfied, and seven baskets of leftovers were gathered. This is a distinct event from the feeding of the five thousand, not a second telling of it: different crowd, different numbers, different place, and Jesus himself later treats the two feedings as separate.

Discussion

The disciples' forgetfulness is the point: yesterday's miracle did not guarantee today's trust. Compassion, not calculation, set the table. Reflect: Where are you asking 'where will it come from?' after God has already provided? What seven loaves — small, insufficient-looking resources — are you holding back? How does gathering leftovers honour God's abundance?

Significance

The second feeding confirms Jesus' compassion for Gentile crowds as well as Jewish ones and exposes how slowly even eyewitnesses learn to trust.
