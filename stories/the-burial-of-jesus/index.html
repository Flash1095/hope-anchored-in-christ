The Burial of Jesus

Story
The Burial of Jesus
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 27:57-66; Mark 15:42-47; Luke 23:50-56; John 19:38-42
Retelling
As evening approached on Preparation Day, Joseph of Arimathea, a rich and respected member of the council who had not consented to Jesus' condemnation and was himself waiting for the kingdom of God, went boldly to Pilate and asked for Jesus' body. Pilate, surprised he was already dead, confirmed it with the centurion and gave the body to Joseph. With Nicodemus, who brought about a hundred pounds weight of myrrh and aloes, Joseph took the body, wrapped it in clean linen cloths with the spices, and laid it in his own new tomb cut out of rock, in a garden near where Jesus was crucified. He rolled a great stone across the entrance and went away, while Mary Magdalene and the other Mary sat opposite the tomb. The next day the chief priests asked Pilate to secure the tomb, and they sealed the stone and posted a guard.
Discussion
Joseph and Nicodemus, quiet sympathisers until now, stepped into the open at the costliest moment: the burial made Jesus' death public, honourable, and verifiable. Reflect: 1) What does Joseph's boldness at evening say about courage that arrives late but still counts? 2) Why does the sealed, guarded tomb matter for the resurrection claim? 3) How do the women sitting opposite the tomb model faithful presence when nothing can be done?
Significance
Jesus was truly buried by named, honourable witnesses in a new rock tomb, sealed and guarded, so that the empty tomb three days later could not be explained as confusion about where he lay.
