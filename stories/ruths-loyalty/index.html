Ruth's Loyalty

Ruth's Loyalty

Retelling

In the days of the judges, famine drove Naomi and her family to Moab, where her sons married Moabite women — and then Naomi lost her husband and both sons. Broken and bitter, she told her daughters-in-law to return to their own people, but Ruth clung to her and said, 'Where you go I will go, and where you stay I will stay. Your people will be my people and your God my God.' Ruth gleaned in the fields of Boaz to keep them alive, Boaz redeemed the family land and married Ruth, and their great-grandson was David, king of Israel — through whom Jesus would come.

Themes

Loyalty; Redemption; Kindness

Discussion

Ruth's story is small and quiet — no battles, no miracles, just loyalty, hard work, and kindness — and God weaves it into the lineage of the Messiah. Faithfulness in ordinary life is never wasted. Reflect: 1) Who has shown you Ruth-like loyalty? 2) How has God provided for you through ordinary people? 3) What does Ruth teach about belonging to God's people?

Significance

In the dark days of the judges, quiet loyalty and kindness carried the line of the Messiah. Ruth anchors hope that God weaves ordinary faithfulness — and outsiders welcomed in — into His greatest stories.
