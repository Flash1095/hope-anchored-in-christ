Jacob and Laban

Story
Jacob and Laban
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Genesis 29-31
Retelling
Jacob journeyed to the land of the people of the east and came to a well near Haran, where he met Rachel, Laban's daughter, coming with her father's sheep, and rolled the stone from the well's mouth to water the flock. Laban welcomed his sister's son, and Jacob stayed with him a month before agreeing to serve seven years for Rachel, his younger daughter. The seven years seemed to him but a few days, for the love he had for her; but on the wedding night Laban brought Leah, the elder daughter, instead, and Jacob served seven other years for Rachel as well. Leah bore Reuben, Simeon, Levi and Judah; Bilhah, Rachel's maid, bore Dan and Naphtali; Zilpah, Leah's maid, bore Gad and Asher; Leah then bore Issachar, Zebulun and a daughter, Dinah; and at last God remembered Rachel and opened her womb, and she bore Joseph. When Jacob asked to leave, Laban urged him to stay, saying the LORD had blessed him for Jacob's sake, and they agreed Jacob's wages would be the speckled and spotted cattle and the brown sheep. Jacob's flocks increased exceedingly, but Laban's sons complained that Jacob had taken all their father's wealth, and Laban's countenance was no longer toward him as before. The LORD told Jacob to return to the land of his fathers, and Rachel and Leah agreed, saying their father had counted them strangers and devoured their money. So Jacob fled with his wives, sons, cattle and goods toward Canaan, while Rachel took her father's household images without Jacob's knowledge. Laban pursued seven days' journey and overtook him in Mount Gilead, but God warned Laban in a dream by night to speak to Jacob neither good nor bad. Laban searched every tent and found nothing, and Jacob answered that he had served twenty years in Laban's house, fourteen years for his two daughters and six years for his cattle, with his wages changed ten times, and that only the God of his father had kept him from being sent away empty. Then they made a covenant: Jacob set up a pillar and a heap of stones called Galeed, and Mizpah, as a witness that neither would pass the heap to harm the other. They ate together on the mount, and in the morning Laban kissed his sons and his daughters, blessed them, and returned to his place.
Key People

Themes
Family; God's Providence; Faithfulness of God; Perseverance
Discussion
Jacob was deceived as he had deceived, yet God was at work through long, unfair years, building a family and keeping his promise. Faithfulness is often proved not in a moment but over twenty years of ordinary labour. Reflect: 1) How do you keep trusting God when a situation feels unfair or slow to change? 2) Where have you seen God provide even when others changed the terms on you? 3) What would it look like to end a strained relationship, as Jacob and Laban did, with a clear and peaceful boundary?

Significance
In Laban's house the twelve tribes of Israel began: the sons born to Leah, Rachel, Bilhah and Zilpah became the fathers of the nation God had promised. God's covenant advanced through a flawed, quarrelling household, not around it.
