The Second Passover

Story
The Second Passover
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Numbers 9:1-14
Retelling
In the second year after leaving Egypt, in the first month, God commanded Israel to keep the Passover at its appointed time in the wilderness of Sinai, and they did so. But certain men were defiled by a dead body and could not keep the Passover on that day. They came before Moses and Aaron and asked why they should be kept back from offering the LORD's offering among Israel at its appointed time. Moses told them to stand by until he heard what the LORD would command. God answered that if any man was unclean by a dead body or on a far journey, he should keep the Passover in the second month, on the fourteenth day, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs, keeping all its ordinances. But the man who was clean and not on a journey and yet neglected the Passover would be cut off from his people.
Discussion
The second Passover holds two truths together: God's appointed times matter, and God's mercy makes room for the defiled and the distant. A missed feast need not mean exclusion. Reflect: 1) How does this ruling show both order and mercy? 2) Where have you felt kept back from worship, and what does this case say to you? 3) Why does deliberate neglect still carry a serious consequence?
Significance
The second Passover extends God's feast to the unclean and the traveller without loosening its ordinances, showing a God who keeps his appointments and opens a way back into them. Doubt: this is a brief case ruling prompted by a request, rather than a full narrative episode; it is included because the men, their question, and God's ruling are narrated, but it should be weighed as borderline if the collection requires fuller stories only.
