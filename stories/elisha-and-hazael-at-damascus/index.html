Elisha and Hazael at Damascus

Story
Elisha and Hazael at Damascus
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Kings 8:7-15
Retelling
Elisha came to Damascus, and Ben-hadad, king of Syria, was sick. When the king heard the man of God had come, he sent Hazael with a present of forty camels' burden of all the good things of Damascus to ask whether he would recover. Elisha told Hazael to tell the king he would certainly recover, and then added that the LORD had shown him the king would surely die. Elisha stared at Hazael and wept, until Hazael asked why his lord wept. Elisha answered that he knew the evil Hazael would do to Israel: he would set their strongholds on fire, slay their young men with the sword, dash their children, and rip up their women with child. Hazael protested, 'But what, is thy servant a dog, that he should do this great thing?' Elisha answered, 'The LORD hath shewed me that thou shalt be king over Syria.' Hazael returned, told Ben-hadad he would recover, and the next day took a thick cloth, dipped it in water, and spread it over the king's face so that he died, and Hazael reigned in his stead.
Discussion
Elisha wept while prophesying Hazael's rise, because he saw the suffering a new king would bring to Israel. Hazael's protest, 'is thy servant a dog,' became his own history within a day. Reflect: 1) How do you respond to knowledge of coming suffering that you cannot prevent? 2) What ambitions in you protest innocence while already moving toward power? 3) Why does Elisha weep instead of merely announcing?
Significance
Elisha's visit to Damascus announced Hazael's kingship over Syria with tears, for he foresaw the evil Hazael would do to Israel.
