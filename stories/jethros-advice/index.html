Jethro's Advice

Story
Jethro's Advice
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Exodus 18
Retelling
Jethro, Moses' father-in-law and priest of Midian, heard all God had done for Moses and Israel, and brought Zipporah and her sons to Moses at the mount of God. He rejoiced, offered sacrifice, and ate bread with Aaron and the elders before God. The next day Jethro watched Moses sit alone from morning to evening judging the people, who stood around him all day. He warned Moses that this was not good: both Moses and the people would surely wear away. He advised Moses to teach the people God's statutes, to choose able men who feared God, loved truth, and hated covetousness, and to set them as rulers over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens, judging small matters themselves and bringing only great matters to Moses. Moses listened to his father-in-law's voice and did all that he said.
Discussion
Jethro, an outsider, sees what Moses cannot: a leader doing everything alone will wear out himself and his people. Wise counsel delegates judgment and preserves the leader for God. Reflect: 1) Where are you doing alone what should be shared? 2) How do you receive correction from someone outside your circle? 3) What qualities does Jethro look for in a judge, and why do they matter?
Significance
Jethro's advice establishes shared leadership for Israel, teaching that God's servant leads best not by carrying every burden alone but by appointing trustworthy judges under God's law.
