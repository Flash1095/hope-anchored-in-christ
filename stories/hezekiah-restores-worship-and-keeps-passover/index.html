Hezekiah Restores Worship and Keeps Passover

Hezekiah Restores Worship and Keeps Passover

Retelling

In the first year of his reign, in the first month, Hezekiah opened the doors of the house of the LORD, which his father Ahaz had shut, and repaired them. He gathered the priests and Levites and told them to sanctify themselves and cleanse the house, for their fathers had trespassed, turned their faces from the LORD's habitation, shut the doors, put out the lamps, and ceased to burn incense or offer burnt offerings, so that God's wrath had fallen on Judah. The Levites cleansed the temple for eight days, carried the filth to the brook Kidron, and sanctified the house by the sixteenth day, and Hezekiah offered seven bullocks, seven rams, seven lambs, and seven he-goats as a sin offering for the kingdom, the sanctuary, and Judah. Then Hezekiah sent letters throughout Israel and Judah, from Beersheba to Dan, inviting all Israel to come to Jerusalem to keep the Passover to the LORD, for they had not kept it in great numbers as it was written. Some laughed the messengers to scorn, but some humbled themselves and came, and a very great congregation kept the Passover in the second month, since the priests had not sanctified themselves in time for the first. Many from Ephraim, Manasseh, Issachar, and Zebulun ate the Passover without being cleansed, and Hezekiah prayed for them, 'The good LORD pardon every one that prepareth his heart to seek God, although he be not cleansed according to the purification of the sanctuary,' and the LORD hearkened and healed the people. They kept the feast seven days with gladness, and then kept another seven days, and the whole assembly of Judah, the priests, the Levites, and the strangers from Israel rejoiced, for since the days of Solomon there had not been such joy in Jerusalem. Afterward the people went out, broke the images and high places throughout Judah, Benjamin, Ephraim, and Manasseh, and Hezekiah appointed the courses of priests and Levites and commanded the people's contributions, so that there were heaps of firstfruits and tithes.

Discussion

Hezekiah's reform began with cleaning a shut temple and ended with a Passover of unprecedented joy, in which the king prayed for unclean worshippers and God healed them. Order and mercy met: the temple was cleansed, and the feast was kept by people who were not yet clean. Reflect: 1) What shut doors in your life need opening before anything else can be repaired? 2) How does Hezekiah's prayer for the unclean shape the way you welcome imperfect worshippers? 3) Why does the chronicler say there had been no such joy since Solomon?

Significance

Hezekiah's restoration of worship and Passover cleansed Ahaz's shut temple and gathered a joyful, imperfect multitude that God pardoned and healed.
