The Walls of Jericho

The Walls of Jericho

Retelling

After forty years in the wilderness, Israel stood at the edge of the promised land facing Jericho, a city with walls so thick that houses were built into them. God gave Joshua a battle plan that made no military sense: march around the city once a day for six days, then seven times on the seventh day, with priests blowing trumpets and the people shouting. When they did exactly what God said, the walls fell flat, and Israel took the city. Only Rahab and her family were spared, because she had hidden Israel's spies and believed Israel's God was the true God.

Themes

Obedience; Faith; Victory

Discussion

Jericho fell to a strategy that looked foolish — because the battle belonged to the Lord, not to Israel's army. Obedience that looks strange is often the very thing God uses. Reflect: 1) What 'strange' obedience might God be asking of you? 2) How does Rahab's story show that no one is beyond God's mercy? 3) Where are you trusting your own strength instead of God's plan?

Significance

Jericho fell not by siege engines but by obedient faith, and a scarlet cord saved an outsider who trusted Israel's God. Hope here is for impossible walls and unlikely people alike.
