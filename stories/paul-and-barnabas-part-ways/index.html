Paul and Barnabas Part Ways

Retelling

Some days after the Jerusalem Council, Paul said unto Barnabas, Let us go again and visit our brethren in every city where we have preached the word of the Lord, and see how they do. And Barnabas determined to take with them John, whose surname was Mark. But Paul thought not good to take him with them, who departed from them from Pamphylia, and went not with them to the work. And the contention was so sharp between them, that they departed asunder one from the other: and so Barnabas took Mark, and sailed unto Cyprus, and Paul chose Silas, and departed, being recommended by the brethren unto the grace of God. And he went through Syria and Cilicia, confirming the churches.

Discussion

Two faithful men, who had risked their lives together, disagree so sharply over Mark that they part, and the mission doubles rather than dies: Barnabas takes Mark to Cyprus, and Paul takes Silas through Syria and Cilicia. Luke does not hide the contention or resolve it with blame. Reflect: 1) How do you disagree with a fellow worker without breaking fellowship? 2) What does it mean that God works through a painful parting? 3) How did Barnabas's second chance to Mark later bear fruit?

Significance

The parting of Paul and Barnabas shows that even apostles disagree sharply, that a disagreement over a person can be honest on both sides, and that the mission goes forward through two teams instead of one.

Disagreement; Second Chances; Mission; Fellowship
