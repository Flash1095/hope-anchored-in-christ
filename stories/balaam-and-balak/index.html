Balaam and Balak

Balaam and Balak

Retelling

Balak, king of Moab, terrified by Israel's approach, hired Balaam, a prophet-for-hire, to curse them. God first forbade Balaam to go, then permitted the journey with a warning to speak only God's word. On the road, Balaam's donkey saw the angel of the Lord barring the way and refused to pass; Balaam beat her three times until God opened the donkey's mouth to protest and opened Balaam's eyes to the angel with drawn sword. At Moab, Balak took Balaam to three high places, but each time God turned the intended curse into blessing, and Balaam instead foretold a star rising out of Jacob. Balak sent him home empty-handed, unable to curse a people God had blessed.

Discussion

No payment, fear, or ritual can reverse God's blessing, and God can use even a donkey to stop a prophet in his tracks. Reflect: 1) Where are you tempted to buy or bargain for spiritual influence? 2) How do you respond when God blocks a path you wanted? 3) What does Balaam's blessing of Israel say about God's faithfulness to his promises?

Significance

Three times a hired prophet tried to curse Israel and three times God made him bless them, proving that God's blessing on his people cannot be bought, frightened, or cursed away.
