Jeremiah Wears the Yoke

Jeremiah Wears the Yoke

Retelling

The LORD told Jeremiah to make bonds and yokes and put them on his neck, and to send word by messengers to the neighbouring kings and to Zedekiah, king of Judah: submit to the yoke of the king of Babylon and live, for God had given the nations into Nebuchadnezzar's hand, and resistance would bring sword, famine, and pestilence. In the Temple, the prophet Hananiah broke the yoke from Jeremiah's neck and declared that within two years God would break Babylon's yoke and bring back the Temple vessels and the exiles. Jeremiah answered that the LORD had not sent Hananiah, who made the people trust in a lie, and that Hananiah would die that year — and he did, in the seventh month.

Discussion

Two prophets, two messages, and only one from God. Jeremiah's yoke was an unpopular truth worn in public, while Hananiah offered the crowd what it wanted to hear. Reflect: 1) How do you tell a comforting lie from a hard truth? 2) What unpopular obedience might God be asking you to wear openly? 3) Why does God sometimes command submission to a painful path rather than escape from it?

Significance

True prophecy is tested by God's word and by events, not by how welcome the message is. Doubt: this is a prophetic sign-act with a narrative frame, so it is drafted as borderline rather than as an ordinary historical episode.
