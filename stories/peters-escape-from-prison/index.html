Peter's Escape from Prison

Peter's Escape from Prison

Retelling

Herod Agrippa had killed James, the brother of John, with the sword, and, seeing that it pleased the Jewish leaders, arrested Peter too, intending to bring him out after the Passover. Peter was kept in prison while the church prayed earnestly for him. The night before Herod was to bring him out, an angel of the Lord appeared in the cell, struck Peter's side, and woke him: Get up quickly. The chains fell off, and the angel told him to dress, put on his sandals and cloak, and follow. Peter thought he was seeing a vision until they passed the guards, the iron gate opened by itself, and the angel left him on the street. Coming to himself, Peter went to the house of Mary, the mother of John Mark, where believers were praying — and when the servant girl Rhoda recognised his voice at the gate, she ran in without opening it, while the praying believers insisted it must be his angel.

Discussion

The church prayed for Peter's release and then disbelieved it when it knocked. God answered beyond their faith, not because of it. Reflect: Do you pray expecting an answer, or merely performing prayer? What iron gates in your life need an angel rather than a strategy? How does Rhoda's joy — forgetting to open the door — describe your own answered prayers?

Significance

Peter's escape shows the praying church answered beyond its own expectation and marks the end of Peter's Jerusalem leadership as the mission turns to Paul.
