Amos and Amaziah

Story
Amos and Amaziah
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Amos 7:10-17
Retelling
After Amos proclaimed judgment on Israel, Amaziah, priest of Bethel, sent word to Jeroboam, king of Israel, accusing Amos of conspiracy and claiming the land could not bear his words. Amaziah then told Amos to flee to Judah and prophesy there, and never again at Bethel, the king's sanctuary. Amos answered that he was no prophet and no prophet's son, but a herdsman and a dresser of sycamore trees, whom the LORD had taken from following the flock and told to prophesy to Israel. He then declared God's word against Amaziah himself: his wife would fall in the city, his children by the sword, his land divided, and he would die in an unclean land, while Israel would surely go into exile.
Discussion
Amos had no credentials except God's call, and that was enough to stand before priest and king. Institutions often prefer a quiet prophet who blesses what they already do. Reflect: 1) Whose approval are you tempted to need before speaking truth? 2) How does Amos's ordinary background as a herdsman encourage you? 3) What happens when religious authority is used to silence God's word rather than to hear it?
Significance
God's call, not office or pedigree, authorises His messenger — and silencing the messenger does not silence the message. Doubt: this is a brief confrontation embedded in a cycle of visions, so it is drafted as borderline rather than as a standalone story.
