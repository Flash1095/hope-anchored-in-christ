David Spares Saul at Ziph

David Spares Saul at Ziph

Retelling

Saul was hunting David again, this time in the wilderness of Ziph, and David crept into Saul's camp at night with Abishai. The whole camp lay in a deep sleep from the LORD, and Saul lay asleep with his spear stuck in the ground by his head. Abishai urged David to let him pin Saul to the earth with a single thrust, promising he would not need a second. David refused: who could stretch out his hand against the LORD's anointed and be guiltless? Instead he took Saul's spear and water jug from beside his head and withdrew to a hilltop. From a safe distance he called out, waking the camp, and showed what he had taken. Once again Saul confessed he had sinned and blessed David, and David went on his way, still refusing to kill the king who was trying to kill him.

Discussion

The second sparing is harder than the first, because this time David's men offered him a clean, one-blow solution, and he still said no. Mercy repeated becomes character. Reflect: 1) Why is a second chance to do wrong sometimes harder to refuse than the first? 2) What does David's restraint teach about revenge dressed up as justice? 3) How do you respond when others urge you to settle a score?

Significance

At Ziph, David spared Saul a second time, confirming that his refusal to seize the throne by murder was conviction, not a single moment of pity.

Themes

Mercy; Trust; Leadership
