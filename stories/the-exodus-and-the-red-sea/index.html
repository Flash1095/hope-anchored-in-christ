The Exodus and the Red Sea

The Exodus and the Red Sea

Retelling

After ten plagues broke Pharaoh's grip, Israel left Egypt in the night, protected by the blood of the Passover lamb. But Pharaoh changed his mind and chased them with chariots, and Israel found itself trapped between an army and the sea. Moses stretched out his hand, God drove the sea back with a strong wind, and the people walked through on dry ground with walls of water on either side. When the Egyptians followed, the waters returned, and Miriam led the women in a song of triumph: the Lord had hurled horse and rider into the sea.

Themes

Deliverance; Freedom; God's Power

Discussion

The Exodus is the Bible's great picture of salvation: people who could not free themselves, rescued entirely by God's power, through water, into freedom. When you feel trapped between an army and a sea, the way forward is God's to open. Reflect: 1) What 'Egypt' do you need deliverance from? 2) How does the Passover lamb help you understand Jesus? 3) What song of deliverance could you sing today?

Significance

The Exodus is Israel's defining rescue and a pattern of all God's saving work. When there is water in front and an army behind, the God who makes a way through the sea can be trusted.
