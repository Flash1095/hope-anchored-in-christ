Abijah's Victory over Jeroboam

Abijah's Victory over Jeroboam

Retelling

Abijah, king of Judah, stood on Mount Zemaraim and addressed Jeroboam and all Israel before battle, reminding them that God had given the kingdom to David and his sons for ever by a covenant of salt, and that Jeroboam, Solomon's servant, had rebelled, gathering vain men to himself when Rehoboam was young and tenderhearted. Abijah charged that Jeroboam had cast out the priests of the LORD and made priests of the lowest of the people, while Judah still had the sons of Aaron ministering, the Levites in their charges, and the daily burnt offerings, lamps, and shewbread in order. As Abijah spoke, Jeroboam set an ambush behind Judah, so that Judah was attacked before and behind. The men of Judah cried to the LORD, the priests sounded the trumpets, and as Judah shouted, God smote Jeroboam and all Israel before Abijah and Judah. Israel fled, and five hundred thousand chosen men of Israel fell, while Judah prevailed because they relied on the LORD God of their fathers. Jeroboam never recovered strength in Abijah's days, and Abijah grew mighty, taking fourteen wives and fathering twenty-two sons and sixteen daughters.

Discussion

Abijah's speech before battle makes the war a theological argument: legitimate priesthood and covenant against a rebel kingdom's invented worship. Judah, ambushed front and back, won by crying out and sounding trumpets, not by tactics. Reflect: 1) How do you tell a just cause from mere self-justification before a conflict? 2) What does reliance on God look like when you are ambushed from behind? 3) Why does the chronicler stress Judah's ordered worship before the battle?

Significance

Abijah's victory over Jeroboam vindicated Judah's covenant and priesthood against the northern kingdom's invented worship, after Judah was ambushed on two sides.
