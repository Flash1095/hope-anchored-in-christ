Othniel Delivers Israel

Story
Othniel Delivers Israel
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Judges 3:7-11
Retelling
After Joshua's generation, Israel did evil in the sight of the LORD, forgot him, and served Baalim and the groves. In anger the LORD sold them into the hand of Chushan-rishathaim, king of Mesopotamia, who oppressed them for eight years. When the children of Israel cried to the LORD, he raised up a deliverer: Othniel, son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother. The Spirit of the LORD came upon Othniel, he judged Israel, went out to war, and the LORD delivered Chushan-rishathaim into his hand, so that his hand prevailed against the king. The land had rest for forty years, until Othniel died.
Discussion
Othniel is the first judge, and his story sets the whole pattern of the book: sin, oppression, crying out, a Spirit-empowered deliverer, and rest. He is also the least famous, a reminder that faithfulness matters more than fame. Reflect: 1) What cycle in your own life keeps repeating, and what would crying out honestly look like? 2) How does the Spirit's empowering change ordinary courage? 3) Why does rest follow deliverance rather than precede it?
Significance
Othniel's deliverance opens the judges cycle and establishes its pattern: when Israel cried out, God raised a deliverer and gave the land rest.
