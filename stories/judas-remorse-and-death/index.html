Judas' Remorse and Death

Judas' Remorse and Death

Retelling

When Judas, who had betrayed Jesus, saw that Jesus was condemned, he was seized with remorse and returned the thirty pieces of silver to the chief priests and elders, saying, I have sinned, for I have betrayed innocent blood. They answered, What is that to us? That's your responsibility. Judas threw the money into the temple and left, and went away and hanged himself. The chief priests, unwilling to put blood money into the treasury, used it to buy the potter's field as a burial place for foreigners, which is why it is called the Field of Blood. Matthew notes this fulfilled what was spoken by the prophets about thirty pieces of silver and the potter's field.

Discussion

Judas confessed his sin truly, but he took his confession to men who would not carry it, and despair, not repentance, ended his story: remorse without return to Jesus is not repentance. Reflect: 1) What is the difference between Judas' remorse and Peter's tears? 2) Where do you take guilt when the people you confess to shrug it off? 3) How does the Field of Blood warn against pricing loyalty?

Significance

Judas' remorse, rejected by the priests he served, ended in suicide, and the blood money bought a burial field, fulfilling prophecy and standing as the dark counterpart to Peter's restoration.
