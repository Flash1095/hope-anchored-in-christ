The Pharisee and the Tax Collector

Story
The Pharisee and the Tax Collector
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Luke 18:9-14
Retelling
Jesus told this parable to some who trusted in themselves that they were righteous and despised others. Two men went up to the temple to pray: a Pharisee and a tax collector. The Pharisee stood and prayed about himself, thanking God he was not like other men — extortioners, unjust, adulterers, or even like this tax collector — and listing his fasting twice a week and his tithing of all he got. The tax collector stood far off, would not even lift his eyes to heaven, but beat his breast and said, God, be merciful to me, a sinner. Jesus concluded that the tax collector went home justified rather than the Pharisee, for everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted.
Discussion
The Pharisee's prayer was a résumé; the tax collector's was a plea. Justification went to the man who claimed nothing. Reflect: Do your prayers sound like thanking God you are not like others? What would you pray if you stood far off and told the truth? Where is self-trust disguised as thankfulness in your spiritual life?
Significance
The Pharisee and the tax collector teach that justification comes through humble repentance, not religious performance.
