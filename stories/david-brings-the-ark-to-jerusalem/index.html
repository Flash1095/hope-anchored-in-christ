David Brings the Ark to Jerusalem

Story
David Brings the Ark to Jerusalem
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Samuel 6; 1 Chronicles 15-16
Retelling
After capturing Jerusalem, David decided to bring the ark of God, the symbol of God's presence, up to his new capital. The first attempt ended in disaster: the ark was carried on a new cart, the oxen stumbled, Uzzah reached out to steady it, and he died on the spot for touching what God had commanded only Levites to carry. David, afraid and angry, left the ark at the house of Obed-edom for three months, and God blessed that house. Learning how the ark was to be carried, David tried again, this time with Levites bearing it on their shoulders, with sacrifices every six steps. As the procession entered Jerusalem, David danced before the LORD with all his might, wearing a linen ephod, while Michal, watching from a window, despised him in her heart. The ark was set in the tent David had prepared, with burnt offerings and peace offerings before the LORD.
Discussion
God's presence is not a good-luck object to be handled carelessly, and worship is not a performance to be judged from a window. David learned reverence and joy together. Reflect: 1) How do you balance reverence and joy in worship? 2) Where might you be handling holy things casually? 3) What does Michal's scorn say about worship that fears looking foolish?
Significance
Bringing the ark to Jerusalem made the new capital the centre of worship, and taught Israel that God's presence must be approached on God's terms, with both reverence and rejoicing.
