God Blesses Jacob at Bethel

God Blesses Jacob at Bethel

Retelling

God said to Jacob, "Arise, go up to Bethel, and dwell there: and make there an altar unto God, that appeared unto thee when thou fleddest from the face of Esau thy brother." Jacob told his household and all who were with him to put away the strange gods among them, to be clean, and to change their garments, and they gave him all the strange gods in their hands and the earrings in their ears, which Jacob hid under the oak by Shechem. Then they journeyed, and the terror of God was upon the cities round about them, so that they did not pursue after the sons of Jacob. Jacob came to Luz in the land of Canaan, that is, Bethel, and built an altar there, calling the place El-bethel, because God had appeared to him there when he fled from his brother. There Deborah, Rebekah's nurse, died, and was buried beneath Bethel under an oak, called Allon-bachuth. God appeared to Jacob again when he came out of Padan-aram, and blessed him, saying his name would no longer be Jacob, but Israel. God said, "I am God Almighty: be fruitful and multiply; a nation and a company of nations shall be of thee, and kings shall come out of thy loins; and the land which I gave Abraham and Isaac, to thee will I give it, and to thy seed after thee will I give the land." Then God went up from him, and Jacob set up a pillar of stone in the place where God had talked with him, poured a drink offering and oil on it, and called the place Bethel.

Themes

Covenant; Worship; Promise; Repentance

Discussion

Jacob returned to the place where God had first met him, and the return began with putting away idols and being made clean. Renewal often starts with going back to where God spoke, and clearing away what does not belong. Reflect: 1) What "strange gods" or divided loyalties might God be asking you to put away? 2) How does returning to God's promises renew you when the past has been messy? 3) What act of worship could mark a fresh start in your walk with God?

Significance

At Bethel God confirmed Jacob's new name, Israel, and renewed the covenant of land, descendants and kings. The nation that would bear that name traces its identity to this blessing.
