The Resurrection

The Resurrection

Retelling

On the first day of the week, before dawn, Mary Magdalene and the other women came to the tomb and found the stone rolled away and the tomb empty. Angels announced that Jesus, who was crucified, had risen just as He said. Mary wept in the garden until the risen Jesus called her by name, and she clung to Him in joy. That evening He appeared to His disciples behind locked doors, showed them His hands and His side, and breathed peace over them. Thomas, who had doubted, touched His wounds and cried, 'My Lord and my God!' Death had been defeated, and the story of the world had turned.

Discussion

If the resurrection is true, then death is not the end of any story — not yours, not your loved ones', not the world's. Hope is not wishful thinking; it is anchored in an empty tomb. Reflect: 1) How does the resurrection change the way you face death and loss? 2) Why does it matter that Jesus rose bodily, not just as an idea? 3) What new life is God calling you into today?

Significance

The Resurrection is the anchor itself — Hebrews 6:19's hope holds because the tomb is empty. If God raised Jesus, then death, grief, and failure are never the end of the story for His people.
