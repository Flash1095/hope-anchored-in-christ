The Blasphemer Is Stoned

Story
The Blasphemer Is Stoned
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Leviticus 24:10-23
Retelling
The son of an Israelite woman, whose father was Egyptian, went out among the children of Israel and fought with a man of Israel in the camp. In the quarrel he blasphemed the Name and cursed. He was brought to Moses; his mother was Shelomith, daughter of Dibri of the tribe of Dan; and he was put in ward until the mind of the LORD could be shown. The LORD told Moses to bring the blasphemer outside the camp, have all who heard him lay their hands on his head, and have the whole congregation stone him. God then gave the standing law: whoever curses God shall bear his sin, whoever blasphemes the Name shall surely be put to death, and the same law applies to stranger and native alike.
Discussion
A camp quarrel becomes a test case for the holiness of God's Name, and the ruling is deliberately even-handed: one law for the native-born and the stranger. Reflect: 1) Why does Scripture treat speech about God's Name as so serious? 2) How does waiting for God's mind, rather than rushing to judgment, shape this case? 3) What does one law for stranger and native teach about justice?
Significance
The blasphemer's case fixes the sanctity of God's Name in law through a single narrated judgment, showing that holiness governs speech as well as worship. Doubt: this is a brief legal case rather than a full narrative episode, included because the case itself is narrated with persons, a dispute, and a ruling; it should be weighed as borderline if the collection requires fuller stories only.
