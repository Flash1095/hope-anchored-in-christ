The Parable of the Wicked Tenants

Story
The Parable of the Wicked Tenants
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 21:33-46; Mark 12:1-12; Luke 20:9-19
Retelling
A landowner planted a vineyard, put a wall around it, dug a winepress, built a tower, leased it to tenants, and went away. At harvest he sent servants to collect his fruit, but the tenants beat one, killed another, and stoned another. He sent more servants, and they did the same. Last of all he sent his son, thinking they would respect him, but the tenants said, This is the heir; come, let us kill him and seize his inheritance, and they threw him out of the vineyard and killed him. Jesus asked his hearers what the owner would do, and they answered that he would destroy the wicked tenants and lease the vineyard to others. He quoted the stone the builders rejected, which had become the cornerstone, and the chief priests knew he had spoken the parable against them, and wanted to arrest him, but feared the crowds.
Discussion
The tenants' crime was not bad farming but refusal to give the owner his due, ending in murder of the son. The hearers condemned themselves by answering rightly. Reflect: What fruit of God's vineyard are you keeping as if it were yours? How do you respond when a parable is spoken against you — repentance or anger? Is Jesus the cornerstone or the rejected stone in your building?
Significance
The wicked tenants indict Israel's leaders for rejecting God's messengers and his Son, and announce the vineyard's transfer to others who will bear fruit.
