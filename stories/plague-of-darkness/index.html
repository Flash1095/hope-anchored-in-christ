The Plague of Darkness

The Plague of Darkness

Retelling

God told Moses to stretch out his hand toward heaven, that there might be darkness over Egypt, darkness that could be felt. Moses did so, and for three days there was thick darkness in all Egypt. No one rose from his place or saw another person for three days. But all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings. Pharaoh called Moses and offered to let the people go if they left their flocks and herds behind. Moses refused: not a hoof would be left behind, for Israel must have animals to sacrifice. Pharaoh in anger told Moses to leave his presence and never see his face again, or he would die. Moses answered, You have spoken well; I will see your face again no more.

Discussion

Darkness that can be felt stops Egypt entirely for three days, while Israel's homes have light. Pharaoh's last bargain, over the flocks, fails, and the audience ends. Reflect: 1) What does light in Israel's dwellings say about God in the midst of judgment? 2) Why does Pharaoh cling to the flocks as his final hold? 3) How does Moses' calm farewell show that the contest is effectively over?

Significance

The darkness reverses Egypt's order, light for Israel and paralysis for Egypt, and closes Pharaoh's negotiations, leaving only the final judgment still to come.

Themes

Judgment; Light; Deliverance
