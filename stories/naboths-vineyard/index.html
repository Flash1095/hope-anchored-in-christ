Naboth's Vineyard

Naboth's Vineyard

Retelling

Naboth the Jezreelite owned a vineyard near Ahab's palace, and Ahab wanted it for a garden of herbs. He offered Naboth a better vineyard or its price in money, but Naboth refused, saying, 'The LORD forbid it me, that I should give the inheritance of my fathers unto thee.' Ahab went home sullen and would not eat. Jezebel mocked his sulking and took matters into her own hands: she wrote letters in Ahab's name, sealed with his seal, ordering the elders to proclaim a fast, set Naboth on high, and bring two false witnesses to accuse him of blaspheming God and the king. Naboth was carried outside the city and stoned to death, and Ahab went down to take possession of the vineyard. But Elijah met him there with God's word: in the place where dogs licked Naboth's blood, dogs would lick Ahab's blood too. Ahab humbled himself, and the judgment on his house was delayed, but not cancelled.

Discussion

A king who could not buy a vineyard stole it by murder, and a queen who could not bear a refusal forged a trial. Power that treats law as a tool is the oldest form of injustice. Reflect: 1) How do you respond when you cannot have what you want? 2) Where do you see legal process used as a cover for theft or revenge? 3) What does Ahab's late humility, and its limits, teach about repentance?

Significance

Naboth's vineyard exposes royal injustice in Israel: a poor man's inheritance was worth more than a king's garden, and God heard the murdered man's blood.

Themes

Justice; Greed; Judgment
