Jonathan's Victory at Michmash

Jonathan's Victory at Michmash

Retelling

One day Jonathan, Saul's son, said to his armourbearer, 'Come, and let us go over to the Philistines' garrison,' without telling his father. Between the cliffs of Bozez and Seneh, Jonathan proposed a sign: if the Philistines said, 'Come up to us,' they would go up, for the LORD had delivered them into Israel's hand. The Philistines called them up, and Jonathan and his armourbearer climbed on hands and feet, killing about twenty men in the first attack. Then there was trembling in the Philistine host, in the field, and among the garrison, and the earth quaked, so that it was a very great trembling. Saul's watchmen saw the multitude melting away, and Saul joined the battle with all his men, and the LORD saved Israel that day. But Saul had bound the people with an oath, cursing any man who ate food until evening, and Jonathan, not having heard the oath, tasted honey from the ground and was refreshed. The people, faint and famished, flew upon the spoil and ate meat with the blood, and Saul would have killed Jonathan for breaking the oath, but the people rescued him, saying he had wrought with God that day.

Discussion

Jonathan's faith saw no barrier in numbers: twenty men against a garrison was enough if the LORD was with them. Saul's rash oath then nearly turned victory into tragedy, and only the people's intervention saved the hero of the day. Reflect: 1) What impossible-looking step is faith asking of you? 2) How do rash vows and hasty rules harm the people they claim to protect? 3) When should a community stand up for someone a leader has condemned?

Significance

Jonathan's faith at Michmash broke the Philistine army, while Saul's rash oath nearly destroyed the very man God had used to win the day.
