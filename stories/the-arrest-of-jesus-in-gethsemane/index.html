The Arrest of Jesus in Gethsemane

Story
The Arrest of Jesus in Gethsemane
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 26:47-56; Mark 14:43-52; Luke 22:47-53; John 18:1-11
Retelling
While Jesus was still speaking in Gethsemane, Judas arrived with a large crowd armed with swords and clubs, sent by the chief priests and elders. Judas had arranged a sign: the one he kissed would be Jesus. Going at once to Jesus, he greeted him, Rabbi, and kissed him, and Jesus answered, Do what you came for, friend. The men seized Jesus and arrested him. One of those with Jesus drew a sword and cut off the ear of the high priest's servant, but Jesus told him to put his sword back, for all who draw the sword will die by the sword, and healed the servant's ear. Jesus asked the crowd why they had come with swords and clubs as against a robber, when he had sat teaching daily in the temple, and said this had taken place so the Scriptures would be fulfilled. Then all the disciples deserted him and fled.
Discussion
The arrest combined a friend's kiss, a sword, and a surrender: Jesus stopped his own defence and went willingly, so that Scripture would be fulfilled. Reflect: 1) How does Jesus' refusal of the sword challenge the instinct to defend God's cause by force? 2) What does it mean that the disciples' flight is recorded without softening? 3) Where do you recognise a kiss of false friendship in your own story?
Significance
Jesus was arrested at night by a kiss of betrayal, yet he remained in command of the scene, forbidding violence and submitting to the Scriptures' fulfilment.
