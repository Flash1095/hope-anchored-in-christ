Jeremiah in the Cistern

Story
Jeremiah in the Cistern
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Jeremiah 38
Retelling
Shephatiah, Gedaliah the son of Pashhur, Jucal the son of Shelemiah, and Pashhur the son of Malchiah heard Jeremiah's words: He that remaineth in this city shall die by the sword, by the famine, and by the pestilence, but he that goeth forth to the Chaldeans shall live. They told Zedekiah that Jeremiah weakened the hands of the men of war and of all the people, and ought to be put to death. The king answered, Behold, he is in your hand, for the king is not he that can do any thing against you. So they took Jeremiah and cast him into the dungeon of Malchiah the son of Hammelech, which was in the court of the prison; they let him down with cords, and in the dungeon there was no water, but mire, and Jeremiah sank in the mire. Ebed-melech the Ethiopian, one of the eunuchs in the king's house, heard of it, went to the king at the gate of Benjamin, and said, My lord the king, these men have done evil in all that they have done to Jeremiah the prophet, whom they have cast into the dungeon, and he is like to die for hunger in the place where he is, for there is no more bread in the city. The king commanded Ebed-melech to take thirty men and lift Jeremiah up out of the dungeon before he died. They took old cast clouts and old rotten rags, let them down by cords into the dungeon, and told Jeremiah to put them under his armholes, and they drew him up. Jeremiah remained in the court of the prison. Zedekiah then sent for him secretly at the third entry of the house of the LORD and asked him to hide nothing. Jeremiah warned him: if thou wilt assuredly go forth unto the king of Babylon's princes, then thy soul shall live, and this city shall not be burned with fire, and thou shalt live, and thine house. Zedekiah, afraid of the Jews who had fallen to the Chaldeans, did not go — and Jeremiah told him to keep the matter secret, saying to the princes only that he had entreated the king not to send him back to Jonathan's house to die there.
Discussion
Jeremiah sank in the mud of a cistern for saying surrender was the way to live, and an Ethiopian courtier saved him with old rags and ropes. Reflect: 1) Why does the advice that saves lives sound like treason in a siege? 2) How does Ebed-melech, an outsider, shame the princes of Judah? 3) What do you do when the king asks for truth in secret but will not act on it in public?
Significance
The prophet who said surrender and live was left to die in the mud for it; his rescue came from an outsider's courage, not from the king who knew the truth.
