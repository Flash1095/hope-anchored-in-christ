The Macedonian Call

Story
The Macedonian Call
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Acts 16:6-10
Retelling
Paul and his company went throughout Phrygia and the region of Galatia, and were forbidden of the Holy Ghost to preach the word in Asia. After they were come to Mysia, they assayed to go into Bithynia, but the Spirit suffered them not. And they passing by Mysia came down to Troas. And a vision appeared to Paul in the night: there stood a man of Macedonia, and prayed him, saying, Come over into Macedonia, and help us. And after he had seen the vision, immediately we endeavoured to go into Macedonia, assuredly gathering that the Lord had called us for to preach the gospel unto them.
Discussion
This is a vision, not a historical road map: Paul sees a Macedonian man in the night calling for help, and the company reads closed doors together with the vision as God's call. Note the change to we: Luke himself joins the party at Troas. Reflect: 1) How do you read closed doors alongside open ones? 2) What does it mean to decide a call together, not alone? 3) How does a cry for help from strangers become a mission?
Significance
The Macedonian call carries the gospel from Asia into Europe: closed doors in Asia and Bithynia, a night vision at Troas, and an immediate crossing that changes the map of the mission.
