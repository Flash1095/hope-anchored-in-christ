Elisha and the Moabite Campaign

Story
Elisha and the Moabite Campaign
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Kings 3
Retelling
After Ahab's death, Mesha, king of Moab, rebelled against Israel. Jehoram, king of Israel, went out with Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, and the king of Edom, marching seven days through the wilderness of Edom until there was no water for the host or their cattle. Jehoshaphat asked for a prophet of the LORD, and Elisha was found, who at first refused Jehoram, telling him to go to the prophets of his father and mother, but for Jehoshaphat's sake he agreed to inquire. Elisha called for a minstrel, and as the minstrel played, the hand of the LORD came upon him. He told them to make the valley full of ditches, for though they would see neither wind nor rain, the valley would be filled with water, and they would drink, with their cattle and beasts. He also promised that the LORD would deliver Moab into their hand, and they would smite every fenced city and choice city, fell every good tree, stop all wells, and mar every good piece of land with stones. In the morning, when the meat offering was offered, water came by the way of Edom, and the country was filled with water. The Moabites, rising early, saw the water red as blood in the sunrise and thought the kings had fought and slain one another, so they cried, 'Moab, to the spoil!' But Israel rose and smote them, pursuing them into their cities, and the campaign pressed on until Mesha, in desperation, offered his eldest son as a burnt offering on the wall, and great indignation came upon Israel, so that they departed from him and returned to their own land.
Discussion
The Moabite campaign pairs a miracle of provision with a war that ends in horror: water without wind or rain saved an army, and Mesha's sacrifice of his son stopped it. Elisha's word was fulfilled, and still the campaign ended in retreat. Reflect: 1) How does God provide in a wilderness where you see neither wind nor rain? 2) What does it mean that a fulfilled prophecy can still end in an unsatisfying campaign? 3) How do you respond to evil, like child sacrifice, that human armies cannot answer?
Significance
Elisha's Moabite campaign brought water to a thirsty army and victory over Moab, ending in retreat after Mesha's desperate sacrifice on the wall.
