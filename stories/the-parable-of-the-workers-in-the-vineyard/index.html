The Parable of the Workers in the Vineyard

Story
The Parable of the Workers in the Vineyard
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 20:1-16
Retelling
The kingdom of heaven is like a landowner who went out early to hire workers for his vineyard, agreeing with them for a denarius a day. He hired more at nine, at noon, at three, and even at five in the afternoon, promising whatever was right. In the evening he paid them, beginning with the last: each received a denarius, including those who had worked only an hour. The first workers grumbled that the last had been made equal to them, though they had borne the burden of the day and the scorching heat. The landowner answered that he had done them no wrong — had they not agreed for a denarius? — and asked if he might not do what he wished with his own money, or whether their eye was evil because he was good. So the last will be first, and the first last.
Discussion
Grace offends the arithmetic of fairness: the eleventh-hour worker got the same wage as the all-day worker, and the complaint exposed envy, not injustice. Reflect: Do you resent God's generosity to latecomers? Are you serving for the wage or for the landowner? What would change if you stopped comparing your hours with others'?
Significance
The workers in the vineyard teach that God's grace is not wages for hours worked, and that envy of grace is itself a kingdom failure.
