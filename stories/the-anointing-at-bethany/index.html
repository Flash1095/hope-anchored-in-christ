The Anointing at Bethany

The Anointing at Bethany

Retelling

Six days before the Passover, Jesus ate at Bethany in the house of Simon the leper, where Lazarus, whom he had raised, sat at table with him and Martha served. Mary took a pound of costly perfume of pure nard, anointed Jesus' feet, and wiped them with her hair, and the house filled with the fragrance. Some objected, led by Judas, that the perfume could have been sold for a year's wages and given to the poor. Jesus defended her: she had kept it for the day of his burial, and wherever the Gospel was preached, what she had done would be told in memory of her. Matthew and Mark place the anointing on his head as well as his feet; John names Mary and sets the scene at a dinner in Jesus' honour days before his death.

Discussion

Mary spent in one act what others called waste, and Jesus called it preparation for burial. True worship sometimes looks extravagant to accountants. Reflect: What offering of yours would others call wasteful? Do you defend the poor as a reason to withhold worship, or do you do both? How does knowing Jesus was heading to burial change the value of Mary's gift?

Significance

The Bethany anointing anticipates Jesus' burial and honours devotion that sees his death coming when his disciples do not.
