Israel's Sin at Peor and Phinehas

Story
Israel's Sin at Peor and Phinehas
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Numbers 25; Psalms 106:28-31; 1 Corinthians 10:8
Retelling
While Israel camped at Shittim, on the edge of the promised land, the people joined in worshipping Baal of Peor, eating sacrifices offered to idols and committing sexual sin with Moabite women. A plague broke out, and God ordered the ringleaders punished. In the midst of the camp, Zimri, an Israelite prince, brazenly brought Cozbi, a Midianite woman, into his tent before Moses and the weeping congregation. Phinehas, Aaron's grandson, took a spear, followed them, and killed them both, and the plague stopped. God gave Phinehas a covenant of peace and a lasting priesthood, because his zeal had turned away God's wrath from Israel.
Discussion
Standing for holiness is costly when the sin is public and popular. Idolatry rarely arrives as a single act; it comes through compromise, appetite, and open defiance, while Phinehas acted when others only wept. Reflect: 1) What compromises make idolatry look harmless in your life? 2) When is zeal faithful, and when is it merely anger? 3) How does God's covenant of peace follow judgment here?
Significance
At Peor, Israel's worship collapsed into idolatry and immorality, and Phinehas's decisive zeal stopped the plague, earning a covenant of peace that shows God both judges idolatry and honours those who stand for his holiness.
