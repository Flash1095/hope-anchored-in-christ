Jeremiah in the Stocks

Jeremiah in the Stocks

Retelling

When Pashhur, the son of Immer the priest, who was also chief governor in the house of the LORD, heard that Jeremiah prophesied these things, he struck Jeremiah the prophet and put him in the stocks at the high gate of Benjamin, which was by the house of the LORD. On the next day Pashhur brought Jeremiah out of the stocks, and Jeremiah said to him, The LORD hath not called thy name Pashhur, but Magor-missabib — fear round about. For thus saith the LORD: Behold, I will make thee a terror to thyself, and to all thy friends, and they shall fall by the sword of their enemies, and thine eyes shall behold it. And I will give all Judah into the hand of the king of Babylon, and he shall carry them captive into Babylon, and shall slay them with the sword. Pashhur himself, with all his house, would go into captivity to Babylon, die there, and be buried there, he and all his friends to whom he had prophesied lies.

Discussion

Jeremiah was beaten and locked in the stocks for preaching in the Temple — and answered with a new name for his persecutor. Reflect: 1) What happens when religious authority, not the king, is the one silencing God's word? 2) How do you keep speaking when the cost is public shame? 3) Why does Jeremiah rename Pashhur 'terror all around' — whose terror is it, in the end?

Significance

Speaking God's word in God's own house can bring beating and stocks from God's own officers; the prophet's answer names the coming terror for what it is.
