The Unworthy Servants

Story
The Unworthy Servants
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Luke 17:7-10
Retelling
Jesus said, Suppose one of you has a servant ploughing or looking after the sheep. Will he say to the servant when he comes in from the field, Come along now and sit down to eat? Won't he rather say, Prepare my supper, get yourself ready and wait on me while I eat and drink; after that you may eat and drink? Will he thank the servant because he did what he was told to do? So you also, Jesus concluded, when you have done everything you were told to do, should say, We are unworthy servants; we have only done our duty. The picture is brief and deliberately unsentimental: obedience is duty, not merit, and it earns no extra praise. Doubt: this is a short illustrative saying about duty rather than a story-parable with a developed plot, so its standing as a parable is debatable.
Discussion
Obedience is duty, not merit: even complete obedience leaves the servant unworthy of extra praise, because the work was owed. Reflect: 1) Where do you expect thanks for doing what you were told to do? 2) How does unworthy servant correct both pride in service and resentment at serving? 3) What would serving without keeping score change in your work for God?
Significance
Even servants who have done everything commanded should say only, We are unworthy servants; we have only done our duty, for obedience earns no surplus merit. Doubt: this is a short illustrative saying about duty rather than a story-parable with a developed plot, so its standing as a parable is debatable.
