Jesus Before the Sanhedrin

Story
Jesus Before the Sanhedrin
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 26:57-68; Mark 14:53-65; Luke 22:63-71
Retelling
Those who had arrested Jesus took him to Caiaphas the high priest, where the scribes and elders had assembled, and Peter followed at a distance. The chief priests and the whole Sanhedrin sought false evidence against Jesus to put him to death, but found none that agreed, though many false witnesses came forward. At last two claimed he had said he could destroy the temple and rebuild it in three days. The high priest demanded, Are you the Messiah, the Son of God? Jesus answered, You have said so, and added that from now on they would see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power and coming on the clouds of heaven. The high priest tore his clothes, declared blasphemy, and the council condemned him as worthy of death, while the guards mocked, blindfolded, and struck him.
Discussion
Jesus' own answer condemned him in their court: he would not deny being the Messiah and Son of God, even to save his life. Reflect: 1) What does Jesus' refusal to trim his claim under threat say about truth under pressure? 2) How do false witnesses and a rushed night trial warn you about justice done in anger? 3) Where are you tempted to soften who Jesus is to stay safe?
Significance
Before Israel's council Jesus openly claimed to be the Messiah and Son of God enthroned at God's right hand, and that confession became the charge on which he was condemned.
