Saul's Death at Gilboa

Story
Saul's Death at Gilboa
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
1 Samuel 31; 2 Samuel 1
Retelling
The Philistines fought Israel on Mount Gilboa, and the battle went badly. Saul's sons Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malchi-shua were killed, the archers found Saul, and he was sorely wounded. Rather than be captured and abused, Saul told his armour-bearer to kill him, and when the man refused, Saul fell on his own sword, and his armour-bearer did the same. The next day the Philistines found the bodies, cut off Saul's head, stripped his armour, and fastened his body to the wall of Beth-shan. When the men of Jabesh-gilead heard what had been done, they marched all night, took down the bodies of Saul and his sons, burned them, buried their bones under a tree, and fasted seven days. Then David, hearing the news, tore his clothes and sang a lament for Saul and Jonathan, the beauty of Israel slain on the high places.
Discussion
Saul's reign ends in tragedy, but David's lament refuses to celebrate his enemy's fall. Honouring even a failed king, and grieving a friend, shows the heart God was shaping in David. Reflect: 1) How do you speak about leaders who have failed? 2) What does David's lament over Saul teach about honouring people we disagreed with? 3) Where does Saul's slow drift away from God warn you about your own small compromises?
Significance
Gilboa closes Saul's reign in defeat, yet David's lament honours both king and friend, and clears the way for the king God had chosen.
