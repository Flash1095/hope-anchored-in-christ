Uzziah's Pride and Leprosy

Story
Uzziah's Pride and Leprosy
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Chronicles 26:16-23; 2 Kings 15:1-7
Retelling
Uzziah, king of Judah, reigned fifty-two years, sought God in the days of Zechariah, and prospered, building towers, digging wells, and equipping a great army. But when he was strong, his heart was lifted up to his destruction, and he went into the temple of the LORD to burn incense on the altar of incense, a duty belonging only to the priests, the sons of Aaron. Azariah the priest followed him with eighty priests of the LORD, men of valour, and confronted him, saying, 'It appertaineth not unto thee, Uzziah, to burn incense unto the LORD, but to the priests the sons of Aaron, that are consecrated to burn incense: go out of the sanctuary; for thou hast trespassed; neither shall it be for thine honour from the LORD God.' Uzziah, holding a censer, became furious, and as he raged against the priests, leprosy rose up in his forehead before them in the house of the LORD beside the incense altar. The priests thrust him out, and he himself hastened to go out, because the LORD had smitten him. Uzziah was a leper to the day of his death, living in a separate house, cut off from the house of the LORD, while his son Jotham judged the people of the land. He was buried with his fathers in the field of burial for kings, for they said, 'He is a leper.'
Discussion
Uzziah's long, prosperous reign ended at the incense altar, where a king who had done everything right tried to do one priestly thing and was struck with leprosy in his forehead. Strength lifted his heart, and the temple he loved became the place of his judgment. Reflect: 1) How does long success prepare the ground for pride? 2) What boundaries has God set that even a good king must not cross? 3) How do you respond when faithful people confront you in the moment of your anger?
Significance
Uzziah's pride at the incense altar ended a fifty-two-year reign in leprosy, cutting the king off from the temple he had tried to serve as priest.
