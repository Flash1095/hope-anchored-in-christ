Jacob's Dream at Bethel

Retelling

Jacob went out from Beer-sheba toward Haran, and as the sun set he stopped for the night, taking stones of that place for his pillow. He dreamed, and behold, a ladder set up on the earth reached to heaven, with the angels of God ascending and descending on it, and the LORD stood above it. The LORD called himself the God of Abraham and the God of Isaac, and promised Jacob the land on which he lay, seed as the dust of the earth spreading west, east, north and south, and blessing for all the families of the earth through him and his seed. He promised, too, to be with Jacob, to keep him wherever he went, and to bring him back into this land, and not to leave him until he had done what he had spoken. Jacob awoke and said, "Surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not," calling it the house of God and the gate of heaven. Early in the morning he took the stone from under his head, set it up for a pillar, poured oil on top of it, and called the place Bethel, though the city had first been called Luz. Then Jacob vowed that if God would be with him, keep him on his way, give him bread to eat and raiment to put on, and bring him back to his father's house in peace, then the LORD would be his God, the stone would be God's house, and of all God gave him he would surely give the tenth.

Themes

Faith; Promise; God's Providence; Guidance

Discussion

God met Jacob not in a temple but on a lonely road, and the promise came before Jacob had done anything to earn it. Grace often finds us far from home, and God's presence turns an ordinary stopping place into holy ground. Reflect: 1) Where have you sensed God with you in an unexpected place? 2) What promise of God do you need to hold on to on the road you are travelling now? 3) How does Jacob's vow challenge you to respond to God's faithfulness with worship and generosity?

Significance

At Bethel God renewed to Jacob the covenant promise first given to Abraham: land, descendants, and blessing for all the families of the earth. It is the promise that carries the whole story of Israel, and of hope, forward.
