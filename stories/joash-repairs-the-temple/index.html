Joash Repairs the Temple

Story
Joash Repairs the Temple
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Kings 12; 2 Chronicles 24
Retelling
Joash, who had been preserved from Athaliah as a child and crowned under Jehoiada the priest, set his heart on repairing the house of the LORD, which Athaliah's sons had broken up and whose dedicated things had been given to Baalim. At first the priests and Levites were slow to gather the money, for the people were taxed for the temple levy of Moses, and the collection lagged. So Joash ordered a chest to be made and set beside the altar on the right side, with a hole in its lid, and proclaimed throughout Judah and Jerusalem that the people should bring the levy to the LORD. All the princes and all the people rejoiced and brought their money until the chest was full, day after day, and the king and Jehoiada gave it to the workmen, who repaired the house of God and set it in its state. As long as Jehoiada lived, burnt offerings were offered continually in the house of the LORD, but after Jehoiada's death the princes of Judah came and persuaded Joash to abandon the temple, and he listened to them, served groves and idols, and even had Zechariah, Jehoiada's son, stoned in the temple court for protesting. So the reign that began with temple repair ended in temple apostasy.
Discussion
Joash's chest shows joyful giving repairing what a wicked queen had broken, and his later apostasy shows how quickly a king who followed a mentor can fall when the mentor dies. Reflect: 1) What in God's house, or in your own life, needs repair that everyone keeps postponing? 2) How do you keep faith your own when the person who taught it to you is gone? 3) What does Zechariah's stoning in the temple court say about a reform that did not reach the heart?
Significance
Joash's temple repair, funded by a chest at the altar, restored the house Athaliah had broken, but his reign ended in apostasy after Jehoiada's death.
