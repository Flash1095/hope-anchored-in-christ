Jesus Heals Malchus' Ear

Story
Jesus Heals Malchus' Ear
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Luke 22:50-51; John 18:10-11; Matthew 26:51; Mark 14:47
Retelling
During the arrest in Gethsemane, Simon Peter drew a sword and struck the high priest's servant, cutting off his right ear. John names the servant as Malchus. Jesus commanded Peter to put his sword away, saying, Shall I not drink the cup the Father has given me? and adding that all who draw the sword will die by the sword. Luke alone records the healing itself: Jesus touched the man's ear and healed him, even as the crowd seized him. The last miracle before the cross was therefore a healing of an enemy's wound, inflicted by his own disciple in his defence.
Discussion
Jesus healed the ear his own disciple had cut off, in the middle of his arrest: his last miracle before the cross was mercy to an enemy. Reflect: 1) How does healing Malchus confront the instinct to wound for Jesus' sake? 2) What does it mean that Jesus stopped his own rescue to heal his captor's servant? 3) Where are you called to repair damage done in misguided defence of God?
Significance
As he was arrested, Jesus healed the ear of Malchus, the high priest's servant, whom Peter had wounded with a sword, forbidding violence in his defence.
