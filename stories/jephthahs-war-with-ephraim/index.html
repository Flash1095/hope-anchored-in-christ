Jephthah's War with Ephraim

Story
Jephthah's War with Ephraim
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Judges 12:1-7
Retelling
After Jephthah's victory over Ammon, the men of Ephraim gathered and crossed to Zaphon, angry that they had not been called to the battle. They threatened to burn Jephthah's house over his head. Jephthah answered that he had called them and they had not delivered him, and that the LORD had given Ammon into his hand, so why had they come to fight him now? The men of Gilead fought Ephraim and struck them down, and the Gileadites seized the passages of the Jordan. When fleeing Ephraimites tried to cross, they were asked to say 'Shibboleth'; if they said 'Sibboleth', for they could not pronounce it rightly, they were slain at the fords. Forty-two thousand Ephraimites fell. Jephthah judged Israel six years, then died and was buried in the cities of Gilead.
Discussion
A victory over a foreign enemy collapsed into civil war over wounded pride. Ephraim's complaint was not about justice but about being left out, and Jephthah's answer, though factually right, ended in the slaughter of brothers at a river crossing. Reflect: 1) How does feeling overlooked turn into conflict in your own relationships? 2) What is the difference between defending the truth and escalating a quarrel? 3) How can one word, Shibboleth, reveal how easily identity becomes a weapon?
Significance
Jephthah's war with Ephraim shows the judges cycle turning inward: after deliverance from Ammon, Israel fought itself over honour, and the fords of Jordan became a place of fratricide.
