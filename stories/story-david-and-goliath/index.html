David and Goliath

David and Goliath

Retelling

For forty days the Philistine giant Goliath — nearly ten feet tall, armored head to toe — taunted Israel's army, and no one dared to face him. David, a shepherd boy sent to bring his brothers food, heard the taunts and asked why no one trusted God to fight. King Saul offered his armor, but David refused it and walked into the valley with only his sling and five smooth stones. With one stone he struck Goliath down, and Israel routed the Philistines. David's victory came not from size or weapons but from faith in the God who had delivered him from lions and bears.

Discussion

David did not win because he was brave; he was brave because he knew who God was. The giants in your life are not fought with someone else's armor but with trust in God's past faithfulness. Reflect: 1) What giant is taunting you right now? 2) How has God delivered you before, and how does that fuel courage now? 3) Whose armor are you tempted to wear instead of trusting God?

Significance

David ran toward the giant because he knew whose battle it was. Whatever giant mocks your faith today, hope is anchored not in your size but in the living God who fights for His people.
