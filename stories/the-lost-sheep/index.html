The Lost Sheep

The Lost Sheep

Retelling

When tax collectors and sinners drew near to hear Jesus, the Pharisees grumbled that he welcomed them and ate with them. Jesus answered with a story: suppose a man has a hundred sheep and one goes astray. Does he not leave the ninety-nine on the hills and go in search of the one that is lost? And when he finds it, he lays it on his shoulders rejoicing, calls his friends and neighbours, and says, Rejoice with me, for I have found my sheep that was lost. So, Jesus said, there is more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous persons who need no repentance. In Matthew's telling the same story guards little ones from being despised, for it is not the Father's will that one of them should perish.

Discussion

Heaven's economics offend respectability: one found sheep out-joys ninety-nine safe ones. The shepherd's search, not the sheep's return, drives the story. Reflect: Who is your 'one' — the person you have written off as astray? Do you rejoice over repentance, or resent the attention it gets? Are you a ninety-nine person who needs no repentance, or a found sheep?

Significance

The lost sheep reveals God's seeking love for the straying and heaven's joy over one repentance.

Themes

Seeking God; Repentance; Joy; God's love
