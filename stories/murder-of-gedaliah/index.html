The Murder of Gedaliah

Story
The Murder of Gedaliah
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Jeremiah 40-41; 2 Kings 25:22-26
Retelling
After Jerusalem fell, the king of Babylon made Gedaliah the son of Ahikam governor over the poor of the land who were left. Jeremiah, released by Nebuzar-adan the captain of the guard, chose to stay with Gedaliah at Mizpah. The captains of the forces in the fields, among them Johanan the son of Kareah, came to Gedaliah and warned him: Dost thou certainly know that Baalis the king of the Ammonites hath sent Ishmael the son of Nethaniah to slay thee? Gedaliah did not believe them. In the seventh month Ishmael came to Mizpah with ten men, ate bread with Gedaliah, and then rose up and struck Gedaliah with the sword, and killed him, with the Jews and the Chaldeans who were with him at Mizpah. The next day, before anyone knew, eighty men from Shechem, Shiloh, and Samaria came with shaven beards, torn clothes, and cuttings, bringing offerings and incense to the house of the LORD; Ishmael went out weeping to meet them, lured them into the city, and slew them, casting their bodies into the pit that Asa the king had made — sparing only ten who said, Slay us not, for we have treasures in the field, of wheat, and of barley, and of oil, and of honey. Ishmael then took captive all the residue of the people at Mizpah, including the king's daughters, and departed to go over to the Ammonites. But Johanan and all the captains heard, fought Ishmael at the great waters of Gibeon, recovered the captives, and Ishmael escaped with eight men to the Ammonites.
Discussion
The remnant's one safe governor was murdered at his own table, and the fragile peace after the fall collapsed in a day. Reflect: 1) Why did Gedaliah refuse to believe the warning — trust, or naivety? 2) How does a single assassination unravel a whole community's future? 3) What do you do when the leaders left after disaster cannot hold the remnant together?
Significance
Gedaliah's murder ended the last ordered life in Judah after the fall and drove the frightened remnant toward the flight to Egypt they had been warned against.
