The Cleansing of the Temple

Story
The Cleansing of the Temple
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 21:12-17; Mark 11:15-19; Luke 19:45-48; John 2:13-22
Retelling
Entering the temple courts, Jesus found merchants selling oxen, sheep, and doves, and money changers at their tables. He drove them out, overturned the tables, and poured out the changers' coins, declaring that his Father's house was to be a house of prayer, but they had made it a den of thieves. The blind and lame then came to him in the temple and he healed them, while children cried, Hosanna to the Son of David. The chief priests and scribes were indignant and demanded by what authority he did these things; he answered by pointing to the temple of his body, which, if destroyed, he would raise in three days — a saying his disciples only understood after his resurrection.
Discussion
Jesus' anger was not at commerce itself but at a system that profited from worshippers and crowded out prayer. The same courts then became a place of healing. Reflect: What in your worship has become business — profitable, busy, but no longer prayer? Who is crowded out of meeting God by the way you run things? How does Jesus' claim about the temple of his body change where you expect to meet God?
Significance
The cleansing asserts Jesus' authority over the temple and previews its replacement by his own body, raised after destruction. Doubt: John places a temple cleansing at the start of Jesus' ministry while the Synoptics place it in Passion week, so readers differ over whether there were two cleansings or one event told in different positions.
