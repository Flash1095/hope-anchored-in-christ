The Remnant Flees to Egypt

Story
The Remnant Flees to Egypt
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Jeremiah 42-43; 2 Kings 25:26
Retelling
After Gedaliah's murder, Johanan the son of Kareah and all the captains and all the people, from the least even unto the greatest, came to Jeremiah and said, Let our supplication be accepted before thee, and pray for us unto the LORD thy God, that He may show us the way wherein we may walk, and the thing that we may do. They promised, Whether it be good, or whether it be evil, we will obey the voice of the LORD our God. After ten days the word of the LORD came to Jeremiah: If ye will still abide in this land, then will I build you, and not pull you down, and I will plant you, and not pluck you up; neither fear ye the king of Babylon. But if ye say, We will not dwell in this land, and go into Egypt, where ye shall see no war, nor hear the sound of the trumpet, nor have hunger of bread, then the sword which ye fear shall overtake you there in the land of Egypt, and the famine whereof ye were afraid shall follow close after you there, and there ye shall die. When Jeremiah had finished, Johanan and all the proud men said, Thou speakest falsely: the LORD our God hath not sent thee to say, Go not into Egypt to sojourn there — but Baruch the son of Neriah setteth thee on against us. So Johanan took all the remnant of Judah, men, women, children, and the king's daughters, with Jeremiah the prophet and Baruch, and they came into the land of Egypt, even to Tahpanhes. There the word came to Jeremiah: Take great stones in thine hand, and hide them in the clay in the brickkiln which is at the entry of Pharaoh's house in Tahpanhes, in the sight of the men of Judah, and say unto them, Thus saith the LORD of hosts: Behold, I will send and take Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon, My servant, and will set his throne upon these stones that I have hid, and he shall spread his royal pavilion over them.
Discussion
The remnant promised to obey whatever God said — then disobeyed the moment He said stay. Reflect: 1) Why do we ask God for guidance when we have already chosen the answer? 2) How does fear of Babylon in Judah become fear of Babylon in Egypt? 3) What does it mean that Jeremiah was carried to Egypt against his own prophecy, yet kept prophesying there?
Significance
A promised obedience that collapses at the first unwelcome word is no obedience at all; the flight to Egypt carried the very judgment the remnant fled Judah to escape.
