Jesus Appoints the Twelve

Story
Jesus Appoints the Twelve
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Mark 3:13-19; Luke 6:12-16
Retelling
After spending the night in prayer on a mountainside, Jesus called to himself those he wanted, and appointed twelve, whom he also named apostles, to be with him and to be sent out to preach and to have authority to drive out demons. They were Simon, whom he named Peter, James son of Zebedee and his brother John, Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, Simon the Zealot, and Judas Iscariot, who would betray him. The appointment followed a night of prayer and preceded their public mission.
Discussion
Jesus chose ordinary, clashing men after a night of prayer, and named them to be with him before sending them anywhere: presence precedes mission. Reflect: 1) What does it mean that discipleship starts with being with Jesus, not doing for him? 2) How does the mix of tax collector, zealot, and betrayer in one group challenge your idea of a worthy team? 3) Why does Luke stress the night of prayer before the choice?
Significance
The Twelve were appointed as the authorised core of Jesus' mission, chosen in prayer and charged to preach and to confront evil, and their number recalled the twelve tribes of Israel.
