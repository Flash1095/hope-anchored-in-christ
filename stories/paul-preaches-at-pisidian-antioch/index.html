Paul Preaches at Pisidian Antioch

Story
Paul Preaches at Pisidian Antioch
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Acts 13:13-52
Retelling
Paul and his company loosed from Paphos and came to Perga in Pamphylia, where John departing from them returned to Jerusalem. They came to Antioch in Pisidia, and went into the synagogue on the sabbath day and sat down. After the reading of the law and the prophets, the rulers of the synagogue sent unto them, saying, Ye men and brethren, if ye have any word of exhortation for the people, say on. Paul stood up and preached the history of Israel, from the choosing of the fathers through Egypt, the wilderness, the judges, Samuel, Saul, and David, and declared that of David's seed God had raised unto Israel a Saviour, Jesus. He told of John's baptism, of Jesus' condemnation in Jerusalem, his burial, and his resurrection, witnessed by those who came up with him from Galilee, and of the promises fulfilled in the Psalms. Through this man, he said, is preached unto you the forgiveness of sins, and by him all that believe are justified from all things, from which ye could not be justified by the law of Moses. When the Jews were gone out of the synagogue, the Gentiles besought that these words might be preached to them the next sabbath. The next sabbath day came almost the whole city together to hear the word of God. When the Jews saw the multitudes, they were filled with envy, and spake against those things which were spoken by Paul, contradicting and blaspheming. Then Paul and Barnabas waxed bold and said, It was necessary that the word of God should first have been spoken to you, but seeing ye put it from you, and judge yourselves unworthy of everlasting life, lo, we turn to the Gentiles. For so hath the Lord commanded us, saying, I have set thee to be a light of the Gentiles, that thou shouldest be for salvation unto the ends of the earth. When the Gentiles heard this, they were glad, and glorified the word of the Lord, and as many as were ordained to eternal life believed. And the word of the Lord was published throughout all the region. But the Jews stirred up the devout and honourable women, and the chief men of the city, and raised persecution against Paul and Barnabas, and expelled them out of their coasts. But they shook off the dust of their feet against them, and came unto Iconium. And the disciples were filled with joy, and with the Holy Ghost.
Discussion
Paul's first recorded sermon is a history lesson with a verdict: everything Israel's story promised has come in Jesus, and forgiveness and justification are offered through him, not through the law. The same sermon divides its hearers: Gentiles rejoice, and the Jewish leaders expel the preachers. Reflect: 1) How does Paul use Israel's history rather than bypassing it? 2) What does justification by faith mean in contrast to justification by the law? 3) Why does a joyful church remain after its teachers are expelled?
Significance
Pisidian Antioch sets the pattern for the Gentile mission: the gospel offered first to Jews, then turned to the Gentiles when rejected, received with gladness, and followed by persecution that cannot stop the word.
