David Among the Philistines

Story
David Among the Philistines
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
1 Samuel 27; 1 Samuel 29
Retelling
Fleeing Saul, David said in his heart that he would one day perish by Saul's hand, and that there was nothing better than to escape into the land of the Philistines. He went with six hundred men to Achish, king of Gath, who gave him Ziklag, where he lived a year and four months. From Ziklag David raided the Geshurites, Girzites, and Amalekites, leaving none alive to tell Gath, and reported to Achish that he had raided against the south of Judah, so that Achish trusted him completely, believing Israel now hated him. When the Philistines gathered against Israel, Achish took David and his men as his bodyguard, but the Philistine lords objected, remembering the songs about David slaying his ten thousands, and demanded he be sent back. Achish told David he was upright in his sight, but the lords would not have him, so David returned to Ziklag, spared from fighting against his own people.
Discussion
David's Philistine years are the murkiest of his life: a fugitive telling half-truths to a foreign king, trusted by his enemy and distrusted by his enemy's lords. God used even the Philistines' suspicion to keep David from fighting Israel. Reflect: 1) What do you do when every safe option feels compromised? 2) How does God sometimes protect you through other people's distrust? 3) Where is the line between wise survival and deception?
Significance
David's sojourn among the Philistines kept him alive under Saul's pursuit, but at the cost of deception, and God turned Philistine suspicion into protection from civil war.
