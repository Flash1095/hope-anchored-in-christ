Nehemiah's Prayer for Jerusalem

Story
Nehemiah's Prayer for Jerusalem
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Nehemiah 1
Retelling
In the twentieth year of Artaxerxes, while Nehemiah, the king's cupbearer, was in the citadel of Susa, his brother Hanani came from Judah with certain men, and Nehemiah asked them about the Jews who had escaped the captivity and about Jerusalem. They answered, 'The remnant that are left of the captivity there in the province are in great affliction and reproach: the wall of Jerusalem also is broken down, and the gates thereof are burned with fire.' When Nehemiah heard these words, he sat down, wept, mourned for days, fasted, and prayed before the God of heaven, saying, 'I beseech thee, O LORD God of heaven, the great and terrible God, that keepeth covenant and mercy for them that love him and observe his commandments: let thine ear now be attentive, and thine eyes open, that thou mayest hear the prayer of thy servant, which I pray before thee now, day and night, for the children of Israel thy servants, and confess the sins of the children of Israel, which we have sinned against thee: both I and my father's house have sinned.' He confessed Israel's corruption, remembered God's promise that if they returned to him he would gather them from the uttermost part of heaven to the place he had chosen, and asked God to grant him mercy in the sight of the king, whose cupbearer he was.
Discussion
Nehemiah's prayer begins not with a plan but with weeping, fasting, and confession that includes himself: 'both I and my father's house have sinned.' The wall would be rebuilt by a man who first took his share of the blame. Reflect: 1) What bad news has moved you to weeping rather than to a plan? 2) How does confessing your own share of a community's sin change your prayers for it? 3) What does Nehemiah's use of God's own promise teach about praying Scripture back to God?
Significance
Nehemiah's prayer for Jerusalem turned bad news from Judah into confession, fasting, and a plea grounded in God's covenant promise of return.
