The Rich Young Ruler

Story
The Rich Young Ruler
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 19:16-30; Mark 10:17-31; Luke 18:18-30
Retelling
A rich young ruler ran to Jesus, knelt, and asked what good thing he must do to inherit eternal life. Jesus pointed him to the commandments, and the young man claimed he had kept them all from his youth. Mark adds that Jesus looked at him and loved him. Then Jesus gave the one command he could not keep: go, sell what you have, give to the poor, and come, follow me. The young man's face fell, and he went away sorrowful, for he had great possessions. Jesus told his disciples how hard it is for the rich to enter the kingdom — easier for a camel to go through a needle's eye — and when they asked who then could be saved, he answered that with men it is impossible, but with God all things are possible. Peter protested that they had left everything, and Jesus promised a hundredfold return, with persecutions, and life in the age to come.
Discussion
The ruler wanted one more deed to complete a good record; Jesus exposed the one idol the record protected. Wealth was not his problem alone — it was his security. Reflect: What one thing, if Jesus asked for it, would make you walk away sorrowful? Is your obedience selective where your security is concerned? Do you trust that what is impossible with men is possible with God?
Significance
The rich young ruler exposes wealth as a rival master and teaches that salvation is God's impossible gift, not a deed added to a good life.
