Zelophehad's Daughters

Zelophehad's Daughters

Retelling

Zelophehad, of the tribe of Manasseh, died in the wilderness, leaving no sons, only five daughters: Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah. The daughters stood before Moses, Eleazar the priest, the princes, and the whole congregation at the door of the tent of meeting, and said, Our father died in the wilderness, not in Korah's company, and he had no sons; why should our father's name be taken away from his family because he had no son? Give us a possession among our father's brothers. Moses brought their cause before the LORD, and God answered that the daughters of Zelophehad spoke what was right, and commanded that they be given a possession of inheritance among their father's brothers, and that their father's inheritance pass to them. God then gave the standing law: if a man dies without a son, his inheritance passes to his daughter, then to his brothers, then to his father's brothers, then to the nearest kinsman. Later, before entering the land, the heads of Manasseh raised a further case: if the daughters married outside the tribe, their inheritance would pass to another tribe. God ruled that the daughters should marry within their father's tribe, and they did so, marrying their father's brothers' sons, so that no inheritance would move from tribe to tribe.

Discussion

Five daughters who have no brother become lawgivers by asking a just question in public, before Moses and the whole congregation. God affirms their plea and then guards it with a second ruling. Reflect: 1) What does it take to bring a just cause before leaders and God? 2) How does this ruling protect both daughters and the wider tribe? 3) Why does Scripture record the daughters' names?

Significance

Zelophehad's daughters establish inheritance for daughters in Israel, showing that God's law makes room for those the old custom would have left without a share, while keeping each tribe's inheritance intact.

Inheritance; Justice; Family
