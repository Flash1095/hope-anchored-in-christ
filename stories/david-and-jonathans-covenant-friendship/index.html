David and Jonathan's Covenant Friendship

Retelling

After David defeated Goliath, Saul's son Jonathan loved him as his own soul. Jonathan, the heir to the throne, stripped off his robe, his armour, his sword, his bow, and his belt, and gave them to David, making a covenant with the shepherd who would one day be king. When Saul's jealousy turned murderous, Jonathan warned David and pleaded his case before his father, but Saul hurled a spear at his own son for defending David. At the stone Ezel, Jonathan and David met in secret. Jonathan shot arrows as a signal, sent his boy away, and the two friends wept together, renewing their covenant that God would stand between their descendants forever. Then David fled, and Jonathan returned to the city, loyal to his father and faithful to his friend at the same time.

Discussion

Jonathan gave up a throne for a friend, and that costly loyalty is one of the Bible's finest pictures of friendship. True friendship does not compete; it rejoices in God's calling on the other person. Reflect: 1) What would it cost you to rejoice in a friend's success instead of envying it? 2) How does Jonathan model loyalty when loyalties pull in two directions? 3) Who is a friend you should encourage in God's calling this week?

Significance

Jonathan's covenant with David shows friendship as self-giving loyalty: the prince surrendered his claim so that God's chosen king could live.
