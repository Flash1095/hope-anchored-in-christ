Micaiah and Ahab's Death at Ramoth-Gilead

Micaiah and Ahab's Death at Ramoth-Gilead

Retelling

For three years there was no war between Syria and Israel, and then Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, came down to Ahab, king of Israel, who proposed they recover Ramoth-gilead from Syria together. Jehoshaphat agreed, but asked that they inquire at the word of the LORD first. Ahab gathered about four hundred prophets, who all said, 'Go up; for the Lord shall deliver it into the hand of the king.' Jehoshaphat, unsatisfied, asked whether there was another prophet of the LORD, and Ahab answered that there was one, Micaiah son of Imlah, but that he hated him, for he never prophesied good about him, only evil. Micaiah was summoned, and at first he echoed the four hundred, but when Ahab adjured him to tell the truth, Micaiah said he saw all Israel scattered on the hills as sheep without a shepherd, and the LORD saying they had no master and should return in peace. He described a vision of the LORD on his throne, with the host of heaven around him, and a lying spirit sent to persuade Ahab's prophets, so that Ahab would go up and fall at Ramoth-gilead. Ahab struck him on the cheek through Zedekiah, and ordered Micaiah fed bread of affliction and water of affliction until he returned in peace. Micaiah answered, 'If thou return at all in peace, the LORD hath not spoken by me.' Ahab disguised himself for battle while Jehoshaphat wore his robes, and the Syrian captains, ordered to fight only the king of Israel, chased Jehoshaphat until he cried out and they turned away. But a certain man drew a bow at a venture and smote Ahab between the joints of the harness, and though he was propped up in his chariot facing the Syrians all day, he died at evening, and the blood ran out into the chariot. Dogs licked his blood at the pool of Samaria where harlots washed, as the word of the LORD by Elijah had said.

Discussion

Four hundred voices said what a king wanted to hear; one voice told him the truth and was imprisoned for it. Ahab's disguise and a random arrow together show that no precaution outruns God's word. Reflect: 1) How do you tell a true prophet from four hundred agreeable ones? 2) What do you do when the truth you hear is unwelcome? 3) Why did Ahab go to battle even after hearing Micaiah's warning?

Significance

Micaiah's true prophecy and Ahab's death at Ramoth-Gilead vindicated the lone prophet against four hundred court prophets and fulfilled Elijah's word against Ahab.
