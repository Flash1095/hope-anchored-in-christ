Moses and Aaron Confront Pharaoh

Retelling

Moses and Aaron went to Pharaoh and said, Thus says the LORD God of Israel, Let my people go, that they may hold a feast to me in the wilderness. Pharaoh answered, Who is the LORD, that I should obey his voice to let Israel go? I do not know the LORD, nor will I let Israel go. He then ordered the taskmasters to stop supplying straw while keeping the same quota of bricks, so the people had to gather stubble instead. The foremen were beaten, the people blamed Moses, and Moses complained to God that things had only grown worse. God answered that Pharaoh would now be compelled to let them go, and renewed his promise to bring Israel out, to be their God, and to give them the land promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

Discussion

The first confrontation makes things worse before they get better, which is often how deliverance begins. Pharaoh's question, Who is the LORD, is the question the plagues will answer. Reflect: 1) How do you pray when obedience seems to have made things worse? 2) What does God's renewed promise teach you when the people stop listening? 3) Where are you tempted to measure God's faithfulness by immediate results?

Significance

This bridge episode between the burning bush and the plagues shows Pharaoh's open defiance, Israel's despair, and God's renewed covenant promise, setting up the contest in which God will make himself known to Egypt.
