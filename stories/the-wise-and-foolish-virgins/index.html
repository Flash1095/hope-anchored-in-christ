The Wise and Foolish Virgins

The Wise and Foolish Virgins

Retelling

The kingdom of heaven is like ten virgins who took their lamps and went to meet the bridegroom. Five were wise and took oil in flasks along with their lamps; five were foolish and took no extra oil. As the bridegroom delayed, all ten slept. At midnight the cry came, Here is the bridegroom, come out to meet him. The foolish virgins found their lamps going out and asked the wise for oil, but the wise answered that there might not be enough for both, and told them to go and buy their own. While they were gone, the bridegroom came, and those who were ready went in with him to the feast, and the door was shut. When the others returned and begged, Lord, open to us, he answered, Truly, I do not know you. Watch therefore, for you know neither the day nor the hour.

Discussion

Readiness could not be borrowed at midnight: oil is the one thing that must be gathered beforehand. The delay exposed who had prepared and who had presumed. Reflect: What readiness are you postponing until the midnight cry? Are you relying on someone else's oil — family faith, church attendance, borrowed devotion? How does 'you know neither the day nor the hour' shape this week?

Significance

The virgins warn that readiness for Christ's return is personal, prepared in advance, and cannot be transferred at the last moment.
