David's Census and the Plague

David's Census and the Plague

Retelling

Late in his reign, David ordered Joab to number Israel and Judah, counting fighting men from Dan to Beersheba. Joab protested, but the king's word prevailed, and after more than nine months the count came back: eight hundred thousand men in Israel and five hundred thousand in Judah. David's conscience struck him at once, and he confessed that he had sinned greatly in doing this foolish thing. Through the prophet Gad, God offered him three punishments, and David chose to fall into the hand of the LORD, whose mercies are great, rather than into the hand of man. A plague struck Israel, and seventy thousand people died, until the angel of the LORD stopped at the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebusite. David bought the threshing floor and the oxen at full price, built an altar there, and offered sacrifices, and the plague was stayed. That ground would later become the site of the temple.

Discussion

Counting soldiers looks like good management, but David's census was trust in numbers instead of trust in God. His repentance, and his refusal to offer a sacrifice that cost him nothing, point to the heart of worship. Reflect: 1) What numbers do you trust instead of God: money, followers, strength? 2) How does David's choice of punishment show trust in God's mercy? 3) What would it mean to refuse a sacrifice that costs you nothing?

Significance

The census and plague show a king learning late that security comes from God, not from counted swords, and the altar on Araunah's floor became the future temple site.

Pride; Repentance; Mercy
