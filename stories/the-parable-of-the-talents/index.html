The Parable of the Talents

Story
The Parable of the Talents
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 25:14-30; Luke 19:11-27
Retelling
A master going on a journey entrusted his property to his servants: five talents to one, two to another, one to another, each according to his ability. The first two traded and doubled their sums; the third dug a hole and buried his talent, then blamed his master's hardness for his fear. On return, the master praised the faithful servants — well done, good and faithful servant; you have been faithful over a little, I will set you over much — and took the buried talent from the fearful one and gave it to the one with ten. Luke tells the same story as the parable of the minas, where a nobleman gives ten servants one mina each before travelling to receive a kingdom, and rewards or judges them on his return. In both versions the point is identical: what is entrusted must be put to work, and fear is no excuse for burying it.
Discussion
The master rewarded faithfulness, not size: the two-talent servant heard the same praise as the five-talent one. Only the servant who did nothing lost everything. Reflect: What talent — money, skill, opportunity, time — have you buried out of fear? Are you comparing your five with someone else's two instead of trading your own? What would faithful risk look like this month?
Significance
The talents teach stewardship: God entrusts gifts unequally but judges faithfulness equally, and fearful inaction is itself unfaithfulness.
