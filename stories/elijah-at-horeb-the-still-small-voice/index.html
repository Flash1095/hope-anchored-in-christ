Elijah at Horeb: The Still Small Voice

Story
Elijah at Horeb: The Still Small Voice
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
1 Kings 19
Retelling
After the triumph on Carmel, Jezebel threatened Elijah's life, and the prophet who had faced down four hundred and fifty prophets of Baal ran for his life into the wilderness. Under a juniper tree he prayed to die, saying he was no better than his fathers. An angel fed him twice with cake and water, and in the strength of that food he travelled forty days and nights to Horeb, the mount of God. There he lodged in a cave and poured out his complaint: he alone was left, and they sought his life. God told him to stand on the mount. A wind tore the mountains, then an earthquake, then a fire, but the LORD was not in any of them. After the fire came a still small voice, asking, 'What doest thou here, Elijah?' God then gave him new work: anoint Hazael, Jehu, and Elisha, and told him that seven thousand in Israel had not bowed to Baal.
Discussion
Elijah expected God in the spectacular, and found him in a whisper. Burnout, fear, and loneliness do not disqualify a prophet; God meets them with food, rest, honest questions, and fresh work. Reflect: 1) When have you looked for God only in dramatic moments? 2) How does God's care for Elijah's body and rest speak to your exhaustion? 3) What 'seven thousand' might God be reminding you that you cannot see?
Significance
Horeb corrects the idea that God only speaks in fire and earthquake: after Carmel's drama, he speaks in a still small voice and gives a weary prophet his next task.
