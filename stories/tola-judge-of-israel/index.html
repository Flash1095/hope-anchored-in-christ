Tola, Judge of Israel

Story
Tola, Judge of Israel
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Judges 10:1-2
Retelling
After Abimelech, Tola, son of Puah, son of Dodo, a man of Issachar, arose to defend Israel. He lived in Shamir in the hill country of Ephraim and judged Israel for twenty-three years. Then he died and was buried in Shamir. No enemy, battle, or deed is recorded; the text remembers only his name, his tribe, his home, the length of his judgeship, and his burial place. His judgeship falls between Abimelech's violent reign and the Ammonite oppression that followed Jair, a quiet interval in a turbulent book.
Discussion
Tola is remembered for faithful duration rather than a dramatic deed. Twenty-three years of judging Israel, with no crisis recorded, may itself be the deliverance: steady leadership that kept the peace. Reflect: 1) How do you value quiet, steady faithfulness compared with dramatic victories? 2) What does it mean to 'defend' a people without a battle? 3) Would you be content with a life remembered only for where you served and how long?
Significance
Tola's twenty-three years of judgeship represent the quiet side of the judges period: steady leadership with no recorded crisis. Doubt: the text records no enemy, battle, or specific deed, so this row preserves a notice of judgeship rather than a narrated deliverance.
