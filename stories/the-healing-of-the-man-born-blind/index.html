The Healing of the Man Born Blind

The Healing of the Man Born Blind

Retelling

Passing a man blind from birth, Jesus' disciples asked who had sinned, the man or his parents, that he was born blind. Jesus answered that neither had sinned in this way; he was born blind so that God's works might be displayed in him. He spat on the ground, made mud, spread it on the man's eyes, and told him to wash in the Pool of Siloam. The man went, washed, and came back seeing. His neighbours argued over his identity, and the Pharisees interrogated him and his parents, because the healing had happened on the Sabbath. Expelled from the synagogue for defending Jesus, the man met Jesus again, who asked if he believed in the Son of God. The man answered, Lord, I believe, and worshipped him.

Discussion

The disciples wanted a cause for suffering; Jesus offered a purpose. The healed man grew from 'the man called Jesus' to worship, while the sighted religious leaders grew blind. Reflect: Do you treat suffering as a puzzle to blame someone for, or as a place for God's work? Where has defending Jesus cost you belonging? How has your own understanding of him grown step by step?

Significance

The man born blind contrasts physical sight with spiritual blindness and shows faith growing through interrogation into worship.

Themes

Sight and blindness; Healing; Faith; Sabbath controversy
