The Dedication of Jerusalem's Wall

Story
The Dedication of Jerusalem's Wall
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Nehemiah 12:27-47
Retelling
At the dedication of the wall of Jerusalem, the Levites were sought out from all their places and brought to Jerusalem to keep the dedication with gladness, thanksgiving, singing, cymbals, psalteries, and harps. The priests and Levites purified themselves, the people, the gates, and the wall. Then Nehemiah brought the princes of Judah up on the wall and appointed two great companies of thanksgiving to walk in opposite directions around it: one company went to the right toward the dung gate with Ezra the scribe before them, and the other went to the left with Nehemiah following, each with singers, trumpets, and instruments, until the two companies met in the house of God. The priests sounded trumpets, the singers sang loud with Jezrahiah as their overseer, and the people offered great sacrifices and rejoiced, for God had made them rejoice with great joy, and the wives and children also rejoiced, so that the joy of Jerusalem was heard even afar off. On that day men were appointed over the chambers for the treasures, the offerings, the firstfruits, and the tithes, to gather into them the portions required by the law for the priests and Levites, for Judah rejoiced over the priests and Levites who ministered.
Discussion
The wall's dedication was a procession, not a ribbon-cutting: two choirs walking the whole circuit in opposite directions, meeting at the temple, with joy heard far outside the city. A defensive wall became an instrument of thanksgiving. Reflect: 1) How do you dedicate a completed work to God rather than merely admiring it? 2) What would it mean for the joy of your community to be heard afar off? 3) Why does the dedication end with provision for priests and Levites, not with celebration alone?
Significance
The dedication of Jerusalem's wall turned a defensive circuit into two choirs of thanksgiving meeting at the temple, with joy heard far beyond the city.
