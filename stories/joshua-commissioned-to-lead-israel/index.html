Joshua Commissioned to Lead Israel

Retelling

After the death of Moses, the LORD spoke to Joshua, son of Nun, Moses' assistant, and told him to lead Israel across the Jordan into the land promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Three times God commanded him to be strong and courageous, promising to be with him as he had been with Moses and never to leave or forsake him. God charged him to keep the Book of the Law always before him, meditating on it day and night and obeying all that was written in it, so that he would prosper and succeed. Joshua then ordered the officers to prepare the people: within three days they would cross the Jordan. The Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh pledged to fight alongside their brothers until all Israel had rest, and the whole assembly answered, 'All that thou commandest us we will do.'

Discussion

Leadership passes, but God's presence does not. Joshua inherited Moses' task, not Moses' authority in himself, and his strength was tied to obedience to God's word rather than to his own confidence. Reflect: 1) What new responsibility are you facing that feels too large for you? 2) How does daily attention to Scripture change the way you face it? 3) Who has God given you to stand with, as the eastern tribes stood with Israel?

Significance

Joshua's commission sets the pattern for the whole conquest: God's presence, God's word, and God's people moving together, with courage defined as obedience rather than fearlessness.
