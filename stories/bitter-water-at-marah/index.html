Bitter Water at Marah

Story
Bitter Water at Marah
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Exodus 15:22-27
Retelling
Three days after crossing the sea, Israel came into the wilderness of Shur and found no water. At Marah the water was bitter and they could not drink it, and the people murmured against Moses, asking what they should drink. Moses cried to the LORD, and the LORD showed him a tree. When Moses cast it into the waters, the waters were made sweet. There God made a statute and an ordinance, and proved them, saying that if they would diligently listen to his voice and do what is right, he would put none of the diseases of Egypt upon them, for he is the LORD who heals. Then they came to Elim, where there were twelve wells of water and seventy palm trees, and they camped there by the waters.
Discussion
The first thirst after deliverance becomes the first complaint, and God answers complaint with both provision and instruction. Marah tests whether a delivered people will trust the Deliverer. Reflect: 1) How quickly does gratitude turn to complaint when a new need appears? 2) What does God's statute at Marah teach about obedience following deliverance? 3) How does Elim's abundance after Marah shape your view of God's provision?
Significance
Marah is the wilderness pattern in miniature: need, complaint, God's provision, and a call to listen, showing that deliverance from Egypt must become trust in the wilderness.
