Solomon's Apostasy

Story
Solomon's Apostasy
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
1 Kings 11
Retelling
King Solomon loved many strange women, together with Pharaoh's daughter: women of the Moabites, Ammonites, Edomites, Zidonians, and Hittites, nations of which the LORD had said, 'Ye shall not go in to them, neither shall they come in unto you: for surely they will turn away your heart after their gods.' Solomon clung to these in love, and he had seven hundred wives, princesses, and three hundred concubines, and his wives turned away his heart. For when Solomon was old, his wives turned his heart after other gods, and his heart was not perfect with the LORD his God, as was the heart of David his father. He went after Ashtoreth, goddess of the Zidonians, and Milcom, the abomination of the Ammonites, and built a high place for Chemosh, the abomination of Moab, and for Molech, the abomination of Ammon, on the hill before Jerusalem, and did the same for all his foreign wives, who burned incense and sacrificed to their gods. The LORD was angry with Solomon, because his heart was turned from the LORD God of Israel, who had appeared to him twice, and who had commanded him concerning this very thing, that he should not go after other gods. The LORD said he would rend the kingdom from Solomon's son, leaving only one tribe for David's sake and for Jerusalem's sake, and he stirred up adversaries against Solomon: Hadad the Edomite, Rezon of Damascus, and Jeroboam, Solomon's own servant, who had lifted up his hand against the king.
Discussion
Solomon's fall was gradual and relational: love for foreign wives became worship of foreign gods, and the wisest king in Israel built high places for Chemosh and Molech within sight of the temple. Reflect: 1) How do small compromises in relationships become large compromises in worship? 2) Why does the text stress that God had appeared to Solomon twice? 3) What adversaries have your own compromises stirred up?
Significance
Solomon's apostasy turned the builder of the temple into a builder of high places, and cost his son all but one tribe of the kingdom.
