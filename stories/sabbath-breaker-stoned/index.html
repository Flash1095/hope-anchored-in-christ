The Sabbath Breaker Is Stoned

Story
The Sabbath Breaker Is Stoned
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Numbers 15:32-36
Retelling
While Israel was in the wilderness, they found a man gathering sticks on the Sabbath day. Those who found him brought him to Moses, Aaron, and the whole congregation, and put him in ward, because it was not declared what should be done to him. The LORD told Moses that the man should surely be put to death, and the whole congregation should stone him with stones outside the camp. So the whole congregation brought him outside the camp and stoned him with stones, and he died, as the LORD commanded Moses.
Discussion
A small act, gathering sticks, becomes a test case for the Sabbath command, and again the community waits for God's ruling before acting. The severity shocks modern readers, and Scripture presents it as guarding the sign of the covenant. Reflect: 1) Why does Scripture treat Sabbath-breaking as a covenant matter, not a private choice? 2) What does waiting in ward teach about judgment? 3) How do you hold together God's holiness and his mercy in this case?
Significance
The Sabbath breaker's case fixes the seriousness of the Sabbath in law through a single narrated judgment in the wilderness. Doubt: this is a brief legal case rather than a full narrative episode, included because the finding, custody, ruling, and execution are narrated; it should be weighed as borderline if the collection requires fuller stories only.
