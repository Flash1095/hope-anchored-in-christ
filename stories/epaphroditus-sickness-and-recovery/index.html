Epaphroditus' Sickness and Recovery

Story
Epaphroditus' Sickness and Recovery
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Philippians 2:25-30
Retelling
Paul writes that he supposed it necessary to send to the Philippians Epaphroditus, his brother, and companion in labour, and fellowsoldier, but their messenger, and he that ministered to his wants. For Epaphroditus longed after them all, and was full of heaviness, because they had heard that he had been sick. For indeed he was sick nigh unto death, but God had mercy on him, and not on him only, but on Paul also, lest he should have sorrow upon sorrow. Paul therefore sent him the more carefully, that when the Philippians saw him again, they might rejoice, and that Paul might be the less sorrowful. Receive him therefore in the Lord with all gladness, Paul writes, and hold such in reputation, because for the work of Christ he was nigh unto death, not regarding his life, to supply the lack of service toward Paul on the Philippians' part.
Discussion
Epaphroditus is the church's messenger who nearly dies carrying its gift to a prisoner, and Paul's letter honours him precisely for risking his life in service. His recovery is called mercy, not a reward, and his return is a cause for gladness. Reflect: 1) How do you honour messengers and servants who bear the cost of ministry? 2) Why does Paul say God had mercy on him too, lest he have sorrow upon sorrow? 3) What does it mean to hold such in reputation?
Significance
Epaphroditus's sickness and recovery show the human cost of the gospel's supply lines: a Philippian messenger nearly dies serving a prisoner, God spares him, and Paul sends him home to be received with gladness and honour.
