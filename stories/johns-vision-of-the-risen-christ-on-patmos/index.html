John's Vision of the Risen Christ on Patmos

Story
John's Vision of the Risen Christ on Patmos
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Revelation 1:9-20
Retelling
This is a vision John saw, not an event that happened on earth: John, who also am their brother, and companion in tribulation, and in the kingdom and patience of Jesus Christ, was in the isle that is called Patmos, for the word of God, and for the testimony of Jesus Christ. He was in the Spirit on the Lord's day, and heard behind him a great voice, as of a trumpet, saying, I am Alpha and Omega, the first and the last, and, What thou seest, write in a book, and send it unto the seven churches which are in Asia: unto Ephesus, and unto Smyrna, and unto Pergamos, and unto Thyatira, and unto Sardis, and unto Philadelphia, and unto Laodicea. And he turned to see the voice that spake with him, and being turned, he saw seven golden candlesticks, and in the midst of the seven candlesticks one like unto the Son of man, clothed with a garment down to the foot, and girt about the paps with a golden girdle. His head and his hairs were white like wool, as white as snow, and his eyes were as a flame of fire, and his feet like unto fine brass, as if they burned in a furnace, and his voice as the sound of many waters. And he had in his right hand seven stars, and out of his mouth went a sharp twoedged sword, and his countenance was as the sun shineth in his strength. And when John saw him, he fell at his feet as dead. And he laid his right hand upon him, saying unto him, Fear not; I am the first and the last: I am he that liveth, and was dead, and, behold, I am alive for evermore, Amen, and have the keys of hell and of death. Write the things which thou hast seen, and the things which are, and the things which shall be hereafter. The mystery of the seven stars which thou sawest in my right hand, and the seven golden candlesticks: the seven stars are the angels of the seven churches, and the seven candlesticks which thou sawest are the seven churches.
Discussion
Everything in Revelation hangs on this framing: John is exiled on Patmos, in the Spirit on the Lord's day, and what follows is what he saw and was told to write. The risen Christ is described in symbols, white hair, fiery eyes, brass feet, a sword from his mouth, and he speaks as the first and the last who was dead and is alive for evermore. Read every later vision in this book as vision, not as earthly history. Reflect: 1) Why does the book begin with a command to write rather than with an event? 2) What does it mean for an exiled prisoner to see Christ holding the churches' stars? 3) How does Fear not, from the one who holds the keys of death, steady a persecuted church?
Significance
The Patmos vision commissions the whole book: an exiled apostle sees the risen Christ among the seven churches and is told to write what he has seen, what is, and what shall be hereafter, and every vision that follows is given under that command.
