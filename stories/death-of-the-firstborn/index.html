The Death of the Firstborn

Story
The Death of the Firstborn
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Exodus 11:1-10; Exodus 12:29-32
Retelling
God told Moses that one plague more would fall, and after it Pharaoh would thrust Israel out altogether. At midnight the LORD smote all the firstborn in Egypt, from Pharaoh's firstborn on the throne to the firstborn of the captive in the dungeon, and the firstborn of cattle. There was a great cry in Egypt, for there was not a house where someone was not dead. Pharaoh called Moses and Aaron by night and said, Rise up, get out from among my people, you and Israel, go and serve the LORD as you have said, and take your flocks and herds and bless me also. Israel, sheltered by the blood of the Passover lamb, left Egypt in haste.
Discussion
The final plague touches every Egyptian house, including Pharaoh's, and at last breaks his refusal. The same night that brings death to Egypt brings release to Israel, sheltered by blood. Reflect: 1) Why does this plague, unlike the others, end all bargaining? 2) How does the Passover blood change the meaning of judgment for Israel? 3) What does Pharaoh's request, bless me also, reveal about a broken opponent?
Significance
The death of the firstborn is the judgment that finally frees Israel, and it can only be understood together with the Passover, by which God's people are sheltered from the judgment that falls on Egypt.
