Elisha at Dothan

Story
Elisha at Dothan
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Kings 6:8-23
Retelling
The king of Syria was at war with Israel, but his plans kept reaching Israel's king through Elisha. Furious, the Syrian king sent horses, chariots, and a great army to surround Dothan and seize Elisha. In the morning Elisha's servant saw the city ringed with soldiers and cried out in fear. Elisha answered, 'Fear not: for they that be with us are more than they that be with them.' He prayed that his servant's eyes would be opened, and the young man saw the mountain full of horses and chariots of fire around Elisha. When the Syrians came down, Elisha prayed that they would be struck with blindness, then led the blinded army to Samaria. There, with eyes opened, the king of Israel asked whether he should kill them. Elisha said no: set food and water before them and send them home. They ate and drank, returned to their master, and Syrian raids ceased.
Discussion
Elisha's servant saw only the enemy's army; Elisha saw the army of God around them both. Fear shrinks when we pray for opened eyes, and victory here ends not in slaughter but in a shared meal. Reflect: 1) What surrounding army fills your view right now? 2) How do you pray for eyes to see God's protection? 3) When has mercy towards an enemy proved stronger than revenge?
Significance
At Dothan, unseen protection became visible, and Elisha answered an army with a feast, showing that God's power serves mercy as well as defence.
