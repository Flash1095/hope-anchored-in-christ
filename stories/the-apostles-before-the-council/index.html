The Apostles Before the Council

The Apostles Before the Council

Retelling

Jealous at the apostles' growing following, the high priest and the Sadducees arrested them and put them in public prison. During the night an angel opened the doors, brought them out, and told them to stand in the temple and speak the words of this life. By morning they were teaching in the temple again, and the council, finding the prison locked but empty, brought them in quietly. The high priest charged them with filling Jerusalem with their teaching. Peter answered, We must obey God rather than men, and preached Christ crucified and exalted. The council wanted to kill them, but Gamaliel, a respected teacher of the law, counselled restraint: if this work were of men it would fail, but if of God, no one could overthrow it. The council beat the apostles, ordered them not to speak in Jesus' name, and released them, and the apostles left rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer dishonour for the name, teaching daily in the temple and from house to house.

Discussion

Obeying God rather than men cost the apostles a beating and earned them joy. Gamaliel's counsel remains the church's patience test: time exposes human movements and vindicates God's. Reflect: Where does obeying God conflict with obeying human authority in your life? Do you rejoice in dishonour for the name, or only in honour? How do you tell a work of God from a work of men?

Significance

The council scene establishes the apostles' principle of obedience to God over men and Gamaliel's enduring test for distinguishing God's work.
