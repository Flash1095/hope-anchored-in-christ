The Canaanite Woman's Daughter

The Canaanite Woman's Daughter

Retelling

Jesus withdrew to the region of Tyre and Sidon, where a Canaanite woman came crying after him, asking mercy for her daughter, who was grievously tormented by a demon. At first Jesus answered her nothing, and the disciples urged him to send her away. When he said he was sent only to the lost sheep of Israel, she knelt and said, Lord, help me. He answered that it was not right to take the children's bread and throw it to the dogs. She replied that even the dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their masters' table. Jesus praised her great faith, and told her it would be as she wished; her daughter was healed from that very hour.

Discussion

This is the hardest conversation in the Gospels to read, and its point is the woman's refusal to leave: she turned an apparent refusal into a reason to trust further. Persistence, not pedigree, won the day. Reflect: When God seems silent, do you walk away or press in? What crumb of grace are you holding onto in a desperate situation? How does this outsider's faith judge insider complacency?

Significance

The Canaanite woman's faith previews the Gospel's reach to the Gentiles and shows persistence that will not be turned away.

Faith; Persistence; A Light to the Gentiles; Mercy
