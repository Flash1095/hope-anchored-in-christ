Philip Preaches in Samaria

Philip Preaches in Samaria

Retelling

After Stephen's death scattered the Jerusalem believers, Philip went down to a city in Samaria and proclaimed Christ there. Crowds listened eagerly as unclean spirits came out with loud cries and many paralysed and lame people were healed, and there was great joy in the city. Samaria had long followed a sorcerer named Simon, who amazed people claiming to be someone great, but when Philip preached the good news of the kingdom and the name of Jesus Christ, men and women believed and were baptised — and Simon himself believed and was baptised, following Philip in amazement at the signs. When the apostles in Jerusalem heard Samaria had received the word, they sent Peter and John, who prayed for the new believers to receive the Holy Spirit. Simon, seeing the Spirit given through the apostles' hands, offered money to buy the power, and Peter rebuked him sharply: your money perish with you, because you thought the gift of God could be purchased. Simon begged Peter to pray for him.

Discussion

The Gospel crossed Samaria's old hostility and exposed its celebrity spirituality: Simon wanted the power, not the Lord. Peter's rebuke draws a hard line against buying God. Reflect: Where do you want the Spirit's power without the Spirit's Lord? How does the church receive new believers from hostile ground today? What 'Simon' tendencies — status, spectacle, purchase — live in your own faith?

Significance

Philip in Samaria carries the Gospel beyond Jerusalem to a despised neighbour people and confronts the attempt to buy spiritual power.
