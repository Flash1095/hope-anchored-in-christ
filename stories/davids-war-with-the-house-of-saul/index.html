David's War with the House of Saul

Retelling

After Saul's death, David went up to Hebron and was anointed king over Judah, while Abner, Saul's captain, made Ish-bosheth, Saul's son, king over the rest of Israel. The two houses met at the pool of Gibeon, where twelve young men from each side seized each other by the head and thrust swords into each other's sides, and all twenty-four fell together. A fierce battle followed, and Abner killed Asahel, Joab's swift brother, in self-defence as Asahel refused to turn aside. The war between the houses was long, with David growing stronger and the house of Saul growing weaker. Abner quarrelled with Ish-bosheth over Rizpah, Saul's concubine, and defected to David, promising to bring all Israel over to him. Joab, nursing revenge for Asahel, murdered Abner at Hebron, and David publicly mourned him, fasting and lamenting, so that all the people knew the king had no part in it. Then two captains, Baanah and Rechab, murdered Ish-bosheth in his bed and brought his head to David, expecting reward. David instead had them executed for slaying a righteous man in his own house, and buried Ish-bosheth's head in Abner's grave at Hebron. Then all the tribes of Israel came to David at Hebron.

Discussion

The civil war after Saul shows David refusing to seize the kingdom by murder, even when murderers brought him the crown. He mourned Abner, executed Ish-bosheth's killers, and let the tribes come to him freely. Reflect: 1) How do you respond when someone offers you advancement through wrongdoing? 2) What does public mourning for an enemy commander say about David's kingship? 3) Where are you tempted to hurry God's timing by accepting a shortcut?

Significance

David's war with Saul's house ended not by assassination but by the tribes freely coming to Hebron, after David refused the fruits of two murders.
