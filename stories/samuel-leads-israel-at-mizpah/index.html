Samuel Leads Israel at Mizpah

Retelling

After the ark had rested twenty years at Kirjath-jearim, Samuel spoke to all Israel: if they returned to the LORD with all their hearts, they must put away the strange gods and Ashtaroth and prepare their hearts to serve him only. All Israel did so, gathering at Mizpah, drawing water and pouring it out before the LORD, fasting, and confessing, 'We have sinned against the LORD.' While Samuel offered a sucking lamb as a burnt offering, the Philistines drew near to battle, but the LORD thundered with a great thunder on the Philistines that day and discomfited them, and Israel pursued and smote them to Beth-car. Samuel set up a stone between Mizpah and Shen and called it Ebenezer, saying, 'Hitherto hath the LORD helped us.' The Philistines were subdued and came no more into Israel's coast in Samuel's days, and Samuel judged Israel all his life, going on a yearly circuit to Bethel, Gilgal, and Mizpah, and returning to Ramah.

Discussion

Mizpah pairs repentance with deliverance: Israel put away its idols, confessed its sin, and only then saw God thunder against its enemies. Ebenezer marks help received, not victory claimed. Reflect: 1) What idols need putting away before you ask God to fight for you? 2) How does confession change the way you pray about enemies? 3) What Ebenezer stone would mark God's help in your life so far?

Significance

At Mizpah, repentance, prayer, and God's thundering deliverance restored Israel, and Ebenezer became the memorial of help received.
