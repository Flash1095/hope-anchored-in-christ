Pentecost

Story
Pentecost
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Acts 2; Joel 2:28-32
Retelling
Fifty days after the resurrection, the disciples were gathered in Jerusalem when suddenly a sound like a rushing wind filled the house and tongues of fire rested on each of them. Filled with the Holy Spirit, they began speaking in languages they had never learned, declaring the wonders of God to Jews from every nation who were in the city for the Passover feast. Peter stood and preached that Jesus, crucified and risen, was Lord and Christ, and the crowd was cut to the heart. Three thousand people were baptized that day, and the church was born.
Discussion
Pentecost reverses Babel: where pride scattered the nations, the Spirit gathers them into one people. The same Spirit who made fishermen bold lives in every believer. Reflect: 1) How does the Holy Spirit's power differ from mere enthusiasm? 2) What would Spirit-filled boldness look like in your life this week? 3) How does Pentecost shape your view of the global church?
Significance
Pentecost reversed Babel's scattering and launched Christ's church in power. The same Spirit who fell then still fills ordinary believers to witness — that is hope with wind and fire in it.
