Saul's Unlawful Sacrifice at Gilgal

Saul's Unlawful Sacrifice at Gilgal

Retelling

Saul reigned one year, and in his second year he chose three thousand men of Israel, keeping two thousand with himself at Michmash and one thousand with Jonathan at Gibeah. Jonathan smote the Philistine garrison at Geba, and the Philistines gathered a great host against Israel, with thirty thousand chariots, six thousand horsemen, and people as the sand of the seashore. The men of Israel, distressed and hiding in caves, thickets, rocks, and pits, began to slip away, and Saul remained at Gilgal with a trembling remnant. Samuel had appointed seven days, and when the days passed and Samuel had not come, Saul offered the burnt offering himself. As he finished, Samuel arrived and asked what he had done. Saul explained that the people were scattering and the Philistines were gathering, so he had forced himself to offer. Samuel answered that Saul had done foolishly: he had not kept the commandment of the LORD, and therefore his kingdom would not continue, for the LORD had sought a man after his own heart.

Discussion

Saul's sacrifice looked pious but was disobedience under pressure: he took the priest's role because waiting felt riskier than breaking rank. Fear of a scattering army overrode fear of God. Reflect: 1) What do you do when God's timing and your deadline collide? 2) How do you tell the difference between initiative and disobedience? 3) What has fear already cost you that waiting might have saved?

Significance

At Gilgal, Saul's unlawful sacrifice under pressure cost him the permanence of his kingdom, and Samuel announced that God had sought a man after his own heart.
