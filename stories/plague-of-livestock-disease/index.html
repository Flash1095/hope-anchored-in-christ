The Plague on Livestock

Story
The Plague on Livestock
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Exodus 9:1-7
Retelling
Moses warned Pharaoh that if he still refused, the hand of the LORD would fall on Egypt's livestock in the field, on horses, asses, camels, oxen, and sheep, with a grievous murrain. The next day the LORD did it: all the livestock of Egypt died. But of Israel's livestock not one died, a separation God had announced beforehand. Pharaoh sent men to check, and found that not one of Israel's animals had died. Yet Pharaoh's heart was hardened, and he did not let the people go.
Discussion
Livestock was Egypt's wealth, transport, and food, and its death strikes the economy while sparing Israel's herds entirely. Pharaoh verifies the distinction and still refuses. Reflect: 1) How do you respond when God protects others and judges what you trusted? 2) What wealth or security would its loss expose as an idol? 3) Why does first-hand verification not change Pharaoh?
Significance
The death of Egypt's livestock shows God's judgment falling on Egypt's strength while preserving Israel, and Pharaoh's refusal continuing even after he has checked the facts himself.
