Ezekiel's Siege Signs

Story
Ezekiel's Siege Signs
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Ezekiel 4-5
Retelling
In Babylon, God told Ezekiel to take a clay tile, draw Jerusalem on it, and lay siege against it with a ramp, battering rams, and camps round about, with an iron pan set as a wall between him and the city. He was to lie on his left side for the iniquity of Israel and then on his right side for Judah, day for year, and to eat rationed bread baked over dung, a picture of the defiled bread the exiles would eat among the nations. Then he shaved his head and beard, dividing the hair: a third burned inside the city, a third struck with the sword around it, and a third scattered to the wind, picturing how Jerusalem's people would fall by famine, sword, and scattering.
Discussion
Ezekiel preached with his body before he preached with words. The siege signs made coming judgment visible and unavoidable, so no one could say they had not been warned. Reflect: 1) Why does God sometimes make His warning impossible to ignore? 2) How do you respond when God's warning is about your own community, not just someone else's? 3) What does Ezekiel's costly, uncomfortable obedience teach you about sharing hard truths faithfully?
Significance
God's warnings are vivid because judgment is real — and the prophet's strange obedience was itself the sermon. Doubt: these are prophetic sign-acts, staged pictures of a coming siege, so they are drafted as borderline rather than as ordinary historical episodes.
