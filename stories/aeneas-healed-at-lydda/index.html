Aeneas Healed at Lydda

Story
Aeneas Healed at Lydda
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Acts 9:32-35
Retelling
As Peter passed throughout all quarters, he came down also to the saints which dwelt at Lydda. There he found a certain man named Aeneas, which had kept his bed eight years, and was sick of the palsy. Peter said unto him, Aeneas, Jesus Christ maketh thee whole: arise, and make thy bed. And he arose immediately. And all that dwelt at Lydda and Saron saw him, and turned to the Lord.
Discussion
Aeneas is healed in a single sentence, and the healing's whole purpose is the turning of two towns to the Lord. Peter claims no power of his own: Jesus Christ makes whole. Reflect: 1) Why does Luke record this brief healing between Saul's conversion and Tabitha's raising? 2) What does it mean for a healing to point beyond itself to turning to the Lord? 3) How do you speak of Christ's power without claiming it as your own?
Significance
Aeneas's eight-year paralysis ends in a moment, and the quiet miracle at Lydda prepares the way for Tabitha's raising and for Peter's journey to the Gentile mission, showing Christ's healing power at work through his apostle beyond Jerusalem.
