The Tabernacle Is Built

The Tabernacle Is Built

Retelling

After the golden calf and the second tablets, Moses gathered Israel and called for willing offerings for the tabernacle: gold, silver, brass, blue, purple, scarlet, fine linen, skins, wood, oil, and spices. The people brought so much that Moses had to command them to stop bringing. Under Bezalel and Oholiab, whom God had filled with wisdom and skill, the craftsmen made the ark, mercy seat, table, lampstand, altars, curtains, and priestly garments, exactly as God had shown Moses on the mountain. When all the work was finished, Moses inspected it, saw it was done as the LORD commanded, and blessed the people. Then the cloud covered the tent of meeting, the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle, and Moses could not enter. From then on, whenever the cloud lifted, Israel journeyed, and when it stayed, they remained, with fire by night over the tabernacle in the sight of all Israel.

Discussion

The tabernacle turns a wandering camp into a dwelling place: God, who brought Israel out, now comes to live among them, by their own willing gifts and skilled hands. Reflect: 1) What does the people's overflowing generosity teach about giving? 2) Why does exact obedience, as God commanded, matter in worship? 3) How does the cloud's guidance shape daily trust?

Significance

The building of the tabernacle is the goal of the Exodus made visible: a holy God dwelling among a redeemed people, guiding them by cloud and fire from the place where atonement is made.

Themes

Worship; God's presence; Obedience
