Abram in Egypt

Retelling

A grievous famine came upon the land, and Abram went down into Egypt to sojourn there. As he drew near to enter Egypt, he told Sarai his wife that she was a fair woman to look upon, and he feared that when the Egyptians saw her they would say she was his wife and kill him, but save her alive. He therefore asked her to say that she was his sister, so that it might be well with him for her sake and his soul might live because of her. When Abram came into Egypt, the Egyptians beheld that the woman was very fair, and the princes of Pharaoh saw her and commended her before Pharaoh, and she was taken into Pharaoh's house. Pharaoh entreated Abram well for her sake, and Abram gained sheep, and oxen, and he asses, and menservants, and maidservants, and she asses, and camels. But the LORD plagued Pharaoh and his house with great plagues because of Sarai, Abram's wife. Pharaoh called Abram and asked why he had not told him that she was his wife, and why he had said she was his sister, so that Pharaoh might have taken her to him to wife. Pharaoh then said, behold thy wife, take her, and go thy way, and he commanded his men concerning Abram, and they sent him away, and his wife, and all that he had.

Themes

Fear; Truth; God's Protection

Discussion

Abram's fear in Egypt put Sarai in danger, yet God protected her and plagued Pharaoh's house until the truth came out. Fear can make us reach for a half-truth, but God remains faithful even when our faith falters. Reflect: 1) Where does fear tempt you to hide the truth instead of trusting God? 2) How does God's protection of Sarai show His care for those caught up in someone else's fear? 3) What does this story teach you about confessing the truth rather than managing the danger yourself?

Significance

God guarded His promise through Abram's failure in Egypt: Sarai was restored untouched, and the covenant line was preserved not by Abram's plan but by God's protection.
