The Crucifixion

The Crucifixion

Retelling

After a night of trials, Jesus was flogged, mocked with a crown of thorns, and led outside Jerusalem to a hill called Golgotha. There He was nailed to a cross between two criminals, with a sign above His head reading 'Jesus of Nazareth, King of the Jews.' Darkness covered the land at midday, and at three in the afternoon Jesus cried out and gave up His spirit. The temple curtain tore from top to bottom, the earth shook, and a Roman centurion said, 'Truly this was the Son of God.' Joseph of Arimathea laid His body in a new tomb, and a stone sealed the entrance.

Discussion

The cross is where God's justice and God's love meet: sin is not overlooked, it is borne — by Jesus, in our place. There is no deeper proof of love than this. Reflect: 1) What does it mean that Jesus died 'for you' — not just for the world? 2) How does the cross change the way you view your own guilt and shame? 3) What does the torn curtain mean for your access to God?

Significance

The cross is where God's justice and love meet, and where this blog's anchor is set. Because Jesus finished the work there, no sin placed on Him is left unpaid for those who trust Him.
