Jehu's Purge of Ahab's House

Story
Jehu's Purge of Ahab's House
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
2 Kings 9-10
Retelling
Elisha sent a prophet to Ramoth-gilead to anoint Jehu, commander of Israel's army, as king, with a commission to destroy the house of Ahab, who had led Israel into Baal worship. Jehu drove to Jezreel, where King Joram was recovering from wounds. Joram rode out to meet him and asked, 'Is it peace, Jehu?' Jehu answered, 'What peace, so long as the whoredoms of thy mother Jezebel and her witchcrafts are so many?' Joram turned to flee and was shot through the heart, and his body was thrown onto Naboth's field. Jezebel, painted and at her window, was thrown down by her own eunuchs, and dogs ate her flesh, as Elijah had foretold. Jehu then wrote to Samaria demanding the heads of Ahab's seventy sons, destroyed the worshippers of Baal in their own temple, and broke down Baal's house. But Jehu himself did not turn from the golden calves of Jeroboam, and his dynasty, promised four generations, would not last.
Discussion
Jehu carried out God's judgment on a corrupt house, yet his own story warns that zeal for judgment is not the same as a whole heart for God. He destroyed Baal and kept the calves. Reflect: 1) What is the difference between God's judgment and personal vengeance? 2) Where might zeal be covering a compromise you have kept? 3) How does Jehu's incomplete reform challenge partial obedience?
Significance
Jehu ended Ahab's dynasty and Baal worship in Israel, but his purge stopped short of full obedience, showing that judgment without repentance reforms nothing lasting. Doubt: Hosea later condemns the bloodshed at Jezreel, so whether Jehu's purge stayed within its commission or exceeded it is debated among interpreters.
