Elisha and the Widow's Oil

Elisha and the Widow's Oil

Retelling

A widow of one of the sons of the prophets came to Elisha in distress. Her husband was dead, a creditor was coming to take her two sons as slaves for the debt, and all she had in the house was a small pot of oil. Elisha asked what she had, then told her to borrow empty vessels from all her neighbours, not a few, shut the door behind herself and her sons, and pour. She did as he said, and the oil kept flowing until every vessel was full; when there was no vessel left, the oil stopped. Elisha told her to sell the oil, pay the debt, and live on the rest with her sons. A household that had nothing but a pot of oil became, by obedience behind a shut door, a household set free from debt and slavery.

Discussion

God's provision began with what the widow already had, not with what she wished she had, and it grew as far as her borrowed vessels allowed. Small obedience can hold large provision. Reflect: 1) What small thing in your house might God want to start with? 2) How does 'borrow not a few' challenge small expectations? 3) Where do you need provision that also protects your family from a deeper cost?

Significance

The widow's oil shows Elisha's ministry at its most domestic: God meets debt, slavery, and grief with miraculous provision given through simple obedience.
