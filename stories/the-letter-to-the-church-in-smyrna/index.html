The Letter to the Church in Smyrna

Story
The Letter to the Church in Smyrna
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Revelation 2:8-11
Retelling
In a vision, John was told to write unto the angel of the church in Smyrna: These things saith the first and the last, which was dead, and is alive. I know thy works, and tribulation, and poverty, but thou art rich, and I know the blasphemy of them which say they are Jews, and are not, but are the synagogue of Satan. Fear none of those things which thou shalt suffer: behold, the devil shall cast some of you into prison, that ye may be tried, and ye shall have tribulation ten days. Be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life. He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches. He that overcometh shall not be hurt of the second death.
Discussion
This is a vision-message, and it is the only letter with no rebuke: Smyrna is poor yet rich, facing prison and tribulation, and is told to fear none of it and to be faithful unto death. The crown of life is promised not for success but for faithfulness through suffering. Reflect: 1) How can a poor church be rich? 2) What does faithful unto death mean when death is a real prospect? 3) Why does Christ introduce himself here as the one who was dead and is alive?
Significance
The Smyrna letter is the book's word to a suffering church: poverty is not failure, prison is a trial with a limit, and faithfulness unto death receives a crown of life that the second death cannot touch.
