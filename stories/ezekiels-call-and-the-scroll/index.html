Ezekiel's Call and the Scroll

Ezekiel's Call and the Scroll

Retelling

Among the exiles by the river Chebar in Babylon, the heavens opened and Ezekiel saw a storm wind, living creatures, wheels within wheels, and above them a throne with the likeness of a man, radiant as a rainbow. Falling on his face, he heard a voice sending him to the rebellious house of Israel, whether they listened or not. A hand held out a scroll written front and back with lamentations, and he was told to eat it; it tasted as sweet as honey in his mouth. After sitting overwhelmed among the exiles for seven days, he was appointed a watchman, responsible to warn the wicked and the righteous alike.

Discussion

Ezekiel had to take God's word inside himself before he could speak it to others. His job was faithfulness, not results. Reflect: 1) Are you feeding on God's word before you try to speak for Him? 2) How do you keep going when people refuse to listen? 3) What does it mean for you to be a watchman for those around you?

Significance

God's messenger must first receive the word himself, then speak it faithfully whatever the response. Doubt: this is a visionary call account, not an ordinary historical episode, so its narrative frame is visionary in character.
