Nehemiah's Final Reforms

Story
Nehemiah's Final Reforms
Testament
Old Testament
Scripture
Nehemiah 13
Retelling
After twelve years as governor, Nehemiah returned to the king of Babylon, and after certain days he obtained leave and came back to Jerusalem, where he found evils that had grown in his absence. Eliashib the priest had prepared a great chamber in the courts of the house of God for Tobiah the Ammonite, the very enemy who had opposed the wall, and the temple chambers that should have held offerings had been emptied for him. Nehemiah was grieved, cast out all Tobiah's household stuff, commanded the chambers to be cleansed, and brought back the vessels, offerings, and tithes, restoring the Levites who had fled to their fields for lack of support. He also found that the sabbath was being profaned: men were treading winepresses, loading asses, and bringing goods into Jerusalem on the sabbath, and men of Tyre sold fish and merchandise there on the holy day. Nehemiah contended with the nobles, shut the gates before the sabbath, posted servants at them, and warned the merchants who lodged outside the wall that he would lay hands on them if they came again. And he found Jews who had married women of Ashdod, Ammon, and Moab, whose children could not even speak the Jews' language but spoke half in the speech of Ashdod. Nehemiah contended with them, cursed them, smote some, plucked off their hair, and made them swear they would not give their daughters to foreign sons nor take foreign daughters for their sons, reminding them that Solomon, beloved of God, had been caused to sin by foreign wives. He also cleansed the priesthood, for a son of Joiada had married the daughter of Sanballat the Horonite, and Nehemiah chased him away. He appointed treasurers over the treasuries and ended his book with the prayer, 'Remember me, O my God, for good.'
Discussion
Nehemiah's last chapter is reform by confrontation: a temple chamber emptied of an enemy's furniture, sabbath gates shut against merchants, and mixed marriages broken up with curses and pulled hair. The severity is inseparable from his love for a community slipping back into the sins that had caused the exile. Reflect: 1) What compromises creep back into a community when its reformer leaves? 2) How do you distinguish zealous reform from harshness? 3) Why does Nehemiah end not with a triumph but with a prayer to be remembered for good?
Significance
Nehemiah's final reforms cleansed the temple chamber, restored the sabbath, confronted intermarriage, and purged the priesthood, ending in a prayer to be remembered for good.
