The Hidden Treasure and the Pearl

The Hidden Treasure and the Pearl

Retelling

The kingdom of heaven is like treasure hidden in a field. A man found it, hid it again, and in his joy went and sold all he had and bought that field. Again, the kingdom is like a merchant hunting for fine pearls, who, finding one pearl of great value, went and sold all he had and bought it. In both stories the discovery is sudden and the response total: joy, not duty, drives the sale. The finder does not bargain for a share of the treasure or keep a reserve; everything goes to secure the one thing worth everything.

Discussion

Both finders sell all, but the motive is joy, not grim sacrifice. The kingdom is worth everything because it is worth more than everything. Reflect: What have you actually sold — not just admired — for the kingdom? Is your discipleship joyful or resentful? What reserve are you still keeping back from the purchase?

Significance

Treasure and pearl teach that the kingdom is of supreme value, worth surrendering everything to gain.

Themes

Kingdom value; Joy; Sacrifice; Discipleship
