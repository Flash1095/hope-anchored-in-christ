The Healing of the Paralytic

Story
The Healing of the Paralytic
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 9:1-8; Mark 2:1-12; Luke 5:17-26
Retelling
In Capernaum, so many crowded into the house where Jesus was teaching that four men carrying a paralysed friend could not get through the door. They climbed onto the roof, opened it, and lowered the man on his mat right in front of Jesus. Seeing their faith, Jesus said first, Friend, your sins are forgiven. The scribes silently accused him of blasphemy, since only God forgives sins. Knowing their thoughts, Jesus asked which was easier to say — your sins are forgiven, or rise and walk — and then, to prove the Son of Man had authority on earth to forgive sins, told the man to rise, take up his mat, and go home. He did, glorifying God, and the crowd, amazed, said they had never seen anything like it.
Discussion
The friends solved the access problem; Jesus solved the deeper one first. Forgiveness preceded healing because sin, not paralysis, was the man's greatest need. Reflect: Whose faith is carrying you toward Jesus right now? Are you asking him only for the visible fix while he wants to deal with something deeper? Who could you carry — literally or prayerfully — through a roof?
Significance
The paralytic's healing publicly demonstrates Jesus' authority to forgive sins, the claim at the heart of the Gospel.
