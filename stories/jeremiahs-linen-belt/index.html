Jeremiah's Linen Belt

Jeremiah's Linen Belt

Retelling

The LORD said unto Jeremiah, Go and get thee a linen girdle, and put it upon thy loins, and put it not in water. Jeremiah got the girdle and put it on his loins. Then the word of the LORD came a second time: Take the girdle that thou hast got, which is upon thy loins, and arise, go to Euphrates, and hide it there in a hole of the rock. Jeremiah went and hid it by Euphrates. After many days the LORD said, Arise, go to Euphrates, and take the girdle from the place where thou hast hid it. Jeremiah went, dug, and took the girdle — and behold, the girdle was marred, it was profitable for nothing. Then the word came: After this manner will I mar the pride of Judah, and the great pride of Jerusalem. This evil people, which refuse to hear My words, which walk in the imagination of their heart, and walk after other gods, to serve them, and to worship them, shall even be as this girdle, which is good for nothing. For as the girdle cleaveth to the loins of a man, so have I caused to cleave unto Me the whole house of Israel and the whole house of Judah, saith the LORD, that they might be unto Me for a people, and for a name, and for a praise, and for a glory: but they would not hear.

Discussion

A new linen belt, buried by a river and dug up ruined, preached what pride does to a people. Reflect: 1) Why does God picture His people as a garment meant to cling close to Him? 2) What does it mean that Judah was ruined not by being far from God, but by refusing to cling? 3) How does a small, spoiled object preach louder than a long sermon?

Significance

A people made to cling to God like a belt to the waist, but proud and turned to other gods, ends up like a buried girdle — marred and good for nothing. Doubt: this is a prophetic sign-act, a staged picture or lived sign, rather than an ordinary historical episode.
