The Parable of the Sower

Story
The Parable of the Sower
Testament
New Testament
Scripture
Matthew 13:1-23; Mark 4:1-20; Luke 8:4-15
Retelling
A sower went out to sow, and as he scattered seed, some fell on the path, where birds ate it; some on rocky ground, where it sprang up quickly but withered for lack of root; some among thorns, where it was choked; and some on good soil, where it yielded thirty, sixty, and a hundredfold. When his disciples asked why he taught in parables, Jesus explained that the seed is the word of the kingdom. The path is the hearer from whom the evil one snatches it; the rocky ground is the one who receives it with joy but falls away when trouble comes; the thorns are the cares of the world and the deceitfulness of riches; and the good soil is the one who hears, understands, and bears fruit.
Discussion
The variable in the parable is never the seed or the sower, but the soil. Every hearer is one of the four soils, and soils can change. Reflect: Which soil describes your hearing right now — path, rocks, thorns, or good ground? What thorn — worry or wealth — is choking the word in you? What fruit would show the seed has truly taken root?
Significance
The Sower explains why the same word produces such different results and makes fruitful hearing, not mere hearing, the mark of the kingdom.
