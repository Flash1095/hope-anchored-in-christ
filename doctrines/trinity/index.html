Trinity

Doctrine
Trinity
Summary
The Trinity teaches that God is one in essence and three in Persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Each Person is fully God, distinct from the others, and eternally united, and Christians worship one God, not three gods.
Biblical Support
Matthew 28:19; 2 Corinthians 13:14; John 1:1; John 10:30; Acts 5:3-4
Historical Development
Affirmed against Arianism at the Council of Nicaea in AD 325 and clarified at Constantinople in AD 381, the doctrine was expressed in the Nicene Creed and defended by Athanasius and the Cappadocian Fathers.
Interpretations
All historic Christian traditions affirm the Trinity, though they differ in explaining the procession of the Spirit, including the Eastern Orthodox and Roman Catholic debate over the filioque clause.
Related Topics
God; Jesus Christ; Holy Spirit; Monotheism
Related People
Athanasius; Augustine of Hippo; Basil of Caesarea; Thomas Aquinas
