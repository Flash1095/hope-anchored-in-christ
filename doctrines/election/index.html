Election

Doctrine
Election
Summary
Election is God's gracious choice, before the foundation of the world, of persons to be saved in Christ. It emphasizes that salvation begins with God's initiative and mercy, not human merit.
Biblical Support
Ephesians 1:4-5; Romans 8:29-30; Romans 9:11; John 6:37; 2 Thessalonians 2:13
Historical Development
Taught by Augustine against Pelagianism and developed by Aquinas, Luther, and Calvin, election became a defining Reformed doctrine at the Synod of Dort (1618-1619).
Interpretations
Christians differ on whether election is unconditional and individual, conditional on foreseen faith, or primarily corporate in Christ, with Reformed, Arminian, and Lutheran views stated in their confessions.
Related Topics
Predestination; Salvation; Grace; Sovereignty
Related People
Augustine of Hippo; Thomas Aquinas; John Calvin; Jacobus Arminius
