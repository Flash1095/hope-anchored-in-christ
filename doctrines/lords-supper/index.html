Lord's Supper

Summary

The Lord's Supper, also called Communion or the Eucharist, is the meal Jesus instituted on the night He was betrayed, in which believers remember His death, proclaim the gospel, and share fellowship with Christ and one another through bread and the cup.

Biblical Support

Matthew 26:26-29; Luke 22:19-20; 1 Corinthians 11:23-26; John 6:53-56

Historical Development

Celebrated from the earliest church and described by Justin Martyr (c. AD 155), its meaning was debated at the Reformation, with the Roman Catholic doctrine of transubstantiation defined at the Fourth Lateran Council (1215) and Trent.

Interpretations

Christians differ on Christ's presence in the elements: Roman Catholics teach transubstantiation, Lutherans teach sacramental union, Reformed Christians teach a spiritual presence, and many Baptists and evangelicals teach a memorial view.
