Baptism

Doctrine
Baptism
Summary
Baptism is the Christian rite of initiation with water, commanded by Christ, signifying union with Him in His death and resurrection, cleansing from sin, and welcome into the church. It is an outward sign of God's gracious covenant promise.
Biblical Support
Matthew 28:19; Acts 2:38; Romans 6:3-4; Colossians 2:12; 1 Peter 3:21
Historical Development
Practiced from the New Testament onward and described in the Didache (c. AD 100), baptism was debated at the Reformation, when Anabaptists defended believer's baptism and Reformers such as Luther, Calvin, and Zwingli gave differing accounts.
Interpretations
Christians differ on subjects and mode: Baptist and many evangelical churches baptize professing believers by immersion, while Presbyterian, Methodist, Lutheran, Catholic, and Orthodox churches also baptize infants, by pouring, sprinkling, or immersion.
