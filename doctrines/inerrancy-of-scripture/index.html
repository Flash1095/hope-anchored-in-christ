Inerrancy of Scripture

Doctrine
Inerrancy of Scripture
Summary
Inerrancy is the belief that Scripture, in its original writings, is without error in all it affirms, including matters of faith, history, and the created world. It rests on the character of God, who cannot lie, speaking truthfully through His Word.
Biblical Support
Psalm 19:7; Psalm 119:160; John 17:17; Titus 1:2; Numbers 23:19
Historical Development
Taught in substance by Augustine, Aquinas, and the Reformers, the modern term was clarified by Princeton theologians such as B. B. Warfield and in the Chicago Statement on Biblical Inerrancy (1978).
Interpretations
Many evangelicals affirm inerrancy as defined in Chicago, while other Christians prefer terms such as infallibility or trustworthiness, and Christians differ on how genre, phenomenological language, and textual questions should be handled.
Related Topics
Bible; Inspiration of Scripture; Authority; Truth
Related People
Augustine of Hippo; Thomas Aquinas; B. B. Warfield; Charles Hodge
