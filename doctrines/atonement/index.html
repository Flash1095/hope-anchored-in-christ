Atonement

Summary

The Atonement is Christ's saving work in His life, death, and resurrection, by which sinners are reconciled to God. At the cross Jesus bore sin, defeated evil, and opened the way to forgiveness and new life.

Biblical Support

Isaiah 53:5-6; Romans 3:25; 2 Corinthians 5:21; 1 Peter 2:24; Colossians 2:15

Historical Development

Taught throughout church history, it was explored by Irenaeus as recapitulation, Anselm in Cur Deus Homo (c. 1098) as satisfaction, and the Reformers as penal substitution, with Christus Victor emphasized in early writers.

Interpretations

Christians explain the cross in complementary ways, including penal substitution, Christus Victor, satisfaction, moral influence, and governmental views, differing on which image should be primary.
