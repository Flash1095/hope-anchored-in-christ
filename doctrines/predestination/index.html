Predestination

Doctrine
Predestination
Summary
Predestination is God's eternal purpose to save His people in Christ, worked out in calling, justification, sanctification, and glorification. In its broadest sense it affirms that salvation rests on God's gracious plan from beginning to end.
Biblical Support
Romans 8:29-30; Ephesians 1:5; Ephesians 1:11; Acts 4:28; 2 Timothy 1:9
Historical Development
Developed by Augustine in debates over grace, predestination was taught by Aquinas, emphasized by Luther and Calvin, and debated at the Synod of Dort (1618-1619) and in Arminian responses.
Interpretations
Reformed Christians teach unconditional predestination to salvation, Arminians teach predestination based on God's foreknowledge of faith, and Lutherans and others frame it in distinct ways, while all orthodox views reject salvation by merit.
Related Topics
Election; Salvation; Grace; Providence; Free Will
Related People
Augustine of Hippo; Thomas Aquinas; John Calvin; Jacobus Arminius
