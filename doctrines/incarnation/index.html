Doctrine
Incarnation
Summary
The Incarnation is the belief that God the Son took on true human nature in the person of Jesus of Nazareth, conceived by the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary. Jesus is therefore fully God and fully man in one Person, without mixture or separation.
Biblical Support
John 1:14; Philippians 2:6-8; Colossians 2:9; Hebrews 2:14; 1 Timothy 3:16
Historical Development
Defined at the Council of Chalcedon in AD 451 against Nestorianism and Eutychianism, the Chalcedonian Definition confessed one Person in two natures, building on the work of Athanasius, Cyril of Alexandria, and Leo the Great.
Interpretations
Christians agree on the Chalcedonian confession, while differing in the language they use to describe how Christ's divine and human natures relate, a discussion continued in Miaphysite and Reformed traditions.
