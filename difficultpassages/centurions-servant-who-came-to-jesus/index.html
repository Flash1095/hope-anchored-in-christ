The Centurion's Servant: Who Came to Jesus?

Matthew says the centurion himself came to Jesus and spoke to him, while Luke says the centurion stayed at home and sent Jewish elders, then friends, to speak on his behalf. Both cannot be literally true in the same sense: either the man came in person or he did not. Readers ask whether one writer has simply got the scene wrong.

Explanations

The widely accepted explanation is ancient agency: in the ancient world, and in ordinary language still, a person is said to do what he does through his authorised messengers. Matthew compresses the messengers out and reports the centurion as the actor; Luke spells out the intermediaries. Luke's own version supports this, since the friends carry the centurion's words in the first person ('I am not worthy that thou shouldest enter under my roof'), showing the message counted as his speech. There is no disagreement about the request, the faith Jesus commends, or the healing at a distance.

Site Assessment

Site position — this is compression, not contradiction. Matthew reports the action, Luke reports the means, and both describe the same encounter. The site does not present this as Scripture's own verdict; it is the site's assessment of how the two accounts fit.
