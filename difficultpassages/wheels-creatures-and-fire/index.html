Wheels, Creatures, and Fire

Passage
Wheels, Creatures, and Fire
Reference
Ezekiel 1:4-28
Difficulty
Ezekiel's opening vision — a whirlwind, four living creatures with four faces, wheels within wheels full of eyes, and a throne of sapphire — reads like a machine or a hallucination, and modern readers sometimes treat it as a literal vehicle or an ancient UFO account.
Explanations
The vision is a throne theophany, a symbolic picture of God's glory, mobility, and holiness, built from temple and royal imagery his exiled audience would recognise: cherubim, fire, wheels for movement in every direction, eyes for omniscience. Its details are symbolic, not engineering specifications: the wheels do not turn aside because God's purpose does not swerve, and the creatures' faces gather the highest forms of created life around the throne. Ezekiel himself falls on his face, which is the vision's intended response, not technical analysis.
Site Assessment
Site assessment: The vision is symbolic theophany, not a literal machine. It teaches that God's glory has come to the exiles in Babylon, not that Ezekiel saw a vehicle.
