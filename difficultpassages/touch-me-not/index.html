'Touch Me Not'

Passage
'Touch Me Not'
Reference
John 20:17
Difficulty
The risen Jesus says to Mary Magdalene, 'Touch me not; for I am not yet ascended to my Father.' Yet Matthew says the women 'held him by the feet, and worshipped him,' and a week later Jesus invites Thomas to touch his wounds. Readers ask whether Jesus contradicted himself, whether touching was forbidden on Sunday and allowed later, or whether the translation misleads.
Explanations
The widely accepted resolution is translation: the Greek verb with its present imperative is better rendered 'stop clinging to me' or 'do not hold on to me' than a blanket ban on touching. Mary, weeping and overwhelmed, was clinging to Jesus as if the old companionship could simply resume; Jesus redirects her to the new situation — he is ascending, and she must go and tell his brethren. That fits Matthew, where holding his feet in worship is not rebuked, and Thomas, where touching is invited as evidence. The difference is between clinging to prevent departure and touching in worship or belief, not between two rules about contact.
Site Assessment
Site position — 'do not cling to me' is the better reading, and it removes the apparent contradiction with Matthew 28:9 and John 20:27. The site notes this is a translation judgment, widely shared but not unanimous in wording, and the underlying point — that resurrection changes the mode of Jesus' presence — stands on either rendering.
