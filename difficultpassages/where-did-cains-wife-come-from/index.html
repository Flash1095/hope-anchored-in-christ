Where Did Cain's Wife Come From?

Passage
Where Did Cain's Wife Come From?
Reference
Genesis 4:16-17
Difficulty
After Cain kills Abel, Genesis 4:17 says Cain 'knew his wife; and she conceived.' But the story so far has named only Adam, Eve, Cain, and Abel. If Adam and Eve were the first humans and their children were the only people on earth, there seems to be nobody for Cain to marry, and nobody to populate the city he is said to build. Readers reasonably ask whether the text has quietly assumed other people outside the family, which would sit awkwardly with Adam as the first man.
Explanations
The usual answer reads Genesis 4 together with Genesis 5:4, which says Adam 'begat sons and daughters' over a long life. Cain's wife, on this reading, was a sister or other close female relative, born to Adam and Eve but not named in the compressed early narrative, which names only the children who matter to the storyline. Early in human history, before the later law forbidding close marriage and before the accumulation of harmful genetic effects that such laws also guard against, marriage within the family was the only possibility and is not presented as a moral problem in the text. The city Cain builds is likewise not evidence of a large outside population: in ancient usage a small fortified settlement, built over time by a growing family, could carry the name. An alternative suggestion, that God created other humans alongside Adam, has little support in the text itself, which traces all humanity to the one pair and treats Cain's line as part of that family.
Site Assessment
The site's position is that the text gives no reason to invent a second human origin: Cain married a relative from Adam and Eve's wider family, unnamed because Genesis names only storyline figures at this point. The passage is silent about her identity, and that silence is a gap in detail, not a contradiction.
