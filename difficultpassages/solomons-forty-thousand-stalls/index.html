Solomon's Forty Thousand Stalls

1 Kings 4:26 credits Solomon with forty thousand stalls of horses for his chariots; 2 Chronicles 9:25 gives four thousand stalls. Both agree on twelve thousand horsemen, so readers ask which stall figure is right.

Explanations

Numerals are the most easily corrupted element in hand-copied Hebrew texts, and this is a textbook case: a small difference turns four thousand into forty thousand. The Chronicles figure is also internally more plausible alongside twelve thousand horsemen. The discrepancy is therefore assigned to copyist transmission, not to two different Solomons or two different counts, and the passage's point — Solomon's vast, horse-rich kingdom — is the same on either figure.

Site Assessment

Site assessment: Four thousand is the more likely original figure, and forty thousand a copyist's inflation. The difference is a numeral variant, not a historical contradiction of substance.
