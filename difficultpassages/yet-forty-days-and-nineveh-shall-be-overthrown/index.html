Yet Forty Days, and Nineveh Shall Be Overthrown

Passage
Yet Forty Days, and Nineveh Shall Be Overthrown
Reference
Jonah 3:4-10
Difficulty
Jonah proclaims without qualification, 'Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown,' but Nineveh repents and God does not overthrow it. The prophecy appears to fail, which by the Deuteronomy test would mark Jonah as a false prophet.
Explanations
Prophetic warnings of judgment carry the implied condition stated in Jeremiah 18: if the threatened people turn, God relents. The warning's purpose is repentance, and Nineveh's repentance is the warning's success, not its failure. Jonah himself knows this: his flight and his later anger both assume God is merciful and will relent if Nineveh repents. The forty days are a deadline for repentance, and the city's fasting, including its king, is the response the warning was designed to produce.
Site Assessment
Site assessment: The prophecy did not fail; it was a conditional warning, and it worked. A threat that produces repentance has achieved its purpose, and God's relenting is mercy, not inconsistency.
