Three Days and Three Nights

Passage
Three Days and Three Nights
Reference
Matthew 12:40
Difficulty
Jesus says, 'As Jonas was three days and three nights in the whale's belly; so shall the Son of man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth.' But the Gospels place the burial on Friday afternoon and the empty tomb on Sunday morning — barely a day and a half by modern counting, and only two nights. Readers ask whether Jesus' own prediction fails, or whether the church's Friday crucifixion is wrong.
Explanations
The widely accepted explanation is inclusive reckoning: in Jewish usage of the time, any part of a day counted as a day and a night, so Friday afternoon, Saturday, and Sunday morning count as three days. The Gospels themselves use 'after three days' and 'on the third day' interchangeably for the same interval, which confirms they are not counting seventy-two hours. The Jonah comparison is also typological rather than stopwatch-precise: as Jonah emerged from the fish, so Jesus emerges from the grave, with 'three days' as the conventional expression for a short, complete period. Alternative proposals — a Wednesday or Thursday crucifixion — exist, but they create more problems with the Passover chronology than they solve, and they are not needed if inclusive counting is allowed.
Site Assessment
Site position — inclusive day-counting resolves the difficulty, and 'on the third day' is how the Gospels themselves count it. The site does not present a Wednesday crucifixion as a live option for its readers; it is noted here only to show it was considered and set aside.
