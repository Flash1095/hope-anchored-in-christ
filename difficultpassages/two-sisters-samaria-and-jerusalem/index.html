Two Sisters: Samaria and Jerusalem

Ezekiel 23 names two sisters, Oholah and Oholibah, and describes their sexual promiscuity in even more explicit terms than chapter 16. The passage is often skipped in public reading, and readers ask why Scripture contains pornographic detail at all.

Explanations

As in chapter 16, the sisters are cities, not women: Oholah is Samaria, capital of the northern kingdom, and Oholibah is Jerusalem. Their 'lovers' are Assyria, Babylon, and Egypt, and the sexual language stands for political and religious alliance with idolatrous empires. The explicitness is prophetic rhetoric aimed at a nation that treated idolatry casually; the chapter's judgment, that both sisters fall to the very empires they courted, is the point. Nothing in the passage commends the behaviour it describes or invites imitation.

Site Assessment

Site assessment: The chapter is a political and religious allegory, not erotic writing and not a description of real women. Its graphic language serves a moral purpose — to expose idolatry as betrayal — and it should be taught with that allegorical key stated plainly, especially with younger readers.
