'Hate Father and Mother'

Jesus says, 'If any man come to me, and hate not his father, and mother, and wife, and children, and brethren, and sisters, yea, and his own life also, he cannot be my disciple.' Taken literally, this seems to command hatred of family and even of oneself, contradicting the command to honour parents and love one's neighbour. Readers wonder whether discipleship really requires hating the people they are also commanded to love.

Explanations

The widely accepted explanation is Semitic idiom: in biblical language 'hate' often means 'love less' or 'put second' by comparison, not emotional hatred. Matthew's parallel makes the meaning explicit: 'He that loveth father or mother more than me is not worthy of me' (Matthew 10:37). The context confirms it: Luke 14 is about counting the cost of following Jesus, and the point is that loyalty to Jesus must outrank even the strongest family loyalty when the two conflict. Jesus elsewhere upholds honouring parents and caring for family, so a literal hatred reading would make him contradict himself within the same teaching ministry.

Site Assessment

Site position — 'hate' here is comparative, not a command to feel hatred. Discipleship may cost family approval, and sometimes family closeness, but it never licenses contempt for parents or neglect of family duty. That is the site's reading of the idiom and the parallel text, not a verdict Scripture pronounces on itself.
