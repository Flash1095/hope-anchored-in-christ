'I Came Not to Send Peace, but a Sword'

Passage
'I Came Not to Send Peace, but a Sword'
Reference
Matthew 10:34-39; Luke 12:51-53
Difficulty
Jesus says, 'Think not that I am come to send peace on earth: I came not to send peace, but a sword.' This sounds like a call to violence from the Prince of Peace, and it sits uneasily beside 'blessed are the peacemakers' and his rebuke of Peter's sword in Gethsemane. Readers ask whether Jesus endorsed armed conflict, or family strife.
Explanations
The context and Luke's parallel settle the image: Luke has 'division' where Matthew has 'sword', and Matthew immediately explains the sword as division within households — father against son, mother against daughter — when family members take different sides over Jesus. The sword is metaphorical, for the cutting effect of allegiance, not a weapon to be taken up: the same Jesus forbids his disciples to fight for him at his arrest and teaches love of enemies. The passage is therefore about cost, not combat: following Jesus can divide even families, and disciples must be prepared for that division without creating it by violence or cruelty.
Site Assessment
Site position — the sword is division over allegiance, not a licence for violence. Any use of this verse to justify coercion or family cruelty misreads it, and the site states that plainly as its own reading.
