The Gibeonites' Trick and Israel's Oath

Passage
The Gibeonites' Trick and Israel's Oath
Reference
Joshua 9:3-27
Difficulty
The Gibeonites deceive Israel by posing as travellers from a far country, and Israel's leaders swear a covenant of peace without asking the LORD first. Readers ask why a treaty obtained by fraud should bind Israel at all, and why God would honour an oath made on false pretences.
Explanations
One explanation stresses covenant ethics: in Israel's world a sworn oath was binding even when costly, and breaking such an oath later (2 Samuel 21) brought guilt on the land. Another stresses where the blame lies: the failure was in not seeking counsel, and the Gibeonites' servitude as woodcutters and water carriers was the imposed consequence. A third notes the Gibeonites had heard of the LORD's deeds and sought mercy, so the outcome is read as grace within judgment rather than approval of their deception.
Site Assessment
Site assessment: The oath binds because Israel swore it, and the passage's blame falls on leaders who did not inquire of the LORD, not on keeping a sworn word. Deception explains how the treaty happened; it does not dissolve it.
