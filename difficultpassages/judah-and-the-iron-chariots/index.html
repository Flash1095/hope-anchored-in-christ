Judah and the Iron Chariots

Passage
Judah and the Iron Chariots
Reference
Judges 1:19
Difficulty
The verse says the LORD was with Judah, yet Judah 'could not drive out the inhabitants of the valley, because they had chariots of iron.' Read carelessly, it sounds as if God Himself were stopped by superior military technology.
Explanations
The standard grammatical reading resolves the tension: the subject who 'could not' is Judah, not the LORD. The LORD's presence is affirmed in the first half of the verse; Judah's failure in the second half is a human failure of nerve, obedience or persistence, of the kind Judges repeatedly attributes to Israel's incomplete conquest. No reading requires an impotent God, and the chapter's pattern is partial obedience, not divine defeat.
Site Assessment
Site assessment: The text does not say God was defeated by chariots. It says Judah, though accompanied by the LORD, did not finish the task — a distinction the grammar and the chapter's theme both support.
