Two Animals at the Triumphal Entry?

Passage
Two Animals at the Triumphal Entry?
Reference
Matthew 21:1-9; Mark 11:1-10; Luke 19:28-40
Difficulty
Matthew says the disciples brought both an ass and a colt for the triumphal entry, and Jesus sat on them; Mark and Luke mention only a colt. Readers picture an absurd scene — Jesus riding two animals at once — and ask whether Matthew has misunderstood his own proof-text, Zechariah 9:9, which speaks of a king 'riding upon an ass, and upon a colt the foal of an ass'.
Explanations
Zechariah's lines are Hebrew poetic parallelism: the second line restates the first, describing one animal in two ways, not two animals. Matthew, writing for readers steeped in Scripture, treats the parallelism literally and brings both animals into the scene, with Jesus sitting on the clothes laid over them. Mark and Luke, writing more simply, report the colt that was actually ridden. The entry itself — a colt, garments spread, crowds crying Hosanna — is common to all three, and the difference is Matthew's scriptural style, not a competing memory of the procession. No Gospel claims Jesus straddled two animals simultaneously; the clothes, not a double saddle, are what he sat upon.
Site Assessment
Site position — Matthew's two animals are a literalistic reading of Zechariah's parallelism, and the colt is the animal ridden. The site does not hide that Matthew's handling of the poetry looks odd to modern readers; it explains it as first-century scriptural method, not as evidence that the entry did not happen.
