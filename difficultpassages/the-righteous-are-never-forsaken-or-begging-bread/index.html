The Righteous Are Never Forsaken — or Begging Bread

Passage
The Righteous Are Never Forsaken — or Begging Bread
Reference
Psalm 37:25
Difficulty
'I have been young, and now am old; yet have I not seen the righteous forsaken, nor his seed begging bread.' Honest readers can point to righteous people who have gone hungry, lost everything, or depended on charity — including in Scripture itself. If this is a universal factual claim, it appears false; if it is a promise, it appears broken.
Explanations
The verse is the testimony of an aged observer within a wisdom psalm, not a doctrinal definition of how God treats every righteous person at every moment. Psalm 37 as a whole contrasts the temporary flourishing of the wicked with the lasting security of the righteous, and verse 25 states the psalmist's lifetime observation: as a pattern, God does not abandon his people to destitution, and his people's households are not left to beg as their settled condition. Temporary need, dependence on others' generosity, and seasons of real want are not what the verse denies; a final, forsaken end is. The verse is also framed as personal witness ('I have not seen'), which is weaker in form — and more honest — than a universal law.
Site Assessment
Site position: Psalm 37:25 is observed wisdom about God's ordinary faithfulness to his people over a lifetime, not a promise that no righteous person will ever know want. It should comfort, not be used to shame the poor or to measure anyone's righteousness by their bank balance.
