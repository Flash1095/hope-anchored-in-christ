Wives Seized for Benjamin

Passage
Wives Seized for Benjamin
Reference
Judges 21:1-25
Difficulty
After the civil war against Benjamin, Israel first swears not to give its daughters to Benjamin, then sanctions the killing of Jabesh-gilead's men so their virgins can be given as wives, and finally tells the surviving Benjaminites to seize dancing girls at Shiloh. Readers ask how a book of Scripture can report kidnapping as a solution.
Explanations
As with Judges 19, the agreed reading is descriptive indictment. The chapter shows leaders solving one oath-bound disaster with two further wrongs, each justified by expediency rather than by God's law. The book closes on the same refrain — no king, every man doing what is right in his own eyes — which signals that the narrator regards the whole sequence as moral collapse, not as a divinely approved precedent.
Site Assessment
Site assessment: The seizure of wives is presented as the last failure in a chain of failures, not as God's will. The passage's darkness is its argument: Israel's anarchy is intolerable and needs a king after God's heart.
