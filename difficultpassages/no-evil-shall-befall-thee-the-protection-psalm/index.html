No Evil Shall Befall Thee — the Protection Psalm

Passage
No Evil Shall Befall Thee — the Protection Psalm
Reference
Psalm 91:10-12
Difficulty
'There shall no evil befall thee, neither shall any plague come nigh thy dwelling. For he shall give his angels charge over thee, to keep thee in all thy ways.' Readers who trust God and still suffer illness, bereavement, or violence ask the obvious question: was the promise false, or was their trust defective? The difficulty sharpens because the devil himself quotes verses 11-12 to Jesus in the wilderness temptation (Matthew 4:6), pressing the psalm as a guarantee that God must prevent even a stubbed foot.
Explanations
The psalm is poetic assurance, not a talisman or a contract. Its language is that of refuge and trust (verses 1-2, 9, 14): those who dwell in God's shelter are held in God's care, and the psalm's sweeping 'no evil' language expresses the security of that relationship in the absolute terms poetry uses, not a statistical claim that the faithful never suffer. Jesus' answer to the quotation is itself part of the resolution: to demand spectacular protection on cue is to test God rather than trust him (Matthew 4:7). The psalm's own ending promises deliverance and presence 'in trouble' (verse 15) — help within trouble, not exemption from it — which is why the psalm can be prayed honestly by sufferers rather than only by the untouched.
Site Assessment
Site position: Psalm 91 promises God's keeping presence and ultimate deliverance, not immunity from harm in this life. Reading it as a guarantee that nothing bad will happen misuses the psalm in exactly the way the wilderness temptation models.
