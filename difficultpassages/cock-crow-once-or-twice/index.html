The Cock Crow Once or Twice?

Jesus predicts Peter's denial with a detail that differs by Gospel: Matthew, Luke, and John say Peter will deny him before the cock crows, while Mark says before the cock crows twice. If Mark is right, the other three have the prediction wrong; if they are right, Mark has added a crow. Small as it seems, readers cite it as a clean, checkable contradiction.

Explanations

The explanation is counting, not error. Mark, the fullest account, counts both cock-crows of the night — the first, around midnight, and the second, toward dawn — and says Peter will deny Jesus three times before the second. The other writers mention only the final, decisive crow, the one that triggers Peter's remembering. Mark's own narrative fits this: Peter denies, a first crow sounds, and the denials continue until the second crow, when Peter weeps. No Gospel claims there was only one crow, and Mark does not claim the denials all happened before the first.

Site Assessment

Site position — this is a difference of counting, resolved by Mark's fuller report. It is a useful test case: a seeming contradiction that dissolves once the accounts are read together rather than as competing transcripts.
