Clean and Unclean Laws

Passage
Clean and Unclean Laws
Reference
Leviticus 11:1-47; 19:19
Difficulty
Leviticus forbids eating pork, shellfish, and other creatures, bans mixing kinds of seed and cloth, and calls all of it part of God's law. Yet Christians eat pork and wear mixed fabrics without a second thought. If God's law is God's law, why do Christians ignore whole chapters of it? And if these rules were never about morality at all, why did God give them in such detail, and why do some Christians still appeal to Leviticus on other questions, such as sexual ethics, while setting the food laws aside?
Explanations
The widely shared explanation is that these laws marked Israel out as a distinct, holy people under the old covenant, not that they stated timeless moral rules for all people. Food, farming, and clothing rules functioned as daily boundary markers: every meal and every garment reminded Israel it belonged to God and was set apart from surrounding nations. That is why the New Testament can describe the food laws as fulfilled and set aside for Gentile believers while still treating the moral commands of the law, such as love of neighbour and prohibitions on murder and theft, as enduring. The distinction is sometimes labelled ceremonial versus moral law; scholars debate how tidy that labelling is, and where exactly each command falls, but there is broad agreement on the core point, that the identity-marker function of the clean and unclean code belonged to Israel's covenant situation. Appeals to Leviticus on other questions therefore have to argue, case by case, whether a given command is an identity marker or a moral rule, rather than assuming all of Leviticus stands or falls together.
Site Assessment
The site's position is that the clean and unclean laws were covenant identity markers for Israel, not universal moral law, and that Christians are not bound by them as food or clothing rules. The harder question, which command in Leviticus is moral and which is a marker, has to be argued passage by passage, and this row does not settle every such case by itself.
