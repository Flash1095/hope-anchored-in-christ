The Woman Caught in Adultery

The story of the woman taken in adultery — 'he that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her' and 'neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more' — is one of the best-loved passages in the Gospels. But it is absent from the earliest manuscripts of John, it floats in different places in later copies, and some manuscripts omit it entirely. Readers ask whether a beloved story can be genuine if it was not in John's original Gospel, and whether 'neither do I condemn thee' softens Jesus' teaching on sin.

Explanations

Textual scholars widely agree the passage was not part of John's original text: it is missing from the earliest Greek manuscripts and early writers do not cite it as part of John, and its style fits Luke better than John, which is why some later copies place it after Luke 21. Many scholars still regard it as an ancient, possibly authentic tradition about Jesus that circulated independently before being inserted into John, because it coheres with his mercy toward sinners and his refusal to be used as a trap. The passage itself does not excuse the sin: Jesus both refuses condemnation by the crowd and commands 'sin no more'.

Site Assessment

Site position — the passage should be read with its textual history stated openly: almost certainly not John's own composition, but an early tradition consistent with Jesus as the Gospels portray him. It should not be used to argue that Jesus was indifferent to sexual sin, nor removed from devotional reading; honesty about its status and about its final command belong together.
