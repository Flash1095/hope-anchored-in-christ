The Two Thieves on the Cross

Passage
The Two Thieves on the Cross
Reference
Matthew 27:44; Luke 23:39-43
Difficulty
Matthew says both criminals crucified with Jesus 'cast the same in his teeth' — both mocked him. Luke says one mocked him while the other rebuked the mocker, confessed his own guilt, asked Jesus to remember him, and heard, 'To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.' Readers ask whether the second criminal changed sides, or whether one Gospel has rewritten the scene.
Explanations
The standard explanation is sequence: both men mocked Jesus at first, as Matthew summarises, and one of them then changed — convicted by Jesus' bearing, by his prayer for his executioners, or by the darkness and the conduct of the scene — and turned to him in repentance. Luke, who gives the fuller scene, reports the end of that arc rather than its beginning. Nothing in Luke denies an earlier mockery, and nothing in Matthew denies a later change of heart; each writer selects the phase that serves his account, Matthew the shared shame of the crucifixion, Luke the mercy extended even there.
Site Assessment
Site position — a change of heart during the crucifixion best fits both texts, and the passage should be read as a story of last-minute repentance, not as a contradiction to be resolved by deleting either detail. The site does not claim to know what changed the man's mind; that the texts show a changed man is enough.
