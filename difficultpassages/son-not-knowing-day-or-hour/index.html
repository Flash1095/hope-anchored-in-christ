The Son Not Knowing the Day or Hour

Jesus says of the day and hour of his return, 'knoweth no man, no, not the angels which are in heaven, neither the Son, but the Father.' If Jesus is omniscient God, how can he not know something? If he genuinely did not know, how can he be God? Readers also note that some manuscripts omit 'neither the Son' from Matthew's version, and ask whether the harder reading was softened by copyists.

Explanations

The mainstream orthodox explanation distinguishes the Son's two natures: as God, the Son shares the Father's knowledge; as man, in the state of humiliation, he genuinely lived within human limitations, including not knowing — or at least not revealing — the day and hour. The point of the saying in context is pastoral, not speculative: no one can calculate the end, so watchfulness, not date-setting, is required. Textually, Mark's version retains 'neither the Son' in the earliest witnesses, and Matthew's omission in some copies looks like scribal softening of a hard saying, which if anything confirms the saying's authenticity — copyists tend to remove difficulties, not invent them.

Site Assessment

Site position — the saying is best understood as the incarnate Son speaking within his genuine human limitation, without surrendering his deity. The site does not pretend the mechanics of two-natures knowledge are fully explicable; it holds that the passage teaches human limitation in the incarnation and forbids date-setting, and that is enough to remove the supposed contradiction with Christ's divinity.
