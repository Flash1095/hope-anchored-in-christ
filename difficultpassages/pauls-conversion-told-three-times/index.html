Paul's Conversion Told Three Times

Paul's conversion is told three times in Acts, and the details do not line up at first glance. In chapter 9 the men with Paul 'stood speechless, hearing a voice, but seeing no man'; in chapter 22 they 'saw indeed the light' but 'heard not the voice of him that spake to me'; in chapter 26 Paul says 'we were all fallen to the earth'. Did they stand or fall, hear or not hear, see or not see? Readers ask whether Luke contradicts himself within one book.

Explanations

The differences are largely ones of perspective and sequence, and the Greek verb for hearing helps with the main one: it can mean hearing a sound and it can mean understanding words, so the companions can have heard a sound without hearing — that is, understanding — the voice that spoke to Paul. Similarly, they can have fallen to the ground at the light and then stood speechless afterward; chapter 9 describes them standing in bewilderment, chapter 26 describes the initial fall. Each retelling is also shaped for its audience — a plain narrative in chapter 9, a defence before a Jerusalem crowd in chapter 22, a defence before Agrippa in chapter 26 — so Paul emphasises different details each time. The core is identical in all three: a blinding light, the risen Jesus speaking, Paul's commission to the Gentiles.

Site Assessment

Site position — the three accounts are complementary retellings, not competing versions, and the hearing distinction and the fall-then-stand sequence resolve the apparent contradictions. The site notes the hearing explanation depends on the range of the Greek verb, which is real but should not be overstated as proof; the agreement on the core event is the stronger point.
