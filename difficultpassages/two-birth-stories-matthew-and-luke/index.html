Two Birth Stories: Matthew and Luke

Matthew and Luke tell the birth of Jesus very differently. Matthew has wise men, a star, Herod's plot, and a flight into Egypt; Luke has a census, shepherds, a manger, and a presentation in the temple. Matthew's family seems to live in Bethlehem and then settle in Nazareth after Egypt; Luke's family travels from Nazareth to Bethlehem and returns to Nazareth. Readers ask whether these are two incompatible birth stories stitched together, or one story told from two sides.

Explanations

The accounts are complementary selections, not competing timelines. Matthew follows Joseph's perspective — his dream, his dilemma, his flight — and selects episodes that show Jesus as the threatened king and the new Moses who, like Israel, comes out of Egypt. Luke follows Mary's perspective, with the annunciation, the songs, and the shepherds, and selects episodes that show Jesus as Saviour for the lowly and the nations. Neither writer claims to give every episode: Matthew omits the census and shepherds, Luke omits the wise men and Egypt, and the two sets of episodes can be arranged in sequence (birth, shepherds, presentation, wise men, flight) without forcing either text. The shared core — virgin conception, birth in Bethlehem in David's line, naming by divine direction — is identical.

Site Assessment

Site position — the birth narratives are complementary, not contradictory, and the differences of selection reflect the writers' different emphases, Joseph and kingship in Matthew, Mary and lowliness in Luke. The site does not claim every chronological join between the two is provable; it claims the accounts do not contradict each other on any point either writer actually asserts.
