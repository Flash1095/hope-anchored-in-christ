Answer a Fool — and Do Not Answer a Fool

Verses 4 and 5 sit side by side and appear to give opposite commands. Verse 4 says, 'Answer not a fool according to his folly, lest thou also be like unto him.' Verse 5 says, 'Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he be wise in his own conceit.' A reader who treats each proverb as a universal rule is left with a flat contradiction: whichever verse is obeyed, the other is broken.

Explanations

The pair is read as a matched set, not as two rival rules. Proverbs are situational wisdom, not unconditional promises or laws: each verse names a different danger and a different occasion. Verse 4 warns against descending to a fool's terms — arguing on his ground, with his methods — so that the answerer becomes like him. Verse 5 warns against silence that lets a fool's conceit stand unchallenged. The skill is in judging which danger the moment presents: sometimes engagement only feeds the folly, and silence is wiser; sometimes a brief, truthful answer is needed so folly is not mistaken for wisdom. The two verses together train discernment rather than prescribing one move for every encounter.

Site Assessment

Site position: this is a genre resolution, not a contradiction. Proverbs 26:4-5 is deliberately paired teaching about when to speak and when to stay silent, and neither verse should be quoted alone as a blanket rule for every argument.
