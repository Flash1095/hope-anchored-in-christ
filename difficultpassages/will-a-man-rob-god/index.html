Will a Man Rob God?

Passage
Will a Man Rob God?
Reference
Malachi 3:8-10
Difficulty
Malachi says Israel has robbed God in tithes and offerings and invites them, 'prove me now herewith,' promising blessing until there is not room enough to receive it. Readers ask whether this is a general promise that generous giving guarantees material prosperity, and whether testing God is even allowed.
Explanations
The passage is addressed to covenant Israel under the Mosaic system, where tithes supported the Levites and the temple storehouse, and withholding them was covenant theft, not merely stinginess. The invitation to 'prove' God is a specific covenant challenge in that setting, tied to the land's agricultural blessing, not a universal formula that any gift purchases wealth. The New Testament's giving pattern is cheerful, generous, and sacrificial, but it never repeats Malachi's storehouse promise as a prosperity guarantee.
Site Assessment
Site assessment: The passage concerns Israel's covenant tithes and temple support, not a prosperity formula for all believers. Generosity is commanded, but Malachi 3 does not promise that giving buys material wealth.
