The Adulterous Wife: Jerusalem

Passage
The Adulterous Wife: Jerusalem
Reference
Ezekiel 16:1-63
Difficulty
Ezekiel 16 describes Jerusalem as an abandoned infant raised by God, then as an adulterous wife who pays her lovers, in graphic sexual detail. Readers ask whether such language is appropriate in Scripture, whether it demeans women, and whether God is pictured as an abusive husband.
Explanations
The chapter is an extended allegory of covenant unfaithfulness, not a literal account of a marriage and not an endorsement of its imagery as a model for human relationships. Jerusalem's 'lovers' are foreign gods and alliances; her 'adultery' is idolatry and political apostasy after extraordinary grace. Prophetic shock language is deliberate: it is meant to make comfortable idolatry feel as shocking as it is. The chapter ends not with rejection but with an everlasting covenant and atonement, which shows the allegory's point is restoration after judgment, not humiliation for its own sake.
Site Assessment
Site assessment: The imagery is allegorical covenant language, shocking by design, and it must not be read literally or used to justify any human treatment of a spouse. Its endpoint is grace: God remembers his covenant and restores.
