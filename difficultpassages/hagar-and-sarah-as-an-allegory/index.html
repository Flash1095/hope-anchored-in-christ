Hagar and Sarah as an Allegory

Passage
Hagar and Sarah as an Allegory
Reference
Galatians 4:21-31
Difficulty
Paul calls the story of Hagar and Sarah 'an allegory' and maps the two women onto two covenants. Readers worry this means Paul denied that Hagar and Sarah were real people, or that any Old Testament story may be turned into whatever meaning a reader prefers.
Explanations
The agreed reading is that Paul uses the historical story illustratively: he does not deny the events, he draws a comparison from them, as he says himself by naming Sinai and the Jerusalem above. Allegory here is Paul's own labelled illustration, not a licence for readers to allegorise every narrative at will. The historical account in Genesis and the covenant comparison in Galatians stand together.
Site Assessment
The site's position is that Paul is illustrating from real history, not replacing it. Readers should not take this passage as permission to treat historical narratives as mere symbols.
