The Wording of the Sign over the Cross

The four Gospels do not give the sign over Jesus' head in identical words. Matthew has 'THIS IS JESUS THE KING OF THE JEWS', Mark has only 'THE KING OF THE JEWS', Luke has 'THIS IS THE KING OF THE JEWS', and John has 'JESUS OF NAZARETH THE KING OF THE JEWS'. If the sign said one thing, why do the accounts disagree? A reader who expects a verbatim transcript sees four different inscriptions and wonders whether any of them can be trusted.

Explanations

The main explanation is that no Gospel claims to reproduce the sign word for word. John notes the sign was written in Hebrew, Greek, and Latin, so any single wording in Greek already involves selection and translation from a three-language notice, and each writer quotes the part that serves his account: Matthew keeps the name and the charge, Mark shortens to the charge alone, Luke and John keep fuller forms. A second, compatible point is abbreviation: ancient writers regularly shortened inscriptions and speeches rather than inventing them, and the core charge — that Jesus was executed as 'King of the Jews' — is identical in all four. A minority worry remains only if verbatim reporting is demanded of texts that never promise it.

Site Assessment

Site position — the differences are differences of selection and abbreviation, not competing facts about what the charge was. All four agree on the substance, and the variation is exactly what independent reports of a three-language public notice would produce. This entry does not claim Scripture itself settles the point in these words; that is the site's reading of the evidence.
