Ruth at the Threshing Floor

Passage
Ruth at the Threshing Floor
Reference
Ruth 3:1-18
Difficulty
Naomi sends Ruth to the threshing floor at night, where Ruth lies at Boaz's feet and asks him to spread his skirt over her. Modern readers easily hear sexual impropriety in the scene, and wonder whether Ruth was compromising herself or Boaz was taking advantage.
Explanations
The cultural reading, widely agreed, is that Ruth's request uses the language of protection and marriage (the 'skirt' or cloak as a symbol of a kinsman-redeemer's covering), not seduction. Boaz's response — blessing her, refusing to act until the nearer kinsman has his right, sending her home before dawn so no scandal attaches, and settling the matter publicly at the gate the next day — is presented as honourable restraint. Nothing in the text implies a sexual encounter; the scene's tension is legal and social, resolved in daylight and in public.
Site Assessment
Site assessment: Read in its own culture, the threshing-floor scene is a chaste, lawful appeal for redemption, and Boaz's conduct is the passage's model of restraint, not its violation.
