Clapping Over Nineveh's Fall

Passage
Clapping Over Nineveh's Fall
Reference
Nahum 3:19
Difficulty
Nahum ends with all who hear of Nineveh's fall clapping their hands over her, because her wickedness passed continually over everyone. After Jonah, where God spares repentant Nineveh, Nahum seems to celebrate its destruction, and readers ask whether rejoicing over an enemy's ruin is compatible with mercy.
Explanations
The two books concern different generations, about a century and a half apart. Jonah's Nineveh repented and was spared; Nahum's Nineveh returned to the cruelty, plunder, and treachery the book documents, and after further warning it fell to Babylon and Media in 612 BC. Nahum's 'clapping' is the relief of oppressed peoples at the end of an empire that had terrorised them continually, not a command to gloat over personal enemies. The book's opening also insists God is slow to anger and a stronghold to those who trust him.
Site Assessment
Site assessment: The celebration is relief at justice after prolonged cruelty, in a later generation than Jonah's repentant city. Mercy was offered and refused over time; judgment, when it came, ended an oppressor, and the victims' relief is understandable without making cruelty a virtue.
Related Books
Nahum; Jonah
Related Topics
Judgment; Compassion for nations; Justice; God's wrath and refuge
