The Gerasene Demoniac and the Drowning of the Pigs

Passage
The Gerasene Demoniac and the Drowning of the Pigs
Reference
Matthew 8:28-34; Mark 5:1-20; Luke 8:26-39
Difficulty
Jesus casts demons out of a man (or two men) into a herd of about two thousand pigs, which rush into the sea and drown. Readers stumble on several fronts at once: the number of men differs between Matthew and Mark/Luke, the place-name differs (Gergesenes in Matthew's KJV, Gadarenes in Mark's), and the destruction of a large herd — someone's livelihood — seems a disproportionate price for one healing, and cruel to animals.
Explanations
On the number and place, the same partial-reporting and regional explanations apply as at Jericho: Matthew mentions two demoniacs, Mark and Luke focus on the more prominent one, and Gadara, Gerasa, and Gergesa were towns of the same lakeside region, so writers could name the district differently without disagreeing about the shore. On the pigs, the context matters: this is Gentile territory, the pigs are unclean animals, and the man's deliverance — a human being restored to his right mind, clothed, and sent home to tell his story — is presented as worth more than the herd. The loss also exposes the townspeople's values: they beg Jesus to leave rather than welcome the healed man. The passage does not present animal suffering as unimportant, but it ranks a person's liberation above property, and it does not make pig-drowning a general model for dealing with animals or livelihoods.
Site Assessment
Site position — the differing numbers and place-names are regional and selective reporting, not contradictions, and the moral weight of the passage falls on the delivered man, not on the herd as a price Jesus was careless about. The site acknowledges the economic loss was real and the townspeople's dismay understandable; the passage's point is that they valued the herd over the man, and invites readers to notice which they value.
