God Does Not Afflict Willingly

Lamentations says God 'doth not afflict willingly nor grieve the children of men,' yet asks, 'Out of the mouth of the most High proceedeth not evil and good?' If God afflicts Jerusalem, how can he not afflict willingly, and does evil proceed from God?

Explanations

The two verses describe two different things. Verse 33 says affliction is not God's delight or first desire: he does not enjoy grief for its own sake. Verse 38, in the form of a rhetorical question, acknowledges that both good and calamity come under God's sovereign ordering — again, ra' as calamity, not moral evil. The chapter holds them together: Jerusalem's suffering is real, God is sovereign over it, and it is not what God delights in. The sufferer's response in the chapter is to hope in God's mercies, which are new every morning.

Site Assessment

Site assessment: God can sovereignly permit and ordain calamity in judgment without delighting in affliction. The passage distinguishes God's delight from God's sovereignty, and that distinction resolves the apparent contradiction.
