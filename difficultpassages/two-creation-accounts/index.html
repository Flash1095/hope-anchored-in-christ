Two Creation Accounts

Passage
Two Creation Accounts
Reference
Genesis 1:1-2:25
Difficulty
Genesis 1 and Genesis 2 appear to tell the creation story twice, and in a different order. In Genesis 1, plants appear on day three, animals on days five and six, and man and woman together at the end, after the animals. In Genesis 2, man is formed first, then the garden is planted, then the animals are formed and brought to the man, and only then is woman made from the man's side. If both chapters are straightforward chronological reports, they seem to contradict each other about what came first: plants or man, animals or man, man or woman.
Explanations
The widely shared reading is that the two chapters do different jobs and are not both running on the same timeline. Genesis 1 is a broad, ordered panorama of the whole creation week, ending with humanity as its crown. Genesis 2 zooms in on the sixth day and retells it topically, filling in the garden, the man's work, the naming of the animals, and the making of the woman, without claiming that each event in chapter 2 happened after the previous one in strict sequence. On this reading, the Hebrew narrative style allows a topical retelling to sit beside an ordered overview, and the 'forming' of animals in Genesis 2:19 can be read as a flashback to their creation rather than a second creation after the man. A minority literal-chronological reading tries to harmonise every verb in sequence, but the topical reading is the one most commentators adopt, because Genesis 2 itself signals its narrower focus by opening with 'these are the generations of the heavens and of the earth' and then concentrating on the garden.
Site Assessment
The site's position is that the two accounts are complementary, not contradictory: Genesis 1 gives the wide ordered account and Genesis 2 gives the close-up, human-centred account of the same creation. This row settles only the order question; it does not settle how long the 'days' of Genesis 1 were, which remains a separate debate covered by the existing question 'Did God create the world in six literal days?'
