Why Do the Wicked Prosper? The Psalm That Answers Itself

Passage
Why Do the Wicked Prosper? The Psalm That Answers Itself
Reference
Psalm 73:3, 12-13
Difficulty
The psalmist says plainly, 'I was envious at the foolish, when I saw the prosperity of the wicked,' and 'these are the ungodly, who prosper in the world; they increase in riches' — then, 'Verily I have cleansed my heart in vain, and washed my hands in innocency.' The difficulty is the experience every reader recognises: wrongdoing seems rewarded, faithfulness seems pointless, and the psalm voices that complaint without softening it. The question is whether the psalm endorses the complaint or resolves it.
Explanations
The psalm resolves its own tension, and that arc is the point. The complaint occupies the first half; the turn comes in verse 17, 'until I went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I their end.' Seen from the end rather than the middle, the wicked's prosperity is slippery ground (verse 18) and the psalmist's nearness to God is itself the good (verse 28). The psalm does not deny the observed facts — the wicked do prosper for a time — nor does it claim the righteous never doubt. It relocates the question from present prosperity to final outcome and present communion: what matters is not who is richer now, but where each path ends and who walks with God meanwhile.
Site Assessment
Site position: Psalm 73 is a model for bringing envy and doubt to God honestly rather than suppressing them. Its answer is not that the wicked do not prosper, but that prosperity is not the measure of a life, and nearness to God is.
