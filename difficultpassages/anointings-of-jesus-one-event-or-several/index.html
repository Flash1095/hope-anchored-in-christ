The Anointings of Jesus: One Event or Several?

Four anointing scenes look similar and different at once: a woman anoints Jesus with costly ointment, someone objects to the waste, Jesus defends her. But the settings differ — Simon the leper's house in Bethany near the Passion, a Pharisee's house early in the ministry, Lazarus's household six days before Passover — and the details differ too: head or feet, hair or not, a named Mary or an unnamed 'sinner'. Readers ask whether the writers have confused one event, or whether Jesus was anointed three times.

Explanations

The consensus reading is two events, not one and not three. Matthew, Mark, and John describe the same Bethany anointing near the end: same town, same objection about selling the ointment for the poor, same defence pointing to burial. Their differences are complementary — Matthew and Mark mention the head, John mentions the feet and names Mary, and a single anointing can involve both. Luke's scene is the distinct one: it happens early, in a Pharisee's house, with an unnamed woman known as a sinner, and its point is forgiveness, not burial. Treating Luke's scene as the same event would require moving it years and changing its host, its woman, and its meaning.

Site Assessment

Site position — two anointings, one in Luke's early ministry and one at Bethany in Passion week, best fits all four texts. The site does not claim certainty about every detail of the Bethany scene, but the case for separating Luke's account is strong, and conflating all four into one event does more violence to the texts than keeping two.
