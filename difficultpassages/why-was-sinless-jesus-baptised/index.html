Why Was the Sinless Jesus Baptised?

John's baptism was a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins. Jesus, whom the Gospels present as sinless, comes to be baptised by John, and John himself objects: 'I have need to be baptized of thee, and comest thou to me?' Readers ask why a sinless man would submit to a rite for sinners, and whether this implies Jesus had sins to confess.

Explanations

Jesus' own answer is the key: 'Suffer it to be so now: for thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness.' The baptism is not a confession of personal sin but an identification — Jesus stands with the people he came to save, submits to God's appointed way, and is publicly marked out at the start of his ministry, with the Spirit descending and the Father's voice affirming him. John consents on that basis. The Gospels never suggest Jesus confessed sins at the Jordan; the rite's meaning changes because the person undergoing it changes it, from repentance for one's own sins to solidarity with sinners and obedience to the Father.

Site Assessment

Site position — the baptism is identification and obedience, not repentance for personal sin. The site states this as its reading of Jesus' own explanation, 'to fulfil all righteousness', read with the voice from heaven that follows it.
