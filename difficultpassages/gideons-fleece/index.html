Gideon's Fleece

Passage
Gideon's Fleece
Reference
Judges 6:36-40
Difficulty
Gideon twice asks God for a sign with a wool fleece — dry fleece on wet ground, then wet fleece on dry ground. Readers wonder whether this licenses 'putting out a fleece' as a normal way of making decisions, and whether Gideon was right to test God after already receiving a sign.
Explanations
Most readings agree Gideon is not presented as a model here. He has already been called, given a sign (Judges 6:17-24) and promised the LORD's presence; the fleece is a further plea from fear, which God graciously answers rather than rebukes at this point. The narrative function is to strengthen a fearful deliverer before battle, not to teach a decision-making technique — and later Scripture warns against testing God as a habit, while commending His already-given word and wisdom.
Site Assessment
Site assessment: Gideon's fleece is best read as God's patience with a fearful man, not as a method to copy. A Christian with a clear command, wise counsel and an open Bible does not need a fleece, and should not demand one.
