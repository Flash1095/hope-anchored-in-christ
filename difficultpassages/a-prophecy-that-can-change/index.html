A Prophecy That Can Change

Passage
A Prophecy That Can Change
Reference
Jeremiah 18:7-10
Difficulty
God says that if he threatens a nation with destruction and it repents, he will relent, and if he promises blessing and it does evil, he will change his purpose. If prophecy can be reversed, how can any prophecy be trusted, and does God change his mind?
Explanations
Jeremiah 18 states the principle itself: most prophetic announcements of judgment or blessing carry an implied condition, because their purpose is to move people to repentance or faithfulness. A threat is a warning, not a bare prediction, and a promise of blessing assumes continued obedience. This does not make every prophecy conditional: unconditional promises, such as the promise of a new covenant and of a Davidic king, are stated without that condition and are not revoked by this principle. The test is in the text of each prophecy, not in a rule applied to all of them.
Site Assessment
Site assessment: Conditional warnings and blessings are genuinely conditional, and unconditional promises are genuinely unconditional. Jeremiah 18 explains the first kind; it does not license treating every promise as revocable.
