Be Not Righteous Overmuch

'Be not righteous over much; neither make thyself over wise: why shouldest thou destroy thyself?' Taken alone, the verse sounds like advice to be only moderately good — as if excessive righteousness were dangerous and a little wickedness prudent. That reading collides with the rest of Scripture's call to wholehearted obedience, and with the book's own conclusion to fear God and keep his commandments (Ecclesiastes 12:13).

Explanations

The verse is read in its immediate context (Ecclesiastes 7:15-18), where the Preacher has just observed that the righteous sometimes perish in their righteousness and the wicked prolong life in wickedness. 'Righteous overmuch' is therefore widely understood as over-scrupulous, self-righteous, or showy righteousness — a rigid moral performance that claims more wisdom and virtue than a human possesses and destroys the person practising it — paired with the matching warning in verse 17 not to be overmuch wicked either. The counsel is for balance against two kinds of extremism, not for a middle dose of sin: fear God (verse 18) rather than trusting one's own moral system to control outcomes. The verse warns against weaponised virtue, not against virtue itself.

Site Assessment

Site position: Ecclesiastes 7:16 warns against self-righteous excess and the illusion that perfect performance controls life's outcomes. It is not permission to be half-hearted about obedience, and it must be read with verse 18's call to fear God, not with verse 16 alone.
