The Return Lists: Ezra 2 and Nehemiah 7

The two lists of returned exiles agree in their grand total — 42,360 — but differ in many individual family numbers, for example the children of Arah: 775 in Ezra 2:5 against 652 in Nehemiah 7:10. Readers ask why inspired lists cannot agree on their own arithmetic.

Explanations

The lists are not two independent censuses taken on the same day. Nehemiah 7 says explicitly that he found 'a register of the genealogy of them which came up at the first' — that is, he is copying an older document, and the two copies have travelled through different transmission histories, where numerals corrupt most easily. Some differences may also reflect counting at different stages (those who set out, those who arrived, those registered later). The agreement on the total, alongside divergence in details, is exactly what copied registers look like.

Site Assessment

Site assessment: The lists are two transmissions of one register, not two contradictory censuses. The shared total is the anchor; the differing details are copyist variants in numbers.
