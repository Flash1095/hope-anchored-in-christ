The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah

Passage
The Levite's Concubine at Gibeah
Reference
Judges 19:1-30
Difficulty
A Levite hands his concubine over to a mob, she is abused through the night and dies, and he then cuts her body into twelve pieces and sends them through Israel. Readers ask, with full force, how such horror can be in Scripture, and whether the text in any way excuses the men involved.
Explanations
The widely agreed reading is that the passage is descriptive, not prescriptive: it is told as an indictment, not an endorsement. Its refrain — 'in those days there was no king in Israel: every man did that which was right in his own eyes' (Judges 21:25) — frames the whole closing section as a portrait of a society collapsing into depravity, and the Levite himself is shown as callous and self-protecting, not heroic. The shock is the point: Israel needs righteous rule, and the reader is meant to recoil, not imitate.
Site Assessment
Site assessment: This passage condemns by exposure. Nothing in it praises the Levite, the mob or the tribe that sheltered them, and reading it as approval would invert the narrator's own verdict.
