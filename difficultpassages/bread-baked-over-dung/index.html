Bread Baked Over Dung

Passage
Bread Baked Over Dung
Reference
Ezekiel 4:12-15
Difficulty
God commands Ezekiel to bake his bread over human dung as a sign, then relents and allows cow dung instead. Readers ask why God would command something defiling, and whether the concession means God changed his mind about his own command.
Explanations
The act is a sign, not a diet: during a siege, food would be scarce and defiled, and Ezekiel's daily ration and defiled cooking dramatise that reality to an audience that would not listen to words. The concession matters: Ezekiel protests on grounds of ritual purity, and God grants cow dung, which was ordinary fuel, while keeping the sign's meaning. The passage shows a prophet's objection being heard within obedience, not a God who casually orders defilement and then reverses himself.
Site Assessment
Site assessment: The command is a prophetic sign-act about siege defilement, and the concession preserves both the sign and Ezekiel's conscience. It is not a general permission or prohibition about fuel, and it does not show God contradicting himself.
