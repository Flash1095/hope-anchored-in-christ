One Blind Man or Two at Jericho?

Matthew says two blind men were healed as Jesus left Jericho; Mark names one, blind Bartimaeus, as Jesus left Jericho; Luke says one blind man was healed as Jesus approached Jericho. The number differs, and so does the direction: approaching versus leaving. A reader wonders whether these are two different healings, or whether the writers disagree about a single one.

Explanations

On the number, the standard explanation is partial reporting: Matthew names both men, while Mark and Luke concentrate on one — Mark even names him, Bartimaeus, suggesting he was the better-known or more vocal of the two, the one who did the shouting. Mentioning one man does not deny a second. On the timing, two explanations are offered and are compatible: Jericho had an old site and a newer Herodian town, so a journey could leave one and approach the other; or Luke groups the healing with the approach while Matthew and Mark place it at the departure, the healing belonging to the same pass through the town. The core — blind men calling on Jesus as Son of David, healed at Jericho — is common to all three.

Site Assessment

Site position — the one-versus-two difference is settled by partial reporting and is not a contradiction. The approaching-versus-leaving detail is less certain, and the site does not force it: the two-Jerichos explanation is plausible but not provable from the texts alone. That uncertainty is stated, not hidden.
