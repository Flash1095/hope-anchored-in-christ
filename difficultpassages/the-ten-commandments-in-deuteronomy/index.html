The Ten Commandments in Deuteronomy

Passage
The Ten Commandments in Deuteronomy
Reference
Deuteronomy 5:6-21
Difficulty
Deuteronomy 5 repeats the Ten Commandments, but not word for word. The wording differs in places from Exodus 20, and the reason given for the Sabbath changes: Exodus grounds it in God's rest after creation, while Deuteronomy grounds it in Israel's deliverance from Egypt ('remember that thou wast a servant in the land of Egypt'). If both are God's own words, delivered on Sinai, why do they differ? Either one version is inaccurate, or the claim that God spoke these words needs qualifying.
Explanations
The standard explanation is that Deuteronomy is a covenant renewal sermon, not a second transcript. Moses is addressing a new generation on the edge of the promised land, restating the law for its situation, and restatement in preaching naturally uses fresh wording and draws out a fresh motive. The two Sabbath reasons are complementary, not competing: creation gives the pattern of rest, and the exodus gives Israel's own experience of why rest matters, since slaves in Egypt had no rest. Ancient legal restatement worked the same way, applying and expounding a law for a new setting rather than photocopying it. The differences are therefore exactly what one would expect from a preached republication, and the shared core, the same ten commands in the same order, shows a stable law being re-applied, not two rival laws.
Site Assessment
The site's position is that Deuteronomy 5 is Moses' faithful restatement of the Sinai law for a new generation, and the wording differences, including the two Sabbath motives, are complementary exposition rather than contradiction. Neither version needs to be treated as a verbatim transcript to be trustworthy.
