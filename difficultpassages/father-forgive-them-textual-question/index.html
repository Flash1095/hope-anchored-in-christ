'Father, Forgive Them' — A Textual Question

Passage
'Father, Forgive Them' — A Textual Question
Reference
Luke 23:34
Difficulty
From the cross Jesus prays, 'Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do.' It is one of the most quoted sayings of the crucifixion. But the saying is absent from some early manuscripts of Luke, and readers ask whether words they love were actually spoken, and whether their absence undermines the Gospels.
Explanations
Textual scholars note the saying is missing from several early witnesses, though it is present in many others and was known very early, and it is widely retained in modern editions, often with a note. Its absence in some copies is usually explained by scribal omission or by the difficulty of the saying itself — forgiving executioners is a hard word, and copyists sometimes remove hard words — rather than by late invention, since the sentiment matches Jesus' teaching on forgiving enemies and Stephen's parallel prayer in Acts 7. Unlike the long ending of Mark or the adultery passage, no doctrine hangs on this verse alone: Jesus' forgiveness of enemies is taught throughout the Gospels whether or not this sentence is original to Luke.
Site Assessment
Site position — the saying should be retained in reading with its textual uncertainty noted, not presented as certain or suppressed as spurious. The site's judgment is that its early attestation and coherence with Jesus' teaching make retention reasonable, while honesty requires admitting some early copies lack it.
