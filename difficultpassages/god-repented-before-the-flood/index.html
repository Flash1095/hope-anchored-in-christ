God Repented Before the Flood

Genesis 6:6 says 'it repented the LORD that he had made man on the earth, and it grieved him at his heart.' Yet Numbers 23:19 says God 'is not a man, that he should repent,' and other passages insist God does not change his mind the way people do. Taken at face value, the two claims collide: either God does change his mind, and the denial passages are wrong, or he does not, and Genesis 6 is misleading about what God felt and did.

Explanations

The standard resolution turns on the Hebrew word behind 'repented' and on the kind of language being used. The word (nacham) covers grief, sorrow, and a change of course in response to changed circumstances, not only the human experience of admitting a mistake. Genesis 6 uses vivid, relational language: God grieves over human wickedness as a person grieves, and his dealings with humanity take a new course in judgment and then in preservation through Noah. Numbers 23:19, by contrast, is about God's covenant faithfulness: unlike a human ruler, he does not lie, break a promise, or reverse a settled purpose because of pressure or new information. On this reading both statements are true at different levels: God's character and promises do not waver, while his expressed responses within history genuinely reflect the changing state of his creatures. A related passage, Exodus 32:14, where God 'repented of the evil' he had threatened against Israel after Moses interceded, is treated in the existing question 'Does prayer change God's mind?' rather than as a separate row here.

Site Assessment

The site's position is that 'God repented' in Genesis 6 is sorrowful, relational language describing God's real grief and his changed dealings with a wicked generation, not a claim that God discovered a mistake or abandoned a promise. The denial in Numbers 23:19 guards God's faithfulness, and the two passages answer different questions.
