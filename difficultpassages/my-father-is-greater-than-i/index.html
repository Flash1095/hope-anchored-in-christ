'My Father Is Greater Than I'

Jesus says, 'My Father is greater than I.' Readers who hold that Jesus is fully God ask how the Father can be greater if Father and Son are equal; readers who deny his divinity cite the verse as proof that Jesus himself disclaimed equality. The verse is a favourite proof-text on both sides, which is itself a sign that it needs careful reading.

Explanations

The orthodox reading, held across the early creeds and the mainstream traditions, is that 'greater' refers to role and position in the work of redemption, not to essence or being. The Son, in becoming man and in going to the Father, stands in the position of one sent and obedient; the Father, as sender, is greater in that order of mission. John himself supplies the balancing texts: 'I and my Father are one' (John 10:30) and the Word 'was God' (John 1:1), so John cannot mean by 14:28 that the Son is a lesser being without contradicting his own Gospel. Non-trinitarian readings take 'greater' as ontological, but that requires setting aside those balancing passages.

Site Assessment

Site position — 'greater' denotes the Son's incarnate mission and order, not a lesser divinity. This is the site's doctrinal position, stated as such: the verse is not, on its own, a denial of Christ's deity, and it should be read with John's whole portrait of Jesus, not as an isolated slogan.
