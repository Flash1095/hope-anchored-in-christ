Jonah Angry at Mercy

Passage
Jonah Angry at Mercy
Reference
Jonah 4:1-11
Difficulty
Jonah is angry that God spares Nineveh, argues with God about a plant, and wishes to die. The book ends with a question and no answer from Jonah. Readers ask why the story leaves its prophet unrepentant, and whether God is unfair to care about Israel's enemies.
Explanations
The book is a satirical prophetic critique, and Jonah is its foil, not its hero. His anger exposes a nationalism that wants God's mercy for Israel and God's judgment for Assyria, while God asks whether he should not pity a great city full of people who cannot discern right from left, and also much cattle. The plant that shades Jonah for a day and then withers is the argument in miniature: Jonah pities a plant he did not make, while begrudging God pity for a city he did make. The unanswered ending turns the question on the reader.
Site Assessment
Site assessment: God's mercy to repentant enemies is the book's point, and Jonah's anger is the error it exposes. The ending is deliberately open because the reader is meant to answer it.
