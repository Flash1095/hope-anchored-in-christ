No One Can See God's Face, Yet They Saw God

Passage
No One Can See God's Face, Yet They Saw God
Reference
Exodus 33:18-23
Difficulty
God tells Moses, 'Thou canst not see my face: for there shall no man see me, and live.' Yet the same book says Moses spoke with God 'face to face' (Exodus 33:11), that the elders of Israel 'saw the God of Israel' on Mount Sinai (Exodus 24:9-11), and Jacob earlier claimed, 'I have seen God face to face, and my life is preserved' (Genesis 32:30). Either seeing God is fatal, and these reports are impossible, or it is not, and the warning to Moses is empty.
Explanations
The resolution most readers adopt distinguishes between seeing God's full, unveiled glory and seeing a mediated, accommodated appearance of God. In Exodus 33 itself, God offers Moses a middle way: Moses will see God's 'back parts' as his glory passes by, sheltered in the cleft of the rock, because the full sight would be more than a mortal can bear. The other 'seeings' are of the same accommodated kind: a visible manifestation, a form, a presence on the mountain, described in human language because human language has nothing else to use. The 'face to face' of Exodus 33:11 is explained by its own context as the intimacy of the conversation, like friends speaking, not as a claim that Moses stared at God's essence. Later Scripture keeps the same distinction, insisting no one has seen God in his fullness while also recording appearances of God in forms people could survive.
Site Assessment
The site's position is that there is no contradiction once the distinction is kept: no mortal survives an unveiled sight of God's glory, and every biblical 'seeing' of God is a gracious, veiled manifestation suited to human limits. The warning to Moses is the rule that explains the other passages, not a rival to them.
