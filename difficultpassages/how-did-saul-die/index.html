How Did Saul Die?

1 Samuel 31 says Saul, wounded by archers, fell on his own sword, and his armour-bearer did the same. In 2 Samuel 1 an Amalekite tells David he finished Saul off at Saul's request. The two accounts cannot both be literal history as told, so readers ask which is true.

Explanations

The agreed reading treats the narrator's account in 1 Samuel 31 as the event, and the Amalekite's story as self-serving fabrication. The Amalekite arrives with Saul's crown and bracelet — plunder he could have taken from the corpse — and has an obvious motive: to win favour with David as Saul's successor. David himself treats the claim as a confession of killing the LORD's anointed and executes him, which makes sense whether or not David believed him, since claiming the deed was itself the crime. The tension is therefore between a reliable narrator and an unreliable character, not between two narrators.

Site Assessment

Site assessment: Saul died by his own hand on Mount Gilboa, as 1 Samuel 31 narrates. The Amalekite's version is best read as a lie told for reward, and David's judgment falls on the claim itself.
